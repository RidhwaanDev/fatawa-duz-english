### TITLE
Annulment of the marriage on account of the husband's constant absences

### QUESTION
A woman's husband suddenly disappeared five months after the marriage, telling neither his wife nor anyone else. He came back after a good many days, and a few days later he disappeared again without any word; he returned after some time. He gives no sensible reason for staying away; being absent has become a habit with him. He neither works anywhere nor runs any business or the like. Whatever help the wife used to receive, she used to give some of it to her husband. On one occasion he squandered a large sum of money on the pretext of a business venture, and he also stole his wife's belongings and sold them. He has given his wife neither a house to live in nor made any arrangement for expenses. Those who have counselled him say that the husband will not mend his ways. In view of these circumstances, will the woman have the right to have the marriage annulled or not? What is the sharia's decision in this matter?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, if the woman's statement accords with the true state of affairs, she has the right to bring her case before the Jamʿiyyat al-ʿUlamāʾ. After investigating the matter fully, if the woman's claim is correct, those gentlemen will compel the husband to fulfil her rights; and if he is not prepared to do so, they will compel him to pronounce a divorce. If the oppressive husband is not prepared to accept either course, then, after granting a further month's grace, the Jamʿiyyat al-ʿUlamāʾ will be authorised, upon the woman's demand, to effect a separation between the two. After the separation the woman may complete her ʿiddah (the waiting period) and contract a marriage elsewhere.

Majmūʿah Qawānīn-e Islāmī states:

A man who is known to be alive but whose whereabouts are unknown; or whose whereabouts are known but who neither comes to his wife nor sends for her nor pays her maintenance, so that the woman is left in severe hardship and distress — in such a case the woman may petition the qāḍī for a separation in order to be delivered from her oppressive husband. After the petition has been received:

(a) The qāḍī will direct the wife to establish, by means of two witnesses and an oath, her marriage to the absent husband and the obligation of maintenance upon him — namely, that he did not provide her with maintenance when he left, nor has he sent any maintenance, nor has he made any arrangement here, nor has she waived it.

(b) Once the marriage and the obligation of maintenance have been established, the qāḍī should send an order to the man: either present yourself and fulfil your wife's rights, or send for her (provided there is no danger in her going there), or make arrangements for her from where you are; failing that, divorce her. And if you accept none of these things, then we shall ourselves effect a separation between the two of you.

Now, if the husband complies with the qāḍī's order, well and good; otherwise the qāḍī should grant a further month's grace — or, at his own discretion, a somewhat longer period — and then effect the separation upon the woman's demand, and this separation will count as a revocable divorce.

(مجموعہ قوانین اسلامی: ۱۹۷، دفعہ ۷۸، مسلم پرسنل لا بورڈ) ۔
