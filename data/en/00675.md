### TITLE
Making up missed prayers in place of the regular sunnah prayers when many have been missed

### QUESTION
A man owes a very large number of missed prayers, and making them all up is extremely difficult. If he were to pray the missed prayers instead of the sunnah prayers, is there room for that or not?

### ANSWER
Although making up missed prayers is a weighty matter, the jurists have still held that the regular sunnah prayers must be performed. So in the case described he should pray the sunnahs after the obligatory prayers, and make up the missed prayers in place of the optional ones.

Consider what Shāmī states:

الاشتغال بقضاء الفوائت أولى وأهم من النوافل إلا السنن المفروضة. (شامي: ۲/۷٤، سعيد. وكذا في الهندية: ۱/۱۲٥. وكذا في حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح: ۲٤۳، قديمي)

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

The emphasised sunnahs and tarāwīḥ should not be given up in order to make up missed prayers. In fact the jurists give even the optional prayers reported in the sunnah — such as the mid-morning prayer, the awwābīn prayer and ṣalāt al-tasbīḥ — precedence over making up missed prayers. But this seems to apply where, judging by the outward circumstances, a person can expect to clear his missed prayers before he dies. If the missed prayers are very many and his remaining life appears short, then in principle he must give the missed prayers precedence over the optional ones. (احسن الفتاوی: ٤/۱۹)

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states:

The emphasised sunnah prayers attached to the prayer times should not be left, and missed prayers should be made up in one's free time. It is clear that making up missed prayers is the weightier matter, but if both can be managed — making up the missed prayers while not leaving the emphasised sunnahs — that is better. (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: ٤/۳٤٤)

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
