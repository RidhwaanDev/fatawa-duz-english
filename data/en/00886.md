### TITLE
Conveying reward through recitation broadcast on Radio Islam

### QUESTION
Radio Islam broadcasts the recitation of various reciters. Some people give a sum of money so that the reward of it may reach their deceased relatives. Is it correct to have reward conveyed in this way? And is it correct to take money for it or not? Also, on many occasions the reciter does not recite himself; instead the recitation is played from a tape recording. Will reward be gained by listening to that as well? And will the reward reach the deceased?

### ANSWER
If a reciter takes a sum of money and then recites the Qur'an in order to convey the reward, that is impermissible. But if the reciter takes nothing, and the money is for booking the airtime on Radio Islam and for the station's other expenses, then there can be room for it. Moreover, since reciting the Noble Qur'an earns reward, listening to it also brings reward, so conveying that reward is correct and valid too. Consider what Mishkāt states:

عن أبي سعيد الخدري رضي الله عنه قال جلست في عصابة من ضعفاء المهاجرين وان بعضهم ليستتر ببعض من العري وقارئ يقرأ علينا إذ جاء رسول الله ﷺ فقام علينا فلما قام رسول الله ﷺ سكت القاري فسلم ثم قال ما كنتم تصنعون قلنا كنا نستمع إلى كتاب الله فقال الحمد ﷲ الذي جعل من امتي من أمرت أن أصبر نفسي معهم قال فجلس وسطنا ليعدل بنفسه فينا ثم قال بيده هكذا فتحلقوا وبرزت وجوههم له فقال أبشروا يا معشر صعاليك المهاجرين بالنور التام يوم القيمة تدخلون الجنة قبل أغنياء الناس بنصف يوم وذلك خمس مئة سنة. رواه أبو داود. (مشكوة شريف: ۱/۱۹۱، فضائل القرآن).

As far as reward is concerned, it is realised by the words of Allah's speech falling upon the ear, and it is obtained by listening in silence and with due regard for proper manners.

Consider the evidence that reward is earned for listening to recitation from a tape recording:

قال الله تعالى: ﴿وإذا قرئ القرآن فاستمعوا له وأنصتوا لعلكم ترحمون﴾ (سورة الأعراف: ۲۰٤).

This noble verse shows that when the Noble Qur'an is recited it should be listened to, whoever the reciter may be.

Allah did not say "وإذا قرأتم". This shows that reward is gained by listening from a tape recording as well.

ʿAllāmah Shaykh Muḥammad Bakhīt al-Muṭīʿī al-Ḥanafī, the Mufti of the Egyptian Lands, writes in his treatise "أحكام قراءة الفونوغراف" (ص: ۹):

ومتي علمت أن ما يسمع من الصندوق من ألفاظ القرآن قرآن حقيقة وهو كلام الله بلا شك وأن صدوره منه وسماعه كصدوره من الإنسان وسماعه فإذا صدرت الكلمات القرآنية من ذلك الصندوق مستوفية للشروط بدون أن يكون بها خلل وقصد من رسم مخارج تلك الكلمات في الأسطوانات سماعها للعظة والتدبير فلا شك في الجواز وفي أن السماع عبادة.

Fatāwā Bayyināt states:

Since he is hearing the speech of Allah from the cassette, and the reverence for Allah's speech in his heart is increasing, and he is keeping his ears safe from other sinful things, he will certainly receive reward for listening to the recorded recitation. (فتاوی بینات، جلد چہارم، ص ٤۲۲) ۔

Besides, if there is sin in listening to songs from a tape recording, then there should be reward in listening to recitation from it. Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Yūsuf Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote that there is no reward in listening to recitation from a tape recording; that is his own view, and it is beyond our understanding. See: (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ۳/۲۲۸ ۔ ۲۳۱) ۔

Mawlānā Khālid Sayfullāh says:

The etiquette of listening to the Qur'an applies to every case in which the words of Allah's speech reach a Muslim's ears, whether from the tongue of the reciter himself or by some other means. So the etiquette of listening — keeping silent and giving one's attention to the Noble Qur'an — is necessary when listening to the Qur'an from a tape recording as well; and since it is on this very basis that the listener earns reward, he will, if Allah wills, receive the reward too. (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۱/۱۷۰) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
