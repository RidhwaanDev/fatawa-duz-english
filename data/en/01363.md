### TITLE
The ruling on discharging zakat by giving it to a welfare organisation

### QUESTION
We have started a welfare organisation which collects zakat from people and conveys it to those entitled to it. Those entitled are of two kinds: designated and undesignated. From the designated we have permission to collect zakat money for them and to spend it on them; sometimes, after this has been spent, some zakat money is left over, and we spend it on the undesignated. What we wish to know, with regard to the undesignated recipients, is when the donors' zakat is discharged: when the donors deposit it in the fund, or when we convey it to those entitled? Sometimes a whole year passes in distributing the surplus money. Is it correct to do this or not?

### ANSWER
In our times, in common usage, welfare organisations act as agents on behalf of those entitled to zakat, since they come under the ruling of the collectors of zakat. Accordingly, the zakat is discharged as soon as the donors deposit the zakat money in the fund.

The distinction between designated and undesignated takes effect only when the designation comes from the donors. If a donor states at the time of giving that the zakat is to be given to such-and-such designated persons, then it will be necessary to give it to those designated persons alone; but if there is no such stipulation, the money may be given to anyone entitled to zakat.

In the case asked about, the division of recipients has been made by the welfare organisation and there is no designation on the donors' part, so in this case the designated and the undesignated are equal in entitlement. Payment should not, however, be delayed without excuse; and if there is some sound reason for the delay, there is scope for it.

قال في البحر الرائق: بخلاف ما إذا ضاعت في يد الساعي لأن يده كيد الفقراء كذا في المحيط. (البحر الرائق: ۲/۲۲۷، دار المعرفة). (وكذا في فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۲۷۰، سعيد).

Most of our authorities in fatwa have accepted the principal of a madrasah as the agent both of the students and of the donors. Because he is the students' agent, the zakat of the zakat-payers is discharged the moment the principal and those under him take possession of it. Hence, if it is destroyed without negligence before being spent on the students, no compensation will be due from them, since in relation to the donors they are agents and trustees; and because they are the students' agents, the donors' zakat is discharged. Furthermore, since no natural person has complete ownership of money that has lain collected for several years, the zakat of those years will not have to be paid either. Ḥaḍrat Aqdas Mawlānā Khalīl Aḥmad Muḥaddith Sahāranpūrī (may his secret be sanctified) says that in relation to the donors the people of the madrasah are like the agents of the public treasury, and that they are the representatives of the students and the recipients; therefore zakat will not be due on it, nor can the donors take their zakat back.

Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) has also recorded Mawlānā Khalīl Aḥmad's answer in Imdād al-Fatāwā, and it removes every doubt and hesitation. Kindly consider Ḥaḍrat's wording:

Our patron and guide, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Khalīl Aḥmad (may his blessings endure), al-salāmu ʿalaykum wa raḥmat Allāh. Two subsidiary questions call for investigation. First: the money that comes to a madrasah — if it is an endowment, then where is the benefit derived while the substance itself remains intact? And if it is the donor's property, then after his death must it be returned to his heirs?

Answer: honoured and respected Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā al-Ḥāfiẓ al-Ḥājj Mawlwī Ashraf ʿAlī (may his glory endure), al-salāmu ʿalaykum wa raḥmat Allāh wa barakātuh. In this humble one's view the money of madrasahs is not an endowment; rather, the people of the madrasah, like the agents of the public treasury, are the representatives of the donors and of the recipients. Therefore zakat will not be due on it, nor can the donors take it back.

(1) The agents of the public treasury are appointed by the ruler, and the ruler possesses general authority, so he can become everyone's agent; in the case under comparison there is no general authority, so how will he become the recipients' agent, since appointment as agent is neither explicit nor implicit — whereas in the case compared with it, it is implicit, in that all are under his obedience and he must be obeyed?

In this servant's view the ruler has two attributes: the first is government, whose fruit is the carrying out of the prescribed punishments and retaliation; the second is the administration of public rights. In the first matter no one can take his place; in the second, those who bind and loose can take his place when the need arises. The reason is that the appointment of the ruler itself depends upon the opinion and counsel of those who bind and loose, and this belongs to the sphere of administration. Hence the financial administration of madrasahs, undertaken with the consent of the owners and the students for the preservation of the religion, will be valid all the more. And do reflect a little: the fact that the appointment of an imam by the public for the ordering of the Friday prayer is held valid may well be the parallel to this among the particular cases. That is all. Wa'l-salām. Khalīl Aḥmad, may he be pardoned. (Imdād al-Fatāwā 6/259–263)

Tadhkirat al-Rashīd likewise records:

Difficulty: the money that comes to a madrasah as donations and so forth — is it an endowment or owned property? If it is an endowment, its substance must be preserved and spending it by way of consumption is impermissible. If it is owned property and the principal is merely an agent, then should the donor of the contribution die, it becomes the right of the poor and of the heirs, and it is incumbent on the agent to investigate this. This same difficulty applies to the public treasury of the age of the Lawgiver (peace be upon him) and the caliphs (may Allah be pleased with them). I have thought about it a great deal, but it was not resolved by the principles of the Sacred Law; and the mixing together of different donations ought to constitute consumption, so that when property whose ownership has thus been extinguished is spent, it would be a voluntary donation and the spender would be liable to the owners. If this is so, the people of a madrasah, or the treasurer of an association, are in serious difficulty. I hope you will give a correct and reassuring answer.

The answer: the principal of a madrasah is the caretaker and deputy of all the students, just as a ruler is the deputy of the whole community. So whatever anyone gives to the principal, the principal's taking possession is itself the students' taking possession. By his taking possession it leaves the donor's ownership and becomes the property of the students; and although they are unknown in number and identity, the deputy is a determinate person. Hence, after the donor's death, his heirs can have no ownership in it. The principal may in certain respects also be the donor's agent. In any case, this is neither endowed property, nor will it become the property of the donor's heirs, nor does it remain the donor's own property. واللہ تعالی اعلم۔ (Tadhkirat al-Rashīd, p. 164, "Juristic difficulties and disputed questions")

After the findings of these elders (may Allah have mercy on them), Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ too retracted his earlier fatwa, accepted the findings of these authorities, and published this in Imdād al-Muftīn. Kindly consider the passage:

بسم اللہ الرحمن الرحیم، الحمد للہ و کفی و سلام علی عبادہ الذین اصطفی۔ اما بعد؛

My treatise on the question of transferring ownership of zakat, which was written in the year 1361 AH and has been published again and again as part of Imdād al-Muftīn, stated that the ruling on madrasah principals differs from the ruling on the collectors of charity, and it did so on the basis of a doubt raised by my master Ḥaḍrat Ḥakīm al-Ummat (may Allah have mercy on him) himself. But when Ḥaḍrat laid that doubt before Raʾs al-Fuqahāʾ Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Khalīl Aḥmad Sahāranpūrī (may Allah have mercy on him) and he wrote out an answer to it, Ḥaḍrat Ḥakīm al-Ummat's doubt was removed and he was satisfied with the very ruling that the said master had written: namely that the madrasah principals of today, and those appointed by them to collect donations, come under the ruling of the collectors of charity and are thus agents of the poor. The donors appoint them as agents only to this extent, that they accepted these men as the agents of the poor and handed their donations over to them; so once the money passed into their possession in their capacity as agents of the poor, it became the property of the poor and the zakat of the zakat-payers was discharged. The matter was made plain by this much alone; but further support and corroboration came from the fatwa of the Abū Ḥanīfah of his age, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Rashīd Aḥmad Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him), written in Tadhkirat al-Rashīd in answer to a question of Mawlānā Ṣādiq al-Yaqīn (may Allah have mercy on him), in which it is stated explicitly that even though these poor students are unknown in number and identity, agency on their behalf is nonetheless established for the principals of the madrasah by custom, and their taking possession is the poor's taking possession.

After this reasoned fatwa of Ḥaḍrat Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him), the finding of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Khalīl Aḥmad (may his secret be sanctified), and Ḥaḍrat Ḥakīm al-Ummat's (may Allah have mercy on him) acceptance and endorsement of it, no difficulty remained in the question. Even so, when this lowly one came to Pakistan and established the Dār al-ʿUlūm in Karachi, he adopted this arrangement by way of precaution: on the admission form of the students who are admitted to the Dār al-ʿUlūm, a text of appointment as agent is printed on behalf of every student in favour of the principal of the madrasah or whomever he may appoint; and every student who enrols formally appoints the principal of the madrasah as his agent both to receive zakat on his behalf and to spend it on the needs of the poor at large. In this way the principal of the madrasah is the agent of the particular students admitted each year and is authorised on their behalf to spend on all the students; and in this way not even the doubt about their being unknown in number and identity remains. I therefore retract the passage published on this question in Imdād al-Muftīn and accept the very decision reached by all these elders: namely that the madrasah principals of the present age, and those appointed by them who collect donations or zakat, receive it in their capacity as agents of the poor, and that the zakat of the zakat-payers is discharged as soon as it reaches their possession.

An essential caution: this finding has brought madrasah principals one convenience, in that they no longer need to record each person's zakat wealth and its account separately, and if a donor should pass away before it is spent there is no need to return it to his heirs; and the donors too have gained the benefit that their zakat is discharged at once. But a great burden for the hereafter has fallen upon the necks of madrasah principals, for they are the agents of thousands of poor people whose names and addresses it is not even easy to record and remember, so that if, God forbid, some error occurs in spending this wealth, pardon might be sought from them. Therefore, if the principals of madrasahs spend this wealth on any purpose other than the needs of the poor students, it will be so unpardonable an offence that making good the loss will not be in their power. For this very reason it is incumbent upon all of them that the donation money of madrasahs be spent with the utmost care on none but those needs which pertain to the poor students: their food and clothing, for example, their medicine and treatment, their accommodation needs, the purchase of books for them, and so on. واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم۔ (Imdād al-Muftīn, p. 895, Dār al-Ishāʿat)
