### TITLE
Objections to the sacrifice and their answers

### QUESTION
Objections raised against the sacrifice, and their answers.

### ANSWER
Had this objection been raised some time ago, it is possible that some simple-minded people would have been caught in its net; but nowadays it is the age of fridges, freezers and cold stores, and nobody's meat goes to waste. Indeed, I say in jest that today the fridge and the freezer have taken the place of the poor and the destitute as well: people give less to the poor, and the fridge's share is the larger one.

Yet even in former times this objection was baseless and worthless. In days gone by people ate the meat and fed the poor with it; they would cut the meat into strips, salt it, dry it and eat it for months on end. And if in some country the meat is wasted or buried, that is a defect in that country's system — what fault is it of the sharīʿah? In some countries, when something goes wrong with cars, they throw them away, and graveyards of old cars are to be seen here and there. If, in this matter, someone were to object to Japan and Germany, "Why do you make so many cars that graveyards of them have come into being?", such an objection would be meaningless and as scattered dust. Why do you not set good mechanics to work and repair them, or send them to the poor countries?

Moreover, if someone raises the difficulty that Allah Most High has promised sustenance and yet a great many people die of famine, then among the several answers to it is this: Allah Most High creates sustenance for everyone. Creating sustenance is the work of Allah Most High; conveying it is our work. Sometimes grain is produced in greater quantity in one place, sometimes in another. If we do not convey the sustenance, the fault is ours; it is not the fault of the order of nature.

The second objection: it is that countless animals are destroyed on account of the sacrifices, which is a cause of a shortage of animals. But this objection too is proof of ignorance of the way of Allah Most High and of the law of nature. It is the law of Allah Most High that whatever people expend in large quantities, when it is a thing of need, Allah Most High creates in great abundance — as with water, air and fire, which people expend without measure and which Allah Most High creates without measure. There was a time when camels were reared and slaughtered in great numbers in certain countries; later this practice declined in those countries and the breeding of camels declined too. In some places people gave up making use of horses, and horses became few. In earlier times people made use of their memories, and people had excellent memories; now that the age of notebooks and computers has come, the memories of old are no more, الا ما شاء اللہ ۔

Allah Most High says:

﴿وما أنفقتم من شيء فهو يخلفه﴾ [سبأ، ۳۹]

Whatever you spend, Allah Most High will grant a successor and a substitute for it. Moreover, the animals are not wasted; they are eaten. Otherwise the animals that are slaughtered every day would presumably also fall, in the objectors' view, under the head of extravagance. This is an objection the Hindus too raise: that you wrong the animals by slaughtering countless numbers of them every day. Or someone might say: why do people eat tens of millions of eggs every day — these too are wasted? But whatever has been eaten has not been wasted.

The third objection: it is said that if you wish to help the poor, then give them cash, which the poor can use for their needs. This objection too is proof of the objector's ignorance. If a ruler were to ask a man for a horse of fine breed and this Plato of the age were to send fifty thousand rand instead of the horse, he would probably come to understand that he deserved punishment. Allah Most High asks an animal of us, and we are to give rand and cash — if this is not a defect of understanding, what is it? Besides, many poor people are miserly: if such a one receives cash, he may well feed his wife and children lentils and bread even on the day of ʿĪd and put the money away, and so be deprived of the sunnah of hospitality of Ibrāhīm (upon him be blessings and peace) and of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

The fourth objection: some people say, concerning the sacrifice, that it is not necessary to sacrifice during the days of slaughter; rather the price should be gathered and handed over to the government so that works of public welfare may be carried out. And they argue from the following words of the author of al-Hidāyah. Consider the passage:

والتضحية فيها أفضل من التصدق بثمن الأضحية لا نها تقع واجبة أوسنة والتصدق تطوع محض فتفضل عليه. (الهداية: ٤/٤٤٦، شركة علمية).

That is, offering the sacrifice during the days of sacrifice is superior to giving the price of the sacrificial animal in charity.

See — the author of al-Hidāyah holds offering the sacrifice to be superior to giving its price in charity; but from this it becomes clear that he too holds that by giving the price in charity the sacrifice is discharged. See further: (قرآنی فیصلے، ص ۱۱۳) ۔ What is the answer to this passage?

The answer: (1) All that the author of al-Hidāyah means by this passage is that offering the sacrifice during the days of slaughter is superior to giving money in charity, both in itself and in its quality. In quality, because the sacrifice is obligatory or a sunnah while giving money in charity is neither obligatory nor sunnah but merely voluntary — and it is obvious that an obligatory or sunnah act of worship is in every case superior to and better than a voluntary one. And in itself it is superior because in the sacrifice there is both the offering of a life and the giving of wealth, whereas in giving money in charity there is only the giving of wealth; and it is clearer than the sun that the combination of two acts of worship is superior to a single one. (ملخص از فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند، جلدِ اول، ص ٦۹۲) ۔

(2) The real answer to this difficulty is that being "superior" is not incompatible with being obligatory and binding. On p. 6 of al-Bukhārī there is the hadith:

"أي الإسلام أفضل قال: من سلم المسلمون من لسانه ويده"

Protecting the Muslims from injury and harm is obligatory, and yet the word "superior" has been used for it. Then on p. 8 there is the hadith:

"أي العمل أفضل فقال: إيمان بالله ورسوله قيل: ثم ماذا قال: الجهاد في سبيل الله قيل: ثم ماذا قال: حج مبرور"

See: faith, jihad and an accepted hajj are among the obligatory duties, and they are "superior" as well. Then on p. 76 there is the hadith:

"أي العمل أحب"

and in answer to it there is mention of prayer at its proper time, good conduct towards one's parents, and jihad. All of these are "most beloved" and "best", and they are necessary too.

Likewise, in the noble verse:

﴿فاسعوا إلى ذكر الله وذروا البيع ذلك خير لكم﴾ [الجمعة: ۹].

attendance at the Friday prayer is both "better" and binding. In the noble verse:

﴿ولو آمن أهل الكتاب لكان خيراً لهم﴾ [آل عمران: ۱۱۰].

faith on the part of the People of the Book is "better" and best, and it is obligatory as well. In:

﴿والصلح خير﴾ [النساء: ۱۲۸]

reconciliation, as set against mutual quarrelling, is "better", and as set against quarrelling it is also necessary. In:

"الصلاة خير من النوم"

prayer is both "better" and obligatory. In the noble verse:

﴿ولا تقولوا ثلاثة انتهوا خيراً لكم﴾ [النساء: ۱۷۱].

keeping clear of the doctrine of a trinity is both obligatory and "better". In the same way, here the sacrifice is both obligatory and "better", because what is obligatory — or, as some imāms hold, an emphasized sunnah — is superior to voluntary charity.

The author of al-Hidāyah (may Allah have mercy on him) declared the sacrifice to be obligatory. Consider:

الأضحية واجبة على كل مسلم مقيم موسر في يوم الأضحى

(الهداية: ٤/٤٤۳، كتاب الأضحية).

Yes, this difficulty does arise, that the noun of preference requires the act itself to be present on the other side as well — that is, in the thing surpassed — as in "أفضل الصحابة أبوبكر رضي الله عنه", where the other Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) also possess merit, but Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr (رضي الله عنه) possesses greater merit.

One answer to this is that if a person gave voluntary charity on the day of sacrifice and did not offer the sacrifice, he does obtain the reward of the voluntary charity, even though the punishment for the obligation has become a collar about his neck — just as, if someone shaves his beard while keeping a voluntary fast, he obtains the reward of the fast, even though the sin of shaving the beard has fallen upon him.

Yes, sometimes the noun of preference comes in the sense of the act itself, as in:

﴿أصحاب الجنة يومئذٍ خير مستقراً وأحسن مقيلاً﴾ [الفرقان: ۲٤]

This does not mean that the abode of the people of Paradise is very good and that of the people of Hell somewhat good; rather the abode of the people of Paradise is good and that of the people of Hell is evil. But in the passage of al-Hidāyah this answer does not serve; the first answer is the definite one.

Some wisdoms of the sacrifice:

We now set out briefly some of the wisdoms of the sacrifice, so that they may serve as collyrium for the eyes of those who deny it.

(1) The sacrifice is a memorial of Ibrāhīm (upon him be peace) and of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. To keep this memorial alive is a mark of good sense and of standing firm upon Islam. The sacrifice is, as it were, a magnificent memorial of the patience of Ismāʿīl (upon him be peace), of his mother and of Ibrāhīm (upon him be peace), and of their not shrinking from the greatest of sacrifices for the sake of the religion. How can a lover abandon the name and the ways of his beloved?

(2) In the sacrifice there is a refutation of the idolaters and the calf-worshippers: these animals are for us to eat; they are not our deities.

(3) Financial worship is of two kinds. The first kind: spending cash, and spending commodities such as wheat, barley and the like. The second kind: spending animals. In ʿĪd al-Fiṭr the first has been kept in view, and in ʿĪd al-Aḍḥā the second, so that both ways may be acted upon and the poor may remain glad and happy, the mouth and the heart both pleased.

(4) On the day of ʿĪd, Allah Most High has made the poor equal to the rich, so that all may share in the joy of the ʿĪd and in the eating of meat.

(5) So that there may be a collective opposition to the idolaters: they slaughter animals to please someone other than Allah, or in the name of someone other than Allah, whereas we slaughter to please Allah Most High, in the name of Allah Most High.

(6) Those who rear animals have a boundless love for them; let them offer them in sacrifice on the day of ʿĪd, so that they may sacrifice a beloved thing for the pleasure of Allah Most High.

(7) In the sacrifice there is a display of the power of Allah Most High: Muslims offer sacrifice for the exaltation of the name of Allah Most High, and in return Allah Most High will create countless animals.

(8) In the sacrifice there is a lesson in giving one's life in the path of Allah Most High: Allah Most High asked for the life of my animal and I gave it; and if at some juncture Allah Most High should ask for my own life, I shall not withhold it.

I gave my life, and the life was His own gift;
the truth is, His due was never paid.

For this reason it is better not to cut one's nails and hair during the ten days of Dhū al-Ḥijjah but to cut them on the day of ʿĪd, saying: O Allah, today, on the day of ʿĪd, I have sacrificed my nails and my hair; and should the need arise, my life too is at Your service.

The lover's work is to pour out his heart's blood at the beloved's feet;
the beloved's work is to sprinkle salt upon the hidden wound.

(9) The sacrifice is a means of the increase of animals: as the Muslims and the People of the Book slaughter lawful animals, their stock grows, and grows greatly; whereas they do not slaughter dogs, and dogs are not a tenth of a tenth of the other animals — indeed not even one per cent of them.

(10) On the day of ʿĪd all people are the guests of Allah Most High, and a guest should be fed the chief of foods, which is the meat of lawful animals.

(11) The demand of servitude is that even when the wisdom and the reason of a command are not understood, a man should still act upon it and thereby reach the highest summit of servitude.

Maḥmūd of Ghaznah was reproached for his love of Ayāz. He called for a precious diamond and ordered all his slaves, Ayāz among them, to break it. The other slaves, holding it contrary to wisdom to break it, disobeyed the order; but Ayāz, displaying the perfection of servitude, struck it with a stone and shattered the jewel to pieces, and thus reached the highest summit of belovedness in the eyes of his master.

Mawlānā Rūmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

To break the command is harder than to break the pearl;
inevitably I girded my loins to break it.

Ayāz said: to break the pearl was far easier than to break my master's command, and so I girded myself to break the pearl.

﴿ولكن يناله التقوي منكم﴾ [الحج: ۳۷]

points to just this, that it is not the blood and the flesh that reach Allah Most High, but sincerity and devotion to Allah, the guarding against disobedience to the command of Allah, and the fear of Him. واللہ تعالی اعلم بالصواب ۔

Some rulings relating to the takbīrāt al-tashrīq

Question: What is the ruling on the takbīrāt al-tashrīq?

Answer: It is obligatory to recite the takbīr al-tashrīq once, in a loud voice, after each of the twenty-three obligatory prayers from the Fajr of ʿArafah — that is, the 9th of Dhū al-Ḥijjah — up to the ʿAṣr of the 13th of Dhū al-Ḥijjah. The takbīr al-tashrīq is:

"الله أكبر الله أكبر لا إله إلا الله الله أكبر الله أكبر ولله الحمد"

It is obligatory upon everyone: man and woman, resident and traveller, imām, follower and one praying alone. This is the school of the two companions (may Allah have mercy on them), and the fatwa is upon it. Women, however, should recite it in a low voice.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم

Question: How many times is the takbīr al-tashrīq obligatory?

Answer: It is obligatory once; in the view of some muftis three times is permissible, and twice is an innovation.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم

Question: If the imām forgets the takbīr, what should the followers do?

Answer: The followers should say it at once, so that the imām is reminded; they should not wait for the imām.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم

Question: Should the takbīrāt al-tashrīq be said after the ʿĪd al-Aḍḥā prayer or not?

Answer: They should be said, because in the view of some of the shaykhs it is obligatory.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم

Question: If someone misses a prayer during the days of the takbīrāt al-tashrīq, should the takbīr al-tashrīq be said after that missed prayer or not?

Answer: If he is making up the prayer during the days of the takbīrāt al-tashrīq, saying the takbīr is obligatory; otherwise it is not obligatory.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم

Question: What are the days of tashrīq, and how many are they?

Answer: The seventh of Dhū al-Ḥijjah is called Yawm al-Zīnah, the eighth Yawm al-Tarwiyah, the ninth Yawm ʿArafah, the tenth Yawm al-Naḥr, the eleventh Yawm al-Qarr, the twelfth Yawm al-Nafr al-Awwal and the thirteenth Yawm al-Nafr al-Thānī; and the 11th, 12th and 13th are also called the days of tashrīq. The tenth is a day of slaughter and not a day of tashrīq; the 13th is a day of tashrīq and not a day of slaughter; while the 11th and 12th are shared between the days of slaughter and the days of tashrīq.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم

Question: If a person forgets the takbīr al-tashrīq after the prayer, what should he do?

Answer: If he remembers while still in the mosque, he should say it; and if he has already left the mosque, he should not say it, but should be careful in future and should also recite istighfār.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
