### TITLE
The practice of reciting Sūrah Yā Sīn forty-one times

### QUESTION
Sometimes, in order to have our needs fulfilled, or to remove calamities, or to convey reward to the deceased, we gather in one place and complete a khatm of Sūrah Yā Sīn forty-one times. Some people, however, call this a bidʿah, saying that it is not established from the Qurʾān and the ḥadīth. Is this practice a bidʿah, or is it permissible? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
The basic principle is that it is not necessary for such practices to be established from the Qurʾān and the ḥadīth; it is sufficient that they are not contrary to the Qurʾān and the ḥadīth. The reason for this is that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to certain people: Present your practices to me — that is, if they are contrary to the Sharīʿah they will be rejected, and if not, then there is no harm in them. Since reciting Sūrah Yā Sīn forty-one times is likewise not contrary to the Qurʾān and the ḥadīth, this practice is correct — all the more so because reciting Sūrah Yā Sīn morning and evening is established from the ḥadīth, the details of which are given in the seventh volume of Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā. Reciting it forty-one times is correct in the light of the writings of some of the pious elders.

See what Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Aʿmāl-i Qurʾānī: Sūrah Yā Sīn: whatever need one recites it forty-one times for, that need is fulfilled; if a person is afraid he will be given safety, if he is ill he will be given a cure, and if he is hungry he will be satisfied. (Aʿmāl-i Qurʾānī, p. 27).

The ḥadīth states:

عن عوف بن مالك الأشجعي قال: كنا نرقي في الجاهلية فقلنا يا رسول الله كيف تري في ذلك فقال: اعرضوا على رقاكم لا بأس بالرقي ما لم يكن فيه شرك. (رواه مسلم، رقم: ۲۲۰۰، باب لا باس بالرقي مالم يكن فيه شرك).

In Aḥsan al-Fatāwā, in reply to a question of this kind, it is recorded: Since the object of this practice is ruqyah and treatment, and not reward and worship, the fact that it is not expressly established does no harm. The real prescription is to give up sins and to turn to repentance and seeking forgiveness. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 1/360).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states: It is correct to adopt any treatment or measure for protection that has been established by experience, so long as nothing impermissible is committed in it. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 20/82, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil records: The noble verse ﴿لا إله إلا أنت سبحانك إني كنت من الظالمين﴾ is greatly effective for the removal of calamities. The figure of one hundred and twenty-five thousand recitations is not established from any ḥadīth, but as a treatment for the removal of calamities the experience of the pious elders is sufficient. With reference to Aḥsan al-Fatāwā and Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah. (Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil, p. 141).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
