### TITLE
Whether Hajj is obligatory when the visa does not allow staying until the Hajj

### QUESTION
If a man went for the first time for ʿumrah in Ramadan and stayed on into Shawwāl — the new moon of Shawwāl rose while he was in Saudi Arabia — but he is not legally permitted to remain there, does Hajj become obligatory on him or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, if it is possible to extend the visa and he can afford to stay, or he can afford to go back to his own country and come again, then Hajj is obligatory on him; otherwise it is not. Some scholars, however, say that if he has the means he should have a Hajj performed on his behalf from Makkah.

See Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil:

Sometimes people arrive in Makkah in Shawwāl or Dhū al-Qaʿdah and Hajj becomes obligatory on them, but their visa is not for the Hajj season and legally they cannot stay until the Hajj. In such a case, if the obligation of Hajj still stands against them, they should make every effort to find some way of performing it.

But if legal constraints make it impossible for him to stay until the Hajj, then Hajj is not obligatory on him, because the ability to make one's way there — which the Qur'an lays down as a condition for Hajj to become obligatory — is missing in his case. Also, in view of a misunderstanding some people have, it should be understood that those who have already performed Hajj do not have Hajj become obligatory on them a second time simply because they perform ʿumrah during the Hajj season. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/256, Nuʿaymiyyah).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

If Shawwāl begins while he is there and he also has the expenses for Hajj, then Hajj becomes obligatory on him. If the government does not allow him to stay until the Hajj, there is a difference of opinion about whether Hajj is obligatory on him; the stronger view is that he must have a Hajj performed on his behalf, arranging for it to be done from Makkah itself. If he later becomes able to perform Hajj himself, he should perform it again. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/519).

See further: Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal: 4/35).
