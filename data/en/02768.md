### TITLE

The ruling on saluting the flag

### QUESTION

Nowadays in most countries there is the practice of giving a salute to the flag, and it is regarded as showing respect to the flag. From the point of view of the Sharīʿah, is this correct or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER

It should be borne in mind that the salām of the Sharīʿah is not given to the flag, that is, al-salāmu ʿalaykum and the like are not said; rather it is generally called a salute. It is like standing up out of respect, which is a customary mark of honour and a sign of love for one's homeland and of loyalty to it; so there is not much objection to it when there is a need. In India, in all likelihood, people bow before it; if that is so, then it is impermissible and Muslims must avoid it. If there is pressure from the government, then it is hoped that there will be no sin in doing it unwillingly. Consider what Muftī Kifāyat Allāh Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) says:
The salute to the flag is performed by the Muslim League as well, and it takes place in Islamic governments too. It is a national practice; it can be reformed, but to declare it without qualification an act of polytheism is not correct. (کفایت المفتی: ۹/۳۷۸، دار الاشاعت) ۔
Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:
This is a purely political matter and is the practice of governments; it takes place in Islamic governments as well. To avoid it is good; but if there is a fear of trouble, there will be no accountability for doing it unwillingly, in shā Allāh. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ٦/۲۸۸) ۔
Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal states:
Saluting the flag is a custom that has no sanction in the Sharīʿah, and it should be changed. Love of one's homeland is indeed a sign of faith, but this method of expressing love is an invention of the disbelievers, and it is not proper for Muslims to imitate the disbelievers. (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ۸/۱۵۹، طبع جدید) ۔
Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:
Nowadays in India and in certain other countries a salute is given to the national flag by bowing, by way of respect and reverence. In the Sharīʿah this act is impermissible and Muslims should avoid it; and if they are compelled to it anywhere, they should try, so far as is possible while remaining within the bounds of the law and the constitution, to have themselves exempted from it. Where, however, this is not possible, and this salute has been made part of the conditions of employment, and there is in addition a fear of hardship if that employment is lost, then the salute will be permissible for such a person with inward aversion; for this is a need, and a need, when it reaches the level of necessity, becomes a ground of permissibility for impermissible things temporarily and for the time being. "الحاجة تنزل منزلة الضرورة" and "الضرورات تبيح المحظورات" ۔ (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۱/٤٦۷، ط: نعیمیہ دیوبند) ۔
