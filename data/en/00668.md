### TITLE
Taking payment for leading the tarāwīḥ prayer

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to take a wage or a gift for leading the tarāwīḥ prayer?

### ANSWER
Taking a wage or a gift for tarāwīḥ is not correct. If a person had no prior intention of taking anything and people happened to give him something, then accepting it is permissible. This is the ruling of the scholars of Deoband. Fatāwā Shāmī states:

وأن القراءة لشيء من الدنيا لا تجوز وأن الآخذ والمعطي آثمان لأن ذلك يشبه الاستئجار على القراءة ونفس الاستئجار عليها لا يجوز فكذا ما أشبهه كما صرح بذلك في عدة كتب من مشاهير كتب المذهب وإنما أفتي المتأخرون بجواز الاستئجار على تعليم القرآن لا على التلاوة وعلّلوه بالضرورة وهي خوف ضياع القرآن ولا ضرورة في جواز الاستئجار على التلاوة كما أوضحت ذلك في شفاء العلى ل وسيأتي بعض ذلك في باب الإجارة الفاسدة إن شاء الله تعالى. (شامي: ۲/۷۳، سعيد)

Some muftis say that this is in fact a wage for leading the prayer, because the purpose is not simply to have the Qur'an recited to people, but to have it recited in the course of leading the prayer. It is therefore a wage for leading a sunnah prayer, just as if someone were to ask another to lead the Fajr prayer on a Friday and to recite Sūrat Alif Lām Mīm al-Sajdah and Sūrat al-Dahr in it: both the leading and the payment are permissible there, and so this is permissible too. Moreover, the fact that attention is paid to the tarāwīḥ imam's appearance — that he has a full beard, and that his trousers do not hang below the ankles — also shows that it is the leading of the prayer that is intended; if it were only a matter of reciting the Qur'an to people, such conditions would not be imposed. In addition, this is not, on the face of it, a wage at all, since some people give one thing and others another, some offer a small gift and some a large one. A wage is what is either customary or stipulated; here the custom of giving does exist, but the amounts differ widely.

Consider a parallel to this in a hadith:

عن أنس بن مالك رضي الله عنه أن رجلاً من كلاب سأل رسول الله ﷺ عن عسب الفحل فنهاه فقال: يا رسول الله إنا نطرق الفحل فنكرم فرخص له في الكرامة. وقال الترمذي رحمه الله: وقد رخص قوم في قبول الكرامة على ذلك. (رواه الترمذي وقال: هذا حديث حسن: ۱/۲٤۰، باب ما جاء في كراهية عسب الفحل)

This narration shows that a wage is forbidden, but that there is room for a gift given as a mark of honour.

Consider what Fatāwā Farīdiyyah states:

A ḥāfiẓ's taking money or payment at the completion of tarāwīḥ is payment for leading the prayer, not for the recitation.

Question: is the money and the like that ḥuffāẓ take after completing the Qur'an in tarāwīḥ permissible or impermissible?

The answer: the jurists have declared it impermissible to take a wage for recitation. But in our lands the ḥuffāẓ both recite and lead the prayer, so to treat the money they receive as payment for the recitation alone, and to pass over the leading of the prayer in silence, is unwarranted. And if only the recitation were taken into account, then, since no tarāwīḥ is free of recitation, taking payment for tarāwīḥ at all would be impermissible. In any case, objecting to this money taken by a ḥāfiẓ is an Indian issue, not a Ḥanafī one: it is payment for leading the prayer, not merely for the recitation. (فتاوی فریدیہ: ۱/٦۰۷، مسائل شتی)

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
