### TITLE
There is no difference between taqlīd and ittibāʿ

### QUESTION
Is there any difference between taqlīd (following the ruling of a qualified jurist) and ittibāʿ (following), in the sense that one is held to be impermissible and the other permissible? Is a distinction of this kind reported anywhere from the early scholars or not?

### ANSWER
There is no difference between taqlīd and ittibāʿ; the two are one and the same. Nor is any distinction of meaning between them reported from the early scholars — indeed, the opposite of such a distinction certainly is reported from them. A few passages are given below from which it will be clear that taqlīd and ittibāʿ are one and the same, and that there is no difference between them.

Consider the following. The Shaykh al-Kull of the Ahl-e-Ḥadīth, Mawlānā Sayyid Naẕīr Ḥusayn Dihlawī (d. 1330 AH), writes in his definition of taqlīd:

In common usage taqlīd means to accept the statement of a man of knowledge at a time when one does not know oneself, and to act upon it; and in this customary sense, following the mujtahids is called taqlīd.

He then goes on to say:

So it is established that it is permissible to call the following of the Prophet ﷺ, and the following of the mujtahids, taqlīd.

انتهى بلفظه

(معيار الحق، ص: ٦٦. ٦۷. الكلام المفيد في إثبات التقليد، ص: ۳۰).

Nūr al-Anwār states:

التقليد اتباع الرجل غيره الخ. (نور الأنوار، ص: ۲۲۰).

Ḥāshiyah Nāmī states:

التقليد اتباع الغير على ظن. (حاشية نامي على الحسامي، ص: ۱۹۰).

Kashshāf Iṣṭilāḥāt al-Funūn states:

التقليد اتباع الإنسان غيره فيما يقول. (كشاف اصطلاحات الفنون للشيخ القاضي محمد التهانوي (م ۱۱۹۱ هـ): ۲/ ۱۱۷۸، سهيل اكاديمي).

All these passages make it perfectly clear that taqlīd and ittibāʿ are one and the same, and that even in the view of the Shaykh al-Kull of the Ahl-e-Ḥadīth there is no difference between the two. It is only in common usage that following the imams is called taqlīd and following the Prophet ﷺ is called ittibāʿ. Most of the ghayr muqallidīn (those who reject following a school of law) regard ittibāʿ and taqlīd as two different things, holding that ittibāʿ is praiseworthy and taqlīd blameworthy. Yet when they write against taqlīd, they write the following, contradicting the very fact they themselves have accepted:

﴿بل نتبع ما وجدنا عليه آباءنا﴾

That is, they claim that those who practise taqlīd are like the idolaters, who used to follow their forefathers — and in doing so they themselves call taqlīd "following". But it never occurs to them that the forefathers of the idolaters were never mujtahids, that taqlīd should be illustrated by their example.
