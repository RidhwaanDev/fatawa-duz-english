### TITLE
Names formed as kunyahs

### QUESTION
In our locality one boy has the name Abū Dharr and another the name Abū Hurayrah. Both are names of noble Companions, but what would these names mean?

### ANSWER
Names of this kind are given as a good omen and for blessing, that Allah Most High may make these children like the noble Companions. Their sense may also be taken as follows: dharr means to sow a crop and to cultivate, so Abū Dharr means one who sows a crop, that is, one who conveys good to people; and Abū Hurayrah means one who keeps a cat, that is, one who helps the weak, even to the point of serving weak animals. In the same way Abū Bakr means the owner of a young camel, that is, he rears a young camel so as to use it in the service of the religion and also to make his own living from it — for in earlier times camels were used, like horses, in jihad and in the works of the religion. It should be borne in mind that one sense of prefixing ab and ibn is "possessor of": as Abū Lahab means the man of the flame, and ibn al-sabīl, ṣāḥib al-sabīl, the man of the road.

The word ab also carries the senses of owner, possessor, originator, husband and the like. Consider:
