### TITLE
The ruling on pictorial labels on foodstuffs and the like

### QUESTION
In the present age most goods carry pictorial labels — for example on things to eat and drink, on clothing and dress, on water bottles and on other goods. With these pictures present, will buying and selling be permissible or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, the pictures on these goods are not what is intended, and very often they are extremely small pictures that are generally not clearly visible. On this basis, trading in them incidentally is permissible and correct, and using them too is correct. As for those pictures that are intended in their own right, trading in them is impermissible.

Consider what Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib says:

In buying and selling, if the pictures are not what is intended but come in as subordinate to other things — as figures are often printed on clothes, or as is commonly the practice on utensils and other modern manufactured goods — then buying and selling them incidentally is permissible,

كما يستفاد من بلوغ القصد والمرام معزياً للهيثمي (بلوغ، ص: ۱۸) ولما هو من القواعد المسلمة من فقه الأحناف أن كثيراً من الأفعال لا يجوز قصداً ويجوز تبعاً كما صرحوا في جواز بيع الحقوق تبعاً للدار ولا إصالة وقصداً.

But where the buying and selling of the pictures themselves is what is intended, then both buying and selling are impermissible. (تصویر کے شرعی احکام، ص ۸۸) ۔

Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah states:

If there is a picture on a box and the thing itself is what is really intended and not the box, then there is no objection to this sale; and even supposing that the box too were intended, the picture on it is not intended, so there is no objection to its sale. (فتاوی رشیدیہ، ص۵۲۱) ۔
