### TITLE
A question about the imam of a mosque

### QUESTION
A man used to lead the prayers in a mosque, and a long time has now passed since these events. On one or two occasions it happened that during the prayer there was a possibility, or a strong likelihood, that a drop of urine had come out. On another occasion, on the first night of Ramadan, the sighting of the moon had not yet been announced and the imam had already prayed his witr; then, after the moon was announced, he led the people in witr a second time after the tarawih, although someone else had led the tarawih. At the time, because of the drop of urine — about which he was either in doubt or strongly suspicious — the imam did not announce that the prayer should be repeated. He now has no connection with that place, and most of the worshippers of that time will either have passed away or moved elsewhere. All this happened 30 years ago and there is no longer any benefit in informing anyone. What can the imam now do to clear himself of this?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, since the breaking of the imam's ablution by that drop was doubtful or strongly suspected, the imam should have made an announcement at the time so that the prayer could be repeated. The established Hanafi position is that the imam's prayer carries the prayer of those who follow him; this has already been explained in volume two, page 234. In view of the difficulties in the situation described, however, there is a particular ruling recorded by the jurists which shows that the announcement is not necessary.

Consider what Sunan al-Tirmidhī states:

عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه قال: كنا ننام على عهد رسول الله ﷺ في المسجد ونحن شبان. قال أبو عيسى حديث ابن عمر رضي الله عنه حديث حسن صحيح.
(ترمذي شريف: ۱/۷۳).
وفي رواية البخاري: عن عبد الله بن عمر رضي الله عنه أنه كان ينام وهو شاب أعزب لا أهل له في مسجد النبي ﷺ. (صحيح البخاري: ۱/٦۳).
وأيضاً رواه ابن ماجة في باب النوم في المسجد (
ص ۵٤) ۔

وقال الشيخ عبد الغني المجددي الدهلوي في إنجاح الحاجة: وهذه رخصة لابن السبيل والمسافر فإن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه ما كان له حينئذٍ أهل وأما لغيره فيكره الاعتياد بالنوم فيه ولو دخل أحد للصلاة فنام هنا فلا بأس به لأن الصحابة كانوا يفعلون ذلك. (إنجاح الحاجة: ۵٤).

Al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

ولو وقف على دهن السراج للمسجد لا يجوز وضعه جميع الليل بل بقدر حاجة المصلين ويجوز إلى ثلث الليل أو نصفه إذا احتيج إليه للصلاة فيه كذا في السراج الوهاج ولا يجوز أن يترك فيه كل الليل إلا في موضع جرت العادة فيه بذلك كمسجد بيت المقدس ومسجد النبي ﷺ والمسجد الحرام أو شرط الواقف تركه فيه كل الليل كما جرت العادة به في زماننا كذا في البحر الرائق. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۲/٤۵۹، الباب الحادي عشر في المسجد وما يتعلق به).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muḥammad Yūsuf Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) states:

The mosque's electricity and the like should be used at the times of prayer; at other times those who give the donations may stop it. Sleeping in the mosque is permissible for a person in iʿtikāf and for a traveller, and is disliked for others. Those who do sleep in the mosque should spread a cloth over the mats, so that the floor is not spoiled by perspiration and so that there is no risk of it being made impure while they sleep. (Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal: 2/141).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

A person who is in iʿtikāf, or a traveller who has nowhere to stay, is permitted to remain in the mosque, and so is a person who stays there for the sake of the tahajjud and fajr prayers. One should not, however, turn the mosque into a resting place for oneself. The mosque's fan and the mosque's lighting are meant in the first place for the prayer, and should be used for as long as people are generally praying. If they are used for purposes other than the prayer, then in return one should also do some service for the mosque. A ruling concerning the mosque lamp is mentioned in al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 15/238, Fārūqiyyah).

See further: Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 643, Fārūqiyyah; Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 6/447; and Kitāb al-Fatāwā: 4/261, 263.
