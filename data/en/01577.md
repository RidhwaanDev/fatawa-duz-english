### TITLE
Judicial separation because of a husband's long imprisonment

### QUESTION
If a prisoner's wife cannot live a chaste life, but provision has been made for her expenses, can a judge or a body of Muslims grant her a separation or not, where the prisoner's sentence is a long one?

### ANSWER
In the case described, there would be nothing wrong in giving a fatwa on the position of Imam Mālik (may Allah have mercy on him), given the dire need and in order to protect the woman from unlawful relations. In the Mālikī school, abstaining from marital relations is also a ground for separation. The woman should therefore bring her case before a judge's court or the Jamiatul Ulama and state: my husband so-and-so is in such-and-such a prison, his sentence is a very long one, waiting all that time is beyond me, I am in urgent need of marriage, and otherwise there is a serious danger of my falling into sin. The judge or the Jamiatul Ulama should then investigate properly how far her statement is true. If her statement is proved correct, they should send word to the husband: give your wife her rights, or send for her, or make some arrangement from where you are; otherwise divorce her; and if you accept none of this, we shall grant the separation ourselves. If even then the husband accepts none of these courses, the judge should tell the woman to wait one month more. If during that month nothing comes about that would secure the husband's release or a reduction of his sentence, then the judge or the Jamiatul Ulama should separate the woman from this man. After that she may complete her waiting period and marry elsewhere.

Majmūʿah-e Qawānīn-e Islāmī states:

Abstaining from marital relations and leaving a wife in suspense is also one of the grounds for separation, since fulfilling the rights of marriage is necessary. To withhold those rights and leave a wife in suspense is oppression, and removing oppression is the judge's duty. In such a case, moreover, it is quite possible that the woman will fall into sin, and it is the judge's duty to close off such possibilities. For if the woman brings the above complaint before the judge, he must, after investigating the matter, remove the oppression and open the way for her to be kept safe from sin. In the Mālikī school too, abstaining from marital relations is a ground for separation. (مجموعہ قوانین اسلامی: ۱۹۲، دفعہ ۷۳) ۔

وإذا ثبت لها التطليق بذلك فبخشية الزنا أولي، لان ضرر ترك الوطء أشد من ضرر عدم النفقة، ألا تري أن إسقاط النفقة يلزمها، وإن أسقطت حقها في الوطء فلها الرجوع فيه، ولأن النفقة يمكن تحصيلها بنحو تسلف وسـؤال بخلاف الوطء. (من فتوي العلامة سعيد بن صديق الفلاتي، بحواله: الحيلة الناجزة: ۱۱۸، دار الاشاعت، ديوبند).

Al-Ḥīlah al-Nājizah states:

Although the Hanafi position is, in terms of evidence, extremely strong and built on the utmost caution, some of the later Hanafi jurists, in view of the delicacy of the times and the trials people face, have given their fatwa in this matter according to the school of Imam Mālik. ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him), for instance, quoted al-Quhistānī's words from "الدر المنتقي": "لو أفتي به في موضع الضرورة لا بأس به على ما أظن". (فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۲۹۵، مطلب في الافتاء بمذهب مالك رحمه الله، سعيد)

For a long time now, those who issue fatwas, in India and outside it, have almost all adopted this position, and in one respect the matter has thereby become part of Hanafi fiqh itself. Yet as long as the woman is able to bear her situation, the original Hanafi position must be followed. Only in a case of dire necessity — where no provision can be made for her expenses, or where it is not thought proper for her to remain as she is because of the fear of sin — is there no objection to acting on the Mālikī school; and it is for such situations that this fatwa was drawn up.

(الحيلة الناجزة: ۵۰، دار الاشاعت، ديوبند، من فتوي العلامة سعيد بن صديق الفلاتي المالكي رحمه الله).

Kitāb al-Faskh wa al-Tafrīq states:

If a woman has an established right to demand a divorce because she is not being maintained, then she has that right all the more where unlawful relations are feared, since the harm of being deprived of marital relations is more severe than the harm of not being maintained. A second reason is that maintenance can be obtained by borrowing or by asking others, whereas that is not possible with marital relations. (کتاب الفسخ و التفریق: ص ۷۳) ۔

Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

It is best that a divorce be obtained in some way. If he will not divorce her, she should obtain a khulʿ by giving him some property. If that too proves impossible, she should bring her case before the court of a Muslim ruler, and that ruler should compel the prisoner to divorce her. If he still does not, the ruler himself should decree the divorce, and this decree takes the place of a divorce — provided the ruler is a Muslim.

وهذا في الأصل مذهب الإمام مالك رحمه الله إلا أن علمائنا الحنفية أفتوا عليه لمكان الضرورة الشديدة

(ملخص از امداد المفتین: جلد دوم: ٦۷۲، دار الاشاعت، کراچی) ۔

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm (Kabīr) states:

The substance of the question is this: Zayd was sentenced to thirty years' imprisonment for an offence. Three years have passed and twenty-seven remain. His wife says that she cannot endure so long a stretch without a husband and asks for the marriage to be annulled. Zayd is not willing to divorce her or to let her go; he wants her to stay in the house of a distant relative, which does not set her mind at rest, and it is highly likely that her honour will be violated. What is the ruling of sharia in the present situation? Can the marriage be annulled against his will or not?

Answer: The original Hanafi position in such a case is that the marriage cannot be annulled, and that without a divorce from the husband it is not permissible for the woman to marry again. But some of the other imams hold annulment permissible in such a case, and a Hanafi may act on their view when there is need. So where there is need, a Hanafi has room to have a separation granted and to give a fatwa that she may marry again after her waiting period. (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: ۱۰/۲۳۰) ۔

The Hanafi jurists have also written, in the chapter on ẓihār, that if a man who has made ẓihār does not pay the expiation and leaves his wife in suspense, she may bring her case before the judge, and the judge will remove the harm from her.

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

أن للمرأة أن تطالبه بالوطء وإذا طالبته به فعلى الحاكم أن يجبره حتى يكفر ويطأ لأنه بالتحريم بالظهار أضر بها حيث منعها حقها في الوطء مع قيام الملك فكان لها المطالبة بإيفاء حقها ودفع التضرر عنها وفي وسعه إيفاء حقها بإزالة الحرمة بالكفارة فيجب عليه ذلك ويجبر عليه لو امتنع (بدائع الصنائع: ۳/۲۳٤، أحكام الظهار، سعيد).
