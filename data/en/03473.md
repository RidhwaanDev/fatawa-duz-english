### TITLE
A brief and comprehensive summary concerning collective Qurʾān recitation

### QUESTION
People gather together and recite the Qurʾān in order to convey reward to a deceased person. A great deal has been heard, read and written about this. Would you give a brief and comprehensive summary, so that the matter may be understood and so that it becomes easy to decide whether or not to take part? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
The proofs concerning conveying reward on behalf of a deceased person have been written out in detail in the eighth volume of Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā. In brief, the summary is this: that a Qurʾān-recitation gathering for conveying reward is permissible and correct subject to several conditions:

1. That it should not be regarded as binding and necessary.

2. That money and the like should not be given to those who recite the Qurʾān, nor should they be fed in exchange for it.

3. That pride, ostentation and fame should not be the aim.

4. That it should not be held in the manner of a third-day, fortieth-day or annual commemoration.

5. That there should be no mingling and intermixing of men and women.

If a gathering for Qurʾān recitation is held while observing these conditions, it is permissible. It is better, however, that in a collective Qurʾān recitation the recitation be done quietly, so that no disturbance is caused to another's recitation; moreover, according to some scholars it becomes obligatory to listen to an audible recitation, although according to most authorities it is not obligatory. An indication towards the permissibility of collective recitation is also found in a hadith:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله تعالى عنه، قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ:

" وما اجتمع قوم في بيت من بيوت الله تعالى يتلون كتاب الله ويتدارسونه بينهم إلا نزلت عليهم السكينة وغشيتهم الرحمة وحفتهم الملائكة وذكرهم الله فيمن عنده

(رواه مسلم، رقم: ۲٦۹۹، باب فضل الاجتماع على تلاوة القرآن وعلى الذكر).

The purport is this: that when some people gather in one of the houses of Allah Most High and recite the Qurʾān and go over it together, tranquillity descends upon them, mercy envelops them, the angels surround them, and Allah Most High mentions them among those who are with Him.

Yes, the better and more fitting course is that in a collective recitation it be recited quietly, and that reciting in a loud voice be avoided. Then, if the recitation of the Qurʾān is for the sake of supplication, the permissibility of gathering follows from the passages of the jurists. It is in ʿĀlamgīrī:

قوم يجتمعون ويقرأون الفاتحة جهراً دعاء لا يمنعون عادة والأولى المخافتة. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳۱۷).

If the above conditions are observed, then take part in the Qurʾān recitation; otherwise offer some excuse.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
