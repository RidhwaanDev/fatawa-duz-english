### TITLE
Ruling on a delegated ṭalāq being confined to the sitting

### QUESTION
Does a delegation of ṭalāq remain confined to the sitting in which it is made, or does it last for good?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, if the husband did not give his wife the right for good and did not fix any period either, the delegation of ṭalāq remains confined to that sitting. But if the husband gave his wife the right for good — for instance, by saying, "Bring a ṭalāq into effect upon yourself whenever you wish" — then she has the right for good; and if he fixed a period, she retains the right for that period. In short, the matter is decided by the husband's own words.
