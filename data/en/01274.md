### TITLE
A mistake in the name of the bride or her father at the time of marriage

### QUESTION
If the agent made a mistake in the girl's name, or in her father's name, is the marriage contracted or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, if the girl was present at the marriage gathering and was pointed out, the marriage was contracted. If she was not present, the marriage did not take place in either situation — that is, whether the mistake was in the girl's own name or in her father's name, the marriage did not take place.

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

غلط وكيلها بالنكاح في اسم أبيها بغير حضورها لم يصح للجهالة، وكذا لو غلط في اسم بنته إلا إذا كانت حاضرة وأشار إليها. وفي الشامية: (قوله، لم يصح) لأن الغائبة يشترط ذكر اسمها واسم أبيها وجدها وتقدم أنه إذا عرفها الشهود يكفي ذكر اسمها فقط خلافاً لا بن الفضل وعند الخصاف يكفي مطلقاً، والظاهر أنه في مسألتنا لا يصح عند الكل لأن ذكر الاسم وحده لا يصرفها عن المراد إلى غيره، بخلاف ذكر الاسم منسوباً إلى أب آخر، فإن فاطمة بنت أحمد لا تصدق علي فاطمة بنت محمد، تأمل، وكذا يقال فيما لو غلط في اسمها. (قوله إلا إذا كانت حاضرة) راجع إلى المسألتين، أي فإنها لو كانت مشاراً إليها وغلط في اسم أبيها أو اسمها لا يضر لأن تعريف الإشارة الحسية أقوي من التسمية، لما في التسمية من الاشتراك لعارض فتلغو التسمية عندها. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ۳/۲٦، سعيد).

Fatāwā Farīdiyyah states: If the girl was not present at that gathering, this marriage is not valid,

كما في فتاوى قاضي خان: امرأة وكلت رجلاً بأن يزوجها فزوجها وغلط في اسم أبيها لا ينعقد النكاح إذا كانت غائبة. فتاوى قاضيخان على هامش الهندية: ۱/۳۲٤. (Fatāwā Farīdiyyah: 4/408).

If, however, the mistake was in the name of the girl's father, and the witnesses know the girl well, the marriage is valid.

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states: When the women are well known, the marriage is contracted even if the father's name is given wrongly. The wording of Al-Durr al-Mukhtār does suggest that such a mistake makes a marriage invalid (its wording has been quoted above), but the answer is that, in the first place, the wording of Al-Durr al-Mukhtār contains the word للجهالة. This shows that the ground of invalidity in a case of mistake is uncertainty as to who is meant, and that ground is absent here. (Summarised from Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm: 7/123, Mudallal wa Mukammal).

It is also stated there that if the agent or the qāḍī mistakenly gave the wrong name for the girl, the marriage is still valid.

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states: The apparent wording of the books of fiqh does suggest that in such a case the marriage is contracted with the person whose name was uttered at the time of offer and acceptance. The point to consider, however, is that the qāḍī and the agent are told in advance that so-and-so is to be married to so-and-so, and in this the qāḍī or the agent has no authority to act contrary to the appointment given to him, since he was not appointed as agent to do anything contrary to it. But the wording of Al-Durr al-Mukhtār says: وكذا غلط في اسم بنته (أي لا يصح). The answer to this is that in that passage the father himself performed the marriage contract, whereas in the case in question the qāḍī or the agent conducted the marriage, and when an agent acts contrary to his appointment it carries no weight, کما مر تفصیلہ. (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm: 7/122, Mudallal wa Mukammal; likewise in Imdād al-Aḥkām: 2/230).

In sum: if the girl is well known and the witnesses too know her well, the marriage is contracted and the mistake does no harm — this is what the passage from Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm means. If the girl is unknown, the marriage is not contracted, as is clear from the passages of Al-Durr al-Mukhtār, Qāḍī Khān and Fatāwā Farīdiyyah.
