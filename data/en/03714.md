### TITLE
The ruling on delaying the receipt of the price through a bank in online trade

### QUESTION
A man trades in shoes online and the price is received through a bank. The buyer transfers the money into the seller's account; then, if the seller withdraws the money from the bank immediately, a service charge is applied, but if he withdraws it after 28 days there is no charge. So is there scope for withdrawing it with delay, given that it is also known that the bank will run its interest-based business with this money? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question the bank provides a service and collects a fee in return for it; collecting a fee in return for a service is permissible and correct. Now the bank has laid down the condition that if you withdraw the money after 28 days the fee is waived: this is a conditional gratuity, which is permissible and correct. As for the point that the bank will carry on interest-based business in the meantime, the first point is that the money is not specified — it has become mixed with other sums, so it is not known with certainty that the interest-based business was carried on with your money in particular. The second point is that leaving the money is a remote cause, whereas in the actual interest-based business the bank is the direct agent and the motivating party; therefore there is not much objection for you. Consider what is recorded in Jawāhir al-Fiqh:

ثم السبب إن لم يكن محركاً وداعياً، بل موصلاً محضاً، وهو مع ذلك سبب قريب بحيث لا يحتاج في إقامة المعصية به إلى إحداث صنعة من الفاعل، كبيع السلاح من أهل الفتنة وبيع العصير ممن يتخذه خمراً

فكله مكروه تحريماً بشرط أن يعلم به البائع والآجر من دون تصريح به باللسان، فإنه إن لم يعلم كان معذوراً. (جواهر الفقه: ۲/٤۵۲).

For further detail, see: (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 5/422).
