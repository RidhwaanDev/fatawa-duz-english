### TITLE
Taking food away from the madrasah kitchen

### QUESTION
(a) A student ate his meal in the kitchen and even so took bread away to his room. Is this correct or not? (b) If he does not eat his meal in the kitchen, is it correct for him to take bread away to his room or not?

(c) Is it correct to bring butter and the like from the kitchen into a room, when among those eating in the room there are some who did not have breakfast in the kitchen and some who did?

### ANSWER
All of this belongs to matters of administration. The system of the madrasah is that the students should sit together and eat their meal in the kitchen, and in general the administrator's office does not give permission for food to be taken to the rooms. Yes, if a student is ill or has some other excuse, he may obtain a slip from the administrator's office and bring food to his room. But if obtaining a slip is difficult — for example, the office is closed, or there is some other excuse — then he may take it with the permission of those in charge of the kitchen. Likewise, if a student has not eaten his meal and takes only bread to the extent of his own share with the permission of the kitchen staff, there is scope for that. In any case, it is extremely important for every student to keep to the rules of the madrasah as far as he possibly can, because by signing the rules he himself has entered into a pledge and an undertaking, and observing pledges, undertakings and conditions is established by the noble hadith. It is reported in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

"المسلمون عند شروطهم"

In practice too, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ kept to the terms agreed on the occasion of the Treaty of Ḥudaybiyah, and he instructed the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) to do the same.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
