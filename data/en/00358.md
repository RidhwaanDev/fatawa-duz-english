### TITLE
The length of the gap between true dawn and sunrise, and between sunset and the start of ʿIshāʾ

### QUESTION
Is the gap between true dawn and sunrise, and likewise between sunset and the start of ʿIshāʾ, one hour, or more? Some people hold it to be 18 degrees and others 15. Which view is correct?

### ANSWER
Most authorities have put the gap between these two times at between one hour twenty minutes and one hour thirty-eight minutes. Ḥaḍrat Muftī Rashīd (may Allah have mercy on him), the author of Aḥsan al-Fatāwā, put it at 57 minutes; but this is a position peculiar to him, and the opinion of most authorities is against it. Most of them have adopted the view of 18 degrees.

See what Kifāyat al-Muftī has:

This interval is not always the same. It varies from month to month — that is, over short stretches of days it increases and decreases — but it is never less than one hour twenty-one minutes. In the month of June it is at its longest, one hour thirty-eight minutes, and in September it is at its shortest, one hour twenty-one minutes. (کفایت المفتی: ۳/۷۲)

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states:

After sunset, according to Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), the time of ʿIshāʾ begins when the white twilight has disappeared. Its length is, in some seasons, one hour twenty-four or twenty-five minutes; in some seasons one hour 27 minutes; and in some seasons even longer. So a gap of less than an hour and a half should not be left between Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ; rather, out of caution, a gap of an hour and three quarters should be left. From an almanac giving sunrise, sunset, true dawn and the like, the length of the time can be worked out for any period. (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: ۲/٤۲، ٤٦، از مفتی عزیز الرحمٰن صاحب رحمه الله)۔

Research into true dawn a hundred years ago

About a hundred years ago, in 1313 AH, corresponding to 1896 CE, a treatise on true dawn entitled "حل الدقائق في تحقيق الصبح الصادق" was written by the godly scholar Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muḥammad Luṭfullāh, mufti of the state of Rampur. In it he writes about true dawn:

In short, the period between the break of true dawn and sunrise is equal to the period between sunset and the disappearance of the twilight. Besides the transmitted evidence, the plain reason why these two periods are equal is this: when the sun moves up from below the earth towards its rising, and there remain 18 degrees for it to cover before the horizon, from that moment a light appears spread across the horizon, and this is called true dawn; and this light keeps increasing until the sun comes up.

In the same way, when after setting it travels down towards the earth until it has reached 18 degrees below it, the whiteness that follows sunset — which is called the twilight — disappears.

It is obvious that if the sun gave light at 18 degrees at the time of its rising, then in the same way its light must also disappear after 18 degrees at the time of its setting. Once this twilight has disappeared the time of the ʿIshāʾ prayer begins, and this is what is generally acted upon today. (حل الدقائق: ص: ۳۲)

At the same period Munshī Muḥammad Aʿlā, a landholder of Meerut, also wrote a treatise entitled "صبح صادق", and in it too true dawn is placed at the sun's being 18 degrees below the horizon.

These two treatises were endorsed, with commendations written upon them, by several of the great scholars of Deoband: Shaykh al-Hind Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Maḥmūd al-Ḥasan; Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Khalīl Aḥmad Sahāranpūrī, author of "بذل المجهود"; Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muftī ʿAzīz al-Raḥmān; Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ḥabīb al-Raḥmān ʿUthmānī; and Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ḥāfiẓ Aḥmad, son of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muḥammad Qāsim, the founder of Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband (may Allah sanctify their secrets).

Evidence for the view of 18 degrees regarding the beginning of true dawn:

The author of Rūḥ al-Maʿānī, ʿAllāmah Ālūsī Baghdādī, writes in his commentary on the noble verse of Sūrat al-Takwīr:

﴿والصبح إذا تنفس﴾

أن تنفس الصبح وضياءه بواسطة قرب الشمس إلى الأفق الشرقي بمقدار معين وهو في المشهور ثمانية عشر جزءًا. (روح المعاني: ۳۰/٥۹)

Translation: The breaking of the dawn and its light come about through the sun's approaching the eastern horizon by a set amount, and according to the well-known view that amount is 18 degrees.

Here "the dawn" means true dawn, because just before this ʿAllāmah Ālūsī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

في الآية إشارة إلى الفجر الثاني الصادق وهو المنتشر ضوءه معترضا بالأفق.

Rubʿ al-Mujayyab states:

وعلى قول أبي حنيفة رحمه الله المعتبر في الحصتين أن يكون الشمس منحطة (۱۸ درجة) والدائرة لارتفاع ۱۸ بدرجة النظير هو الحصة لكل منهما فهما مستويان. (ربع المجيب: ۲۳)

In the book "إيضاح القول الحق في مقدار انحطاط الشمس وقت طلوع الفجر وغروب الشفق" by the present-day expert in astronomy, Professor ʿAllāmah Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd al-Wahhāb Marrākishī (may his life be long), it is stated:

(۱) وقد عرف بالتجربة أن انحطاط الشمس عند أول طلوع الفجر ۱۸ جزءاً. (ص ۱۰)

(۲) وممن قال بالثمانية عشر أبو الحسن عبد الرحمن الصوفي البزاز المتوفى ٦۷۳.

(۳) وممن قال بالثمانية عشر في الفجر وفي الشفق الأستاذ الرئيس أبو على الحسن بن عيسى المجاصي فقد قال في رسالته تذكرة أولي الألباب في عمل صفة الاضطراب، فصل في تخطيط أوقات الصلاة أما الفجر والشفق فإن خطيهما هو مقنطرة ثمانية عشر في كل أرض وفي كل زمان. (ص ۱٤)

(٤) عمل طائفة من المتقدمين من فلكي الإسلام على أن حصتي الفجر والشفق متساويان وان ابتداء طلوع الفجر وانتهاء غروب الشفق يكونان عند انحطاط الشمس عن الأفق ۱۸ عشر درجة. (ص ۱٦)

About sixty years ago Mawlānā Muḥammad ʿAbd al-Wāsiʿ, professor of religious studies at Jāmiʿah ʿUthmāniyyah, Hyderabad Deccan (India), after a detailed explanation of dawn and twilight in his book, wrote: Dawn begins, and the white twilight ends, at the moment when the sun is 18 degrees vertically below the horizon. (معیار الاوقات للصیام و الصلوات: ۱۵)

Professor ʿAbd al-Laṭīf of Karachi, after a detailed discussion in his book, writes as follows about the prayer-time charts of the Indo-Pakistani subcontinent:

For many years now, in the subcontinent of Pakistan and India, the moment at which the Fajr call to prayer is given according to the old prayer-time charts and almanacs — the moment that has been fixed as true dawn, also called the end of the time for the pre-dawn meal — is that particular moment when, before rising, the sun reaches the limit of 18 degrees below the horizon; and according to the experts in astronomy the time of true dawn begins at that moment.

(Summarised from the book "برطانیہ و اعلیٰ عروض البلاء پر صبح صادق و شفق کی تحقیق": pp. 73–78, by Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Yaʿqūb Qāsmī, member of the consultative council of Jāmiʿa ʿUlūm al-Qur'ān; published at Jambusar and in Britain, Dewsbury)

In ʿUmdat al-Fiqh, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Sayyid Zawwār Ḥusayn Shāh writes: Nowadays clocks and watches are common, and almanacs and charts giving the times are found in most mosques. It is permissible, indeed commendable, to keep to the prayer times according to them; watches should be kept accurate. In our country the interval from the break of true dawn to sunrise is at least one hour eighteen minutes and at most one hour 35 minutes. (عمدۃ الفقہ: کتاب الصلاۃ حصہ دوم: ص ۲٦ المجددیہ)

On this question it is the view of 18 degrees that is the stronger and more reliable one; it is also the better known, and it is confirmed by experience as well:

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Farīd (may his blessings endure) writes:

Note also that the time of true dawn does not begin an hour and a half before sunrise; at most it begins an hour and a quarter before it.

كما هو يعلم من المشاهدة والرياضي.

(فتاوی فریدیہ: ۲/۱٤۵، باب المواقیت)

Note: the Muftī must have said this with regard to certain days; otherwise on some days the interval is more than an hour and a quarter.

On the basis of our own observation the time of Fajr is most probably an hour and a quarter, and the same for Maghrib. (فتاوی فریدیہ: ۲/۱۵۱، باب المواقیت)

When the sun has certainly set and an hour and a quarter has passed after that, the time of ʿIshāʾ begins. This is established by our own observation and experience. (فتاوی فریدیہ ۲/۱۵٦، باب المواقیت)

Minhāj al-Sunan states:

قلت: وصرح المشايخ بتفاوت الوقت بين طلوع الفجر الصادق وطلوع الشمس وكذا بين غروب الشمس وغيوب البياض بتفاوت المواسم والبلاد والمشاهد في ديارنا قدر ساعة وربع ساعة. (منهاج السنن: ۲/۱۰، باب مواقيت الصلاة)

As far as observation is concerned, one very strong proof of it is the commendation written on the book "تسہیل الفلکیات" (by Professor ʿAbd al-Laṭīf) by Mawlānā Nūr Muḥammad, the rector of Jāmiʿah Dār al-ʿUlūm Waziristan (Wana), a photocopy of which is also available. That commendation runs as follows:

My respected Mr ʿAbd al-Laṭīf (may your glory increase),

السلام علیکم و رحمۃ اللہ وبرکاتہ

I have already sent you my findings on the times you asked about. Now I write again to say that I appointed the following committee of senior scholars of Dār al-ʿUlūm Waziristan, Wana. From 13 June '88 to 21 June they checked the times of true dawn and of sunset and then handed them to me. When I checked them against the times you had sent, they agreed with your chart one hundred per cent — even though I had not told those scholars the times given on your chart. I therefore give you the good news that your chart is entirely correct for the times of Wana.

The names of the scholars on the committee are:

(1) Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Wārith (2) Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Majīd

(3) Mawlānā Iṣlāḥ al-Dīn (4) Mawlānā Farīd Aḥmad

و السلام

Nūr Muḥammad, rector of Dār al-ʿUlūm Waziristan, Wana, and preacher of the central Friday mosque of Wana, South Waziristan, district Dera Ismail Khan.

This commendation makes it clear that Mr ʿAbd al-Laṭīf's chart is entirely correct; and it should be remembered that his chart is based on 18 degrees.

See below the gathering held in Britain on this subject, and the decision of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Maḥmūd Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him), the chief mufti of Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, concerning it:

Since the question of true dawn and of the sighting of the new moon is always disputed in Britain, each party having its own arguments and each insisting on its own opinion, when Ḥaḍrat Muftī Maḥmūd went there in 1403 AH the scholars of that country referred the matter to him. After studying the arguments and evidence of the scholars he wrote a reply, the substance of which is as follows:

In the region of Britain the question of true dawn and of the spreading white twilight has been under debate for a long time. The times of Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ, the time of Fajr, the end of the pre-dawn meal and the beginning of the fast are all bound up with it. This worthless servant had the honour of reading the writings of the scholars, but he confesses with much embarrassment that after studying them he was unable to reach any final decision. The humble Maḥmūd, may he be forgiven. 16 Shaʿbān 1403.

Afterwards, however, all the scholars, after due reflection, agreed upon the proposal of following the nearest days, and all of them accepted that proposal. The Muftī signed it and withdrew his earlier statement.

The agreed decision:

Today, 16 Shaʿbān 1403 AH, a gathering of scholars was held at Bradford under the auspices of the Jamʿiyyat al-ʿUlamāʾ of Britain and under the patronage of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Maḥmūd. In it, after long deliberation on the question of true dawn in Britain, the scholars present reached the following agreed decision: that the nautical twilight of 12 degrees which had previously been treated in Britain as true dawn was altogether wrong.

On those days in Britain when true dawn does occur — that is, when the sun goes 18 degrees below the horizon, which is technically called astronomical twilight — true dawn will be reckoned at that moment on those days, because that in fact is the correct time of true dawn. As for those days on which, at the various latitudes of Britain, the sun does not go 18 degrees below the horizon, it was settled that for those days the beginning of true dawn and the end of the pre-dawn meal should be fixed at the same clock time as the time of true dawn on the last date on which it did occur at that latitude. The servant Shabbīr Aḥmad, may he be pardoned.

Ḥaḍrat praised this decision, congratulated the scholars, and called back his earlier statement.

The statement sent by Ḥaḍrat (may Allah have mercy on him):

On 16 Shaʿbān 1403 AH a session of the Jamʿiyyat ʿUlamāʾ of Britain was held to discuss true dawn, the spreading whiteness and the end of the pre-dawn meal, and this humble one was present at it. Before that I had already studied a number of writings of the scholars on this question, but it is difficult to give preference to any one opinion. Then, however, the scholars discussed the matter and agreed upon a single opinion, and no one declared it invalid; so this humble one also signed it and called back his own statement, which duly reached him. (ملخص از حیاتِ محمود: ص ۲۰۱ ۔ ۲۰۵)۔

Support drawn from the hadiths

That the interval between the break of dawn and sunrise is more than 57 minutes is something the noble hadiths point to. After performing the sunnah prayer the Prophet ﷺ would rest for a while and would then go out for the Fajr prayer; the recitation in Fajr would be fairly long; and even so there was enough room left in the time for a person to make a fresh ablution and perform the Fajr prayer again unhurriedly. Moreover, in some narrations the word ghalas occurs, that is, it was still dark. From all these points together it emerges that more than 57 minutes were available.

عن أنس رضي الله عنه أن زيد بن ثابت رضي الله عنه حدثه أنهم تسحروا مع النبي ﷺ ثم قاموا إلى الصلاة قلت: كم بينهما قال: قدر خمسين أو ستين يعني آية. (بخاري شريف: ۱/۸۱ باب وقت الفجر).

عن عروة بن الزبير أن عائشة رضي الله عنه أخبرته قالت: كن نساء المؤمنات يشهدن مع رسول الله ﷺ صلاة الفجر متلفعات بمروطهن ثم ينقلبن إلى بيوتهن حين يقضين الصلاة لا يعرفهن أحد من الغلس. (بخاري شريف: ۱/۸۲، باب وقت الفجر).

قالت أم سلمة رضي الله عنه قرأ النبي ﷺ بالطور. (بخاري شريف: ۱۰٦، باب القراءة في الفجر).
