### TITLE
The rotation of the earth and the meaning of the verse "والشمس تجري"

### QUESTION
Allah says in the Noble Qur'an:

﴿والشمس تجري لمستقر لها، ذلك تقدير العزيز العليم﴾

From this we learn that the sun moves and revolves. Yet the astronomers of today hold that the sun is stationary and that the earth moves. So what is the meaning of the noble verse? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, the noble verse carries its plain meaning, namely that the sun moves; for the Noble Qur'an states the movement of the sun in unqualified terms, and all astronomers agree that the sun is travelling along with its entire galaxy. Within the solar system, however, all the planets revolve around the sun while the sun remains in its place — this is what is called the solar system — and in that respect the sun is in one place and the earth revolves around it. So the movement of the sun is of two kinds: (1) rotating, which is movement about its own axis; (2) revolving, which is travelling around. Astronomers accept both, and no one denies them. In earlier times, it is true, the theory held was that the earth is stationary and the sun revolves around it; that theory was not correct, because the earth and the other planets all orbit the sun.

Mawlānā Rūḥānī Bāzī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote at page 69 of his book "الهيئة الوسطي مع شرحها النجوم النشطي":

يظن بعض الناس أن الشمس ساكنة وهذا الظن خطأ

والثانية حركتها مع جميع أسرتها.

A statement to the same effect is also found in Falakiyyāt-e-Jadīdah at page 40.

He also writes in "الهيئة الصغرى":

ثم إن الشمس ليست بساكنة بل لها حركتان مشهورتان، الأولي: أنها تسير بأسرتها من جميع أجرام النظام الشمسي بسرعة ۱۱ ميلاً ونصف ميل في الثانية إلى نجم مسمي بالنسر الواقع. والثانية: إنها تدور حول محورها من الغرب إلى الشرق. (الهيئة الصغرى، ص ۳۰)

The World Encyclopaedia states, in substance:

The sun. The earth is one of nine bodies that revolve around the sun. There are 500,000 million stars like our sun in our galaxy, the Milky Way. Like the other heavenly bodies, the sun too is an enormous spinning ball of gas, and the atomic reactions that take place at its centre release energy.

The Jāmiʿ Urdu Encyclopaedia states:

From the sunspots it is known that the sun completes one rotation at its equator in 25 of our days, but at 40 degrees of latitude it takes 30 days, and near the poles, at 80 degrees, it takes 36 days. These differences occur because the sun is not solid. These observations help us to understand the sun's various actions and movements and the reversal of its magnetic cycle every eleven years. It has also been learnt that the axis of the sun's rotation is tilted by 7 degrees from the ecliptic (the ecliptic being the plane of the orbit on which the earth revolves around the sun and along which the sun appears from the earth to move).
