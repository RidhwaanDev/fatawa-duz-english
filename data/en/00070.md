### TITLE
Holding a mawlid gathering

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to hold a mawlid gathering on the Prophet's ﷺ birthday — what people commonly call Mīlād al-Nabī — or not? If it is permissible, what form should it take, and what is the practice of the scholars of Deoband on this question?

### ANSWER
The remembrance of the Prophet ﷺ is so blessed a thing that it ought to run through every fibre of a Muslim's being at all times, and no moment should be empty of his mention. One should not stop at speaking of his blessed birth and his Ascension; rather, everything about him — the way he sat and rose, his food and his dress, his character and his worship, his striving and self-discipline, his deeds and his rulings, his commands and his prohibitions — all of it earns a Muslim reward when he speaks of it. The condition, however, is that it be in accordance with the sunnah.

Consider what Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states.

Qāḍī Thanāʾullāh Pānīpatī (may Allah have mercy on him) (1143–1225 AH) says:

إن القول لا يقبل ما لم يعمل به وكلاهما لا يقبلان بدون النية والقول والعمل والنية لا تقبل ما لم توافق السنة. (ارشاد الطالبين، ص: ۲۸).

That is, words are of no use without action; and neither of the two — words and action — will be accepted without a sound intention; and for words, action and intention to be accepted, they must all be in accordance with the sunnah.

The commentary on the verse ﴿ليبلوكم ايكم احسن عملا﴾ states:

ذكروا في تفسير أحسن عملاً وجوهاً أحدها أن يكون أخلص الأعمال وأصوبها لأن العمل إذا كان خالصاً غير صواب لم يقبل وكذلك إذا كان صواباً غير خالصٍ والخالص أن يكون لوجه الله والصواب أن يكون على السنة. (التفسير الكبير: ۸/۲٤۳).

That is, "أحسن عملاً" means the deed that is accepted, and an accepted deed is one that is both sincere and correct. If a deed is sincere but not correct, it is not accepted; and if a deed is correct but not sincere, it is not accepted either. A sincere deed is one done purely to please Allah, and a correct deed is one that accords with the sunnah.

Al-Iʿtiṣām states:

من عمل عملاً بلا اتباع سنة فباطل عمله

Any deed done without following the sunnah is void. (الاعتصام ۱/۹۵، المكتبة التجارية).

Sufyān al-Thawrī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

لا يستقيم قول وعمل ونية إلا بموافقة السنة.

No word, deed or intention is sound unless it accords with the sunnah of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. (Talbīs Iblīs, p. 9).

Shaykh ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Jīlānī (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

لا يقبل قول بلا عمل ولا عمل بلا إخلاص وإصابة السنة.

(Fatḥ al-Rabbānī: 1/14). No word is acceptable without action, and no action is accepted unless it is sincere and in accordance with the sunnah. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 1/492–493, Maktabat al-Iḥsān, Deoband).

Yet how very sad it is that today those who claim to love him have taken up a new way of remembering the Prophet ﷺ: they celebrate his birthday on the twelfth of Rabīʿ al-Awwal and have called it Mīlād al-Nabī ﷺ. This gathering began only at the end of the sixth century; for six hundred years from the beginning of Islam there was no trace of it, and ʿUmar ibn Muḥammad was the first to introduce it, in the city of Mosul. A great many objectionable things have since become attached to it.

Consider what Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

In the customary mawlid gatherings of today, people assemble and chant the odes of ignorant poets and invented, fabricated narrations to tunes and melodies. Among those present are people who do not pray and people who are openly sinful, and they treat this way of doing things as necessary. It is contrary to the sunnah and an innovation. It is not established from any of the noble Companions, nor from the Successors, nor from those who followed them, nor from any of the great imams. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 1/493, Maktabat al-Iḥsān, Deoband).

Beyond this, they believe that the Prophet ﷺ comes in person to the mawlid gathering. This idea and belief is not correct by the principles of the sacred law: being present and watching everywhere is one of the attributes that belong to Allah alone.

Consider what al-Madkhal states:

ألا ترى أنهم لما خالفوا السنة المطهرة وفعلوا المولود لم يقتصروا على فعله، بل زادوا عليه ما تقدم ذكره من الأباطيل المتعددة. (المدخل: ۱/۱۵۷ بحواله فتاوى رحيميه: ۱/٤۹٤).

The scholars of Deoband have stated plainly that this customary practice, with all the excesses that accompany it, is void and an innovation.

See: (Imdād al-Muftīn 2/173, Dār al-Ishāʿat; Imdād al-Aḥkām: 1/187; Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 1/383; Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 1/492; Khayr al-Fatāwā: 1/587; Kifāyat al-Muftī: 1/147, Dār al-Ishāʿat; Rāh-e Sunnat, pp. 160–167, chapter on the mawlid gathering, Maktabah Ṣafdariyyah).

Rāh-e Sunnat states:

A full six centuries had gone by without this innovation being current anywhere among the Muslims. It did not occur to any Companion, nor to any Successor, nor to any hadith scholar, nor to any jurist, nor to any pious elder, nor to any saint. If it occurred to anyone, it occurred to an extravagant king and to the worldly-minded scholar who kept him company. This innovation was brought into being in the year 604 AH in the city of Mosul, by the order of Muẓaffar al-Dīn Kūkrī ibn Arbil (d. 630 AH), a king who was extravagant and indifferent to religion. (Rāh-e Sunnat, p. 162, on the history of the mawlid gathering, Maktabah Ṣafdariyyah).

In short, the excesses that have been made current in the name of celebrating the birth — and which are added to year after year — run entirely counter to the call of Islam, to its spirit and to its temper. It is therefore necessary to give up all these customs and objectionable practices. May Allah protect us all from innovations and excesses, and grant us true reverence and love for the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and true obedience to him. Āmīn!
