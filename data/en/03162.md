### TITLE
Answers to the objections raised by some scholars against a fatwa of Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā on digital pictures

### QUESTION
Answers to the objections raised by some scholars against a fatwa of Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā on digital pictures.

### ANSWER
All the scholars and muftis of the present age agree that there is room for having a photograph taken and made for an identity card, an ID, a passport, and for employment and educational papers. Then, whatever kind of journey is undertaken with a passport — the journey of the obligatory Hajj, or a journey for a voluntary Hajj or ʿumrah, or a journey to visit people, or a journey for sightseeing — every kind of journey is included in it. Now if a passport and the like is obtained for a merely permissible journey, or for sightseeing, or for a recommended journey, an objection arises: how has something unlawful or impermissible been tolerated for the sake of a recommended or merely permissible act? For those who hold this to be a real picture, one would have to pass through an unlawful or impermissible act for the sake of a recommended act, and that is not correct in the sharia. Some muftis, however, have found this solution: that a real picture is the likening of a manufactured thing to a creature of Allah — which is supported by those hadiths in which words of likening are expressly used for pictures — whereas the picture in use today is the capturing of the shadow of a creature; so this picture is not a real picture but only something resembling a picture. Therefore, if there is no harm in it in terms of the sharia, and there is a religious benefit, and there is need and necessity, and it is not for indulgence or for the pleasure of the eye, and there is no aspect of illicit looking in it, then in the present age some muftis hold that there is room for it.

It should be remembered that saying it is not a real picture does not mean giving such pictures a free hand. One must therefore look at whether there is harm in it in terms of the sharia; if there is, then this thing that merely resembles a picture should be altogether forbidden — as when people commonly take pointless photographs, or take photographs of mixed gatherings of men and women, or when photographs are taken of some ordinary meeting or function. But if there is a religious benefit in it, then in the view of some scholars the act will be permissible; for instance, there will be room for having a photograph taken for a journey of voluntary Hajj and ʿumrah, or for a journey abroad to visit people. In citing this view of some scholars my purpose is not to deny that it is harmful; my purpose is to look for a way of escaping the threats that have come in the hadiths — for example:

أشد الناس عذاباً يوم القيمة المصورون، يقال لهم أحيوا ما خلقتم" (مسند أحمد، رقم: ٤۷۹۲، قال الشيخ شعيب: صحيح وهذا اسناد ضعيف لضعف عاصم بن عبيد الله بن عاصم بن عمر)

The people who will suffer the severest punishment are the picture-makers; they will be told: give life to what you have made.

أشد الناس عذاباً يوم القيامة الذين يضاهون بخلق الله، (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۵۹۵٤، باب ما وطئ من التصاوير، وصحيح مسلم، رقم: ۲۱۰۷)، من صور صورة في الدنيا كلف يوم القيامة أن ينفخ فيها الروح، (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۵۹٦۳، باب من صور صورة، وصحيح مسلم، رقم: ۲۱۱۰)

This was a device for saving people from these threats. Otherwise, if the angels ask why you took so great a threat upon your head for the sake of a merely permissible or recommended journey, the answer is easy: that this is not a picture in the sense of the sharia — that is, I did not make a manufactured thing resembling a creature of God; rather, at the press of a button I preserved the shadow of a creature of Allah, and there is a religious benefit in it and no harm, or it is a way of exercising the right to travel. Yes, if a photograph of some gathering is taken to no purpose, then even if it is given the name of a keepsake it is still not correct.

The religious purposes for which the scholars permit photography with a modern camera include, besides voluntary Hajj and ʿumrah, government documents and certificates, and the installation of cameras to identify thieves. Furthermore, in some countries mountains of oppression are being brought down upon Muslims; by publishing pictures of this a voice is raised against the oppressors and the way is smoothed for helping the oppressed. Through these pictures the Muslim and non-Muslim nations of the world turn their attention to assisting them, and the oppressed find refuge. In volume seven of Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā we wrote, in the light of the statements of certain muftis, that there is room for this in religious programmes; what is meant is a religious programme of this kind, not the ordinary religious meetings and processions, in the photographing of which there is no benefit but only harm. Our earlier writing should be read in the light of this one.

I have also heard that if a man from a country such as Pakistan or India is living in America or Canada and his marriage takes place in his own country, Pakistan or India, then until there is a video recording of the marriage gathering these countries do not grant the girl a visa. Likewise, people give donations to certain relief organisations, which then distribute food grains and other things among the afflicted, and pictures of this are taken so that the donors may be satisfied that their donations are being spent in the right place. Similarly, those in authority in some African countries complain that wealthy Muslims spend their charity in their own countries in Asia and do not spend it in Africa, where they live; so pictures of charity being distributed among Africans satisfy them that work is being done there too. Those Deobandi scholars who hold that digital pictures are permitted, or that there is room for them, hold this in such cases; they do not hold it for pictures taken out of fondness, for keepsake pictures, for pictures of every religious meeting and procession, for pictures of mixed gatherings, or for pictures of some assembly taken merely to win applause.

To come to the point: a religious scholar of the province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa in Pakistan published a treatise on the prohibition of pictures, adorned with the signatures of various scholars and muftis, in which he also made certain passages of our Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā a target of criticism. The compiler sent this treatise to this humble servant during the Baqrah ʿĪd holidays, at a time when I had my foot in the stirrup for a journey to South Africa. This critical essay reached me through our friend and relative Mawlānā Maḥbūb Sulṭān (may his blessings endure). I kept the critical essay by me and thought that, if it contained things worth accepting, I would accept them, and that if it was an essay calling for a reply I would write a brief reply to it. This essay lay by me for many days while I kept wondering whether or not to write an answer to some of its points. Sometimes it came to my mind that it was better not to write, because Ḥālī long ago composed this couplet:

Then the thought came to me that this essay of the Mawlānā is giving rise to certain misunderstandings about me — that I regard every kind of picture as correct, whereas that is not so, as I have explained — and that in a written reply some useful points would also come before readers. So I resolved to write a brief answer.

Before the answer let me submit this: since in the Mawlānā's view committing something unlawful is permissible for the sake of a recommended or merely permissible matter — as, for example, it is permissible to pay a bribe in order to obtain one's own right, the obtaining of which is not obligatory — then if some committee invites a Mawlānā to give a speech and also tells him that there will be photography, according to the compiler the Mawlānā ought not to refuse to go, because a gathering is a recommended matter, that is,

ما يكون فعله أولي من تركه. (حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح، ص ۷۵، فصل من آداب الوضوء، قديمي، وفتاوى الشامي: ۱/۱۰۲، سعيد،
Dhikr-e Ijtimāʿī Jahrī Sharīʿat ke Āʾīne meṉ, p. 263, published by Zamzam),

so on the Mawlānā's own statement the committing of something impermissible ought to be allowed for it — whereas the Mawlānā does not hold this. And the statements of the jurists are not merely arguments used to silence an opponent; they are also statements of the actual position. So how can the committing of something unlawful ever be permissible for the sake of a sunnah or recommended matter?

Now we come to the main claim. The rule or principle we stated — that committing something unlawful is not permissible for the sake of a recommended or sunnah act — has been mentioned by numerous jurists. The manuscript of Khizānat al-Riwāyāt records:

في الذخيرة: أن المسلم يختتن ما لم يبلغ فإذا بلغ لم يختن لأن ستر عورة البالغ فرض والختان سنة فلا يترك الفرض للسنة والكافر إذا أسلم يختن بالاتفاق لمخالفته دين الإسلام وهو بالغ. (خزانة الروايات، ص ۳٦۱، مخوطة)،

A photocopy of the manuscript of Khizānat al-Riwāyāt is present in the library of our Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā in South Africa, and this passage was also cited by Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him) in Majmūʿat al-Fatāwā. In the Urdu translation of Majmūʿat al-Fatāwā the passage runs thus. Question: is circumcision necessary for an adult who has not been circumcised, or for an unbeliever who has become a Muslim, or not? Answer: circumcision is not obligatory; according to the more correct view it is an emphasised sunnah, and therefore there is no need to circumcise a grown man. Yes, if an adult has himself circumcised, or buys a slave-woman who performs circumcision, or marries a woman who performs circumcision and has himself circumcised, it is permissible. And an unbeliever who has become a Muslim should be circumcised. (Khizānat al-Riwāyāt, p. 361). From (Muʿallim al-Fiqh, the Urdu translation of Majmūʿat al-Fatāwā: 2/85, published at Aram Bagh, Karachi). In this passage of Khizānat al-Riwāyāt the rule is stated explicitly, namely that an obligation will not be abandoned — that is, something unlawful will not be committed — in order to fulfil a sunnah. This principle is mentioned in the books of the other schools too, which we shall mention later, if Allah Most High wills.

2. Reconciliation is better and recommended in comparison with litigation and an outright judgment. Allah says:

﴿والصلح خير﴾

Imam al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) set up a chapter:

باب فضل الإصلاح بين الناس. (۱/۳۷۳)

and he set up another chapter:

باب إذا أشار الإمام بالصلح فأبي حكم عليه بالحكم البين. (۱/۳۷۳)۔

And ʿAllāmah al-ʿAynī wrote at the beginning of the Book of Reconciliation in al-Bukhārī:

ذكر هذه الآية في بيان فضل الإصلاح بين الناس وأن الصلح أمر مندوب إليه. (۹/۵۷۲)،

In the Noble Qur'an reconciliation is called good, and Tafsīr al-Samarqandī states:

والصلح خير من الخصومة، [النساء: ۱۲۸]،

that is, reconciliation is better than litigation. But the jurists forbade lying for the sake of reconciliation and told people rather to use allusions and hints, so that something unlawful is not committed for a recommended act. Sakb al-Anhur (4/221) and al-Durr al-Mukhtār with Radd al-Muḥtār (6/427, published by Saʿīd) state:

والكذب حرام إلا في الحرب للخدعة وفي الصلح بين اثنين في إرضاء الأهل وفي دفع الظالم عن الظلم والمراد التعريض لأن عين الكذب حرام،

In this passage outright lying, and the committing of something unlawful for the sake of a good work such as reconciliation, is forbidden. And where some authorities do permit outright lying, that view is either the weaker one, or it is for saving an oppressed person from being killed.

3. Imam al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) cited those reports in which praying with one's clothes or hair gathered up is forbidden; yet along with this, if a man's cloth is short, he is directed to gather it up and pray, because uncovering the private parts is unlawful — so something unlawful will not be committed in order to complete a sunnah or a recommended act. That is why Imam al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) set up the chapter باب عقد الثياب. Ḥaḍrat Shaykh al-Ḥadīth Mawlānā Muḥammad Zakariyyā (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote in his commentary on the chapter headings:

والغرض من عقد هذا الباب ههنا أن ما سيأتي من النهي عن كف الثياب محمول على الأمن من الكشف أما إذا خاف كشف العورة فلا بد من عقدها، لأن الفرض أهم من المستحبات. (الأبواب والتراجم لصحيح البخاري، ص ۹۵، ط: سعيد).

And ʿAllāmah al-ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote:

أن في ضم الثوب أمناً من كشف العورة. (عمدة القاري: ٤/۵٦۱، ط: دار الحديث، ملتان)،

In this passage ʿAllāmah al-ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) and Shaykh al-Ḥadīth Mawlānā Zakariyyā (may Allah have mercy on him) have plainly stated the rule that covering the private parts is obligatory, so uncovering them is unlawful, and therefore an unlawful act will not be done in order to complete a recommended one.

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

الصلح جائز بين المسلمين إلا صلحاً حرم حلالاً أو أحل حراماً

إسناده صحيح. (سنن الترمذي، رقم ۱۳۵۲، باب ما ذكر عن رسول الله ﷺ في الصلح بين المسلمين)،

The jurists have cited this hadith in various places in their books. The meaning of this hadith is that every reconciliation is permissible except a reconciliation by which something unlawful becomes lawful, or the reverse — that is, the unlawful is not to be made lawful for the sake of reconciliation; in other words, something unlawful is not to be committed.

Objections and answers. To come to the point: the Mawlānā who compiled the fatwas about pictures has raised this objection against the writing of Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: that uncovering the private parts for the circumcision of an adult is the committing of something unlawful for the sake of a sunnah — in other words, that the principle that something unlawful should not be committed for a recommended act or a sunnah is not sound.

The answer to this objection is that adults are of two kinds. 1. An adult who is a new Muslim: his circumcision is necessary in any case, unless he cannot bear it, in which case it will be left. Since he has left unbelief and entered Islam, it is necessary for him to abandon the marks of unbelief and to come right out of the shell of unbelief; no mark of unbelief should remain upon him, and so circumcision is emphasised in his case. The jurists agree that he is to be circumcised, so that this new Muslim may show his complete renunciation of the marks of unbelief. Circumcision is therefore necessary for him — that is, here the sunnah is joined by the distinguishing symbols of Islam and by the removal of the marks of unbelief, and so circumcision became necessary and the uncovering of the private parts became permissible. It is as when the people of a settlement abandon the call to prayer: war is waged against them, because the call to prayer, besides being an emphasised sunnah, is among the distinguishing symbols of Islam, and so its abandonment is a ground for fighting. The new Muslim is under the command:

﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا ادخلوا في السلم كافة﴾ [البقرة: ۲۰۸]،

He should not do as certain new Muslims did, who said: we shall act upon the law of Moses and give up camel meat, we shall also honour the Sabbath day, and we shall act upon the rulings of Islam as well. Concerning them this verse was revealed:

﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا ادخلوا في السلم كافة﴾ [البقرة: ۲۰۸]،

This is the substance of a fatwa in Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah. Yes, if a non-Muslim has not the strength to bear circumcision, then circumcision will be left.

الشيخ الضعيف إذا أسلم ولا يطيق الختان إن قال أهل البصر لا يطيق يترك لأن ترك الواجب بالعذر جائز فترك السنة أولي كذا في الخلاصة. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳۵۷).

The upshot is that if a new Muslim can bear it, his circumcision is necessary. And if a born Muslim was not circumcised in the days of his childhood, will he now be circumcised after reaching puberty or not? There are two views on this. 1. One view is that he will not be circumcised. Yes, if he is able to circumcise himself, or his wife can do it, then it will be done; otherwise not. This view was adopted by the author of al-Dhakhīrah, the author of Khizānat al-Riwāyāt, Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him) and others. The proof they gave for it is that the private parts will not be uncovered and something unlawful will not be committed for the sake of a sunnah. The Mālikīs adopted this same view.

Ḥāshiyat al-ʿAdawī ʿalā Sharḥ Kifāyat al-Ṭālib al-Rabbānī states:

والنظر لعورة الكبير المراهق أو البالغ حرام لقول اللخمي المناهز ككبير ولا يرتكب محرماً لفعل سنة ويظهر أنه يؤمر بختن نفسه لأن المكلف مأمور بفعل ما تم له إسلامه. (۱/۵۹٦، دار الفكر)،

And Ashal al-Madārik Sharḥ Irshād al-Sālik fī Madhhab Imām al-Aʾimmah Mālik (3/364, Dār al-Fikr) states:

أما الكبير فإنه يومر باختتان نفسه إن أمكن كمن أسلم بعد البلوغ بحرمة نظر عورة البالغ،

And بلغة السالك لا قر ب المسالك states:

ولا يجوز للبالغ أن يكشف عورته لغيره لا جل الختان بل إن لم يمكنه الفعل بنفسه سقطت السنة. (حاشية الصاوي: ۲/۱۵۲، دار المعارف).

Yes, since in the view of the Shāfiʿīs and the Ḥanbalīs circumcision is obligatory, an adult's uncovering of his private parts for it is sound. Al-Tahdhīb fī Fiqh al-Imām al-Shāfiʿī states:

و يجوز كشف عورة المختون لا جل الختان ولولا وجوبه لما جاز ترك الواجب لا جله. (۱/۲۱۸)،

And Imam al-Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in المجموع شرح المهذب:

فلوكان الختان سنة لما كشفت العورة المحرم كشفها له. (۱/۳۰۰، باب السواك، دار الفكر).

The second view is that an adult will be circumcised. In the soundest view of the Ḥanafīs circumcision is an emphasised sunnah; but since it is among the distinguishing symbols of Islam, and such symbols are attached to the obligations, circumcision will be performed. Al-Durr al-Mukhtār with al-Shāmī states:

الأصل أن الختان سنة كما جاء في الخبر وهو من شعائر الإسلام، وخصائصه فلو اجتمع أهل بلدة على تركه حاربهم الإمام فلا يترك إلا لعذر وعذر شيخ لا يطيقه ظاهر. (الدر المختار مع فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۷۵۱، مسائل شتي، ط: سعيد).

Imdād al-Aḥkām states: The jurists have expressly stated regarding circumcision that it is permissible to circumcise an adult and to look at and touch the body to the extent needed; but exposure beyond the need is not permissible. So no one should look at anything but the place of circumcision, and the rest of the body should be kept covered even from the circumciser. And circumcision is not merely a sunnah; it is also among the distinguishing symbols of Islam. (Imdād al-Aḥkām by Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānwī, may Allah have mercy on him: 4/428, published by Maktabah Dār al-ʿUlūm, Karachi).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states: Although it is not permissible even to look at the private parts of a four-year-old child without need, it is permissible to uncover the private parts when treatment requires it; and circumcision is one of the distinguishing symbols of Islam, so uncovering them for it is permissible with all the more reason. The same ruling applies to an adult and to a new Muslim. However, if he can circumcise himself, or can have it done by his wife or his slave-woman, then uncovering the private parts before another is not permissible. And if for some reason he cannot bear the pain of circumcision, this ruling lapses. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 9/61, published by Saʿīd).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states: in answer to a question about an uncircumcised adult he said: if such a person cannot circumcise himself, and nowadays there are no slave-women who could perform the circumcision, then he may have himself circumcised. (12/351, published by Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states: Circumcision is an emphasised sunnah and is among the distinguishing symbols of Islam; without a severe excuse it does not lapse even in the case of an adult. (19/466, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

In al-Mabsūṭ, concerning fighting those who abandon the call to prayer, it is written that the abandonment of anything that belongs to the distinguishing symbols of Islam is a dishonouring of the religion, and that fighting will be undertaken over its abandonment. ʿAllāmah al-Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

وقال محمد: ما كان من أعلام الدين فالإصرار على تركه استخفاف بالدين فيقاتلون على ذلك. (المبسوط للسرخسي: ۱/۱۳۳، ط: إدارة القرآن والعلوم الإسلامية، كراتشي).

Al-Shāmī with al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

والقتال عليه لما أنه من أعلام الدين. (فتاوى الشامي مع الدر: ۱/۳۸٤، سعيد).

In al-Durr al-Mukhtār (1/384) it is written: وهي كالواجب. And in another place al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ينظر الطبيب إلى موضع مرضها بقدر الضرورة إذ الضرورات تتقدر بقدرها، وكذا نظر قابلة وختان، (الدر المختار: ٦/۳۷۰، سعيد)

On this ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) states:

لأن الختان سنة للرجال من جملة الفطرة لا يمكن تركها. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۳۷۱، سعيد).

The upshot is that committing something unlawful for the sake of a recommended act is not correct, and this is why, according to some jurists, the circumcision of an adult or of a boy near puberty will be left; while according to other jurists he will be circumcised, since circumcision, besides being an emphasised sunnah, is among the distinguishing symbols of Islam and is attached to the obligations, and therefore the uncovering of the private parts was tolerated for it. Concerning the abandonment of the call to prayer, al-Shāmī states:

لو اجتمع أهل بلدة على تركه قاتلهم عليه ولو تركه واحد ضربته وحبسته وعامة المشايخ على الأول (أي كونه سنة) والقتال عليه لما أنه من أعلام الدين. (فتاوى الشامي: ۱/۳۸٤، سعيد).

The second issue from which the Mawlānā who criticised our writing argued that something unlawful may be committed for the sake of a recommended act is the paying of a bribe in order to obtain one's own right. The Mawlānā says: a bribe is unlawful and it is obligatory to keep away from it; but for a right of one's own, the obtaining of which is neither obligatory nor necessary, one may pay a bribe:

إذا دفع الرشوة خوفاً على نفسه أو ماله فهو حرام على الآخذ غير حرام على الدافع.

This threat does not apply to one who pays a bribe in order to recover his own right or to save himself from oppression. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 18/463, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

The Mawlānā's point is that obtaining one's right is not obligatory at all, and yet paying a bribe for it — that is, committing something unlawful — is permissible; so, on his view, committing something unlawful for a recommended or sunnah act would be sound.

The answer to this is that giving something in order to recover one's own right is outwardly a bribe, but in reality it is not a bribe, because one definition of a bribe, which is commonly well known, is:

الرشوة ما يعطي لا بطال حق أو لا حقاق باطل. (التعريفات للجرجاني، ص ۱۱۱، ط: دار الكتب العلمية).

Wealth given in order to obtain one's own right does not fall under bribery, nor does it fall under what is unlawful. ʿAllāmah ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him), the son of ʿAllāmah Ibn ʿĀbidīn Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) — who was a great scholar of research, was among the compilers and authors of al-Majallah al-ʿAdliyyah, and compiled the supplement to al-Shāmī — compiled a useful book for young people whose name is الهدية العلائية, in which the late scholar says:

دفع المال للحاكم الجائر لدفع الظلم عن نفسه وماله أو استخراج حق له لا يتوصل إليه إلا به ليس برشوة في حق الدافع.
(al-Hadiyyah al-ʿAlāʾiyyah, 219, published by Dār Ibn Ḥazm).

That is, giving wealth to a tyrannical ruler in order to repel oppression, or to reach a right which a man cannot reach without it, is not a bribe on the part of the giver. Al-Rasāʾil al-Zayniyyah fī al-Madhhab al-Ḥanafiyyah states:

أما الرشوة في اصطلاح الفقهاء فقد عرفها الجرجاني بأنها ما يعطي لا بطال حق أو لا حقاق باطل، وقال النووي رحمه الله: الرشوة محرمة على القاضي وغيره من الولاة مطلقاً لأنها تدفع إليه ليحكم بحق أو ليمتنع من ظلم وكلاهما واجب عليه فلا يجوز أخذ العوض عليه. وأما دافع الرشوة فإن توصل بها إلى باطل فحرام عليه وهو المراد بالراشي وإن توصل بها إلى تحصيل حق ودفع ظلم فليس بحرام. (الرسائل الزينية في مذهب الحنفية، ص ٦۰۵، ط: دار السلام، وفقه المعاملات: ٤/۷۷، والفتاوى الاقتصادية: ۱/۸۸)

The substance of Imam al-Nawawī's words (may Allah have mercy on him) is that it is not permissible for a judge to take a bribe, because the judge is bound to give a correct judgment and to save people from oppression, and taking a payment for that is impermissible. As for the payment of a bribe: if the aim is to obtain something impermissible, then paying it is unlawful; and if the aim is to obtain one's own right or to repel oppression, then paying it is not unlawful. There is further detail on this in Fatḥ al-Qadīr, Kitāb al-Ṣulḥ, and in Qurrat ʿAyn al-Akhyār; to avoid undue length we have omitted the passages of these and other books.

From the detail and the passages given above it is clear that wealth given in order to obtain one's own right does not come under bribery at all; and if outwardly it does come under it, it is not unlawful but is excepted from the unlawful. Hence there is here no case of committing something unlawful for the sake of something that is not obligatory.

The third objection, which the Mawlānā has raised, is that medical treatment is a sunnah while covering the private parts is obligatory, yet the jurists have permitted the uncovering of the private parts for the sake of treatment — and this is the committing of something unlawful for the sake of a sunnah. He then cited references from the jurist Abū al-Layth and from Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah, Fatāwā al-Nawāzil and others, to the effect that if a person dies because he did not eat he is disobedient and sinful, whereas if a person dies because he did not seek treatment he is not sinful; and at the end he drew the conclusion that an unlawful act was performed in order to fulfil a sunnah.

The answer to this is that to call medical treatment a sunnah in every situation, and not to call it binding and necessary, is open to question. The noble jurists do indeed write:

قال الفقيه أبو الليث: ولو مرض ولم يعالج حتى مات لم يأثم بخلاف الجائع إذا لم يأكل حتى مات بالجوع يأثم به. (فتاوى النوازل، ص ۲۰۰، كتاب الكراهية، ط: آرام باغ كراتشي).

But the jurists' statement is to be understood of the case where the success of the treatment is not certain. Yes, if it is certain, or all but certain, that the treatment will hit the mark and that death will be averted, then treatment too is binding. Imam al-Ghazālī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn:

أحدهما أن معالجة الجوع والعطش بالماء والخبز جلي واضح يدركه كافة الناس ومعالجة الصفراء بالسكنجبين يدركه بعض الخواص فمن أدرك ذلك بالتجربة التحق في حقه بالأول. (إحياء علوم الدين: ٤/۲۷۸)
