### TITLE
Raising the hands for the supplication after the adhan

### QUESTION
Should the supplication after the adhan be made with the hands raised, or without raising them?

### ANSWER
Supplications for which particular wordings have been transmitted, but for which the raising of the hands is not specifically established, have the status of formulas of remembrance. It is better not to raise the hands for them — for example, the supplication after the adhan, and the supplications for eating and drinking and for entering the lavatory.

Fayḍ al-Bārī states:

(باب الدعاء عند النداء) والمسنون في هذا الدعاء ألا ترفع الأيدي، لأنه لم يثبت عن النبي ﷺ رفعها والتشبث فيه بالعمومات بعد ما ورد فيه خصوص فعله ﷺ لغو، فإنه لم يرد فيه خصوص عادته ﷺ لنفعنا التمسك بها، وما إذا نقل إلينا خصوص الفعل فهو الأسوة الحسنة لمن كان يرجو الله والدار الآخرة. (فيض الباري: ۲/۱٦۷، باب الدعاء عند النداء)

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

Supplication is of two kinds. (1) Asking for some need in one's own words, without any set wording. (2) Supplications with fixed wordings, whether tied to a particular occasion or not. The hadiths about raising the hands relate to the first kind, not to the second — إلا ما ورد فيه النص. Accordingly, in the transmitted supplications after ablution, on entering and leaving the mosque, on entering and leaving the house, on entering and leaving the lavatory, and so on, no one holds that the hands are raised. (احسن الفتاوی: ۲/۲۹۸)

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states:

Raising the hands is not specifically established for this occasion. (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: ۲/۱۱۰، از مفتی عزیز الرحمٰن صاحب رحمه الله) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
