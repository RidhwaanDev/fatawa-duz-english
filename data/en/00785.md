### TITLE
Delivering the sermon in a language other than Arabic

### QUESTION
Is it necessary to deliver the sermon in Arabic, or is it a sunnah? And if someone delivers the sermon in a language other than Arabic, what is the ruling?

### ANSWER
Delivering the sermon in Arabic is an emphasised sunnah and is required. Delivering it in any other language is strictly disliked. Throughout their lives, the practice of Rasūlullāh ﷺ and the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) was to deliver the sermon in Arabic. They spread through the east and the west, conquered non-Arab lands and delivered sermons there, yet there is not a single instance of their delivering the Friday or Eid sermon in a language other than Arabic.

Some people raise the objection that the sermon is a reminder and an admonition, so it ought to be in the language of the listeners. But they should consider that the sermon is an act of remembrance. Allah says in the Noble Qur'an:

﴿فاسعوا إلى ذكر الله﴾

That is: hasten to the remembrance of Allah. According to the scholars of hadith and the commentators, "the remembrance of Allah" here means precisely the Friday sermon. A hadith too describes the sermon as remembrance:

فإذا خرج الإمام حضرت الملائكة يستمعون الذكر. (بخاري شريف: ۱/۱۲۱/۸۷۱، باب فضل الجمعة)

That is: when the imam stands up to deliver the sermon, the angels come to listen to it.

It is clear from this that the sermon is an act of remembrance, just as prayer and the adhan are. So, just as prayer and the adhan must be in Arabic, the sermon must be in Arabic too.

Shāh Walī Allāh al-Muḥaddith al-Dihlawī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote in his commentary on the Muwaṭṭaʾ:

وأما كونها عربية فلاستمرار أهل الإسلام في المشارق والمغارب به مع أن في كثير من الأقاليم كان المخاطبون أعجمين. (مصفي شرح موطأ: ص ۱٥٤ باب التشديد على من ترك الجمعة من غير عذر)

That is: the sermon must be in Arabic because all the people of Islam in the east and the west have kept to this practice, even though in many regions those addressed were non-Arabs. It is therefore necessary to keep to Arabic for the Friday and Eid sermons.

ʿAllāmah al-Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him) also ruled that a sermon in a language other than Arabic is strictly disliked:

فإنه لا شك في أن الخطبة بغير العربية خلاف السنة المتوارثة من النبي ﷺ والصحابة ث فيكون مكروهاً تحريماً. (عمدة الرعاية على شرح الوقاية: ۱/۲۰۰، رقم الحاشية ۲
