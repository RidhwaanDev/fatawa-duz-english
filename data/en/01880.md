### TITLE
Taking back the goods sold when the price is not paid

### QUESTION
Zayd bought a machine from ʿAmr on instalments, agreeing to pay 500 rand a month. He paid the instalments for a while and then stopped paying, and because of this the seller took the machine back. Must he now return the price he has collected from the buyer, or not?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, the buyer is in the first place the owner of that machine, so it was neither permissible nor correct to take the machine from him. Even so, if the seller took the machine back with the buyer's consent, this comes under the ruling of iqālah (mutual cancellation of the sale), and in iqālah, when the goods sold are given back, it is necessary and obligatory to return the price as well. Otherwise both counter-values would end up together in the ownership of one person, which is contrary to the law of the sharia.

See what is stated in Sharḥ al-Majallah:

البيع مع تأجيل الثمن وتقسيطه صحيح. أي والتأجيل لا زم، فليس للبائع حبس المبيع حتى يقبضه ولا المطالبة به قبل حلول الأجل. (شرح المجلة لمحمد خالد الأتاسي، ۲/۱٦٦).

This shows that in a sale by instalments the buyer is the owner of the goods sold, so the seller cannot take possession of them. But if he does take them back, it will come under the ruling of iqālah, and it will be necessary to return the price the buyer has paid.

See what is stated in al-Hidāyah:

الإقالة جائزة في البيع بمثل الثمن الأول

فإن شرطا أكثر منه أو أقل فالشرط باطل ويرد مثل الثمن الأول. (الهداية: ۳/٦۹).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

There is no prohibition on there being a difference between the cash price and the credit price, provided the instalments are fixed, and provided it is not the case that, when an instalment is not received at its appointed time, a further increase is made in the price, or the sums already received are confiscated and the motorcycle is lost as well. If it is like that, then this transaction is not valid in the sharia; rather, it involves interest and gambling, and the prohibition of both of these is mentioned in the sacred texts. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱٦/٤٦، مبوب و مرتب).

It is recorded in Āp ke Masāʾil:

The condition that, if for some reason he is unable to pay three months' instalments, the seller will take the vehicle into his possession and the instalments already paid will be forfeited, is wrong in the sharia. The seller has the right to recover his instalments through legal means, but he is not entitled to take the vehicle into his possession, nor is he entitled to swallow up the instalments already paid. (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ٦/۱۵٤).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

Charging a higher price on account of credit is permissible, but the condition that, if all the instalments are not paid, the goods sold will be taken back and the instalments already paid will be confiscated, is a corrupt condition, and for that reason this transaction is not permissible. (احسن الفتاوی: ٦/۵۱۹).

Some scholars, however, hold that when the buyer does not pay the price, the seller may rescind the contract unilaterally and take back the goods sold. In the present age this is the easier view, and it is more appropriate to give the fatwa according to it.

See what is stated in al-Hidāyah:

ومن قال لا خر اشتريت مني هذه الجارية فأنكر الآخر إن أجمع البائع على ترك الخصومة وسعه أن يطأها لان المشتري لما جحد كان فسخاً من جهته إذا الفسخ يثبت به كما إذا تجاحدا فإذا عزم البائع على ترك الخصومة تم الفسخ وبمجرد العزم وإن كان لا يثبت الفسخ فقد اقترن بالفعل وهو إمساك الجارية ونقلها وما يضاهيه ولأنه لما تعذر استيفاء الثمن من المشتري فات رضاء البائع فيستبد بفسخه. (الهداية، ۳/۱٤٦، باب التحكيم).

Another passage of al-Hidāyah, however, appears to say the opposite. See:

قال: ومن اشترى عبداً فغاب والعبد في يد البائع وأقام البائع البينة أنه باعه إياه فإن كانت غيبته معروفة لم يبع في دين البائع لأنه يمكن إيصال البائع إلى حقه بدون البيع. (الهداية: ۳/۱۰۳، مسائل منثورة).

That is, if the buyer disappears before paying the price and his whereabouts are known, it is not permissible to sell the goods and recover the price from them, because the seller can obtain his right (through the judge).

The answer to this is that in that situation recovering the price is possible, since it is easy to bring a case before the judge and recover the price, whereas in the present age this is not possible: if a case is filed one has to wait several years, and on top of that the costs of litigation and so on are incurred. The better course, therefore, is to act upon the first passage and to rescind the contract unilaterally and take back the goods sold.

Ḥaḍrat Mufti Walī Ḥasan (may Allah have mercy on him) also used to give the fatwa on this basis.
