### TITLE
Divorce pronounced in a play by way of narration

### QUESTION
If a husband and wife act in a play and, in the course of some story, the husband divorces the wife, has this divorce taken effect or not? For instance, the story of Laylā and Majnūn is staged and the husband and wife play the parts of Laylā and Majnūn.

### ANSWER
Ḥaḍrat Muftī Walī Ḥasan (may Allah have mercy on him) used to say that divorce does not take effect in this, because this divorce is by way of narration and reproduction: as though Majnūn had divorced Laylā, or Farhād had divorced Shīrīn, which is the depiction of an imaginary event. This is not the bringing of a divorce into effect; it is narrating and reproducing what belongs to others, just as the author of the Maqāmāt reproduced the tales of Abū Zayd al-Sarūjī and al-Ḥārith ibn Hammām as fiction. That is why the author of the Maqāmāt incurs no sin of lying either, since imaginary tales are not false reports about anything that actually happened.

See what al-Fiqh al-Islāmī wa Adillatuhu says:

يشترط بالاتفاق القصد في الطلاق وهو إرادة التلفظ به ولو لم ينوه فلا يقع طلاق فقيه يكرره ولا طلاق حاك عن نفسه أو غيره لأنه لم يقصد معناه، بل قصد التعليم والحكاية. (الفقه الإسلامي وأدلته: ۷/۳٦۸، شروط الطلاق، دار الفكر).

Fatḥ al-Qadīr states:

لو كرر مسائل الطلاق بحضرة زوجته ويقول: أنت طالق ولا ينوى طلاقاً لا تطلق. (فتح القدير: ٤/٤، باب إيقاع الطلاق، دار الفكر).
