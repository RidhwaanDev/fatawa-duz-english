### TITLE
The ruling on Rainbow Chicken

### QUESTION
What is your opinion about Rainbow Chicken? Is it lawful to eat or not? If you have visited this abattoir and seen how they work, please shed detailed light on the question, since nowadays everyone, ordinary people and the learned alike, is involved in eating what they slaughter. بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
This humble servant, together with his colleagues, visited the Rainbow Chicken abattoir on Friday 5 December 2008. The purpose of going there was to see their procedure and whether it conforms to the sharia or not.

We saw the part of the abattoir where the chickens are hung on iron shackles and are then slaughtered by the slaughterers as they move along. We were told, and we also saw for ourselves, that care is taken right to the end to make sure that no bird dies before it is slaughtered. The man who hangs the bird first satisfies himself that it is alive; the slaughterer then checks it as he slaughters it; and after the slaughtering, a little further down the line, another man is stationed who sets aside any bird whose colour has changed, even if blood did flow from it at the time of slaughter.

There were four slaughterers in the abattoir, and each man slaughtered every fourth bird, so that he can comfortably recite the name of Allah over it. As the slaughterer was slaughtering, we too kept reciting bismillāh, and we were able to do so calmly and without difficulty. It is true that the bismillāh could not be heard above the noise of the machinery; but the slaughterers were Muslims, and thinking well of them requires us to assume that they were reciting it. A hadith says:

عن عائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها أن قوماً قالوا: يا رسول الله إن قوماً يأتونا باللحم لا ندري أذكر اسم الله عليه أم لا؟ فقال: "سموا عليه أنتم وكلوه، قالت: وكانوا حدثي عهد بكفر". (رواه البخاري: ۲/۸۲۹).

ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her) says that some people said: Messenger of Allah, certain people (new Muslims) bring us meat, and we do not know whether they recited bismillāh over it (at the time of slaughter) or not. The Prophet ﷺ said: recite bismillāh yourselves and eat it.

This hadith shows that if the slaughterers are Muslims and they affirm that they recite bismillāh, one may eat what they have slaughtered, and one should not think ill of them and suppose that they will not have recited it.

We also learnt that each slaughterer is given a break of twenty minutes after every forty minutes, or of half an hour after every hour, so that he can rest a little. Moreover, one Muslim supervisor is appointed by the company and another by SANHA, and the two of them keep watch over the slaughtering.

In our view, therefore, and according to what we observed, the chickens slaughtered there are lawful and Muslims may eat them. If some change has been made since then, or if they no longer take care, we are not answerable for that; we have written what we saw for ourselves.

Objections and their answers:

Some scholars raise objections to this slaughtering, and it seems necessary to mention them and to answer them. The objections and their answers are therefore set out below:

(1) The first objection is that before being slaughtered the chickens are hung upside down on iron shackles, which causes them distress and torment.

Answer: The answer is that, in my view, having the head down and the feet up is not a cause of distress or torment for birds and chickens.

In the villages we see chickens and birds day and night climbing up walls, with their heads up and their feet down, and then coming down again from the wall or the roof with their heads down and their feet up. This shows that in this respect chickens are not like human beings: for a man to have his head down and his feet up is distressing and a hardship. It is also a matter of common observation in the villages that when country people buy chickens in the market they hold them by the feet with their heads hanging down, and the birds suffer no particular distress, since they do not flap about much. Besides, hanging them upside down in the abattoir serves a purpose: their beaks touch the water below and this brings on a state of daze, numbness or reduced awareness. In short, they are fastened in this way for a good reason.

(2) The second objection: some people have said that dipping their beaks in the water gives them an electric shock, and that this is torture and torment, which is against the sharia. The sharia has commanded kindness. A hadith says:

عن شداد بن أوس رضي الله عنه قال: ثنتان حفظتهما عن رسول الله ﷺ، قال: إن الله تعالى كتب الإحسان على كل شيء فإذا قتلتم فأحسنوا القتلة، وإذا ذبحتم فأحسنوا الذبح، وليحد أحدكم شفرته وليرح ذبيحته. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۱۵۲، باب الامر بإحسان الذبح والقتل).

Shaddād ibn Aws (may Allah be pleased with him) says: there are two things I have committed to memory from the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ said: Allah has prescribed excellence in everything. So when you kill (a disbeliever), kill him well; and when you slaughter (an animal), slaughter it well. Let each of you sharpen his blade and give his animal ease.

The hadith shows that the animal being slaughtered should be given ease, whereas applying an electric current is contrary to that.

Answer: The answer is that the means of giving ease change from age to age. In our own time, for instance, when a person is operated on he is given an anaesthetic injection, which leaves him numb, dazed or unconscious, so that the surgery can be carried out easily. If a man's molar were to be pulled out without an anaesthetic, how much pain he would suffer! Indeed, the doctor would not be able to operate at all, and the patient would writhe in pain. But because of the injection that part of the gum becomes numb and the operation proceeds smoothly, which is nothing short of a great blessing for the patient.

In the same way, for the chickens this process is like an injection: it brings on a degree of daze and makes the slaughtering easy. Just as the pain of the injection is slight compared with the pain of an operation without one, so it is with the discomfort of the electrified water.

The current in this water, moreover, is generally between sixteen (16) and thirty (30) volts. As for those senior scholars who have written against this stunning process, we do not know what form it took in their day; indeed, the time of some of them was the early period of scientific progress. Nor do we know whether they actually saw the process or not. Had those senior scholars observed this procedure, they would in all likelihood have supported it. In any case, there is nothing apparently wrong with the process. We have seen in the abattoirs for cattle that a stunning shot is fired at the animal's forehead, after which it is slaughtered easily; whereas in the villages, on the other hand, several men take hold of the beast and put ropes round its legs, and sometimes it takes fright and bolts so violently that it injures several men in the crowd, and out of fear the animal's bowels loosen. Compared with that, the use of a shot appears the easier course — provided that the shot only produces a daze, and is not lethal or extremely painful. Where mechanical equipment is not available, of course, the old method is the right one.

(3) A third objection that has been raised is that contact with the electrified water carries a risk of death, so that these chickens become doubtful, and one should keep away from what is doubtful. The well-known hadith says:

الحلال بين والحرام بين، وبينهما مشتبهات، فمن اتقي الشبهات فقد استبرأ لدينه وعرضه". أو كما قال

That is: the lawful is clear and the unlawful is clear, and between them are doubtful matters. Whoever guards against doubtful matters has kept his religion and his honour unblemished. So one should be careful of doubtful things.

Answer: The answer is that when it is observed that the bird is alive after its beak has touched the electrified water, and its being alive is then confirmed by the slaughterer's inspection, by the slaughtering itself and by the flow of blood, it cannot be accepted that it is doubtful. If the bird fell asleep after touching the water there would still be some suspicion of death; but it does not even fall asleep — indeed its feathers move quite vigorously. We further learnt that if the birds are left alone after being stunned, they begin to walk about again after two or three minutes, which shows that after the stunning the chickens are fully alive.

When life is certain at the time of slaughter, any doubt carries no weight. Consider what Fatāwā Bayyināt states:

Where the animal is certainly alive after being stunned, an animal slaughtered in that state is lawful. Accordingly, chickens stunned by passing through a current of 70 to 90 volts, in which life is certain, will be lawful if slaughtered in that state. A chicken that has passed through a current of 100 to 120 volts, on the other hand, will not be lawful until it is certain that life remained in it before it was slaughtered. If life is certain before the slaughtering, then eating it afterwards is permissible; otherwise it is not. (Fatāwā Bayyināt: 4/550, Kitāb al-Dhabāʾiḥ).

(4) The fourth objection: it is also objected that the bird is put into hot water in order to remove its feathers, while there is filth and impurity in its belly, and there is a risk of that impurity mixing with the flesh and soaking into it.

Answer: The answer is that the bird merely passes through the water. When the line of chickens comes out, one does not even notice them remaining or halting inside; in so short a time impurity does not soak into the flesh. This point is set down in Marāqī al-Falāḥ, al-Ṭaḥṭāwī, Shāmī, al-Baḥr and other works.

Consider what Ḥāshiyat al-Ṭaḥṭāwī states:

لو ألقيت الدجاجة حال غليان الماء قبل أن يشق بطنها لنتف، أو كرش قبل أن يغسل إن وصل الماء إلى حد الغليان ومكث فيه بعد ذلك زماناً يقع في مثله التشرب والدخول في باطن اللحم لا تطهر أبداً إلا عند أبي يوسف رحمه الله كما مر في اللحم، وإن لم يصل الماء إلى حد الغليان أو لم تترك فيه إلا مقدار ما تصل الحرارة إلى سطح الجلد لانحلال مسام السطح عن الريش والصوف تطهر بالغسل ثلاثاً. (حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح، ص ۱٦۰).

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

لكن العلة المذكورة لا تثبت ما لم يمكث اللحم بعد الغليان زماناً يقع في مثله التشرب والدخول في باطن اللحم، وكل واحد منهما غير محقق في السميط. (فتاوى الشامي: ۱/۳۳٤، سعيد).

وللمزيد من البحث راجع: (فتح القدير: ۱/۲۱۱، دار الفكر، والبحر الرائق: ۱/۲۳۹، كوئته).

The gist of these passages is that if the water is not boiling, or it is boiling but the chickens remain in it only a very short time, then in both cases the chickens are pure — provided that, once they are taken out of that water, they are washed three times.

Consider what Fatāwā Bayyināt states:

If the chickens are slaughtered in the manner the sharia requires, that is, Muslims slaughter them with their own hands after reciting bismillāh, and after slaughtering they are put into the hot water and taken out at once, so that the filth and impurity inside them does not soak into them through the pores, then in that case eating these chickens will be lawful. Even so, before cooking, the meat should be washed well and made clean, so that any water carrying impurity from chickens slaughtered contrary to the sharia is removed.

And if they are kept in the hot water long enough for the heat to make the effects of the filth and impurity inside soak into the pores of the flesh, then in that case the meat too will be unlawful and eating it will not be permissible. (Fatāwā Bayyināt: 4/591, Kitāb al-Dhabāʾiḥ).

Note: we learnt afterwards that the water is in fact not boiling, since boiling water requires a temperature of 80 to 85 degrees, whereas the temperature of this water is 52 degrees, at which water does not boil, though it is certainly hot. The abattoir staff then examine the birds' livers, and if the colour of a liver has changed they set that bird aside — and the liver is something that cooks very quickly. It is clear from this that no effect of the water appears in a bird that passes through it, beyond the loosening of its feathers.

(5) The fifth objection is that in removing the feathers no wait is made for the birds to grow cold; rather the chickens are put into the hot water and cleaned while they are still twitching.

Answer: On this we would say that some of the jurists have written that it is disliked to remove the feathers before the bird has grown cold and the life has completely left it. Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd al-Laṭīf ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn Malak has written in Sharḥ Tuḥfat al-Mulūk:

وكل زيادة لا يحتاج إليها في الذكاة، مكروهة، كجر المذبوح برجله إلى المذبح وسلخه قبل أن يتم موته، وكذا لو مات ولم يبرد أي لم يسكن من الاضطراب يكره سلخه أيضاً عند البعض لما فيه من زيادة الألم. (شرح تحفة الملوك، ص ۱۷۷۲).

That is: every additional act that is not needed for the slaughtering is disliked, such as dragging the slaughtered animal to the place of slaughter by its leg, or skinning it before its death is complete. Likewise, if the animal has died but has not grown cold and its movement has not ceased, some jurists hold that skinning it then is disliked as well, because it involves causing extra pain.

This passage shows that in the view of some scholars it is disliked to remove the skin before movement has ceased. If the feathers or the skin are being removed while the bird is still twitching, the hot water should be moved a little further down the line; even so the slaughtered bird is lawful. As the author of al-Hidāyah says:

إلا أن الكراهة لمعني زائد وهو زيادة الألم قبل الذبح أو بعده فلا يوجب التحريم فلهذا قال (الإمام القدوري) توكل ذبيحته. (الهداية: ٤/٤۳۹، كتاب الذبائح).

In short, the dislikedness here arises from something extraneous, so the slaughtered animal does not become unlawful; rather, as Imam al-Qudūrī says, what he has slaughtered will be eaten. Still, it is the mark of a Muslim to avoid even disliked things as far as he can — but that is piety, not a legal ruling.

(6) The sixth objection: it has also been objected that the chickens are not slaughtered facing the qiblah, whereas al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وكره ترك التوجه إلى القبلة لمخالفة السنة. وقال الشامي: قوله لمخالفة السنة أي الموكدة لأنه توارثه الناس فيكره تركه بلا عذر. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٦/۲۹٦، كتاب الذبائح، سعيد).

Answer: The answer to this objection is that the slaughterers do face the qiblah. They should of course be instructed and advised to turn the bird's head towards the qiblah as well; and even supposing that a bird were not slaughtered facing the qiblah, it would still be lawful.

Consider what al-Ṭaḥṭāwī's notes on al-Durr al-Mukhtār state:

وتوجيه القبلة سنة مؤكدة وترك السنة لا يوجب الحرمة. (حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ٤/۱۵۲، كوئته).

Al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah records:

إذا ذبحها بغير توجه القبلة حلت ولكن يكره كذا في جواهر الأخلاطي. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۲۸۸).

Al-Muḥīṭ al-Burhānī states:

وإذا ذبحها متوجهة إلى غير القبلة حلت، ولكن يكره. (المحيط البرهاني: ٦/٤٦۲، كتاب الذبائح، الفصل الثاني في صفة الذكاة).

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

ويستحب أن يكون الذابح حال الذبح متوجهاً إلى القبلة. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/۷۹، سعيد).

The opposite of what is recommended is merely less than the best course. The upshot is that the slaughtered bird is lawful.

(7) The seventh objection: some scholars have objected that slaughtering chickens in the manner described above is not reported from the pious predecessors.

Answer: The answer is that we have not come across the method ordinary people follow reported from the pious predecessors either. Does any hadith state that when slaughtering a chicken you should put one foot on its wings and the other on its legs, take hold of its head and slaughter it?

That method was adopted simply because it was convenient for ordinary people. The object is that the animal be slaughtered in the manner the sharia lays down; the arrangements leading up to it change from age to age, and everywhere the easiest available method is adopted. In the same way the object is the acquisition of religious knowledge, but the syllabus for it keeps changing; or the object is the remembrance of Allah, but the methods for it — Naqshbandī, Chishtī and so on — keep changing; or the object is fighting in the way of Allah, and the weapons for it change from age to age; or the object is the pilgrimage to the House of Allah, but in former times people travelled for it on camels, whereas now the means of travel have changed and people travel by car and by aeroplane. Likewise, using a ballpoint pen, a calculator and a typewriter in place of the old pen and paper, using electric bulbs and lights in place of the old lamps, and using a wristwatch in place of a sundial is neither an innovation nor contrary to the sharia. How, then, can it be an innovation or contrary to the sharia to stun a cow by a modern method instead of having a group of men wrestle it to the ground? And how can it be an innovation or contrary to the sharia to control a chicken and slaughter it by a modern method instead of standing on its wings and its legs?

It is true that carelessness lets some disliked practices creep in, but these do not make the slaughtered animal unlawful. To draw up a list of disliked practices and on that basis pronounce the slaughtered animal unlawful is to take liberties with the ruling of the sharia.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
