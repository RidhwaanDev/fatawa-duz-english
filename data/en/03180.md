### TITLE
Punishing a student for coming late

### QUESTION
If a student comes late and the teacher becomes angry with him or punishes him, is there room for this in the sharia or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question it is permissible and sound to punish a student who comes late, and the following hadith points towards its permissibility.

عن جابر بن عبد الله رضي الله عنه قال: بعثني رسول الله ﷺ في حاجة له فانطلقت ثم رجعت وقد قضيتها فأتيت النبي ﷺ فسلمت عليه فلم يرد على فوقع في قلبي ما الله أعلم به فقلت في نفسي لعل رسول الله ﷺ وجد على إني أبطأت عليه ثم سلمت عليه فلم يرد على فوقع في قلبي أشد من المرة الأولي

الخ. (رواه البخاري: ۱/۱٦۲، باب لا يرد السلام في الصلاة، ط: فيصل).

Ḥaḍrat Jābir (may Allah be pleased with him) says: the Noble Prophet ﷺ sent me on some errand. I came back late and greeted him with salām, but he ﷺ did not reply. I thought that perhaps he was displeased because I had been late. In this narration coming late is given as the reason for his not returning the greeting or speaking.

In another narration of Bukhārī Sharīf it is stated that Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) used to put a rope or the like on the feet of his student ʿIkrimah. (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Tuḥfat al-ʿUlamāʾ: a man submitted, "O Messenger of Allah, so-and-so's prayer is so long that I fear I may lose heart and abandon the congregation." At this the Messenger of Allah ﷺ became so incensed that he had never been seen so incensed before. Then he said: "You people drive others away; whoever leads the prayer should make it light." Two things are established from this as well. The first is that if certain lessons are entrusted to one's student or to a junior teacher and a complaint is made about him, then the complaint should be heard and, after investigation, an arrangement should be made; it should not be that the complainant is disregarded merely because he is a student. The second is that if some improper act is committed by a student or a pupil, and it is known that greater benefit will come from speaking to him in anger, then to become angry is indeed the better course. (Tuḥfat al-ʿUlamāʾ 1/145, on hearing a complaint and issuing a reprimand for it).

The case of the three Companions who stayed behind from the expedition of Tabūk without an excuse — that no one spoke to them for fifty days and that their wives did not come near them — is mentioned in the books of hadith.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
