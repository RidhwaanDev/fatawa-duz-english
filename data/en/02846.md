### TITLE
The ruling on watching games on television

### QUESTION
Nowadays cricket and football have become worldwide. What is the position on watching these games on television, and what is the position on supporting the team of one's own choosing in them? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, because the following evils and corrupting features are found in the worldwide games current in our times, watching them and showing them are all impermissible. Consider some of the corrupting features and evils:

(1) These games have come to be regarded as an end in themselves.

(2) The absorption of those who take an interest in these games and players has increased to such a degree that disruption has begun to occur in necessary tasks, in the obligatory duties, and in the discharging of rights.

(3) Generally a great deal of time is being wasted in these games; even the blessed day of Friday is falling prey to sport.

(4) The players are being presented as national and communal heroes, so that the new generation, instead of taking the character of the Prophet, the Companions, the friends of Allah and the scholars as their pattern and example, are making these openly sinful and wicked players their ideal. This is a successful weapon of Western civilisation, and a poisoned sweet, for effacing Islamic civilisation.

(5) In some games the player's ʿawrah becomes visible, and a stern warning has come in the aḥādīth against looking at anyone's ʿawrah.

(6) Because of supporting one's favourite team, great quarrels are born over winning and losing.

(7) Along with the game, advertisements and pictures of naked or half-naked women are shown on television; moreover, in the playing ground there is intermingling of men and women. These scenes destroy morals, inflame the carnal passions, and become the forerunner of all sorts of crimes and sins.

In Jāmiʿ al-Fatāwā it is said:

Islam is a serious and pure religion; it teaches one to live a dignified life. It shows the undesirability and impropriety of games and shows and of futile occupations with which no sound and beneficial religious or worldly purpose is bound up — occupations whose whole yield is merely the passing of time, an imaginary and momentary carnal pleasure and diversion, a want of foresight and mere fanciful intoxication. For this reason it declares every idle sport, amusement and vain thing forbidden. Watching and listening to the game of cricket is of this very kind of vanity, idle sport and amusement. Furthermore, inclining towards the gatherings of people who are free of religion, having an attachment of the heart to them, mingling with them, mentioning them with interest, praising and lauding them, and many other such matters, cause the prohibition to become all the more severe. And the television is itself an instrument of amusement whose use it is obligatory to abandon; then upon it there also come naked and half-naked pictures of those who are not one's unmarriageable relatives, which are a complete representation of the person portrayed — whereas it is not permissible to look even at the clothes of a strange woman with desire and pleasure. Taking pleasure in what is impermissible, wasting time, increasing heedlessness of the Hereafter, and its leading on to many things such as the cinema, singing and so forth, all further confirm and establish its being impermissible. For this reason there can be no permission for it at all. (Jāmiʿ al-Fatāwā: 1/620).

In Maḥmūd al-Fatāwā it is said:

Who does not know that the attachment which exists to cricket in India, Pakistan and other countries, to football in Western Europe, to rugby in certain other European countries, and to tennis in America, is even greater than the description given above? As a result of it, it is not merely that this preoccupation is a waste of time and wealth; rather, this very attachment is many a time the cause of mutual clashes and disturbances. Moreover, in those cases in which the game itself will be forbidden, watching it will also be impermissible. When this is the ruling on being a spectator in the ground, then on television it is forbidden all the more. In the commentary too there is nothing but a waste of time along with an encouragement of the present-day cult of sport; for this reason that too is forbidden. (Maḥmūd al-Fatāwā: 3/128).
