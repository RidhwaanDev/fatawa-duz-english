### TITLE
The authenticity of the report about joining the heels in prostration

### QUESTION
Some hadiths mention joining the heels together while in prostration — for example the hadith found in Ṣaḥīḥ Ibn Khuzaymah, al-Bayhaqī, al-Ṭaḥāwī and elsewhere: "فوجدته ساجداً راصاً عقبيه". What is the standing of this report?

### ANSWER
This hadith is recorded in various books through different chains, but these words — "فوجدته ساجداً راصاً عقبيه" — are narrated by Yaḥyā ibn Ayyūb alone, and in narrating them he contradicts the other reliable narrators; the addition is therefore anomalous. A detailed assessment of the hadith follows. It is recorded in these books:

الخلاصة: الحديث أصله صحيح في صحيح مسلم: (۱/۳٥۲) وأحمد: (٦/۸٥، ۲۰۱) وأبو داود: (۱/٥٤۷) والنسائي: (۱/۱۰۲) وابن عبد البر: (۲۳/۳٤۹) ومالك: (۱/۲۱٤) والترمذي: (٥/٤۸۹) والطحاوي: (۳٤۱) والبغوي: (٥/۱٦٦) وليس عندهم رص العقبين، فهذا شاذ كما ذكر الحاكم. (ملخص من رسالة "لا جديد في أحكام الصلاة").

وللاستزادة انظر: (تحرير تقريب التهذيب: ٤/۷۸/۷٥۱۰، وتهذيب الكمال مع التعليقات: ۳۱/۲۳٦، ۲۳۸۲۳۷، وكتاب الضعفاء والمتروكين لابن الجوزي: ۳/۱۹۱/۳٦۹٤).

In short, Yaḥyā ibn Ayyūb is a narrator about whom scholars differ: some have criticised him, others have declared him reliable. If we grant, for the sake of argument, that he is weak, this report is denounced, because a weak narrator has contradicted reliable ones. And even if we accept him as acceptable, the report is still anomalous, because he has contradicted narrators of a higher rank.

It cannot be treated as an addition made by a reliable narrator, because in this hadith Yaḥyā ibn Ayyūb has contradicted the other reliable narrators. They report "فوقعت يدي على قدميه" or words to that effect, whereas in place of those words he reports "راصاً عقبيه". The rule is that an addition by a reliable narrator is accepted, but not where it conflicts with what others narrate; and beyond that, Yaḥyā ibn Ayyūb himself has been criticised.

قال ابن الحنبلي في قفو الأثر: وعلى قياس ما سبق: لا تقبل زيادة الضعيف إذا خالفت رواية الثقة. (ص ٦۰).
وقال في قواعد في علوم الحديث: وقد قدمنا أن من اختلف في توثيقه وتضعيفه حسن الحديث أيضاً، فتقبل زيادته لكونه من رواة الحسن،

قلت: هذا مقيد بما إذا لم تكن الزيادة منافية كما هو الظاهر. (قواعد في علوم الحديث، ص ۱۲۲).
وللمزيد من البحث انظر: (شرح شرح النخبة للملا على القاري مع التعليقات، ص: ۳۱٥. ۳۲۰، والتحرير لابن الهمام: 2/۳۷۸، وقف والأثر لابن الحنبلي، مع التعليقات ص: ٦۰. ٦۲، وقواعد في علوم الحديث، ص: ۱۲۳).

Even supposing the report were established, "راصاً" means keeping things level while leaving a little space between them, just as "تراصوا في الصفوف" in the hadith means straightening the rows. It does not mean pressing ankle against ankle in the way that those who reject the schools of law do.

Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ states:

ويقال: تراص القوم: تصافوا وتلاصقوا في القتال أو الصلاة. (ص: ۳٤۸).

Objection: some people say that you have argued from the contents of a treatise by Bakr ibn ʿAbdullāh Abū Zayd, who is a Salafī.

الجواب: In reply we say that he is to all appearances a Ḥanbalī, and that he wrote his treatise on precisely those questions in which some Salafīs go against the four schools of law. On this very question, for instance, he writes:

"تَحَصَّل من هذا أنه لا ذكر لجمع العقبين حال السجود في شيء من المذاهب الأربعة". (لا جديد في أحكام الصلاة، ص ٦۹)؛

The treatise also takes up a number of other questions, namely:

(1) A refutation of joining one's ankles to those of one's neighbour in prayer.

(2) A refutation of the practice of some Salafīs of placing the hands below the throat while standing.

(3) A refutation of spreading the hands out too widely in prostration.

(4) A refutation of the practice of some Salafīs of pointing with the index finger while sitting between the two prostrations.

(5) A refutation of the belief of some people that counting the glorifications with the right hand alone is a sunnah.

(6) One further matter, connected with the hadith of ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Zubayr (may Allah be pleased with him).

Moreover, we have not relied on his research alone; we have investigated Yaḥyā ibn Ayyūb, the narrator of this hadith, further ourselves.

And even supposing he is a Salafī, it is a cause for gratitude that on this question Bakr ibn ʿAbdullāh has agreed with Fatāwā Imdād al-Aḥkām, which was written under the supervision of our elders Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him) and Mawlānā Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him). Most of the fatwas in Imdād al-Aḥkām owe their existence to the pen of Shaykh al-Islām Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him).
