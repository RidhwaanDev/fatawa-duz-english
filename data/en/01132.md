### TITLE
A person in iʿtikāf going out for the Friday bath

### QUESTION
A man is in iʿtikāf. May he go out on Friday for the sunnah bath or not?

### ANSWER
On this question the senior scholars (may Allah have mercy on them) fall into two groups. In the view of some it is not correct to go out — for example Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him), Muftī Maḥmūd Ḥasan Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him), Muftī Farīd Ṣāḥib and others. The proofs on which these scholars rely are found in Shāmī, Badāʾiʿ and other works.

Consider what Badāʾiʿ states:

وما روي عن النبي ﷺ من الرخصة في عيادة المريض وصلاة الجنازة

ويجوز أن تحمل الرخصة على ما إذا كان المعتكف خرج لوجه مباح كحاجة الإنسان أو للجمعة، ثم عاد مريضاً أو صلي جنازة من غير أن كان خروجه لذلك قصداً وذلك جائز. (بدائع الصنائع: ۲/۱۱٤، سعيد. وكذا في شامي: ۲/٤٤۵، سعيد. وإمداد الأحكام: ۲/۱٤۲. وفتاوى محموديه: ۱۰/۲٤۳، مبوب ومرتب. وفتاوى فريديه: ٤/۱۹۷).

In the view of certain other scholars it is permissible to go out for the sunnah bath — for example Shaykh ʿAbd al-Ḥaqq, the hadith scholar of Delhi (may Allah have mercy on him), Muftī Rashīd Aḥmad Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him), Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh and others.

Consider what Ashiʿʿat al-Lamaʿāt states:

وكان لا يدخل البيت إلا لحاجة الإنسان

As for the bath for Friday, I have not found any explicit narration about it in the principal works, except that the commentary Imdād says: he may go out for a bath, whether it be obligatory or optional. (Ashiʿʿat al-Lamaʿāt: 2/120, Mujaddidiyyah).

The meaning of this passage is that no explicit narration concerning the Friday bath is to be found in the books of fiqh, except that Sharḥ Imdād states that the person in iʿtikāf may go out for a bath, whether it is obligatory or optional.

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

That it is permissible to leave the mosque for ablution and for a bath, whether obligatory or optional, is established by the following proofs:

(۱) نقل في المتانة عن فتاوى الحجة: ويجوز لمعتكف أن يخرج من المسجد في سبعة أشياء: البول، والغائط، والوضوء، والاغتسال، فرضاً كان أو نفلاً، والجمعة، يخرج أيضاً لحاجة السلطان، ويخرج أيضاً لا مر لا بد، ثم يرجع بعد ما فرغ من ذلك الأمر سريعاً. (المتانة في مرمة الخزانة: ۳۷۸).

(۲) نقل الرواية المذكورة عن فتاوى الحجة: المخدوم محمد قاسم التتوي رحمه الله تعالى في بياضه المعروف بالبياض الهاشمي.

(۳) ونقلها العلامة العثماني رحمه الله عن الإكليل عن الخزانة عن فتاوى الحجة. (أحكام القرآن: ۱/۱۹۰).

(٤) Makhdūm al-Tattawī (may Allah have mercy on him) said in Ḥayāt al-Ṣāʾimīn: Tenth: among the needs recognised by the Sacred Law are ablution and bathing, so it is permissible for a person in iʿtikāf to go out of the mosque for that, whether it be obligatory or optional — thus in al-Muḍmarāt, al-Fatāwā al-Ḥujjah, al-Tātārkhāniyyah, Kanz al-ʿUbbād and Matānat al-Riwāyāt.

(الفتاوى المحمد يه: ۲/۸۰، للمفتي محمد الهالائي السندي).

(۵) وفي مضمرات الأنوار: يجوز للمعتكف الخروج للبول والغائط والوضوء والاغتسال فرضاً كان أو نفلاً. (the reference mentioned above).

(٦) The passage of Ashiʿʿat al-Lamaʿāt already quoted.

This ruling is transmitted without any hesitation in thirteen books: Fatāwā al-Ḥujjah, al-Matānah, al-Khazānah, al-Bayāḍ al-Hāshimī, al-Iklīl, Aḥkām al-Qurʾān, Ḥayāt al-Ṣāʾimīn, al-Muḍmarāt, al-Fatāwā al-Tātārkhāniyyah, Kanz al-ʿUbbād, Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah, Maẓhar al-Anwār and Ashiʿʿat al-Lamaʿāt. Some of these books are, admittedly, not well known, and Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy (may Allah have mercy on him) graded Khazānat al-Riwāyāt and Kanz al-ʿUbbād as weak in al-Nāfiʿ al-Kabīr; but the remaining books are well known and reliable. Beyond that, the fact that so many scholars and muftis — among them so eminent a jurist as Makhdūm Thaṭṭawī (may Allah have mercy on him) — all transmit this without any denial and without mentioning any disagreement is itself an independent proof.

Moreover, commenting on the words of al-Durr al-Mukhtār

"وحرم عليه الخروج إلا لحاجة الإنسان"

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says: "ولا يمكث بعد فراغه من الطهور". (شامي ۲/٤٤۵، سعيد)

Here "purification" primarily suggests an optional ablution, and presumably even those who hold the opposing view do not deny that going out for this is permissible. Some have drawn a distinction between an ablution made for an optional prayer and an ablution that is itself optional, holding that going out is permissible for the first but not for the second. Quite apart from being unreasonable, this distinction also runs counter to the particular ruling of Shāmī just cited. There is therefore no ground for counting an optional ablution among the essential needs while refusing to count an optional bath among them. (Summarised from Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/503).

In sum, a person in iʿtikāf may go out on Friday for the sunnah bath.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
