### TITLE
The ruling on names that declare a person pure

### QUESTION
A man's name is Ṭāhir. Someone objected to this name, saying that Ḥaḍrat Zaynab (may Allah be pleased with her) was called Barrah, which means "righteous and God-fearing," and someone said that this amounts to praising oneself:

فقيل لها تزكي نفسها، فسماها رسول الله ﷺ زينب

— the Messenger of Allah ﷺ changed Barrah and gave the name Zaynab. Apparently this same cause is found in names such as Ṭāhir, Ṣāliḥ, Sājid, ʿĀbid and the like. So will it be permissible to give these names or not? Explain and be rewarded.

### ANSWER
It is permissible and correct to give names such as Ṭāhir, Ṣāliḥ, Sājid, ʿĀbid and the like, because names of this sort are given by way of a good omen, that Allah Most High may make the name a reality; self-purification, that is, praising oneself, is not what is intended. As for the reports from which a prohibition apparently appears, see the following few explanations of them:

Names that have an aspect of praise and commendation, where a good omen is intended, may be given — such as Rabāḥ, Nāfiʿ, ʿAlāʾ and so on. The Prophet ﷺ had intended to forbid such names, but then he gave up that intention.

Names from which people's minds turn towards an evil omen should not be given: for example, if someone's name were "Good Fortune" or "Blessing" or "Felicity," and in answer to someone it were said, "There is no good fortune," or "There is no blessing," the mind would go towards an evil omen.

As for names that indicate purification, purity and praise, he first intended to forbid giving them but then left that, or this ruling was abrogated.

This dislike is to be understood as referring to the time when the prohibition of taking evil omens had not yet come. When taking evil omens was forbidden, the aspect of evil omen in these names came to an end, and under the principle

إنما الأعمال بالنيات

a good omen came into them in place of self-praise; therefore giving such names became permissible.

If someone raises the difficulty that there are three incidents of the name Barrah being changed: (1) Ḥaḍrat Juwayriyah bint al-Ḥārith (may Allah be pleased with her), the wife of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ; (2) Zaynab bint Jaḥsh (may Allah be pleased with her), the wife of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ; (3) Zaynab bint Umm Salamah (may Allah be pleased with her), the stepdaughter of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. (Sharḥ al-Ubbī: 7/300). All three of these were named Barrah, and the Prophet ﷺ changed it. Yet in some places he kept a name on account of a good omen and did not change it. What is the reason for this?

The answer that appears is that Barrah means al-tawassuʿ fi'l-khayr, that is, to perform all works of good on a very wide and lofty scale, and this is an emphatic form. The Noble Qurʾān states:

﴿ليس البر أن تولوا وجوهكم قبل المشرق والمغرب ولكن البر من آمن بالله واليوم الآخر والملائكة والكتاب والنبيين وآتي المال على حبه ذوي القربي واليتامى والمساكين وابن السبيل والسائلين وفي الرقاب وأقام الصلاة وآتي الزكاة والموفون بعهدهم إذا عاهدوا والصابرين في البأساء والضراء وحين البأس أولئك الذين صدقوا وأولئك هم المتقون﴾. (البقرة: ۱۷۷)

In this verse the word

"البِرّ"

covers beliefs, bodily and financial acts of worship, then all the kinds of financial worship — near relatives and distant, resident and traveller, the needy and the not needy, the one who asks and the one who does not — then collective good, that is, fulfilling covenants, gratitude and patience, and then all the kinds of patience. Even if it were taken as a good omen, it would still be difficult to live up to; that is why he changed it.

Yes, if Barrah is taken in the general sense of "God-fearing," as ordinary people use it, then there is no harm in this name. Allah Most High has taught us this supplication:

وتوفنا مع الأبرار.

Here abrār means the God-fearing; that is why some of the pious elders were named Abrār al-Ḥaqq. Among the female Companions there is Barrah bint Abī Tijrāh (Al-Iṣābah: 8/48), and Barīrah too can mean one who possesses piety and God-consciousness.

The evidence for this issue has already been given above, and may be looked up there.
