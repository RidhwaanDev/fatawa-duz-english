### TITLE
The ruling on differing with one's shaykh in subsidiary matters

### QUESTION
Zayd is a scholar: does he have the right to differ with his shaykh in subsidiary matters or not? Further, if an apparent conflict is seen between the practice of the shaykh and a ruling of the Sharīʿah, which should be given preference? A certain scholar said that the rulings are one thing and the temperament of the Sharīʿah is another; is such a thing possible? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, a scholar, in his capacity as a scholar, has room to differ with his shaykh in subsidiary matters and in juristic investigations; no effect will be produced upon the inward benefit he receives on account of this. And the subsidiary rulings of the Sharīʿah and the temperament of the Sharīʿah are one and the same thing. Yes, it is possible that at times a subsidiary ruling of the Sharīʿah has two aspects — the concession (rukhṣah) and the more resolute course (ʿazīmah) — and if the shaykh acts upon the more resolute course, then by the principle
"الأجر بقدر التعب"
he will receive a double reward on that account.

Ḥaḍrat Ḥājī Imdād Allāh Muhājir Makkī (may Allah have mercy on him) used to take part in the mawlid of the Prophet ﷺ in Makkah al-Mukarramah. When someone mentioned this to Ḥaḍrat Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him), he replied that we pledged allegiance to Ḥājī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) for the reformation of the self; we did not pledge allegiance in juristic rulings. Derived from (Tuḥfat al-ʿUlamāʾ, pp. 302–303).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Taqī ʿUthmānī Ṣāḥib has written in the monthly al-Balāgh concerning Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him):

After the responsibility of issuing fatwas came upon him, the character of his correspondence with Ḥaḍrat Ḥakīm al-Ummat (may Allah have mercy on him) also changed considerably: in these letters a large portion was now taken up with intricate investigations. Sometimes the master and the disciple would differ in their investigation of some question. Page after page would be written by letter, each setting out his own proofs and answering the other's objections. Sometimes the disciple would bow his head in submission before his master's finding; sometimes the master would prefer the disciple's finding and retract his own opinion. And if at times, despite reflection upon each other's proofs and mutual consultation, the difference of opinion remained, then the difference would be disclosed to the questioner, so that he might act according to whichever verdict he felt more at ease with. (Monthly al-Balāgh, p. 157).

In another place he writes:

In the journey of knowledge and investigation there come such stages as require a student to differ with some other scholar, and in some places to differ even with his own elders. In this connection the practice of my honoured father was that neither did respect and reverence for anyone prevent him from expressing a difference of opinion with him, nor did a difference of opinion ever cause the slightest breach in respect and reverence. In certain matters he differed even with very great scholars; indeed, he held a difference of opinion in a number of juristic questions even with his own shaykh and mentor, Ḥakīm al-Ummat Thānawī (may his secret be sanctified). (Al-Balāgh, p. 445).

It is mentioned in Aḥsan al-Fatāwā:

The Sharīʿah is the sum of the outward and inward rulings, while ṭarīqah is the name given to the inward rulings alone. Ṭarīqah is therefore not something separate from the Sharīʿah; rather it is a branch of the Sharīʿah itself. Through the complete following of all the outward and inward rulings of the Sharīʿah, certain creational and legislative realities are disclosed: this is a fact. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 1/549).

It is in Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband (his distinction in the khānqāh of Thānah Bhawan):

Although all of the successors of Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) are shining suns of knowledge and practice, of integrity and God-consciousness, and of reform and guidance, yet it would not be out of place to say that the one who adopted the scholarly temperament of his spiritual master most fully was Ḥaḍrat Muftī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him). This is precisely why, in the matter of fatwas, Ḥaḍrat had the greatest confidence in the scholarly investigations of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him). Repeatedly he would assign a scholarly investigation to several of his successors, each to present his own written finding; Ḥaḍrat Muftī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) would be among them, and most often it was his writing that the spiritual master liked best, and he would pray for him. How much confidence Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) had in his fatwas may be gauged from this: that several times Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) sought a fatwa from the aforementioned Ḥaḍrat Muftī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) in his own personal affairs and acted upon it. Once, in some personal matter of his, Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) put a question to Ḥaḍrat Muftī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him); when the Muftī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) sent his reply, Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote to him in a letter: "Your fatwa reached me — may Allah Most High lengthen your life. On reading it I felt two joys: one, that knowledge was gained; and the other, that all praise be to Allah Most High, there are people to carry on the work after me." (Imdād al-Muftīn, vol. 2, p. 65, Introduction, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

Between Ḥaḍrat Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) and Imām Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) too there was disagreement in a great many juristic questions, the details of which are well known in the books of fiqh.

Between Ḥaḍrat Imām al-Shāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) and Imām al-Muzanī too there was disagreement in certain juristic questions.

Consider what is in al-Majmūʿ Sharḥ al-Muhadhdhab:

قد ذكرنا أن الصحيح من مذهبنا أن الإفراد أفضل، وبه قال عمر بن الخطاب رضي الله عنه وعثمان رضي الله عنه وعلي رضي الله عنه وابن مسعود رضي الله عنه وابن عمر رضي الله عنه وجابر رضي الله عنه وعائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها ومالك رحمه الله والأوزاعي رحمه الله وأبو ثور رحمه الله، وقال أبو حنيفة رحمه الله وسفيان الثوري رحمه الله وإسحاق بن راهويه رحمه الله والمزني رحمه الله وابن المنذر رحمه الله وأبو إسحاق المروزي: القران أفضل، وقال أحمد: التمتع أفضل، وحكي أبو يوسف رحمه الله أن التمتع والقران أفضل من الإفراد. (المجموع شرح المهذب: ۷/۱۵۲، دار الفكر).

Shaykh Muḥammad ʿAwwāmah says in Adab al-Ikhtilāf:

حكم الاختلاف في الفروع: لا يخفي على العاقل جواز الاختلاف في فروع الأحكام الشرعية عقلاً وشرعاً وأدل دليل على جوازه عقلاً: وقوعه، وأدل دليل على جوازه شرعاً: وقوعه من صدر الأئمة وأفضلها بعد أنبياء الله تعالى ورسله (عليهم السلام) وهم الصحابة رضوان الله عليهم، وفيهم أبو بكر رضي الله عنه وعمر رضي الله عنه وبقية الخلفاء الراشدين، وبقية العشرة المبشرين وفقهاء الصحابة وعلماء هم بكتاب الله تعالى

وتابعيهم إلى يومنا هذا. (أدب الاختلاف للشيخ محمد عوامة، ۲۵، ط: دار البشائر الإسلامية).
