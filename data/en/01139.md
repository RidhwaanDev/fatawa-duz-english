### TITLE
When the family objects to a man going for an obligatory Hajj

### QUESTION
A man wants to go for Hajj, but his wife and children are afraid to stay in the house and tell him not to go. What should he do? And does it make any difference whether the Hajj is obligatory or voluntary?

### ANSWER
A person on whom Hajj has become obligatory must perform it that same year; delaying it is not correct. His wife and children's refusal is not a valid ground, and he should not take it into account, so long as the conditions for Hajj are met, there is nothing preventing him, and the man himself does not think there is any real danger to his wife and children. If, however, the Hajj is voluntary, then the wider benefit will be considered: he should first make arrangements to set his family's minds at rest and then perform it. He should weigh what is best and decide himself.

See Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ:

وروينا عن النبي ﷺ أنه قال: من ملك زاداً وراحلة تبلغه إلى بيت الله الحرام، فلم يحج فلا عليه أن يموت يهودياً أو نصرانياً، ألحق الوعيد عمن أخر الحج عن أول أوقات الإمكان. (بدائع الصنائع: ۲/۱۱۹، سعيد).

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

وكذا إن كرهت خروجه زوجته وأولاده أو من سواهم ممن تلزمه نفقته وهو لا يخاف الضيعة عليهم فلا بأس بأن يخرج. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۲۲۱. وكذا في الفتاوى التاتارخانية: ۲/۵۷۷).

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

Once Hajj has become obligatory, one must go and perform it that very year, so long as there is no valid excuse recognised by the sharia. Whoever delays it a year or two without reason is sinful and a transgressor, and if he then fails to perform it at all, he deserves the severe warning and punishment mentioned for that. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 5/215).
