### TITLE
Reciting the Chihil Kāf as a litany

### QUESTION
What is the Chihil Kāf? Who composed it? What does it mean? Do its meanings accord with the Sharīʿah or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In works on spiritual practices the Chihil Kāf is generally attributed to Ḥaḍrat Shaykh ʿAbd al-Qādir Jīlānī (may Allah have mercy on him), but despite extensive searching no chain of transmission for it could be found anywhere. Some ascribe it to Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him), yet this report too stands only at the level of what has been handed down from one heart to another.

The wording of the Chihil Kāf is as follows:

Some scholars have transmitted other wordings of the Chihil Kāf, which are close to these words.

كفَاك رَبُك كم يكفِيك وَاكفَة
ظ
كفكافها ككمِينٍ كانَ مِن لُكك
تَكرُّ كرّاً ككرِّ الكرِّ في كبِد
ظ
تَحكي مُشَكشِكة كلُكلُك لُكك
كفَاك مَابِي كفَاك الكاف كربَتَه
ظ
يا كوكباً كانَ يحكي كوكبَ الفَلَك

Consider the following sense-conveying translation: Your Lord is enough for you; He will amply suffice you against every calamity that descends upon you — calamities that lie waiting to strike just as an army lies in ambush to fall upon its enemies. These calamities come again and again, and they are hard to ward off, like a twisted rope whose strands are so entangled with one another that they are difficult to untie; and in their severity, their hardship and the harm they inflict they resemble an armed host and the toughest flesh of a she-camel. O my Lord, You are sufficient against all calamities and hardships. O star (the heart) that resembles a star of the sky in its glitter and radiance!

A brief explanation of the difficult words is given below:

٭
واكفة؛ وكف، يكف، وكفاً، سال وقطر،
This means the dripping of water and the like, little by little. (المعجم الوسیط، ۱۰۵٤، و القاموس الوحید: ۲/۱۸۹۳)۔
By
واكفة
are meant the calamities that descend — that is, those which keep coming continuously like drops of rain.

٭
كفكافها: كفكف
It is derived from this, and it means "to turn away"; it is also used transitively.
كففته عنه؛ دفعته وصرفته كفكفته خلف، هو لا زم ومتعد. (القاموس المحيط: ۱/۱۰۹۸).

٭
الكمين: كمن
It is derived from this, and it means to lie in ambush.
كمن في المكان، كموناً: تواري، الكمين: القوم يكمنون في الحرب حيلة. (المعجم الوسيط، ۷۹۹).

٭
لكك: لكاك
It is the plural of this, and it means: الزحام, a crowd, a throng. (القاموس المحیط: ۱/۱۲۲۹، و المعجم الوسیط، ۸۳۷)۔ And in one version the word is
كلك
.

٭
تكر: كر
It is derived from this, and it means to return. (لسان العرب: ۵/۱۳۵)۔

٭
الكر: خلاف الفر
, a rope of palm leaves used for climbing a date palm, the rope of a ship's sail; plural:
كرور. (
القاموس الوحید: ۲/۱۳۹۷)۔

٭
الكبد: الشدة والمشقة. (تاج العروس: ۹/۹۲).

٭
مشكشكة: الشكشكة
It is derived from this, and it means sharp-edged weaponry. It is a descriptive term, and the noun it qualifies —
جماعة ذات السلاح
— is omitted.
الشكشكة: السلاح الحاد. (المحيط: ۱/۱۲۲۰، والمعجم الوسيط، ٤۹۰).

٭
اللكلك: كهدهد، القصير والضخم من الإبل. (المحيط: ۱/۱۲۲۹).
Here
لكك
refers to the tough flesh of a she-camel.

At the end the heart is likened to a star: that is, just as a star depends upon Allah Most High for its light, so too the heart depends upon Allah Most High for deliverance from its afflictions.

Another reason may also be this: just as a star stands on high, so too the heart is lofty in resolve, in that it does not lose courage in the face of calamities and hardships but places its trust in Allah Most High.

In sum, the words and meanings of the Chihil Kāf do not conflict with the Sharīʿah; rather they are in accord with it. Its chain of transmission, however, has not been found. For further detail, the treatise Chihil Kāf of Ḥaḍrat Shāh Rafīʿ al-Dīn Ṣāḥib Dehlawī (may Allah have mercy on him) may be consulted.

Note: There are various versions concerning the Chihil Kāf; we have adopted only one of them.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
