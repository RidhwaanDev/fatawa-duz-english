### TITLE
The ruling on the expiation due for a death in a road accident

### QUESTION
A man was driving a vehicle when someone happened to come in front of him, and that person died in the accident. The scholars say that an expiation is due in such a case, and they give the order of the expiation as follows: (1) freeing a slave; if one has not the means for that, then fasting for two consecutive months; and if one has not the means for that either, then feeding sixty poor people.

Now the question is this: a man is resident in South Africa, and people say that slaves exist in certain African countries and that he could go there and free a slave. In such a case, will this man be permitted to perform the expiation by fasting or by feeding, or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question this man will perform the expiation by fasting; it is not necessary for him to free a slave. The jurists have given many examples of the kind in which, when a person cannot readily obtain a slave nearby, or a slave exists but for some reason is not available to him, it is correct for him to perform the expiation by fasting. For example: if a restriction has been placed upon a free man on account of his foolish spending and his property is not in his own possession, then that person should perform the expiation by fasting. It is in al-Hidāyah, al-Shāmī and elsewhere that if a person is a traveller who has no property with him and an expiation becomes binding upon him, then he should perform the expiation by fasting. (al-Shāmī: 3/478, Saʿīd).

Likewise, if a man is wealthy but many people have taken money from him as a loan, or someone has usurped it from him, then too he should perform the expiation by fasting. (al-Kifāyah: 8/199).

Similarly, if a person has money but it is due to go towards the discharge of a debt and he cannot buy a slave with what remains, then it is not binding upon that person to buy a slave and free him; rather he should perform the expiation by fasting. (al-Durr al-Mukhtār and al-Shāmī).

قال في الهداية: وهذ ا (أي جواز إنفاق المحجور ماله في حقوق الله) بخلاف ما إذا حلف أو نذر أو ظاهر حيث لا يلزمه المال، بل يكفر يمينه وظهاره بالصوم لأنه مما يجب بفعله. (الهداية: ۳/۳۵٦، ۳۵۷).

وفي الكفاية: قوله بل يكفر يمينه وظهاره بالصوم، لأن يده مقصورة عن ماله فهو بمنزلة ابن السبيل المنقطع عن ماله، وبمنزلة من يكون له دين على إنسان، أو غصب غاصب من يده وهو ما كان يعطيه فله أن يكفر بالصوم. (الكفاية على هامش فتح القدير: ۸/۱۹۹، مكتبه رشيديه، كوئته).

وفي الدر المختار: والعبد

وكذا الحر المحجور عليه بالسفه على المعتمد لا يجزئه إلا الصوم. (الدر المختار: ۳/٤۷۸، سعيد).

The upshot is that if a person is not able, either in reality or in law, to free a slave, then it is correct for him to perform the expiation by fasting.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
