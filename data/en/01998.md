### TITLE
The ruling on selling a trade licence and a pension

### QUESTION
Is it permissible in the Sharīʿah to sell a trade licence, a pension and the like, or not?

### ANSWER
(1) For business convenience and ease, a trader or a company has a licence approved by the government, and by virtue of this licence it is protected from official obstruction in importing and exporting goods. But a licence is not a material object; it is the name of a right to sell goods and move them to another city or market, and to purchase goods from another country or city and bring them in. It is a right in the obtaining of which considerable effort and money too must be expended. For the following reasons, therefore, the sale and purchase of a licence is permissible.

(1) Its sale must be sound in law. If the law does not permit its sale and purchase, then it is not permissible. For example, if it stands in the name of a particular individual or a particular company and the law does not permit its transfer to another company, then the sale of that licence will not be permissible.

(2) The practice of selling it is found in the market, and in the custom of merchants a "trade licence" is dealt with as property; it therefore has the ruling of property.

(3) It is capable of being benefited from, and deriving benefit from it is not forbidden in the Sharīʿah.

(4) This right belongs to its holders in its own right and is not for the repelling of harm, and it is also in existence at present.

(5) It is capable of storage, that is, fit to be retained, because a trade licence is legally safeguarded by means of a written certificate or registration.

For further detail see: (Fiqhī Maqālāt: 1/222; Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/188; Ḥuqūq awr un kī Kharīd o Farokht, p. 193; Īḍāḥ al-Nawādir: 1/43; and Naʾe Masāʾil awr ʿUlamā-e-Hind ke Fayṣle, p. 105).

The ruling on selling a pension:

(2) The sale of a pension may take two forms. (1) He sells it to the government: this is in reality not a sale at all but the converting of a deferred gratuity into an immediate one, and in terms of the Sharīʿah it is permissible and sound with the government's consent. (2) Selling it to anyone other than the government is not permissible: first, because the government itself does not consent to it; and second, because a number of defects are involved in it — for instance, the sale of one country's currency for the currency of that same country on a deferred basis is not permissible, and so on.

Consider what Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

A pension is a kind of gratuity; so long as the employee has not taken possession of it he does not become its owner, and its sale is therefore not permissible. Selling it to the government itself, however, is not in reality a sale; only the name and the outward form are those of a sale. Its reality is that the large gratuity which the government had promised to pay in instalments it is now paying in cash as a lump sum of a smaller amount; this transaction with the government is therefore permissible. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 6/521).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

Concerning the government buying it back, he said: This is a sale in outward form; otherwise, in reality it is an independent gratuity on the government's part, and it is therefore permissible with the government's consent. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 4/580, Miscellaneous Questions).

See further: (Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥall: 6/53, "It is permissible for an employee to sell his pension to the government"; Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil: 4/168; and Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah: 6/39).
