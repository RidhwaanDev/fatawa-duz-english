### TITLE
A second view concerning the beginning of a journey

### QUESTION
In the second volume of Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā this question is mentioned, that a journey begins from the last house of the town. But one of the muftīs of the present age has written that a journey begins from one's own house, especially in large cities. بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
Some of the muftīs of the present age have held that the travel distance begins from one's own house. Thus Mawlānā Marghūb Aḥmad Ṣāḥib has quoted a fatwa of Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi, the summary of which is set out below:

As for the starting point of the rulings of travel, the passages of the jurists are entirely explicit — and some narrations also support them — that these rulings will begin only after leaving all the houses of the town. But will the beginning and the end of the distance also be measured from the last point of the town's limits, or will they be connected with some other point? We consulted the sources on this subject, but we did not find in the words of the jurists any explicit passage or narration from which it would be expressly known that, just as the rulings of travel begin after leaving the town, so too the starting point of the travel distance begins from the last of the town's buildings. However, in the course of consultation and search, certain things were found which indicate that the determination of the travel distance itself rests upon custom, and that therefore its beginning and its end will also be connected with custom. Hence, just as the custom of people is the deciding factor in determining the travel distance, so too custom alone will be the deciding factor in determining its beginning and end: when a person who is setting out on a journey leaves his house with the intention of travel, then whatever place is understood in the custom and usage of people to be the beginning of his journey — that very place will be the starting point of the journey. This is because the beginning of the travel distance and the end of the travel distance are a part of the distance itself, not of the rulings of travel; and it is not established by any legal proof that the travel distance and the rulings of travel must, in the Sacred Law, necessarily come into being together. Since, generally, when one who intends a journey sets out from his house with the intention of covering the recognised distance, it is understood in custom that the man has begun his journey — and the traveller himself also understands that his journey has begun, which is why he recites the supplications of travel and the like at that very time — it is on this basis that some other senior scholars have given the verdict that the travel distance begins from one's dwelling and home.

Those people who live at the outermost limit of a city or settlement, and whose dwelling is the very last building of the settlement: when they set out from their house with the intention of covering the travel distance outside the city or settlement, then for them too the beginning and the end of the journey will be their house itself, and in general custom too that is understood to be the beginning and the end of their journey. It is thus learnt that in the matter of this determination it is not necessary for the custom to be one and the same for every kind of traveller; rather, whatever the custom is with regard to a particular traveller, the ruling will be given in accordance with it. (علمی مکاتیب، ص ۱۹۱ ۔ ۱۹٤، جمع و ترتیب از مولانا مرغوب احمد صاحب لاجپوری) ۔
