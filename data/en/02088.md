### TITLE
Borrowing a ṣāʿ of wheat

### QUESTION
If someone sells one ṣāʿ of wheat in exchange for one ṣāʿ of wheat to be handed over a month later, this is impermissible. But if he lends one ṣāʿ of wheat on the understanding that it will be returned after a month, this is permissible and is the standing practice — even though, under the rule "الاعتبار للمعاني", the upshot and substance of both is one and the same. The jurists say that a guarantee (kafālah) made on condition that the debt is discharged is a transfer of debt (ḥawālah), and a transfer of debt made on condition that the debtor pays is a guarantee, because it is the meanings that are taken into account. When it is the meanings that are taken into account, why is a loan permissible?

### ANSWER
What the rule requires is that this loan too should be impermissible. But since the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to take loans, and passed away in a state in which he had taken a loan from a Jew, it is permissible by way of juristic preference on the basis of the hadith — just as salam is an exception to the sale of what does not exist. Moreover there is need in a loan. Giving and taking a loan is therefore permissible.

See what Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī states:

عن عائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها قالت: توفي رسول الله ﷺ ودرعه مرهونة عند يهودي بثلاثين صاعاً من شعير. (رواه البخاري: ۲/٦٤۱، فيصل).

Another narration states:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: "من أخذ أموال الناس يريد أداء ها أدي الله عنه (أي يسر عليه أداء دينه) ومن أخذ يريد إتلافها أتلفه الله. (رواه البخاري: ۱/۳۲۱، باب من أخذ أموال الناس).

Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) says: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: Whoever takes a loan from someone with the intention of repaying it, Allah Most High makes the repayment of that loan easy for him; and whoever takes a loan with the intention of withholding it, Allah Most High destroys him — that is, He does not make matters easy for him in the future but rather afflicts him with further hardship.

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

القرض يسلك به مسلك العارية

والدليل على أنه يسلك به مسلك العارية أن لا يخلو إما أن يسلك به مسلك المبادلة وهي تمليك الشيء بمثله أو يسلك به مسلك العارية لا سبيل إلى الأول لأنه تمليك العين بمثله نسيئة وهذا لا يجوز، فتعين أن يكون عارية فجعل التقدير كأن المستقرض انتفع بالعين مدة ثم رد عين ما قبض وإن كان يرد بدله في الحقيقة وجعل رد بدل العين بمنزلة رد العين. (بدائع الصنائع:، فصل في شرائط القرض، كتاب القرض: ۷/۳۹٦، سعيد).

ʿĀlamgīrī states:

وعن أبي يوسف رحمه الله في رواية يجوز استقراضه (الدقيق) وزناً استحساناً إذا تعارف الناس ذلك وعليه الفتوى كذا في الغياثية. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۳/۲۰۱).

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

In those foodstuffs in which people customarily allow a little latitude, the purpose of giving and taking a loan is not to earn a profit but merely to meet a temporary need. A slight shortfall or excess in them does not fall under interest, and so interest will not be established in these things. (جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام: ۱/۱۸۷) ۔

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

Because it is an exchange of a kind for the same kind (mubādalat al-jins bi'l-jins), there was room for the misunderstanding that it is interest; but in reality there is no interest in this case, for it is a loan. Interest occurs when there is an exchange of a kind for a different kind, or an exchange of a kind for the same kind in which the word "sale" or "exchange" or "compensation" has been used. If the transaction is one of giving a kind and taking the same kind back, but the words "sale", "exchange" or "compensation" have not been used, then it is a loan, whether or not the word "loan" is uttered, and it is beyond doubt permissible. (احسن الفتاوی: ۷/۱۷۳) ۔

ʿIṭr-e Hidāyah states:

This form of loan too was interest, since it falls under selling on credit for a thing of the same single kind. But since its primary object, by the very way it is instituted, is kindness and favour — an exchange does take place, yet the exchange is not the primary object, the primary object being favour and kindness — and since the Lawgiver has therefore declared it permissible, indeed recommended, and there is the consensus of the community upon its permissibility. The marginal note on al-Hidāyah states:

قوله وهو ربا وهذا يقتضي فساد القرض لكن ندب الشارع إليه وأجمع الأمة على جوازه. (حاشية الهداية: ۳/۷٦، رقم الحاشية ۱۹).

(عطر ہدایہ، ص ٤۱٦) ۔
