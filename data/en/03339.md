### TITLE
The ruling on taking out insurance on mosques

### QUESTION
We have built a mosque in a Western country, and non-Muslims there commonly demolish mosques. May we take out insurance on mosques? Explain and be rewarded.

### ANSWER
In the view of scholars generally, taking out insurance is not permissible, because it involves interest and gambling. Some scholars, however, declare it permissible in the two cases set out below; even so, the preferable and more virtuous course is to keep away from insurance, and in the case of mosques, endowments and the like caution is certainly better. Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah has granted permission for it at a time of need; see: (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 5/174, Deoband). The two permissible cases according to some scholars are as follows:

1. That the insured person recovers from the insurance company only as much as he had paid in instalments. If the insurance company pays more, the excess should be given away in charity without the intention of reward, for this excess is impermissible by agreement.

2. That the insurance company pays no money at all, but rather takes on the service, that is, the responsibility for repairs. This form is permissible, because in this case there is no مبادلۃ المال بالمال; rather, a service is given in exchange for wealth, as in the case of medical aid. This form falls under hire.

An objection may, however, be raised against this case: from one side the payment of money is binding, while from the other side it is uncertain and not definite, so that there is uncertainty in the transaction. The answer is that the uncertainty here is slight and does not lead to dispute, and for this reason there is scope for it. Awjaz al-Masālik states:

قال الباجي: ما كثر فيه الغرر وغلب عليه حتى صار البيع يوصف بالغرر فهذا الذي لا خلاف في المنع منه. وأما يسير الغرر فإنه لا يؤثر في فساد عقد بيع فإنه لا يكاد يخلو عقد منه. (أوجز المسالك: ۱۳/۸۸).

زاد المعاد states:

ليس كل غرر سبب للتحريم، والغرر إذا كان يسيراً أو لا يمكن الاحتراز منه لم يكن مانعاً من صحة العقد. (زاد المعاد: ۵/۸۲۰).

In ordinary circumstances insurance is forbidden and impermissible. Consider what Imdād al-Fatāwā records:

Insurance, in terms of its outward form, is gambling,

لأنه تعليق الملك على الخطر والمال في الجانبين

and in terms of its reality it is interest,

لعدم اشتراط المساواة في الجانبين فيما يجب فيه المساواة

and gambling and interest are both unlawful. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 3/161)

For further detail see: (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā, volume five, p. 442, and 5/427, Zamzam)

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
