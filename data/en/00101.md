### TITLE
The correct interpretation of ﴿اصبروا وصابروا ورابطوا﴾

### QUESTION
Some people say that when we sit in a circle we are forming a rābiṭah (a bond), and they use this verse as evidence:

﴿اصبروا وصابروا ورابطوا﴾ الخ.

It is the last verse of Sūrah Āl ʿImrān.

They say that rābiṭah means forming a connection with Allah, but by way of the friends of Allah. I, however, have read that it means guarding the frontiers of the Muslim lands, or waiting for one prayer after another.

They say that it is not within everyone's power, and that not everyone has the rank, to form a connection with Allah Almighty directly, without any intermediary. To form a connection with Allah one must reach a particular rank, and that rank is very difficult to attain, so a direct connection with Allah is not possible. For this reason they say it is necessary to take the friends of Allah as a means.

### ANSWER
(1) ﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا اصبروا وصابروا ورابطوا﴾ الخ.

قال أبو سلمة بن عبد الرحمن هذه الآية في انتظار الصلاة بعد الصلاة ولم يكن في زمان رسول الله غزو يرابط فيه رواه الحاكم في صحيحه واحتج أبو سلمة بقوله عليه السلام: ألا أدلكم على ما يمحوا الله به الخطايا ويرفع به الدرجات انتظار الصلاة بعد الصلاة فذلكم الرباط رواه مالك وقال ابن عطية: والقول الصحيح هو أن الرباط هو الملازمة في سبيل الله أصلها من ربط الخيل، قال رسول الله ﷺ: رباط يوم وليلة خير عند الله من الدنيا وما فيها. (تفسير القرطبي: ٤/۲۰٦).

(2) وقال محمد بن جرير الطبري رحمه الله: قال بعضهم معنى ذلك اصبروا على دينكم وصابروا الكفار ورابطوا الكفار أي رابطوا في سبيل الله وقال الآخرون معنى ذلك رابطوا على الصلوات أي انتظروها واحدة بعد واحدة. (جامع القرآن للطبري: ٤/ ۱٤۸).

ʿAllāmah al-Suyūṭī (may Allah have mercy on him) and Sulaymān ibn ʿUmar, known as al-Jamal, have given this same meaning, and Muftī Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) has written the same: that ribāṭ means either waiting for one prayer after another, or it is used for guarding the frontiers of the Muslim lands in the path of Allah. (معارف القرآن: ۲/۲۷٤).

So the meaning that some people give — that ribāṭ means forming a connection with Allah by way of the friends of Allah — is not correct, because this meaning is not reported from the pious predecessors, nor can it be drawn from the verse.

As for their saying that it is not possible for ordinary people to form a connection with Allah Almighty without the friends of Allah as intermediaries: that much is true, but this connection can be formed through all of them — the prophets (peace be upon them), the scholars, the righteous, the friends of Allah, the mujtahids, and others.

These people have taken the Arabic word rābiṭah to mean what the Urdu word rābitah means, that is, contact or attachment. What Urdu speakers call taʿalluq (attachment) is called in Arabic ṣadāqah, maḥabbah, rifāqah and the like. A friend or companion is called rafīq, ṣadīq, zamīl, muṣāḥib, mujāwir and so on; he is not called murābiṭ. Rabṭ means to tie, not to befriend. In Urdu one says that Zayd's connection with Madrasah Zakariyyā is one of formal regulation, while ʿUmar's connection with Madrasah Zakariyyā is one of contact rather than formal regulation. When translating a word, one must keep the idiom of the language in view.
