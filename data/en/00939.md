### TITLE
Zakat on nine-carat gold

### QUESTION
If a woman has jewellery made of 9-carat gold, is zakat due on it or not? For the gold is the lesser part in it and the other metal predominates.

### ANSWER
In the light of the primary texts and the standard books of fiqh, when the other metal predominates zakat is not binding; even so, it is better to pay zakat as a precaution. But if the other metals can easily be separated out by melting, then zakat is binding on the gold. The simplest way is to take the jewellery to a goldsmith and give away a fortieth of whatever value he states.

Consider what al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

فإن غلب الغش فليس كالفضة كالستوقة فينظر إن كانت رائجة أو نوي التجارة اعتبرت قيمتها فإن بلغت نصاباً من أدني الدراهم التي تجب فيها الزكاة وهي التي غلبت فضتها وجبت فيها الزكاة وإلا فلا، وإن لم تكن أثماناً رائجة ولا منوية للتجارة فلا زكاة فيها، إلا أن يكون ما فيها من الفضة يبلغ مائتي درهم بأن كانت كثيرة ويتخلص من الغش، لأن الصفر لا تجب الزكاة فيها إلا بنية التجارة، والفضة لا يشترط فيها نية التجارة، فإن كان ما فيها لا يتخلص فلا شيء عليه، لأن الفضة فيه قد هلكت، كذا في كثير من الكتب، وفي غاية البيان: الظاهر أن خلوص الفضّة من الدراهم ليس بشرط بل المعتبر أن تكون في الدراهم فضة بقدر النصاب. (البحر الرائق: ۲/۲۲۸، باب زكاة المال، كوئته).

Bihishtī Zēwar states:

If gold or silver is not pure but has some alloy in it — say, tin mixed into silver — then see which is the greater, the silver or the tin. If the silver is greater, it carries the same ruling as silver: that is, if it comes to the amount stated above, zakat is necessary. If the tin is greater, it is not counted as silver, and the ruling that will be given later for goods such as brass, copper, iron and tin applies to it instead. (بہشتی زیور، زکوٰۃ کا بیان، تیسرا حصہ: ۲۳۹، مسئلہ نمبر ۷) ۔

ʿIṭr-e Hidāyah records that gold and silver can be separated out easily. Consider the passage:

It is established, well known and widely recognised from the observation and experience of goldsmiths, silver-workers and refiners — indeed even of people who are not craftsmen at all — that when new rupees are melted down and the customary methods and the assayer's techniques are applied to them, their silver separates out completely from the base alloy mixed with it. Many books of fiqh state explicitly, in the course of a number of rulings, that from coins of this sort — (دراہم غالۃ الغش) — the silver can be separated out, and that this silver is taken into account for the rulings of zakat, of ribā (interest) and of currency exchange. Of course, as Shaykh Ibn al-Humām states expressly, and as the principles require, where the silver in such heavily alloyed dirhams is like a mere plating and is so slight that it does not separate out but is lost in the mixture, it is certainly not taken into account. In the books of the Shāfiʿīs it is stated that even the silver of a heavy plating is taken into account, because it can be separated out; and since the principles of the Hanafis support this, some Hanafi scholars too have distinguished between light and heavy plating and have said that if the silver or gold plating is thick enough to be separated out, then it too must be taken into account. Thus Radd al-Muḥtār (p. 329) states:

ونقل الخير الرملي نحوه من المحيط ثم قال: وأقول: يجب تقييد المسألة بما إذا لم تكثر الفضة أو الذهب المموه أما إذا كثر بحيث يحصل منه شيء يدخل في الميزان بالعرض على النار يجب حينئذٍ اعتباره ولم أره لا صحابنا لكن رأيته للشافعية وقواعدنا شاهدة به فتأمل.

In any case, whether or not one distinguishes between light and heavy plating and between adulterated dirhams of the two kinds, it is expressly laid down that in most heavily alloyed dirhams — those which, like the new rupees, contain even more silver than a heavy plating — the pure silver can be separated out and the silver in them is taken into account, and there is no narration to the contrary. It would be the settled position in any case, since what the matter turns on is the possibility of separating the silver out. (Even if one cannot make use of that silver at present, in the future one can melt it down whenever one wishes and make use of it — and this, as Fatḥ al-Qadīr (1/523) states expressly, is the

وإن لم يخلص فلا شيء عليه لأن الفضة هلكت فيه إذا لم ينتفع بها لا حالاً ولا مآلاً فبقي العبرة للغش أصل مناط"

that is, the essential ground for treating it as one of the two currencies.) And that such separation is possible is established by observation and experience, and is well known and widely recognised. (عطر ہدایہ، جدید ص: ۳۵٤ ۔ ۳۵٦) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
