### TITLE
Saying "I have left you" three times

### QUESTION
If a man said these words to his wife — "I have left you, left you, left you" — what is the ruling?

### ANSWER
In the case described the expression "I have left you" is allusive, and an allusive expression requires an intention. So if he intended divorce, one irrevocable divorce has taken effect; and if he did not intend divorce, no divorce has taken effect.

Some scholars have counted this among the explicit expressions, in which case a revocable divorce takes effect without any intention.

Muftī Maḥmūd Ḥasan (may Allah have mercy on him) counted it among the explicit expressions and gave a ruling of revocable divorce. See: (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۲/۳٤۰) ۔

Muftī Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him), Mawlānā Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him), Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) and Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him) have likewise written that it is a revocable divorce.

Muftī Kifāyat Allāh (may Allah have mercy on him), however, counted it among the allusive expressions and declared it an irrevocable divorce. Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

(Answer) If the man uttered these words — "I have left you" — three times, an irrevocable divorce has taken effect upon his wife and she has passed out of his marriage. He may, however, marry this woman again, provided that she too consents; ḥalālah is not required. (کفایت المفتی: ٦/٤٤، کتاب الطلاق) ۔

Muftī ʿAzīz al-Raḥmān (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

The answer: ("I have left her, left her, left her.") If the husband's intention was divorce and he spoke these words intending divorce, then one irrevocable divorce has taken effect upon his wife; the second and the third have not taken effect, لأن البائن لایلحق البائن کما فی الدر المختار وغیرہ ۔ (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: ۹/۳۸۸) ۔

Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah states:

To say of one's wife, "I have left her," is an allusive expression of divorce; where an intention is present, an irrevocable divorce takes effect by it. ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him), however, counted it, on the basis of customary usage, among the expressions of revocable divorce, by which a revocable divorce takes effect even without an intention. (فتاوی حقانیہ: ٤/٤۷۹) ۔
