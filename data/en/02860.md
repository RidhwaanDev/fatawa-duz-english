### TITLE
The ruling on reciting a poem in the style of a song

### QUESTION
May one recite a poem, a devotional poem in praise of the Prophet ﷺ, or an anthem in the style of a ghazal or a song, or not — given that it involves resembling the unbelievers and the dissolute? بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
Resembling the unbelievers and the dissolute in matters that are their religious peculiarities and distinguishing marks is not permissible — for example, hanging a cross around the neck, wearing the Hindus' sacred thread (zunnār), or applying the sectarian mark to the forehead. Impermissible too are those matters in which resemblance is found and concerning whose reprehensibility hadiths have come, such as letting the trousers hang below the ankles, or men wearing the dress of women.

There are, however, certain matters in which there is resemblance on the one hand and some other sound purpose on the other, and for that reason they will not be called impermissible. If a person keeps a single fast on ʿĀshūrāʾ, that is, the tenth of Muḥarram, that is not impermissible, because the faster's aim is not to resemble the Jews but to follow Ḥaḍrat Mūsā (peace be upon him) and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. To hold gatherings of preaching and admonition in Muḥarram and Rabīʿ al-Awwal resembles the practice of the people of innovation; but since the aim is to refute superstitions and to expound sound teachings, the scholars have declared it permissible, provided such gatherings are free of superstitions and self-imposed restrictions.

The jurists have likewise written that the study of logic and philosophy is unlawful, as is stated in al-Durr al-Mukhtār; yet students study these subjects for this sound purpose, that they may acquire enough familiarity with the terminology to understand the books and also to refute erroneous ideas.

In the hadiths there is mention of reward for giving water to a thirsty dog, because what was intended there was not the keeping of a dog but the removal of the suffering of a living creature. So if a poem is recited to the metre of some song with the intention that, because of that popular metre, people will turn away from superstition and listen to sound content without musical instruments, a verdict of impermissibility and unlawfulness should not be passed upon it.

The following hadith is narrated in the books of hadith: when the unbelievers in one campaign recited the line:

إن لنا العزي ولا عزي لكم،

that is, "We have al-ʿUzzā and you have no ʿUzzā," the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said, in exactly that same manner:

الله مولانا ولا مولي لكم،

because here the aim was to refute them. Likewise, in answer to the unbelievers' words "اعل هبل اعل هبل" it was said: "الله أعلى وأجل". My aim is by no means that songs and musical instruments should be listened to — listening to them is altogether impermissible. But if someone happens to know a tune because it fell upon his ears by chance, or because the metre is widely known, then there should be no harm in reciting to that metre and in that manner. Indeed, sometimes the metre of a song becomes so well known and commonplace that it is no longer regarded as the metre of any particular song, and on account of its being commonplace there will likewise be latitude.

Our elders found latitude for eating at a table and chair and for riding a bicycle on the ground that these had become commonplace, and they took them out of the category of imitation.

On the subject of reciting these poems to musical metres, consider a fatwa of the Faqīh al-Nafs, the Pole of the Age, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Rashīd Aḥmad Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him):

Question: Are samāʿ, ghinā' and rāg one and the same thing, or are they different? And is it permissible to listen to all three without musical instruments or not?

Answer: All three words bear a single meaning. Listening to rāg without musical instruments is permissible, provided that the singer is not an occasion of temptation and the content of the rāg is not contrary to the Sacred Law; and there is no harm in its conforming to a musical measure. (Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah, p. 607, Maktabah Raḥmāniyyah.)

Concerning resemblance, al-Durr al-Mukhtār together with al-Shāmī records:

فإن التشبه بهم لا يكره في كل شيء بل في المذموم وفيما يقصد به التشبه كما في البحر وفي الشامي: عن شرح الجامع الصغير لقاضيخان ما في الذخيرة قبيل كتاب التحري قال هشام: رأيت على أبي يوسف رحمه الله نعلين مخصوفين بمسامير فقلت: أتري بهذا الحديد بأساً قال: لا، قلت: سفيان وثور بن يزيد كرها ذلك لان فيه تشبهاً بالرهبان فقال كان رسول الله ﷺ يلبس النعال التي لها شعر وإنها من لباس الرهبان فقد أشار إلى أن صورة المشابهة فيما تعلق به صلاح العباد لا يضر. (الدر المختار مع فتاوى الشامي: ۱/٦۲٤، سعيد).

That is, resemblance is not forbidden in everything; rather, there should be no resemblance in blameworthy things, and it is also forbidden wherever the resemblance is deliberately intended. Hishām relates that Imām Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) was wearing shoes to which a second layer of leather had been fixed with iron nails. I said, "Sufyān and Thawr hold this to be disliked, because it involves resembling the monks." He replied, "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ wore shoes with hair on them, and that was the footwear of the monks." From this it is clear that an outward resemblance in which there is some particular benefit is not forbidden. Where, however, a resemblance has been explicitly forbidden in the hadiths, it is folly to search out some benefit in it, for the Messenger of Allah ﷺ declared such benefits null; those benefits are therefore either non-existent or imaginary, or else their harm outweighs their benefit. As the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

ليس منا من تشبه بالرجال من النساء ولا من تشبه بالنساء من الرجال. (أخرجه أحمد في مسنده، رقم: ٦۸۷۵).

That is, a man who makes himself like women, or a woman who makes herself like men, is not one of us. Another hadith states:

لعن الله الرجل يلبس لبسة المرأة والمرأة لبسة الرجل

Furthermore, for the resemblance to a song to be removed, the absence of music is itself sufficient — just as, in the fast of ʿĀshūrāʾ, the resemblance was removed by adding one fast; in the same way, the resemblance is removed by taking one element away.

In sum, in this humble one's view there is no harm in reciting a poem to particular tunes. If anyone considers it incorrect, let him not listen; but let him not impose his own opinion upon others.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
