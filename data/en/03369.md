### TITLE
The wisdom in performing acts of worship in the Arabic language

### QUESTION
Why are the acts of worship that Muslims perform — prayer, recitation of the Qur'an, remembrance of Allah, supplications and so on — in the Arabic language? Why do they not perform them in their own national and local languages? Explain and be rewarded.

### ANSWER
The Arabic language possesses certain features that are not to be had in other languages. If an attempt were made to perform the acts of worship in some other local language, a very great difference would arise, on account of which there would be a strong danger of distortion and alteration in the words and the meanings. For example, if قلب is transferred into English it becomes کلب, and قلب means "heart" while کلب means "dog."

Furthermore, Arabic words are oceans of meaning: a single word carries several meanings, and this would no longer be preserved through translation into another language. Jawāhir al-Fiqh states:
As far as has been established by research, many of the letters that exist in the Arabic language and in the Qur'an do not exist at all in any of these non-Arab languages. For example, in English, Hindi, Tamil and Gujarati no separate form has been assigned to ذ، ز، ظ، ض; rather, they are all rendered by one and the same symbol, even though the meanings change a great deal with the difference between these letters. (جواہر الفقہ: ۲/۸۹، رسالہ: تحذیر الانام عن تغییر رسم الخط من مصحف الامام) ۔

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:
(1) In Arabic there is a difference between ح and ھ; in Gujarati there is not. (2) In Arabic there is a difference between ق and ک; in Gujarati there is not. (3) In Arabic the hamzah and ع are two distinct letters; in Gujarati they are not. (4) In Arabic ت and ط are separate; in Gujarati they are not. (5) In Arabic there is a difference between س, ص and ث; in Gujarati there is not. What this means is that, just as in Arabic there is a marked difference in the written form and in the pronunciation of ھ and ح, ق and ک, ع and ء, ت and ط, س and ص, and ث, ذ, ض, ز and ظ, this difference and distinction does not exist in Gujarati. Even if symbols were devised for them, they would still be deficient; and along with the corruption of the writing and the script there would appear a marked difference in pronunciation, producing scores of mistakes. And since faulty pronunciation changes the letters, the meaning too would change, so that a person would deserve punishment in place of reward and a curse in place of mercy — as the well-known saying has it:
رب تالٍ للقرآن، والقرآن يلعنه
That is, there are many who recite the Qur'an upon whom the Qur'an calls down curses. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ۲/۱۵۹، ط: الاحسان، دیوبند) ۔
