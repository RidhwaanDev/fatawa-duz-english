### TITLE
The ruling on taking an interest-based loan from a bank in order to give the customer a concession on a purchase

### QUESTION
In order to induce customers to buy, some traders offer them the facility of buying through a bank, so that the concession of paying the price in three instalments may be obtained. The form of this is that the bank pays the price to the seller at once in a lump sum on behalf of the buyer, and the buyer pays that same price to the bank in three instalments; then the bank recovers from the seller a certain percentage of interest in proportion to the price of the sold item.

Now there are the following few forms of it:

For a Muslim customer to buy from the shop of a Muslim trader.

For a Muslim customer to buy from the shop of a non-Muslim.

For someone needy and of middling means to enter into such an arrangement on the basis of need.

For a Muslim trader to make such an offer — for if he does not do so, it is quite possible that the customer will go to another shop to make his purchase.

Can this additional percentage of interest be counted as a service charge? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question the above-mentioned arrangement is impermissible because it comprises interest.

1. For a Muslim customer to enter into such an interest-based arrangement with a Muslim shopkeeper is impermissible.

2. The disbelievers have not bound themselves to the rulings of Islam, and for this reason it is permissible to enter into such an arrangement with a disbelieving trader; nevertheless abstaining is better. Yes, it is not permissible to take interest from the disbelievers.

3. So long as there is no form of severe necessity or compulsion, until then it is not permissible to become involved in interest-based dealings, because a mere need does not make an unlawful transaction lawful.

4. It is necessary for a Muslim trader to adopt permissible means for the promotion of his trade and to keep away from impermissible means; and it is not permissible in the Sharīʿah to offer a customer an interest-based arrangement.

5. It is difficult to call the interest of the bank a service charge, because an amount taken in proportion to the price will be called nothing but interest. Kindly consider the following proofs:

قال في الدر المختار: وفي الخلاصة: القرض بالشرط حرام

وفي الأشباه: كل قرض جر نفعاً حرام، وفي رد المحتار: قوله كل قرض جر نفعاً حرام، أي إذا كان مشروطاً كما علم مما نقله عن البحر وعن الخلاصة. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٥/١٦٦، سعيد).

Al-ʿInāyah Sharḥ al-Hidāyah states:

الربا في اللغة هو الزيادة

وفي الاصطلاح هو الفضل الخالي عن العوض المشروط في البيع. (العناية: ٣/٧). (وكذا في الدر المختار: ٥/١٦٨، سعيد).

Muʿjam Lughat al-Fuqahāʾ mentions:

الربا كل زيادة مشروطة في العقد خالية عن عوض مشروع. (معجم لغة الفقهاء، ص ٢١٨). (وكذا في التعريفات الفقهية، ص ١٠٢).

Muntakhabāt-e Niẓām al-Fatāwā records:

Whatever passages the jurists have transmitted concerning the meaning of ribā in the Sharīʿah, the upshot of them all is that a transaction of an exchange contract took place in the goods subject to ribā, and that in that very contract there was an increase without counter-value in favour of one of the parties — that is, in which the gain is stipulated as a condition in the contract of the transaction. (منتخباتِ نظام الفتاوی: ١/١٩٥) ۔

The ruling of invalidity will not be applied to the transactions of the disbelievers among themselves. Consider: it is in Imdād al-Aḥkām:

In the view of the Greatest Imām (may Allah have mercy on him), the ruling of invalidity will not be applied to the transactions of the protected non-Muslims either when they are contrary to the Sharīʿah, provided that those transactions are in accordance with their own religion — because the protected non-Muslims have not bound themselves to the rulings of Islam in transactions where these are contrary to their own beliefs. Therefore, when they carry out such transactions in accordance with their own religion, they will be regarded as valid and the ruling of invalidity will not be given. The upshot of the answer is that although the disbelievers are addressed by the subsidiary rulings in matters of punishments and transactions, yet for a ruling of prohibition and invalidity a general address is not sufficient; rather binding oneself is also a condition. (امداد الاحکام: ٤/٣٩٠) ۔

Muntakhabāt-e Niẓām al-Fatāwā records:

Non-Muslims also carry on interest-based business and interest-based dealings among themselves; they also carry out many void and defective sales; they also make and sell wine and take its price; they also sell pork and take its price; and then they give this money to Muslims as wages, as prizes and so forth. Despite it being known that all this money is unlawful, taking this money is regarded in the Sharīʿah as permissible and not disliked, so long as it is not certain that this thing which he is selling is the very same thing which he brought by stealing it or by robbery, or that the price which he is giving is the very same money which, for example, he brought by stealing it or by robbery — that is, if it is the very item of theft, robbery and so forth, then it is not permissible. In short, taking all of this and using it remains correct. (منتخباتِ نظام الفتاوی، ص ٤٦٨، ط: اصلاحی کتب خانہ) ۔

Yes, if the customer too has to sign this whole agreement, then it is not permissible to take the concession by signing an interest-based arrangement of this kind; it is necessary to keep away from it.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
