### TITLE
The ruling on zakat when tax money is refunded

### QUESTION
A trader paid one million in tax to the tax department. A case was then brought against the department, as a result of which the department refunded two hundred thousand. Is he now obliged to pay the zakat of the past years on it or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, the tax department was liable for the excess amount it had collected, so this became like a debt that is denied; and in the case of a denied debt, the zakat of past years is not due even though evidence for it exists. The jurists have included such a debt under māl ḍimār (wealth a person owns but cannot get at). To all appearances the trader had proof, which is why he took the department to court. The zakat of the past years is therefore not due on this two hundred thousand.

The definition of māl ḍimār and its ruling, in the light of the hadiths and the statements of the jurists, has been given above and may be consulted there. See further what Imdād al-Aḥkām states: he is liable for whatever tax has been levied in excess of the rule. (Imdād al-Aḥkām 2/624)

Khayr al-Fatāwā states: a denied debt (dayn majḥūd) is by agreement counted as māl ḍimār. Many of the scholars have added a further condition for it to become ḍimār, namely that there be no testimony establishing the denied debt; Imam Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him), however, ruled it māl ḍimār even where testimony, that is to say evidence, exists, and a group of the scholars have declared this to be the correct position:

ففي الدر والشامية: وعن محمد لا زكاة فيه وهو الصحيح ذكره ابن ملك وغيره لأن البينة قد لا تقبل صححه في التحفة كما في غاية البيان وصححه في الخانية أيضاً وعزاه إلى السرخسي، بحر، وفي باب المصرف من النهر عن عقد الفرائد: ينبغي أن يعول عليه، قلت: ونقل الباقاني تصحيح الوجوب عن الكافي وهو المعتمد وإليه مال فخر الإسلام.

The authentications of Qāḍī Khān carry very great weight. ʿUqūd Rasm al-Muftī states:

قد قال العلامة قاسم: إن قاضي خان من أحق من يعتمد على تصحيحه. (ص۳۳). (Khayr al-Fatāwā 3/597)
