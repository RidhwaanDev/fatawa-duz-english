### TITLE
Ruling on saying, "If you phone me again, take it that ṭalāq has taken place"

### QUESTION
A man gave his wife two ṭalāqs and then took her back. Recently the two of them were talking on the telephone when a quarrel arose, and in anger the man said to his wife on the phone, "If you phone me again, take it that the third ṭalāq has taken place." Before this sentence the husband had also said, "I will divorce you." The woman says that when she heard the first sentence, "I will divorce you," she put the phone down, so she did not hear anything the husband said after that. Fifteen minutes after this conversation the wife telephoned her husband in order to apologise. Now, since the wife had not heard the condition, has the third ṭalāq taken effect through the fulfilment of the condition or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question no ṭalāq has taken effect by these words, "If you phone me again, take it that the third ṭalāq has taken place."

Al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

داده انگارأوكرده انگار لا يقع وان نوى ولو قال لها بعد ما طلبت الطلاق. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۳۸۰).

Fatāwā Qāḍīkhān states:

امرأة قالت لزوجها مرا طلاق ده قال الزوج داده انگارأوكرده انگار لا يقع الطلاق وإن نوي. (فتاوى قاضيخان: ۲/۲۱۰).

It also states:

كأنه قال لها بالعربية: احسبي أنك طالق وان قال ذلك لا يقع وان نوى. (قاضيخان: ۲/۲۱۰).

Among the Urdu fatwa collections, Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānwī ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written on this question in (امداد الاحکام: ۲/٤۲۳); it may be consulted there.

There remains the question of the husband addressing his wife when she had already left the telephone. On this, the view of this humble one is that the ṭalāq does take effect. It may be understood in this way: if a man writes a letter of ṭalāq to his wife and has the letter delivered to her house, the ṭalāq takes effect even though the wife is not at home.

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ثم المرسومة لا تخلو إما أن أرسل الطلاق بأن كتب أما بعد فأنت طالق فكما كتب هذا يقع الطلاق. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ۳/۲۹٦، سعيد).

This question is also found in the first volume of Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, ʿAzīz al-Fatāwā, although a word seems to have been left out of it there. The heading reads: "It is not a condition for a ṭalāq to take effect that the wife be present." The wording of the question then runs: "Zayd gave his wife, when she was present before him …" — it seems that the word "not" has been omitted here, because the answer contains the words: "It is not necessary for the wife to be present for a ṭalāq to take effect; in short, the woman's being present at the time of the ṭalāq is not a condition." (عزیز الفتاوی: جلد اول: ٤۸۲، دار الاشاعت) ۔
