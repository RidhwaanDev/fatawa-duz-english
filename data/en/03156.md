### TITLE
The ruling on acquiring worldly knowledge

### QUESTION
Is it permissible for a Muslim to acquire worldly knowledge and to attend school, or not?

### ANSWER
Muslims have needs in every field of life, and for those needs experts have to be found. If Muslims take no part in any worldly field, they will be left dependent on non-Muslims, and a great deal of difficulty will result. On this basis it is permissible and correct to acquire worldly knowledge and to advance in it; indeed, if the intention is to serve Islam and the Muslims, a person will be rewarded for it. For Muslim girls, however, special arrangements for veiling and the like must be made, and it is far better, and further removed from temptation, that they study in a school set apart for girls.

Ḥaḍrat Muftī ʿAbd al-Raḥīm Ṣāḥib writes: To establish schools in which English and other worldly branches of learning are taught to the extent required, and to set up classes in crafts and trades that can help a person to earn a lawful living, is without doubt permissible, a good work, and a cause of reward. But religious education, and support for the religious seminaries, must be given precedence. To turn away from religious education, and to become absorbed in worldly education while leaving the religious seminaries in a fragile state, is not a good thing.

First of all, children should be given correct and regular instruction in the Noble Qur'an. They should be made acquainted, in a suitable manner, with the rulings of the sharia and the essentials of the faith. Such an impression of Islam should be fixed in their hearts that their Islamic feelings and the sentiments of faith become firm and enduring, so that no force can cool them and no power can alter the way they live. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 5/118, Maktabat al-Iḥsān, Deoband).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Farīd Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) writes: For girls to study in a school or college is not forbidden in itself. What is forbidden in itself is such things as the abandonment of veiling, free mixing and unrestrained freedom. Moreover, on the principles of the sharia, anything that becomes a means to other forbidden things is itself counted as forbidden. (Fatāwā Farīdiyyah: 1/204, Kitāb al-ʿIlm).
