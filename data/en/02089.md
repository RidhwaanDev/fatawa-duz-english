### TITLE
Repaying a loan of rands in dollars

### QUESTION
A man lent another man one hundred thousand rands for a term of one year. If he then says to the debtor, "After one year you will give me 15,000 dollars instead of rands," is that permissible or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, if the lender says to the borrower that after one year he should return 15,000 dollars in place of the one hundred thousand rands he was lent, and the one hundred thousand rands are handed over in that same sitting, then this arrangement is permissible and valid. No interest or anything of the sort is involved in it, because currency is not money by its very nature (thaman khilqī) but money by convention (thaman ʿurfī). Moreover, the currencies of different countries count as different kinds, so mutual possession within the sitting is not required; the exchange is permissible on a deferred basis as well. Possession of one of the two currencies is, however, necessary, so that a sale of one debt against another (bayʿ al-kāliʾ bi'l-kāliʾ) does not result.

Consider what Fiqhī Maqālāt states:

It is common practice among traders and ordinary people that a man hands the currency of one country to another person on the condition that, in exchange for it, he will hand over the currency of a particular country at a particular place after a set period. In the view of Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) this transaction is permissible, because in his view it is not a condition in the sale of monies that the price be owned by the contracting party at the time of the contract. So when the two kinds differ, a deferred exchange is permissible. Shams al-Aʾimmah al-Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him) accordingly writes:

وإذا اشتري الرجل فلوساً بدراهم ونقد الثمن ولم تكن الفلوس عند البائع فالبيع جائز لأن الفلوس الرائجة ثمن كالنقود، وقد بينا أن حكم العقد في الثمن وجوبها ووجودها معا ولا يشترط قيامها في ملك بائعها لصحة العقد كما لا يشترط ذلك في الدراهم والدنانير. (المبسوط للإمام السرخسي رحمه الله: ۱٤/۲٤، باب البيع بالفلوس، إدارة القرآن).

(ماخوذ از فقہی مقالات: ۱/٤۲) ۔

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

The currencies of two countries fall under different kinds, which is precisely why their names, units and so on differ. Since the two are of different kinds, it is permissible to sell the currency of one country against the currency of another with a difference in amount, and it is permissible to trade in this as well. It is necessary, though, that in the sitting of the contract one of the two parties take possession of the currency due to him. If neither party takes possession of the currency due to him in the sitting of the contract, but rather the two conclude the transaction and then part on a promise of later payment, this is not permissible, because it results in the parties separating with a debt standing against a debt, which is forbidden in the light of the hadith:

قال العلامة برهان الدين المرغيناني: وإذا عدم الوصفان الجنس والمعني المضموم إليه حل التفاضل والنساء لعدم العلة المحرمة والأصل فيه الإباحة وإذا وجدا حرم التفاضل والنساء لوجود العلة وإذا وجد أحدهما وعدم الآخر حل التفاضل وحرم النساء. (الهداية: ۳/۷۹، باب الربا، دار الفكر).

(جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام: ۱/۱۳۹) ۔

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

For a loan to be permissible the thing lent must be fungible. Things that have no equivalent may not be given as a loan either, because in a loan it is legally binding to return the equivalent of whatever was taken.

قال العلامة الصابوني: ونص الفقهاء على أن قرض المكيل والموزون جائز، كاستقراض الحب والشعير، والتمر والزبيب وكالاستقراض السمن والزيت، وكل ما يكال ويوزن، وأما مالا مثل له فلا يجوز إقراضه كاللآلي، والجوهرات، وهذا مذهب أبي حنيفة وأجاز الشافعية والحنابلة إقراض مالا مثله له إذا كان معروف القيمة، فيجب رد القيمة. (فقه المعاملات)

The upshot is that things sold by measure or by weight may be lent, since their equivalents exist, whereas things that are not fungible — those called ẕawāt al-qiyam, goods each valued in its own right — may not be given as a loan. Dirhams, dinars and other currency may also be given as a loan, because the currency of any country is a fungible item within that country. (جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام: ۱/۱۸۳) ۔
