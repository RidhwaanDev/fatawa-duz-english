### TITLE
Saying "If you go to your parents' house, then the third"

### QUESTION
A man had already given his wife two divorces at separate times, so only one divorce remains within his power. On one occasion he said to her, "If you go to your parents' house, then the third for you", without saying the word "divorce" or anything of the kind alongside it. If the wife goes to her parents' house, will the third divorce take effect or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, if the husband intended divorce by "the third", then the third divorce will take effect once she goes to her parents' house; but if he said the word merely to frighten her, no divorce takes place.

Al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

امرأة قال لها زوجها: أنت طالق واحدة، فقالت له المرأة: هزار، فقال الزوج: هزار، فهذا على وجهين: إما أن ينوى شيئاً أو لم ينو، ففي الوجه الأول هو على ما نوى، وفي الوجه الثاني لا يقع. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۳۸۱).

From this passage it is clear that the husband said the word "hazār" (a thousand) in reply to his wife, yet since he had no intention no divorce takes place.

It is mentioned in another place that a husband said to his wife: حیلہ زنان کن

يكون إقراراً بالطلاق إذا نوي
۔
(الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۳۸۶).

That is, the husband said to his wife, "Use the device women use." Apparently the sense is, "Make arrangements for a ḥalālah", since three divorces had taken place. But as he did not utter the word "divorce", the matter rests on the intention: if the intention is there it is a divorce, otherwise not.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
