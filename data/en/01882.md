### TITLE
Destruction of the goods sold before the buyer takes possession

### QUESTION
A man bought something and it was destroyed on the way, before it reached him. Who bears the loss?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, as long as the buyer has not obtained possession in the customary sense, the goods will be regarded as still belonging to the seller, and if they are destroyed the loss falls upon the seller. If, however, he had obtained possession in the customary sense and the goods were destroyed after that, the buyer will be responsible for them.

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

تفيد الملك بالقبض أي يثبت بالبيع أو بالشراء. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۱۳۰، كتاب الإكراه).

That is, ownership is completed by taking possession; so if the goods are destroyed before possession is taken, they are destroyed while in the seller's ownership.

Sharḥ al-Majallah states:

المبيع إذا هلك في يد البائع قبل أن يقبضه المشتري يكون من مال البائع ولا شيء على المشتري

وإذا هلك المبيع بعد القبض هلك من مال المشتري ولا شيء على البائع. (شرح المجلة لمحمد خالد الأتاسي، ۲/۲۲۳، ۲۲۵).

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states:

The Qur'an and the Sunnah do not lay down any one particular definition of taking possession; rather, the hadiths mention various forms of it. In one narration from Ḥaḍrat ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) the instruction is given to move the goods from the place of purchase to another place. It is narrated from Ḥaḍrat Zayd ibn Thābit (may Allah be pleased with him) that traders should move the goods they have bought into their own saddle packs. In the narration of Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) measuring and weighing is treated as taking possession. This is why the jurists are agreed that in this matter the custom of the people is the standard: whatever degree of handling is regarded as possession of a given thing in people's custom will also be regarded as possession of it in the sharia.

ʿAllāmah Kāsānī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

ولا يشترط القبض بالبراجم، لأن معني القبض هو التمكين والتخلي وارتفاع الموانع عرفاً وعادة حقيقة. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/۱٤۸، سعيد. الفتاوى الهندية: ۳/۱٦. (جديد فقهي مباحث: ۱۵/۱۲).

Fiqhī Maqālāt states:

The ruling of the Islamic sharia is that risk (liability) does not pass merely by the sale being concluded and ownership being transferred; it passes only once the buyer has taken possession. So until the buyer, or his agent or representative, takes possession of the goods sold — whether that possession is actual or customary — liability for them will not pass to the buyer. (فقہی مقالات، ۳/۷۳).

Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil states:

The seller has cleared the way and has said: this wheat of yours is lying in my warehouse, take it away whenever you wish; from today I am not responsible for it, and if this wheat is destroyed or spoiled it is your responsibility. In this case, although the buyer has not taken physical possession, the goods have entered the buyer's liability, so any loss to them is now the buyer's responsibility. The position of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) is that physical possession is not necessary; clearing the way is enough. Clearing the way means that the buyer is given the ability to come and take the goods whenever he wishes; when no obstacle to taking possession remains, we regard the clearing of the way as having taken place. For example, there is a box with several things inside it, and its key is handed over to him: once the key has been handed over, then whether he picks the box up or not, possession has been realised. Imām Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) adopted the position of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) here, and he narrated with a connected chain the incident of Ḥaḍrat Jābir (may Allah be pleased with him): the Messenger of Allah ﷺ bought a camel from Ḥaḍrat Jābir (may Allah be pleased with him), and Ḥaḍrat Jābir (may Allah be pleased with him) then travelled on that very camel as far as Madinah. He did not dismount from it, yet the clearing of the way had been realised. Imām Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) says that this shows that possession took place through the clearing of the way. (اسلام اور معاشی مسائل، ۲/۹۸، ۱۰۰).

For further detail see: عطر ہدایہ، ص۸۸، ۹۱ ۔ و جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام، ۱/٤۰، ٤۲ ۔ و قاموس الفقہ، ٤/٤٦٦، ٤٦۷.

In summary: once the buyer has taken possession of the goods sold, they enter his liability and he is responsible for them; before possession the seller is responsible. What counts as possession rests upon custom, and it will also differ according to the nature of the goods.
