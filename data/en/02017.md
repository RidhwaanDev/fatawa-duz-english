### TITLE
The preferred view on interest in the abode of war

### QUESTION
What is the preferred view among the Hanafis on taking interest in the abode of war? The two masters (may Allah have mercy on them) argue from a hadith,
"لا ربا بين المسلم والحربي في دار الحرب"
— is this hadith authentic, and what is the answer to it?

### ANSWER
The position of Imam Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) and of the great majority of the jurists is stronger in terms of evidence and is also better suited to the age. Since in our times Muslims live at ease in most countries and perform their worship and other duties without difficulty, they are not permitted to take interest. Moreover, according to the other three imams interest is not permitted even in the abode of war; and out of regard for their school, and in view of the evidence, permission to take interest will not be given at all. In the Hanafi school, therefore, this is the position held to be preferable for issuing rulings.

Ibn Qudāmah says:

ويحرم الربا في دار الحرب كتحريمه في دار الإسلام وبه قال مالك رحمه الله والأوزاعي وأبو يوسف رحمه الله والشافعي رحمه الله وإسحاق، وقال أبو حنيفة: لا يجري الربا بين مسلم وحربي في دار الحرب. (المغني: ٤/۱٦۲، فصل حرمة الربا في دار الحرب).

Consider the evidence of those who hold it impermissible:

(1) The Qur'an and the hadiths declare interest forbidden without any qualification, and severe warnings are found as well:

(1) قال الله تعالى: ﴿أحل الله البيع وحرم الربا﴾. (سورة البقرة، الآية: ۲۷۵).
(2) قال الله تعالى: ﴿الذين يأكلون الربا لا يقومون إلا كما يقوم الذي يتخبطه الشيطان من المس﴾. (سورة البقرة، الآية: ۲۷۵).
(3) وقال تعالى: ﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا اتقوا الله وذروا ما بقي من الربا إن كنتم مؤمنين، فإن لم تفعلوا فأذنوا بحرب من الله ورسوله﴾. (سورة البقرة: ۲۷۸، ۲۷۹).

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim narrates:

عن جابر رضي الله عنه قال: لعن رسول الله ﷺ: آكل الربا وموكله، الخ. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۲۸، باب الربا، قديمي).
عن عبد الله بن مسعود رضي الله عنه عن النبي ﷺ قال: الربا ثلاثة وسبعون باباً أيسرها مثل أن ينكح أمه، وإن أربي الربا عرض الرجل المسلم. (المستدرك للحاكم: ۲/٤۸، كتاب البيوع، دار ابن حزم).
وأخرج ابن أبي حاتم في تفسيره (۲/۵۵۱) والشافعي عن عمر وبن الأحوص أن رسول الله ﷺ، قال: "ألا إن كل رباً كان في الجاهلية موضوع عنكم كله.

Just as the texts forbidding wine-drinking and fornication are unqualified and apply equally in the abode of Islam and the abode of war without distinction, so too the ruling forbidding interest should be general and apply equally.

(2) When a person from the abode of war enters the abode of Islam under safe conduct, his property is held inviolable on account of that covenant and it is not permissible to take interest from him. In the same way, when a Muslim enters the abode of war under safe conduct, on account of that covenant the property of its people becomes inviolable and protected as far as he is concerned.

(3) According to a well-known incident, Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) won camels through a wager. When he brought these camels to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), he ordered him to give them away in charity:

فجاء به إلى رسول الله ﷺ، قال: تصدق به". (تفسير ابن كثير، سورة الروم، بحواله ابن أبي حاتم).

In this incident the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) ordered them to be given away in charity without distinguishing between the abode of Islam and the abode of war, even though Mecca was at that time the abode of war. Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him), however, was in Medina, and the camels had been sent to him in Medina.

(4) The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) staked sheep on the outcome of a wrestling match with Rukānah (may Allah be pleased with him). When he had thrown him three times and the sheep were handed over to him, he gave them back:

فقام عنه ورد عليه غنمه". (الإصابة، تحت ذكر يزيد بن ركانة: ٦/۵۱٤).

This was so even though Rukānah (may Allah be pleased with him) was not a Muslim at that time.

A look at the evidence of those who permit it:

(1) The first proof is a hadith. Makḥūl narrated it in mursal form from the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him):

لا ربا بين المسلم والحربي في دار الحرب". (معرفة السنن والآثار: ۷/٤۷، بيروت).

The answer: this hadith is mursal, and the great majority of the scholars have rejected it. By way of a sample, consider a few of their statements:

قال المحقق ابن همام رحمه الله في فتح القدير: وهذا الحديث غريب، ونقل ما روي مكحول عن النبي ﷺ أنه قال: ذلك. قال الشافعي رحمه الله: قال أبو يوسف رحمه الله: إنما قال أبو حنيفة رحمه الله هذا لأن بعض المشيخة حدثنا عن مكحول عن رسول الله ﷺ أنه قال: "لا ربا بين أهل الحرب" أظنه قال: "وأهل الإسلام" قال الشافعي رحمه الله: وهذا الحديث ليس بثابت ولا حجة فيه، أسنده عنه البيهقي

وهذا لا يفيد لمعارضة إطلاق النصوص إلا بعد ثبوت حجية حديث مكحول، وقد يقال: لو سلم حجيته فالزيادة بخبر الواحد لا تجوز. (فتح القدير: ۷/۳۹، باب الربا، دار الفكر).

In the marginal notes the scholar Saʿdī Chelebī says:

أقول: قال ابن العز: قال في المغني: هذا خبر مجهول لم يرو في صحيح ولا مسند ولا كتاب موثوق به، وهو مع ذلك مرسل. ويحتمل أن المراد بقوله: لا ربا النهي عن الربا كقوله تعالى: فلا رفث ولا فسوق ولا جدال في الحج. انتهى. وعلي تقدير صحته لا يصلح مقيداً للمطلقات مثل لا تأكل الربا، إذ لا يزاد بخبر الواحد على الكتاب. (حاشية فتح القدير: ۷/۳۹، دار الفكر).
وقال العلامة العيني رحمه الله: هذا حديث غريب ليس له أصل. (البناية في شرح الهداية: ۳/۱٦۵).
وفي معرفة السنن والآثار: قال الأوزاعي: الربا عليه حرام في دار الحرب وغيرها

وقال أبو يوسف رحمه الله: القول ما قال الأوزاعي. وإنما أحل أبو حنيفة رحمه الله هذا لأن بعض المشيخة حدثنا عن مكحول عن رسول الله ﷺ أنه قال: لا ربا بين أهل الحرب أظنه قال: "وأهل الإسلام" قال الشافعي رحمه الله: القول كما قال الأوزاعي وأبو يوسف رحمه الله وما احتج به أبو يوسف رحمه الله لا بي حنيفة رحمه الله ليس بثابت ولا حجة فيه. (معرفة السنن والآثار للإمام البيهقي، ۷/٤۷، باب بيع الدرهم بالدرهمين في ارض الحرب، كتاب السير، بيروت).
وكذا في الأم للإمام الشافعي رحمه الله: ٤/۳۷۹، بيع الدرهم بالدرهمين في ارض الحرب، دار الفكر. وفي نصب الراية لأحاديث الهداية: ٤/٤٤).
وقال الإمام النووي: والجواب عن حديث مكحول أنه مرسل ضعيف فلا حجة فيه ولو صح لتأولناه على أن معناه لا يباح الربا في دار الحرب جمعاً بين الأدلة. (المجموع شرح المهذب: ۹/۳۹۲، باب الربا، دار الفكر).
وقال المحقق ابن قدامة الحنبلي رحمه الله: وخبرهم مرسل لا نعرف صحته ويحتمل أنه أراد النهي عن ذلك، ولا يجوز ترك ما ورد بتحريمه القرآن وتظافرت به السنة وانعقد الإجماع على تحريمه بخبر مجهول لم يرد في صحيح ولا مسند ولا كتاب موثوق به وهو مع ذلك مرسل محتمل. (المغني: ٤/۱٦۳، باب الربا والصرف، بيروت).
وفي حاشية الروض المربع شرح زاد المستقنع: وما روي "ولا ربا بين المسلم وأهل الحرب" خبر مجهول، لا يترك له تحريم ما دل عليه الكتاب والسنة. (حاشية الروض لعبد الرحمن الحنبلي، ص ۱۳۹۲).
وفي مطالب أولي النهي: وحديث مكحول

رد بأنه خبر مجهول، لا تترك له تحريم ما دل عليه القرآن والسنة الصحيحة. (مطالب أولي النهي لمصطفي السيوطي الحنبلي: ۳/۱۸۹، دمشق).
وفي الموسوعة الفقهية الكويتية: ذهب جمهور الفقهاء إلى أن الربا حرام في دار الحرب كحرمته في دار الإسلام، وقالوا: إن النصوص في تحريم الربا عامة، ولم يفرق بين دار ودار، ولا بين مسلم وغيره. (الموسوعة: ۲۰/۲۰۸، الربا في دار الحرب).

(2) ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) had accepted Islam at the Battle of Badr or afterwards, but he had not migrated. Then in the year 10 AH, on the occasion of the Farewell Pilgrimage, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) announced:

ربا الجاهلية موضوع وأول ربا أضعه ربانا ربا عباس بن عبد المطلب رضي الله عنه فإنه موضوع كله. (رواه أبو داود: ۱/۲٦۳، كتاب البيوع).

So it seems that up to the time of the Farewell Pilgrimage the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) placed no ban on the dealings in interest of ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) — or else it was because Mecca was the abode of war and taking interest from its people was permitted.

The answer: the scholars have given several answers to this hadith:

(a) It is possible that ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) was given a special dispensation in this matter.

(b) Imam al-Subkī (may Allah have mercy on him) explained it thus: there is no evidence at all that ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) continued his dealings in interest after accepting Islam; and if he did continue them, it may well have been out of ignorance and lack of awareness. Or this hadith may relate to interest still outstanding from before ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) accepted Islam.

(c) It is possible that ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) gave up interest on deferment once it was forbidden, while regarding interest of excess as permissible.

(d) It is also possible that the prohibition of interest had not yet been revealed at that time, since interest was definitively forbidden only in the year 9 AH, after the revelation of the noble verse:

﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا اتقوا الله وذروا ما بقي من الربا إن كنتم مؤمنين﴾

Two incidents support this view:

(1) When the Banū Thaqīf accepted Islam they laid down several conditions, one of which was that they should have the right to collect the sums of interest still owed to them by people. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) accepted this condition.

(2) After the conquest of Mecca the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) appointed ʿAttāb ibn Asīd (may Allah be pleased with him) as governor of Mecca. At that time sums of interest owed to the Banū ʿAmr ibn ʿUmayr ibn ʿAwf were outstanding against the Banū al-Mughīrah, and the Banū al-Mughīrah, who had by then become Muslims, refused to pay them. ʿAttāb (may Allah be pleased with him) wrote to the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) about the dispute, and it was on this occasion that this noble verse was revealed:

﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا اتقوا الله وذروا ما بقي من الربا إن كنتم مؤمنين﴾.

If we do not accept these explanations, then this incident will be of no use to the Hanafi view either. For after the conquest of Mecca in Ramadan of the year 8 AH, Mecca had become the abode of Islam; so it would follow that ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) carried on dealing in interest even after it had become the abode of Islam — which is forbidden by agreement.

The explanations set out above were gathered by Dr Nazīh Ḥammād (Umm al-Qurā University, Mecca) in his work

أحكام التعامل بالربا بين المسلمين وغير المسلمين"

A summary of it was quoted by Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh in Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil, and that is what has been drawn upon here. See: Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/58–62.

(3) The third proof of the two masters (may Allah have mercy on them) is that the property of a person of the abode of war is not inviolable or entitled to respect, and the prohibition applies to taking inviolable property; so taking interest from such a person is permissible.

The answer: it is settled among the jurists that a covenant of safe conduct is one of the grounds that render property inviolable. That is why it is not permissible to take interest from a protected non-Muslim subject residing in the abode of Islam, and it is likewise not permissible to take interest from a person of the abode of war who comes under safe conduct. So, since a Muslim who goes into the abode of war under safe conduct has entered into a covenant with all its inhabitants collectively, their property too will be held inviolable as far as he is concerned.

In short, permitting interest in this way carries a strong likelihood that the sense of the sanctity of the limits set by the sharia, and of how repugnant it is to breach them, will gradually be erased from the hearts of Muslims. This harm is so great that it alone is enough to warrant prohibition. In this question, therefore, within the Hanafi school the position of Imam Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) is the stronger and the better suited to the circumstances of the age, and so the ruling is given according to his position. (Drawn from Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/62).

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) has said that Imam Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) held the office of chief judge, so that in matters of transactions the ruling is given according to his position because of his greater experience.

Sharḥ ʿUqūd Rasm al-Muftī states:

وكل فرع بالقضا تعلقاً
٭
قول أبي يوسف رحمه الله فيه ينتقى
وفي القنية من باب المفتي: الفتوى على قول أبي يوسف رحمه الله فيما يتعلق بالقضاء لزيادة تجربته وكذا في البزازية، من القضاء، أي لحصول زيادة العلم بتجربته ولهذا رجع أبو حنيفة رحمه الله عن القول بأن الصدقة أفضل من حج التطوع لما حج وعرف مشقته. (شرح عقود رسم المفتي، ص ۲۸، ۲۹).
