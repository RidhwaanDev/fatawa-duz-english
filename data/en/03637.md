### TITLE
The ruling on divorce by saying "You are free as far as I am concerned"

### QUESTION
A man had a quarrel with his wife and in a state of anger he said these words to her: "You are free as far as I am concerned; go wherever you want to go." But he does not know whether he said it three times or once. Did divorce take effect by these words or not? If it did, how many? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question the husband's words, "You are free as far as I am concerned; go wherever you want to go," are not explicit with regard to divorce. Therefore, if the husband said these words with the intention of divorce, one irrevocable divorce has taken effect; and if he said them without an intention, no divorce has taken effect. Note what Al-Hidāyah records:

الكنايات لا يقع بها الطلاق إلا بالنية أو بدلالة الحال، لأنها غير موضوعة للطلاق بل تحتمله وغيره فلا بد من التعيين أو دلالته، وهي على ضربين

وبقية الكنايات إذا نوى بها الطلاق كانت واحدة بائنة وإن نوى ثلاثاً كانت بثلاث

إلا أن يكون في حال مذاكرة الطلاق فيقع بها الطلاق في القضاء ولا يقع فيما بينه وبين الله تعالى إلا أن ينويه. (الهداية: ۲/ ۳۷٤، باب إيقاع الطلاق، سعيد).
(وكذا في الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۳/۲۹٦، سعيد، والفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۳۷٦).

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband records:

Saying the word "to set free" is not an explicit divorce; it is an allusive expression. If the husband's intention by this word was divorce, and he is of age, then by this word one irrevocable divorce has taken effect upon his wife. (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm: 9/268).

In another place it records: "Go wherever you please" is among the allusive expressions of divorce. If the husband's intention in these is divorce, an irrevocable divorce takes effect; otherwise not. So the husband should be asked with what intention he said these words. (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband: 9/259, Dār al-Ishāʿat).
