### TITLE
Leading the Eid prayer in another country after having already prayed it

### QUESTION
A mawlānā performed the Eid prayer in the Ḥaramayn and then came to South Africa the following day, where Eid falls a day later. May the mawlānā lead the Eid prayer here? And how is it for him to pray behind someone else?

### ANSWER
In the case in question the mawlānā cannot lead the Eid prayer in South Africa. He may, however, pray it behind another imam; indeed it is better and preferable for him to pray it, so as to keep in step with the Muslims there. Consider what Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:
An imam had already led the Eid prayer once, and then some people came and asked him to lead it for them as well, and he led them too — this second prayer was not valid. (فتاوی محمودیہ ۸/٤۳٦، باب العیدین ۔ و کذا فی فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: ۵/۲۲٤، مسائل نماز عیدین، دار الاشاعت)

Elsewhere it is stated:
Question: A man broke his fast in Makkah, or performed the Eid prayer there, and then came to India, where people are still fasting and have not yet performed the Eid prayer. What should he do now? Should he fast, and should he perform the Eid prayer again or not?
Answer: احتراماً للوقت وموافقة للمسلمين — he should pray that prayer and keep the fast as well, even though his obligation has already been discharged and completed. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۰/۳۷، کتاب الصوم، جامعہ فاروقیہ)

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

أن النبي ﷺ صلى بالناس صلاة الخوف وجعل الناس طائفتين وصلي لكل طائفة شطر الصلاة لينال كل فريق فضيلة الصلاة خلفه ولو جاز اقتداء المفترض خلف المتنفل لا تم الصلاة بالطائفة ثم نوي النفل وصلى بالطائفة الثانية لينال كل فريق فضيلة الصلاة خلفه من غير الحاجة إلى المشي وأفعال كثيرة. (بدائع الصنائع: ۱/۱٤۳، سعيد).

Imdād al-Fattāḥ states:

وأن لا يكون الإمام أدني حالاً من المأموم كان يكون متنفلاً والمقتدي مفترضاً أو معذوراً والمقتدي خالياً منه. (إمداد الفتاح: ص ۳۳۳، شروط صحة الاقتداء، بيروت. وكذا في الدر المختار: ۱/٥٤۲، باب الإمامة، سعيد)

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā, however, does record that leading the prayer is permissible; see احسن الفتاوی: ٤/۱۲٤ ۔ The gist of the parallel case it presents as evidence is this: if the testimony of someone who sighted the moon of Ramadan has been rejected, then by agreement that person must fast and keep Eid along with everyone else even after he has completed thirty fasts. In the same way, the man who arrives in South Africa must keep Eid a second time, and so it is correct for him to lead the prayer. The more cautious course, however, is that he should not lead but should perform the prayer behind an imam.

But this is only incidental. That is, the fasts of Ramadan can sometimes come to thirty-one, but only incidentally: if a man gave testimony of sighting the moon and it was not accepted, he keeps his own fast and then fasts with everyone else from the next day, so his fasts may come to thirty-one — yet this is only incidental. As for the Eid prayer, once he has prayed it he should not lead others in it.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
