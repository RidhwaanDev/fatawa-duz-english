### TITLE
The ruling on clapping and raising the cry of takbīr at gatherings

### QUESTION
1. It is commonly seen that on the occasion of gatherings and the like people clap. Is this permitted in the eye of the sharia or not? 2. If the cry of takbīr is raised in order to show joy and to rouse enthusiasm, what is the ruling on that? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
1. In the case asked about, clapping was the practice of the unbelievers of Makkah and of the idolaters, and it was their mischief. Allah Most High sent down a verse concerning it:

وما كان صلاتهم عند البيت إلا مكاء وتصدية"
[الأنفال: ۳۵]

Their worship beside the Sacred House was only this: whistling and clapping. In the present age too it is the way of the unbelievers to clap in places of amusement and play; Muslims do not perform such actions in their gatherings and the like. Nor has this been the way of the noble Companions, the Successors, the scholars and the righteous. Accordingly it is necessary to keep away from it.

Shaykh ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Saʿdī, in his commentary, counted clapping among the acts of the ignorant and dull-witted:

قال: أي صفيراً وتصفيقاً فعل الجهلة الأغبياء، الذين ليس في قلوبهم تعظيم لربهم ولا معرفة بحقوقه، ولا احترام لا فضل البقاع وأشرفها. (تيسير الكريم الرحمن في تفسير كلام المنان، ص ۲۸۲، ط: مؤسسة الرسالة).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Kifāyat Allāh (may Allah have mercy on him) writes: Clapping falls under amusement and play. The noble sharia has allowed women, when they are praying and some need arises, to strike one hand upon the other and thus show that they are engaged in prayer; but it taught this manner of striking one hand upon the other, that they should strike the palm of one hand upon the back of the other, because striking palm upon palm so as to make a sound is a form of amusement and play and is the act of dancers. Moreover, clapping in assemblies to show delight is a special way of the unbelievers of Europe. Therefore the people of Islam should refrain from clapping, first because it is a form of amusement and play, and second because it is an imitation of the unbelievers of Europe. To say that no prohibition of it has come in the sharia is based on ignorance; the plain saying of the Master of the Worlds ﷺ is there, that "whoever imitates a people will be one of them". (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 9/116, published by Dār al-Ishāʿat).

The jurists have written that clapping is disliked. Thus ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

وكره كل لهو، أي كل لعب وعبث

والإطلاق شامل لنفس الفعل واستماعه كالرقص والسخرية والتصفيق وضرب الأوتاد من الطنبور

فإنها كلها مكروهة لأنها زي الكفار. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۳۹۵، سعيد).

(وكذا في حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح، ص: ۲۱۵، فصل في صفة الأذكار، والفقه الإسلامي وأدلته: ۲/۱۱٦، ط: دار الفكر، السورية، دمشق).

2. Raising the cry of takbīr on an occasion of joy and in order to rouse enthusiasm is permissible and correct; this is the very way of the Muslims. The noble Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) used to raise the cry of "Allāhu akbar" on occasions of joy. A few examples are presented:

When Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) was honoured with Islam, the noble Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) raised the cry of takbīr out of joy.

On the occasion of the conquest of Makkah the Muslims used to raise the cry of takbīr.

On the occasion of the conquest of Khaybar the takbīr was raised.

Concerning the conquest of Constantinople a prediction is narrated, that in the last age it will be conquered by raising the cry of takbīr.

Consider the reports:

أخرج ابن عساكر في تاريخ دمشق (٤٤/۳۱) وأبو نعيم في حلية الأولياء (۱/٤۰، دار الكتاب العربي، بيروت) عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنه قال: سألت عمر رضي الله عنه لا ي شيء سميت الفاروق
