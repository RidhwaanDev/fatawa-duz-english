### TITLE
The ruling on opening an agency for cricket players

### QUESTION
I need guidance on a question. I wish to open an agency for cricketers. In this agency we will train young players free of charge, on the condition that in future, when a player plays for some team for a fee, one tenth of that fee will come to our agency, since we are the ones who train them. What needs to be determined is whether such a contract is permissible or not. بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, the agency's training of players free of charge is a voluntary favour, and a condition has been attached to it of receiving a share of their future earnings. On this basis this contract may be called a conditional voluntary favour, which in the light of the hadiths and of the statements in the books of fatwa is permissible and correct. See the noble hadith:

عن أنس رضي الله عنه أن جاراً لرسول الله ﷺ فارسياً كان طيب المرق فصنع لرسول الله ﷺ ثم جاء يدعوه، فقال: وهذه لعائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها فقال: لا، فقال رسول الله ﷺ: لا، فعاد يدعوه، فقال رسول الله ﷺ: وهذه، قال: لا، قال رسول الله ﷺ: لا، ثم عاد يدعوه، فقال رسول الله ﷺ: وهذه، قال: نعم في الثالثة، فقاما يتدافعان حتى أتيا منزله. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۱۷٦).

The gist of the above hadith is that a man offered the Messenger of Allah ﷺ an invitation to a meal, and the Prophet ﷺ made it conditional upon the Mother of the Believers, Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her), being invited too. The man refused, but on the third occasion he agreed, and so the Prophet ﷺ went along with Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her).

In the same way, Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) bought a camel saddle from Ḥaḍrat ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him) and asked ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him) to tell his son Barāʾ (may Allah be pleased with him) to carry the saddle for him. ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him) said, "No — only on the condition that you relate to us the account of the migration." So Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) related the account of the migration, and Ḥaḍrat Barāʾ (may Allah be pleased with him) carried the saddle. See what is in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī:

عن البراء رضي الله عنه قال: اشتري أبو بكر رضي الله عنه من عازب رضي الله عنه رحلاً بثلاثة عشر درهماً، فقال أبو بكر رضي الله عنه: لعازب رضي الله عنه مر البراء فليحمل إلى رحلي، فقال عازب: لا، حتى تحدثنا كيف صنعت أنت ورسول الله ﷺ حين خرجتما من مكة والمشركون يطلبونكم، قال: ارتحلنا

الخ. (رواه البخاري ۱/۵۱۵، في مناقب المهاجرين).

From these two hadiths it is clear that a conditional voluntary favour is permissible. A conditional voluntary favour means attaching some condition to a kindness, or stipulating some benefit for oneself in a transaction. The case in question is of exactly this form: players are trained free of charge, and a condition of a share in their earnings has been attached. See further what Imdād al-Aḥkām states:

The question was put: is the agreement in question — that when a purchaser keeps buying goods of the current brand throughout a whole year he is granted a concession of, say, two or three rupees per hundred at the end of the year, whereas if during the year he buys even once from elsewhere he is given nothing at all — permissible in the sharia or not?

الجواب: This agreement is permissible, because the commission per hundred that is given to the purchaser at the end of the year is a voluntary favour on the part of the seller and is not a binding right of the purchaser; and it is permissible to make a voluntary favour subject to a condition. (امداد الاحکام: ۳/۳۸٦)۔

In another place he writes:

Question: is it permissible to take an admission fee and a monthly fee from students in madrasahs, or not?

الجواب: It is permissible, because this is not a wage but a contribution, and a condition in a contribution is permissible, since no compulsion results from it: whoever does not accept the condition has the choice of not enrolling.

ودليله أنه عليه السلام قال لمن أضافه وعائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها، قال: لا، قال: فلا إذن، قال في الثالثة وعائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها قال: نعم

۔ (امداد الاحکام: ۳/٦۰٦)۔

The sense of the noble hadith is that a man wished to invite the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ made his coming conditional upon Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her) coming as well, which the host accepted on the third occasion. The Messenger of Allah's ﷺ going was a voluntary favour, which he made conditional upon the coming of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her).

A point of benefit: Imdād al-Aḥkām is a collection of the fatwas of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him), compiled under the supervision of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him). In the monthly al-Balāgh of Jumādā al-Ūlā 1432 AH an article by Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Mufti ʿAbd al-Shakūr Tirmidhī (may Allah have mercy on him) was published, in which he said: in reality this Imdād al-Aḥkām is a supplement to Ḥaḍrat Thānwī's (may Allah have mercy on him) Imdād al-Fatāwā. (البلاغ، ص ٤۰)۔
