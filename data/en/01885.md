### TITLE
Trading in cloth bearing pictures of living creatures

### QUESTION
May cloth bearing pictures of living creatures be sold to non-Muslims or not?

### ANSWER
The pure sharia has forbidden the making of pictures and statues and the buying and selling of them. The buying and selling of statues is therefore impermissible, and likewise the buying and selling of a picture where the picture itself is the object. As for the pictures found on cloth and on the labels of goods and so on, they are generally not the object: what is really intended is the cloth or the article itself, and so we will not call this business impermissible. Moreover, selling to non-Muslims is a lighter matter still, since a non-Muslim is not addressed by the subsidiary rulings of the sharia. Cloth with large pictures, however, which is sold and worn as a fashion with the picture itself as the object, should be avoided in buying and selling.

عن جابر رضي الله عنه قال نهى رسول الله ﷺ عن الصورة في البيت ونهى أن يصنع ذلك، حديث جابر رضي الله عنه حديث حسن صحيح. (رواه الترمذي في باب ما جاء في الصورة: ۱/۳۰۵).

ʿIṭr-e Hidāyah states:

A curse is recorded in the hadith upon anyone who makes a picture of any living creature, and the Noble Prophet ﷺ said that the angels of mercy do not enter a house in which there are dogs and pictures. (مشکوۃ)

He also said that Allah Most High himself declared: who is a greater wrongdoer than one who seeks to imitate me in creating? Let them create a single speck — an ant — let them create a single grain of barley and show me. (مشکوۃ بحوالہ بخاری و مسلم).

And Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her) says that the Noble Prophet ﷺ would not leave anything bearing a picture in the house. (بخاری و مسلم).

Ruling: making a picture, having one made, buying one or selling one — whether drawn by hand or photographic, whether in relief or printed, whether of the face only or of the whole figure — is a grave sin and is unlawful.

If such pictures are on a book or a utensil and the like, and the price or the desirability of the item is increased because of them, then it is disliked. Even if they do not increase its desirability, it is still not free of dislike. Where avoiding them is difficult, however — as with money, paper, stamps, cards and so on, which have pictures printed on them — those who print them will be sinful, but ordinary people will not be sinning. (عطر ہدایہ ص۱۵۳، ۱۵٤، تصاویر کے احکام).

Some scholars have argued from the following narration of Tirmidhī Sharīf:

وعن عبيد الله بن عبد الله بن عتبة أنه دخل على أبي طلحة الأنصاري رضي الله عنه يعوده فوجد عنده سهل بن حنيف رضي الله عنه قال: فدعا أبو طلحة رضي الله عنه إنساناً ينزع نمطاً تحته فقال له سهل لم تنزعه، قال: لأن فيها تصاوير وقال فيه النبي ﷺ ما قد علمت، قال سهل رضي الله عنه: أولم يقل إلا ما كان رقماً في ثوب قال: بلى ولكنه أطيب لنفسي، هذا حديث حسن صحيح. (رواه الترمذي: ۱/۳۰۵).

قال ابن بطال: اختلف العلماء في الصور فكره ابن شهاب ما نصب منها وما بسط كان رقماً أو لم يكن، على حديث نافع عن القاسم عن عائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها، وقال طائفة: إنما يكره من التصاوير ما كان في حيطان البيوت، وأما ما كان رقماً في ثوب فهو جائز على حديث زيد بن خالد عن أبي طلحة رضي الله عنه، وسواء كان الثوب منصوباً أو مبسوطاً وبه قال القاسم. (شرح صحيح البخاري لا بن بطال: ۹/۱۷۹، كتاب اللباس، باب من كره القعود على الصور).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Magazines that are bought because of pictures of living creatures may not be bought,

"لأن الأمور بمقاصدها"

If the object is to read sound articles, then buying them is valid; the pictures are incidental, and they should be blotted out. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۵/۱۰۷، غیر مبوب).

Taṣwīr ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

In buying and selling, if the pictures are not themselves the object but come in as subordinate to other things — as figures are found on most cloth, or as is now widespread on utensils and other modern manufactures — then buying and selling them is permissible as a subordinate matter,

كما يستفاد من بلوغ القصد والمرام معزياً للهيثمي (بلوغ المرام: ص ۱۸) ولما هو من القواعد المسلمة من فقه الأحناف أن كثيراً من الأفعال لا يجوز قصداً ويجوز تبعاً كما صرحوا في جواز بيع الحقوق تبعاً للدار ولا أصالة وقصداً

But where the buying and selling of the pictures themselves is the object, then both buying and selling are impermissible. And if the figure is made of clay, then in the sharia no price for it is due from anyone; but if it is of some metal or of wood and the like, then a price is due to the extent of what that wood or metal would be worth leaving the figure out of account. (تصویر کے شرعی احکام، ص۸۸، از مفتی محمد شفیع صاحب رحمه الله).
