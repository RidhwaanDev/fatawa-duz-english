### TITLE
Listening to a sermon broadcast on the radio

### QUESTION
When the Friday sermon is being broadcast on the radio, is it necessary to listen to it or not?

### ANSWER
If the preacher's voice is being broadcast live on the radio, then those on whom the Friday prayer is obligatory must also come to where the sermon is being delivered and listen to it; for anyone else it is not necessary. If the voice was recorded and the recording is being broadcast, listening to it is not necessary — the ruling here is the same as for the prostitution of recitation. Consider what Ḥāshiyat al-Ṭaḥṭāwī states:

وفي النهر عن البدائع يكره الكلام حال الخطبة، وكذا كل عمل يشغله عن سماعها من قراءة قرآن أو صلاة أو تسبيح أو كتابة ونحوها بل يجب عليه أن يستمع ويسكت، في شرح الزاهدي يكره لمستمع الخطبة ما يكره في الصلاة من أكل وشرب وعبث والتفات ونحو ذلك، وفي الخلاصة كل ما حرم في الصلاة حرم حال الخطبة ولو أمراً بمعروف، وفي السيد استماع الخطبة من أولها إلى آخرها واجب

والنائي كالقريب. (حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح: ص ٥۱۹، قديمي. وكذا في إمداد الفتاح: ص ٥٦۹، واجبات الجمعة، بيروت)

Niẓām al-Fatāwā states:
As for the radio, in most cases the speaker's address and voice are recorded and it is that recording which is then broadcast. So if it is most likely that this is what has happened, it will not be binding to perform the prostitution of recitation on hearing that voice. But when the speaker is speaking himself, without such means, and recites a verse of prostitution, then the prostitution of recitation is necessary. On the radio, the difference between the speaker's own live voice and a recorded voice can be established from the context in which it is used, and one should act on that basis. (نظام الفتاوی: ۱/۷۲، کتاب الصلاۃ، فقہ اکیڈمی)
