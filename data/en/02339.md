### TITLE
The ruling on saying Bismillāh when a person of the People of the Book slaughters

### QUESTION
At a certain slaughterhouse the People of the Book slaughter without saying Bismillāh. Is it permissible to eat what they slaughter, or not?

### ANSWER
According to the Ḥanafī school, the animal slaughtered by a Muslim or by a person of the People of the Book is lawful only when Bismillāh is said at the time of slaughter. If it is left out by forgetfulness there is no harm; but if it is left out deliberately, the slaughtered animal takes the ruling of carrion.

In the view of the Shāfiʿīs, however, even if Bismillāh is deliberately left out the animal is still lawful, though it is disliked.

See what al-Hidāyah states:

وإن ترك الذابح التسمية عمداً، فالذبيحة ميتة، لا تؤكل وإن تركها ناسياً أكل، وقال الشافعي رحمه الله: أكل في الوجهين والمسلم والكتابي في ترك التسمية سواء. (الهداية: ٤/٤۳۵، كتاب الذبائح).

(وكذا في الاختيار لتعليل المختار: ۱۰. ۱۱، كتاب الذبائح، دار الكتب العلمية، بيروت، ومنية الصيادين، ص: ۱۵۰، وفتاوى الشامي: ٦/۲۹۹، سعيد).

Iʿānat al-Ṭālibīn states:

واعلم أنه يكره تعمد ترك التسمية، فلو تركها ولو عمداً حلت ذبيحته. (إعانة الطالبين: ۲/۷۵٦).

But in the view of the Shāfiʿīs the People of the Book means those who are People of the Book by origin and by descent, that is, those who were People of the Book at the time the Qurʾān was revealed, or whose forefathers were People of the Book at that time. As for those generations that went over to the Christian or Jewish religion later, what they slaughter is not lawful. See what ʿAllāmah Muḥammad Zuhrī al-Ghamrāwī says:

قال في كتاب النكاح: فمن علم أن قومها دخلوا في دينهم بعد بعثة محمد ﷺ أو في دين اليهود بعد بعثة عيسى عليه السلام أوشك في ذلك لا تحل وكذا لا تحل ذبائحهم. (السراج الوهاج: ۳۷٦، المكتبة التجارية، ومغني المحتاج: ٤/۳۱۲، ط: بيروت).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

If the People of the Book do not pronounce the name of Allah over the animal, most scholars agree that such an animal is unlawful. It is clear that the view of the majority is correct, and this is also what is required by the statement of Allah Most High that

﴿ما أهل لغير الله﴾

— what has been dedicated to other than Allah — is unlawful.

The difference of opinion is only over whether it is necessary for a person of the People of the Book to say Bismillāh or not. In the view of Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), the animal slaughtered by a person of the People of the Book is likewise lawful only when he pronounces the name of Allah at the time of slaughter; without that it is not lawful.

والمسلم والكتابي في ترك التسمية سواء. (الهداية مع الفتح: ۹/٤۸۹).

In the view of Imam al-Shāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him), the animal slaughtered by a person of the People of the Book is lawful even if Bismillāh is deliberately left out. Al-Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him) has declared this to be the position of the majority, and has reported support for this view from many scholars, including Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) — a report whose soundness is doubtful. He says:

ذبيحة أهل الكتاب حلال سواء ذكروا اسم الله تعالى أم لا لظاهر القرآن العزيز هذا مذهبنا ومذهب الجمهور وحكاه ابن المنذر عن على رضي الله عنه والنخعي وحماد بن سليمان وأبي حنيفة رحمه الله وأحمد وإسحاق وغيرهم. (المجموع شرح المهذب: ۹/۷۸، دار الفكر).

Among the Ḥanbalīs too it is necessary for a person of the People of the Book to say Bismillāh; in the view of the Mālikīs, however, saying Bismillāh is not a condition for a person of the People of the Book. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 2/216, 217, the animal slaughtered by a person of the People of the Book).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
