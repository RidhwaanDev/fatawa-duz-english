### TITLE
The ruling on a fast when the inside of the body is examined by endoscopy

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to have an endoscopy done while fasting — that is, an examination of the inside of the body by means of an instrument — or not? Will the fast be broken by this procedure or not? If the fast is broken, will the expiation be obligatory or not? And will there be any difference between having it done by choice and having it done under compulsion? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, an endoscope is a kind of instrument by means of which the hollow organs of the body, or the cavity of the body — the stomach, the ears, the intestines and so on — are examined. It is a tube-like instrument with a lens fitted at one end. This tube is passed into the body, and there is an arrangement in it for conveying light as well; at the other end, with the help of a second lens (the eye piece), it becomes easy to view the internal organs.

It should be borne in mind that the tube-like instrument which is passed in has a lens fitted on it (that is, the small mirror of a photographic camera in which the image appears), and what is aimed at by this procedure is only an X-ray; no medicine or the like is applied to it. Taken from (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoscopy).

Since in this procedure there is no medicine or the like on the instrument, and this instrument is in the doctor's hand — that is, one end is outside and the other end is passed in for the internal examination, and it is then drawn out and does not remain inside — the fast will not be broken. This is just as the jurists have written: that if a piece of meat is tied with a thread and lowered into the throat and then drawn out again, the fast will not be broken, so long as no part of the meat becomes detached and remains in the stomach. Consider the proofs and the supporting evidence.

Al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

ولو شد الطعام بخيط وأرسله في حلقه وطرف الخيط في يده لا يفسد الصوم إلا إذا انفصل. (البحر الرائق: ۲/۲۹۵، دار المعرفة).

Fatāwā Hindiyyah states:

ولو طعن برمح

وبقي جوفه فسد وإن بقي طرفه خارجاً لا يفسد. (الهندية: ۱/۲۰۲).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ولو بقي النصل في جوفه فسد أو أدخل عوداً ونحوه في مقعدته وطرفه خارج وإن غيبه فسد وكذا لو ابتلع خشبة أو خيطا ولو فيه لقمة مربوطة إلا أن ينفصل منها شيء ومفاده أن استقرار الداخل في الجوف شرط للفساد. (الدر المختار: ۲/۳۹۷، دار الفكر).

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

وحاصله أن الإفساد منوط بما إذا كان بفعله أو فيه صلاح بدنه ويشترط أيضاً استقراره داخل الجوف فيفسد بالخشبة إذا غيبها لوجود الفعل مع الاستقرار وإن لم يغيبها فلا لعدم الاستقرار. (فتاوى شامي: ۲/۳۹۷، دار الفكر).

Fatāwā Hindiyyah states:

ومن ابتلع لحماً مربوطاً على خيط ثم انتزعه من ساعة لا يفسد وإن تركه فسد. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/ ۲۰۲).

The resolution of the Islamic Fiqh Academy on the things that break the fast in the field of medical treatment:

قرار مجمع الفقه الإسلامي حول المفطرات في مجال التداوي: قرار مجمع الفقه ذكر مسألة (إدخال المنظار أو اللبوب ونحوهما إلى الرحم) وهذا السياق يشعر بأن المراد إدخال ذلك عن طريق المهبل وعلى كل حال فإن التصريح بصورة تنظير الجنين التي ذكرتها موجود في نص القرار ولم أقف عليه لغيره. هذا مخرّج على مسألة إدخال الإصبع في الدبر

عند الحنفية إذا أدخل أصبع وبقي بعضه خارجاً فإنه لا يفطر. (رسالة في المفطرات الطبية العلاجية: ۱/۵۹).

Muftī Inʿām al-Ḥaqq Ṣāḥib Qāsimī writes: Endoscopy. The meaning of endoscopy is "to look inside". It is generally applied to looking inside the human body by means of an endoscope for medical reasons. An endoscope is an instrument by means of which the hollow organs of the body, or the cavity of the body — the stomach, the ears, the intestines and so on — are examined. It is a tube-like instrument with a lens fitted at one end. This tube is passed into the body, and there is an arrangement in it for conveying light as well; at the other end, with the help of a second lens (the eye piece), it becomes easy to view the internal organs. (Ḍamīmah Rozē kē Masāʾil kā Insāʾiklopīḍiyā, p. 14, Bayt al-ʿAmmār, Karachi).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states: If someone tied a piece of meat with a thread and swallowed it, and then drew it out again at once, his fast will not be broken. But if some part of it becomes detached and remains in the stomach, or he lets go of the thread, his fast will be broken. In the same way, if he swallowed a piece of wood and the end of that piece of wood is in his hand, and he then drew it out, his fast will not be broken; but if he swallowed the whole piece of wood, the fast will be broken. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 3/270, Zawwār Academy edition).

In another place it says: This general rule applies when the thing which has been inserted is dry; but if he wetted that thing with water or oil or the like, then, even though one end of it may remain outside, his fast will be broken on account of the water or oil reaching inside, provided that he remembered his fast at the time of inserting it. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 3/309, Zawwār Academy edition).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh Raḥmānī writes: Nowadays, in order to identify certain diseases of the stomach, a tube is passed as far as the stomach by way of the mouth, and it sometimes cuts off a piece of flesh and brings it out with it, upon which the investigation is carried out. In such a case this person's fast will not be broken. In reality, whether the fast breaks or not depends upon this: has the thing which entered the stomach settled inside, or has it come back out? If it has settled there, the fast will be broken; otherwise not. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/186, Nuʿaymiyyah, Deoband).

Muftī Salmān Manṣūrpūrī writes: If, in order to test the stomach or the like, a tube with a viewing device was passed by way of the throat or the nose, and no medicine or lubricant was present in it, and one end of it was outside, then the fast will not be broken merely by the insertion of this tube. But if some other substance is present along with the tube, then the fast will be broken on account of its entering inside. (Kitāb al-Masāʾil: 2/155, Nuʿaymiyyah, Deoband; and likewise in al-Masāʾil al-Muhimmah fīmā Ibtulat bihi al-ʿĀmmah: 1/107, Akkalkuwa edition).
