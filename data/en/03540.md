### TITLE
A patient sleeping with an oxygen device fitted while fasting

### QUESTION
On account of certain illnesses the doctors prescribe a device which is fitted to the nose and the mouth while a person is asleep, by means of which breathing becomes easy. Only clean, pure air comes out of this device; there is no vapour or moisture or anything of the kind in it. Will the fast be broken on account of it or not?

### ANSWER
The name of the device mentioned in the question is the Respironics machine (RESPIRONICS MACHINE SLEEP APNEA). The doctors prescribe this device for patients who are in danger of their breathing stopping while they are asleep. The device is fitted at the time of going to sleep, so that if the breathing begins to stop during sleep, this device may help the patient to breathe by means of clean air. This device does the work of conveying clean air to the patient just as oxygen does; there is no moisture or vapour or the like in it.

Since there is no vapour or moisture or the like in this device, but only air, there is no harm in using it while fasting and the fast will not be broken by it — just as the fast is not broken by oxygen.

Marāqī al-Falāḥ states:

لا يكره للصائم شم رائحة المسك والورد ونحوه مما لا يكون جوهراً متصلاً. (مراقي الفلاح، ص: ۲٤٦).

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

لا يكره للصائم شم رائحة المسك والورد ونحوه مما لا يكون جوهراً متصلاً كالدخان فإنهم قالوا: لا يكره الاكتحال بحال وهو شامل للمطيب وغيره ولم يخصوه بنوع منه. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/٤۱۷، سعيد).

By way of support, consider the Arabic fatwas of some of the Arab scholars. Shaykh Muḥammad ibn Ṣāliḥ ibn Muḥammad al-ʿUthaymīn writes:

ما حكم الأكسجين الذي يوضع على فم المريض أكثر اليوم، هل يفطر؟

الأكسجين الذي يعطى للمريض لا يفطر، لأن هذا الأكسجين ليس له جرم يصل إلى المعدة حتى نقول: إنه أكل أو شرب لكنه بارد يفتح الشرايين حتى يتنفس الإنسان، وعلى هذا لا يكون أكلاً ولا شرباً وما ليس بأكل ولا شرب ولا بمعنى الأكل والشرب فإنه لا يفطر. (جلسات رمضانية للعثيمين: ۱۵/ ۱۹).

Dr Aḥmad ibn Muḥammad Khalīl writes:

المسألة الثانية: غاز الأكسجين.

التعريف به: غاز الأكسجين هو هواء يعطى لبعض المرضى، ولا يحتوي على مواد عالقة، أو مغذية، ويذهب معظمة إلى الجهاز التنفسي.

حكمه: لا يعتبر غاز الأكسجين مفطراً كما هو واضح، فهو كما لو تنفس الهواء الطبيعي. (مفطرات الصيام المعاصرة، للدكتور أحمد بن محمد خليل: ۱/۱۷).

In another place it says:

غاز الأكسجين لا يفطر فهو هواء لا يحتوي على أي مادة تسبب الفطر. (مفطرات الصيام المعاصرة، للدكتور أحمد بن محمد خليل: ۱/۳۳).

Muftī Inʿām al-Ḥaqq Ṣāḥib Qāsimī writes: In illness a patient sleeps with a RESPIRONICS MACHINE fitted to his mouth and nose so that he may have no difficulty in breathing. Using such a machine or device while fasting will not break the fast, because only a strong flow of air comes from it; no vapour or moisture or anything of the kind comes. And the mere passing of air in and out has no effect upon the fast. (Ḍamīmah Rozē kē Masāʾil kā Insāʾiklopīḍiyā, p. 30, Bayt al-ʿAmmār, Karachi).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh Raḥmānī writes: Oxygen is conveyed to a severe asthma patient at the time of an attack. What will the ruling be on taking oxygen in this way while fasting? This is an important question. Keeping the particulars of fiqh in view, the view that comes to mind is that if there is no medicine along with the oxygen, the fast should not break, because this is breathing, and taking in air by way of the breath neither breaks the fast nor is it called eating and drinking. If there are also particles of medicine along with it, then the fast will be broken by it. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/188, Nuʿaymiyyah, Deoband).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Salmān Manṣūrpūrī writes: If a person breathes by means of oxygen while fasting, the fast is not broken by it, because oxygen is merely clean, pure air, and its passing into the body does not break the fast. (Kitāb al-Masāʾil: 2/154, Nuʿaymiyyah, Deoband).
