### TITLE
Explanations of the vocative "یا محمد" in the hadith of tawassul

### QUESTION
Explanations of the vocative "یا محمد" in the hadith of tawassul.

### ANSWER
(1) Mawlānā Sarfarāz Khān Ṣafdar (may Allah have mercy on him) quotes Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Khalīl Aḥmad Sahāranpūrī (may Allah have mercy on him) as saying:

In this incident the Pride of both worlds was himself alive in this world, this act was done at his own instruction, and the man was there in his presence. At that moment, then, no answer or explanation was needed at all. As for the practice after his passing, it is done on the understanding that the words are conveyed to him and that the angels deliver them. Independent knowledge on his part (that is, knowledge without the angels delivering it) is neither implied by this, nor is it correct to recite the supplication with that belief; in that case this too would amount to polytheism. (تسکین الصدور، ص ٤۳۳، بحوالہ البراہین القاطعۃ، ص ۲۱۸).

(2) We follow the wording of the supplication merely by way of quotation; that is, our duty is to follow the words of the supplication as the Prophet ﷺ taught them.

قال الطيبي: نحن نتبع لفظ الرسول بعينه الذي علمه للصحابة.

(3) The vocative is used out of the utmost love and intense grief, and it carries no idea that the one addressed is present and watching.

قال في فضل الله الصمد: وإنما المقصود إظهار الشوق وإضرام نار المحبة، وذكر المحبوب يسخن القلب وينشطه فيذهب انجماد الدم، فيجري في العروق، وهذا هو الفرح، والخطاب قد يكون لا على إرادة السماع. (فضل الله الصمد في توضيح الأدب المفرد: ۲/۲۷۹).

(4) The address is made in the manner of poetic and loving imagination, and there is no harm in that; poets, after all, address walls and ruins. For details, see:

(فضل الله الصمد في توضيح الأدب المفرد: ۲/۲۹۸. ۲۹۹).

(5) Such an address in the texts runs contrary to analogy, so it is not correct to go beyond the texts. In other words, it is not correct to invent vocative wordings of one's own for tawassul and to use them in supplications or anywhere else.

قال في فضل الله الصمد: وما ورد على خلاف القياس فيقتصر على مورده ولا يتجاوز عنه. (فضل الله الصمد: ۲/۳۰۰).

(6) Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) said in "Nashr al-Ṭīb":

No doubt should be entertained here about calling out, for two reasons. First, what comes to mind straight away from the account is that the man was told to go into the Prophet's Mosque, and there the Prophet ﷺ is close at hand, so calling out to one who is absent does not follow. Second, the pious predecessors held sound beliefs, and it was plain from their condition that when they called out they intended that the angels convey it — unlike the ordinary people of our own time, who are extreme in their beliefs, which is why they are forbidden this; indeed, even the learned are restrained from it in order to protect the general public. (نشر الطیب، ص ۲٤۹، کتب خانہ اشاعتِ علوم).
