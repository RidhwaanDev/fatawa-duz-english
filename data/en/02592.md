### TITLE
A doctor disclosing his patient's illness

### QUESTION
A patient suffering from AIDS insisted that his doctor should not tell anyone about his illness. Should the doctor now disclose the illness or not? Where should he disclose it, and where is it not permissible to do so? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
Confidentiality is of great importance in the sharia, and we are urged to keep it; the opposite, giving away another's secret, is condemned. A doctor is the keeper of his patient's secret, and it is his religious, moral, legal and professional duty not to disclose the secret of a patient under his care where doing so could harm the patient or bring him disgrace in his family and in society.

A hadith states:

عن عقبة بن عامر رضي الله عنه قال: إني سمعت رسول الله ﷺ يقول: من رأي عورة فسترها كان كمن أحيا موء ودة. (رواه أبو داود، رقم: ٤۸۹۳ واحمد والبيهقي في سننه الكبري: ۸/۳۳۱).

But if the patient has a fatal or dangerous disease of such a kind that keeping it secret and concealed is likely to lead to serious harm, or could put the wider public interest at risk, then disclosing it is not against medical ethics; rather it is exactly what the public good requires. Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

تباح غيبة مجهول ومتظاهر بقبيح ولمصاهرة. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٦/٤۰۸، ٤۰۹، سعيد).

In the same way, if a young man has a defect such that the woman he has proposed to would not agree to marry him if she knew of it, and she makes enquiries of his doctor, the doctor must give her the details of that defect. Yes, he need not volunteer the information himself.

Likewise, if a man and a woman both have themselves examined by a doctor before marriage, the doctor must report his findings correctly and inform them of the details of any defect.

ويجب على المشاور أن لا يخفي حاله بل يذكر المساوي التي فيه بنية النصيحة. (رياض الصالحين، ص ۵۸۱، باب ما يباح من الغيبة).

Similarly, if a person is suffering from a fatal, infectious disease such as AIDS: if the patient takes all protective measures — for instance, he keeps apart from his wife — then there is no need to disclose his secret. But if the doctor is certain that the patient is not keeping to the protective measures in full, then informing the members of his household and others of the disease is not merely permissible; it is exactly what the situation calls for.

Likewise, if concealing this fatal disease (AIDS) would cause harm to the family and to those in contact with the patient, the doctor is duty-bound to give notice of the disease and not to act on what the patient has asked of him.

يتحمل الضرر الخاص لدفع ضرر العام مثلاً الحائط المتوهن إذا كان في الطريق فإنه يجب نقضه على مالكه دفعاً للضرر العام. (شرح القواعد الفقهية، ص ۱۹۷).

قال الإمام الصنعاني في سبل السلام شرح بلوغ المرام:

واعلم أنه قد استثني العلماء من الغيبة أموراً ستة

الذم ليس بغيبة في ستة ٭ متظلم ومعرف ومحذر

ولمظهر فسقاً ومستفت ومن ٭ طلب الإعانة في إزالة منكر

(سبل السلام شرح بلوغ المرام: ٤/۳۷۰، باب الترهيب من مساوي الأخلاق، ط: دار الكتاب العربي).

That is, mentioning someone's fault is not backbiting in six cases: for a man complaining of a wrong done to him; for identifying a person, as in the case of al-Aʿmash; for warning others and keeping them safe; in the case of a man who displays open sin and immorality; for a person asking for a ruling; and for a person seeking help in putting an end to something contrary to the sharia. The present question falls under case number 3.

Naʾe Masāʾil awr ʿUlamāʾ-e Hind ke Fayṣle states:

4. It is the moral duty of a patient suffering from AIDS to inform the members of his household and those in contact with him about the disease and to observe the necessary precautions himself.

5. If a patient suffering from AIDS insists that the doctor conceal his disease, and in the doctor's judgement there is a strong likelihood that keeping the disease secret will harm his household, those in contact with him and society at large, then it is the doctor's duty to inform the health authorities and the people concerned. (نئے مسائل اور علمائے ہند کے فیصلے، ص ۱٤۵) ۔
