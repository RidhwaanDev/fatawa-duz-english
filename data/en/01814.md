### TITLE
What to do with a mosque's old carpet

### QUESTION
A new carpet has been laid in a mosque. What should be done with the old one? The trustees hold several views on this. (1) It should be distributed among the worshippers. (2) It should be sold to the worshippers and the money used for the mosque. (3) The carpet should be given to some other mosque. Which of these is correct?

### ANSWER
As regards the old carpet: (1) It is not correct or sound to distribute it among the worshippers, because it is endowment property.

(2) It is permissible and correct to sell the old carpet and use its price for the mosque.

(3) If another mosque is in need of it, it is also permissible to give it there.

Consider what Fatāwā ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

متولي المسجد ليس له أن يحمل سراج المسجد إلى بيته وله أن يحمله من البيت إلى المسجد. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۲/٤٦۲، كتاب الوقف. وفتاوى قاضيخان: ۳/۲۹٤. والفتاوى البزازية: ٦/۲۷۰).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

حشيش المسجد وحصيره مع الاستغناء عنهما، وكذا الرباط والبئر إذا لم ينتفع بهما، فيصرف وقف المسجد والرباط والبئر والحوض إلى أقرب المساجد أو رباط أو حوض إليه. (الدر المختار: ٤/۳۵۹، سعيد).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

It is permissible to sell a mosque's old furnishings and rubble that cannot be used in the new construction of that same mosque. It is better that they be sold to a Muslim and that the price be spent on that mosque's building needs, or on something of the same kind as the goods that were sold. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 7/67).

ʿAzīz al-Fatāwā states:

A mosque's goods should be used in the nearest mosque. If there is no need for them at present, they should be kept safe so that they can be used when the need arises, or they may be sold and the proceeds put to use in the nearest mosque. (ʿAzīz al-Fatāwā: vol. 1, p. 567).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

If there are surplus mats in a mosque and there is no way of keeping them safe, so that they are deteriorating and going to waste, it is correct to spread the surplus mats in mosques that need them. The trustee and other people of sound judgement may give them away after consulting together; he should not give them without consultation, so that no discord arises. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 14/588, Fārūqiyyah).

See also: (Fatāwā Shāmī: 4/359, Saʿīd; Ḥāshiyat al-Ṭaḥṭāwī ʿalā al-Durr al-Mukhtār: 2/537, Kitāb al-Waqf; Imdād al-Aḥkām: 2/222, 234; Imdād al-Muftīn: 2/641; Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 9/129).

One point in the above question, however, deserves attention. ʿAllāmah Shāmī, ʿAllāmah Ṭaḥṭāwī and others have drawn a distinction with regard to a mosque's old fittings and furnishings. According to Imam Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him), once the mosque no longer needs them, the owner's ownership of them returns; according to Imam Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him), they are to be transferred to another mosque. They then said that in the case of a mosque's fittings the fatwa is on Imam Muḥammad's view, while in the case of a mosque's building material the fatwa is on Imam Abū Yūsuf's view. Muftī Rashīd Aḥmad Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) adopted this same position in Aḥsan al-Fatāwā. See: (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 6/425–427).

Consider the following words of ʿAllāmah Shāmī:

قوله مثله حشيش المسجد أي الحشيش الذي يفرش بدل الحصير، كما يفعل في بعض البلاد كبلاد الصعيد كما أخبرني به بعضهم قال الزيلعي رحمه الله: وعلى هذا حصير المسجد وحشيشه إذا استغني عنهما يرجع إلى مالكه عند محمد رحمه الله وعند أبي يوسف رحمه الله ينقل إلى مسجد آخر، وعلى الخلاف الرباط والبئر إذا لم ينتفع بهما، وصرح في الخانية بأن الفتوي على قول محمد رحمه الله قال في البحر: وبه علم أن الفتوي على قول محمد رحمه الله في آلات المسجد وعلى قول أبي يوسف رحمه الله في تأبيد المسجد، والمراد بآلات المسجد نحو القنديل والحصير، بخلاف أنقاضه لما قدمنا عنه قريباً من أن الفتوي على أن المسجد لا يعود ميراثاً ولا يجوز نقله ونقل ماله إلى مسجد آخر. (فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۳۵۹، مطلب فيما لو خرب المسجد أو غيره).

The fatawa of our senior scholars (may Allah have mercy on them), however, show that they gave the ruling on Imam Abū Yūsuf's view without qualification: whether the goods and furnishings are of the nature of fittings or of building material, the owner's ownership does not return to any of them; rather they are treated like endowment property.

Furthermore, the earlier jurists used to give the ruling that movable goods could not be endowed, and the question of ownership returning to the owner rests upon that view. The later jurists, however, are agreed in giving the ruling that movable goods may be endowed, so the question of ownership returning to the owner does not arise at all.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
