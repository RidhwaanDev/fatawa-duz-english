### TITLE
The ruling on selling neckties

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to sell neckties or not? And what is the ruling on the income earned from them?

### ANSWER
The necktie is an item used by disbelievers and open sinners. Trading in it is disliked and one ought to keep away from selling it; the income earned from it, however, will not be called unlawful.

See what Radd al-Muḥtār states:

وفي المحيط: لا يكره بيع الزنانير من النصراني والقلنسوة من المجوسي لأن ذلك إذلال لهما وبيع المكعب المفضض للرجل إن ليلبسه يكره، لأنه إعانة على لبس الحرام وإن كان إسكافاً أمره إنسان أن يتخذ له خفاً على زي المجوس أو الفسقة أو خياطاً أمره أن يتخذ له ثوباً على زي الفساق يكره له أن يفعل لأنه سبب التشبه بالمجوس والفسقة. (رد المحتار: ٦/۳۹۲، فصل في البيع، سعيد).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

At one time the necktie was a badge of the Christians, and at that time the ruling on it was also severe. Now non-Christians too wear it in large numbers, so the ruling on it is now lighter: it will not be called polytheism or unlawful, though it is still not free of dislikedness. In some places the dislikedness will be severe, in others slight; where its use has become general, no great emphasis will be placed on forbidding it. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 19/289, arranged by chapter).

Some scholars, however, have declared it impermissible on the grounds that it is a religious badge of the Christians.

See what is written in the notes and marginalia of Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah:

Although wearing the necktie has become common among Muslims as well, it is nonetheless still part of Western dress. Even if it is not considered Western dress, it is in any case the dress of open sinners and transgressors, and so it will be ruled forbidden on account of resembling the sinful. A second point is that righteous people do not care for this dress either, because it is contrary to the dress of the scholars and the pious. A third point is that, over and above this, there is a further evil in the necktie: Christians use it to point to their belief in "the cross of Jesus (peace be upon him)" — that is, to Jesus (peace be upon him) having been crucified — which is contrary to the explicit text of the Qur'an. Therefore, alongside resembling the disbelievers, it is also impermissible to wear it because it is a religious memorial and religious badge of the Christians. (Some scholars, however, dispute that it is a sign of the cross and a religious badge.)

وعن ابن عمر رضي الله تعالى عنهما قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: "من تشبه بقوم" أي من شبه نفسه بالكفار مثلاً في اللباس وغيره أو بالفساق أو الفجار أو بأهل التصوف والصلحاء الأبرار، "فهو منهم" في الإثم والخير، قال الطيبي رحمه الله: هذا عام في الخَلق والخُلق والشعار ولما كان الشعار أظهر في التشبه

(مرقاة المفاتيح، كتاب اللباس، ۷/۱۵۵، رشيدية).

(Taken from the marginalia of Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 19/289, arranged by chapter).

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

Question: The Hindus have a festival called "Raksha Bandhan", in which a sister ties a rākhī on her brother. What is the position on selling rākhīs on the occasion of this festival?

Selling rākhīs amounts to assisting in a religious rite of the disbelievers, so one should certainly avoid it. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 9/206).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

In the view of the sharia of Islam these badges of the disbelievers are nothing to be honoured; rather, in the way they are made there is a humiliation of them. Even so, it is better to avoid such trade and be cautious. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 16/138, arranged by chapter).

In sum: trading in neckties is not free of dislikedness, so it is preferable and better to keep away from it; the income earned from it, however, is not unlawful.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
