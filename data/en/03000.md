### TITLE
A claim will not be heard after 36 years

### QUESTION
A man's father bought a piece of land about 75 years ago. For certain reasons this land was not registered in the name of the buyer, and the seller's name is entered in the papers. The buyer built a house upon it. Then, 75 years later, the heirs of the seller brought a claim against the heirs of the buyer, saying: this land is ours, take the price of the house and leave this place. Both the seller and the buyer have passed away. It should be borne in mind that at that time the price of the land was about a hundred rupees, but now its price is far greater. Will a claim to the land be heard after such a long period or not?

### ANSWER
It appears from the statements of the jurists that if a person sees someone dealing with a piece of land and, despite having the ability and having no valid excuse, brings no claim for thirty-six years, then after this long period the claim will not be heard. On this basis, in the case in question the heirs of the seller saw the buyer dealing with the land for a long period and, despite having the ability, brought no claim; so their claim will not be heard now. Consider what ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

وفي الحامدية عن الولوالجية: رجل تصرف زماناً في أرض ورجل آخر يري الأرض والتصرف ولم يدع ومات على ذلك لم تسمع بعد ذلك دعوي ولده فتترك على يد المتصرف

ثم اعلم أنه نقل العلامة ابن الغرس في الفواكه البدرية عن المبسوط: إذا ترك الدعوى ثلاثاً وثلاثين سنة ولم يكن مانع من الدعوي ثم ادعي لا تسمع دعواه لأن ترك الدعوى مع التمكن يدل على عدم الحق ظاهراً، ومثله في البحر وفي جامع الفتاوى.
وقال المتأخرون من أهل الفتوى لا تسمع الدعوي بعد ست وثلاثين سنة إلا أن يكون المدعي غائباً أو صبياً أو مجنوناً ليس لهما ولي أو المدعي عليه أميراً جائراً يخاف منه كذا في الفتاوى العتابية. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۷٤۲، سعيد).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā records:

According to the sharīʿah there is no reason for a claim not to be heard after three years; the claim will be heard even after this period has passed. The noble jurists (may Allah have mercy on them), however, have ruled that where a claim has not been filed for thirty-six (36) years without any valid excuse it is not to be heard — and that too because to remain silent without excuse for such a long period is evidence that the plaintiff is lying. Hence, if it becomes certain that the plaintiff does in fact have a right, his claim will be heard even after thirty-six years have passed.

قال في الأشباه: إن الحق لا يسقط بتقادم الزمان.

(احسن الفتاوی: ۷/۲۱۲)۔

In Imdād al-Aḥkām a few qualifications are recorded on the authority of Shāmī:

1. The condition and integrity of the plaintiff should not be well established; if he is known for his integrity and there is no likelihood of trickery or fraud on his part, the claim is to be heard in any case.

2. There should be no strong excuse for the delay; if a strong excuse exists, the claim will be heard in any case.

3. The defendant should be denying the claim of the plaintiff; if he admits it, the claim is to be heard, however long a period may have passed. (ملخص از امداد الاحکام: ٤/۱۰۰)۔
