### TITLE
The ruling on receiving profits from the PIL company

### QUESTION
Our government instructed the private sector to open a company whose responsibility would be to import fuel from abroad. Accordingly, the fuel companies existing at that time (for example BP, Engen and so on) started a company which they named PIL. Every fuel company is an equal shareholder in PIL, and their elected members represent their own company on the Board of Directors of the PIL company. The PIL company imports fuel and earns a profit by selling it to the fuel companies of the country. Since the money changing hands runs into very large sums, the PIL company deposits the money in a 45-day fixed deposit account and pays its seller from there; and by keeping the money in this account the PIL company also earns interest. Accordingly, 60 per cent of the PIL company's profit is obtained from interest and 40 per cent from selling fuel; and the companies that hold shares in the PIL company are also given a share of the profit. Answers are now required to the following few questions concerning this:

1. May the fuel companies take profit from the PIL company?

2. Is it correct for the fuel companies to take 40 per cent of the profit and give away the 60 per cent to some poor person in charity without the intention of reward?

3. The directors of the fuel companies receive an allowance from the PIL company in return for their work, while it is not known what portion of the allowance comes from lawful sources and what portion from unlawful ones. Will it be permissible for the directors to take this allowance or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, the predominant income of the PIL company — that is, 60 per cent — is from unlawful sources and from interest, while the smaller portion — that is, 40 per cent — is from a lawful source. The scholars have written that it is not permissible to take profit from a company whose predominant earning is unlawful, unless that same proportion of the profit — that is, 60 per cent — is given away in charity. Even then it is binding upon the shareholder to raise his voice against interest at the annual meeting and to express his disapproval; and the PIL company should be obliged to deposit its funds in some account other than an interest-bearing one, so that they may be saved from interest. If they deposit them in a non-interest-bearing account, then all the profits will be lawful.

3. It is not permissible for a Muslim director to take an allowance out of interest-based earnings. Yes — if it becomes known what percentage is interest, then if he gives away that much in charity the rest will be lawful. Even so, it is preferable to keep away from a transaction of this kind, for the dignity of a Muslim lies in shunning every kind of interest-based dealing.

Consider the noble hadith:

عن جابر رضي الله عنه قال: لعن رسول الله ﷺ، آكل الربا وموكله وكاتبه وشاهديه وقال: وهم سواء. (صحيح مسلم: ۲/۲۷).

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah records:

غلب علي ظنه أن أكثر بياعات أهل السوق لا تخلو عن الفساد فإن كان الغالب هو الحرام يتنزه عن شرائه ولكن مع هذا لو اشتراه يطيب له المشتري شراء فاسداً إذا كان عقد المشتري الأخير صحيحاً كذا في القنية. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳٦٤).
قال الإمام الحموي: ووجهه أن كون الغالب في السوق الحرام لا يستلزم كون المشتري حراماً، لجواز كونه من الحلال المغلوب، والأصل الحل. (غمز عيون البصائر شرح الأشباه والنظائر: ۱/۳۰۹).

Muftī Taqī Ṣāḥib writes: there are four conditions for buying and selling shares:

1. The basic business should be lawful.

2. If the company carries on some interest-based dealings, one's voice should be raised at the annual meeting of the company.

3. When the profits are distributed, whatever portion of that profit has been obtained from interest-bearing deposits should be given away in charity. (فقہی مقالات: ۱/۱۵۱) ۔

Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil records:

Of the companies established today, most are such that their basic business is not unlawful — for example, there are textile companies, automobile companies and so on. But there will hardly be a single company that is not involved in interest-based dealings in one way or another. These companies become involved in interest-based dealings in two ways:

The first way is that these companies take loans from the bank on interest in order to increase their funds, and run their business with that loan. The second way is that whatever surplus and spare money the company has, it has placed in an interest-bearing account and receives interest upon it from the bank; that interest too forms a part of their income.

The opinions of the scholars of the present age differ concerning such companies. One group of scholars says that, since these companies are involved in unlawful activities, it is not permissible for a Muslim to become a partner with such a company in an unlawful activity.

The second group of scholars says that although this defect is found in these companies, nevertheless, if the basic business of a company is on the whole lawful, then there is scope for taking the shares of that company subject to two conditions. This is the position of Ḥakīm al-Ummah Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) and of my honourable father Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him), and in following these two I too regard this position as correct. Those two conditions are these:

1. The first condition is that the shareholder must certainly raise his voice within the company against the interest-based dealings, even if his voice is rejected. In my view, the better way of raising one's voice is that at the annual meeting of the company he should raise this call: that we do not regard interest-based dealings as correct, we are not content with interest-based dealings, and therefore they should be brought to an end. Now it is obvious that in the present circumstances this voice will be like a bird's chirp amid the beating of drums — it will go unheard — and his voice will certainly be rejected; but when he does raise this voice, then, according to the statement of Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him), in such a case that man has fully discharged his responsibility.

The second condition is that when the profits are distributed, that person should ascertain by means of the income statement what percentage of the income has been obtained from interest-bearing deposits. For example, suppose that 5 per cent of that company's total income was obtained by placing money in an interest-bearing deposit; then that person should give away five per cent of his profit in charity. (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل، جلدِ سوم، ص ۲۱ ۔ ۲۳) ۔

Imdād al-Aḥkām states:

If an employee comes to know that this salary which has been given to him has been given out of the price of an invalid sale or out of interest income, then taking it is not correct. But if everything is mixed together and he does not know whether this salary is from the price of a valid sale or of an invalid one, then that salary is lawful. (امداد الاحکام: ۳/۵۳۳) ۔
