### TITLE
Teaching children in the mosque for a salary

### QUESTION
If an imam teaches children in the mosque and takes a salary for the teaching, is this correct or not?

### ANSWER
وفي الهندية: وأما المعلّم الذي يعلم الصبيان بأجر إذا جلس في المسجد يعلّم الصبيان لضرورة الحر وغيره لا يكره. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۱۰، وكذا في خلاصة الفتاوى: ۱/۲۲۹).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states: it is not permissible for a salaried teacher to teach in the mosque. If there is no place available outside the mosque, then teaching in the mosque is permissible on the following conditions:

(1) The teacher should be content with a stipend that covers his needs and keeps him going, rather than being greedy for a salary.

(2) It should not interfere with prayer, remembrance of Allah, recitation of the Qur'an or any other act of worship.

(3) Full care should be taken of the purity and cleanliness of the mosque and of the respect and honour due to it.

(4) Small children who do not yet understand should not be brought into the mosque. (احسن الفتاوی: ٦/٤۵۸) ۔

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Paying a regular salary for teaching inside the mosque is disliked, especially when there is a room near the mosque in which the teaching could be done. Small children cannot tell what is pure from what is impure. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۰ /۱۵۰) ۔

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

The jurists hold that teaching in mosques for a fee is not in order, since mosques are places of worship and admonition, not places for earning a living. The circumstances of our own age, however, demand that permission be given, since it generally happens because of a lack of resources, the unavailability of any other place, and plain necessity. If strictness were shown in this matter, it would do serious harm and would also run against the interests recognised by the sharia: pupils of modern schools, who in this way acquire the basic teachings of the religion in a short while morning and evening, would be deprived even of that, and the closing of such full-time madrasahs would leave the people of the area without any education. Some elders are unduly strict on this point, even though the books of fiqh themselves show that teaching may be done in the mosque where there is need. Of course, if some other place is available, the teaching should be done there rather than in the mosque — on the authority of ʿĀlamgīrī and Khulāṣat al-Fatāwā. (جدید فقہی مسائل ۱/۷۳) ۔

وفي البزازية: "وتعليم الصبيان فيه (أي المسجد) بلا أجر وبالأجر يجوز". (الفتاوى البزازية: ٦/۳۵۷، نوع في المسجد من كتاب الكراهية).

Differing statements are found in the Hanafi books of fiqh about teaching in the mosque for a fee. Some record it as disliked without qualification — for instance Fatḥ al-Qadīr, Sharḥ Munyat al-Muṣallī, al-Ashbāh wa'l-Naẓāʾir, Fatāwā al-Nawāzil and Fatāwā Qāḍī Khān. Others record it as permissible on the ground of need — for instance Khulāṣat al-Fatāwā and Fatāwā ʿĀlamgīrī. Al-Fatāwā al-Bazzāziyyah, however, records it as permissible without qualification, as the passage quoted above shows. So if, in view of the passage in al-Bazzāziyyah, it is now declared permissible without qualification because of the need of the age and the importance of education, then ان شاء اللہ that will not be contrary to what is correct. Care should nonetheless be taken over the respect and honour due to the mosque, so that no contradiction of the sacred texts follows.

In my own view, there should be room for teaching children who are old enough to understand in the mosque and taking a salary for it, because the benefit of this teaching will show itself in the reform of people's children and in their growing up religious. This is not like a private trade; it comes under the general interests of the Muslims (مصالح المسلمین). Just as leading the prayer in the mosque is permissible for a wage, because it amounts to giving the religion currency, so teaching in the mosque is like leading the prayer. Still, if there is a place outside the mosque, the teaching should be done there.
