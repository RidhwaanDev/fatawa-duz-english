### TITLE
Zakat on unlawfully acquired wealth

### QUESTION
If unlawful wealth comes into someone's hands, or he receives interest money, is zakat binding on that money or not?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, if a person holds wealth that is purely unlawful, or interest money, zakat is not binding on it. The reason is that if the owner is known, the money must be returned to him, and if the owner is not known, it must be given away in charity — as Radd al-Muḥtār states:

لأن سبيل الكسب الخبيث التصدق إذا تعذر الرد على أربابها. (شامي).

If a person's lawful wealth has unlawful wealth mixed into it — such as the extra money obtained through a bribe or through interest — and the two can still be told apart, the ruling is the same: if the true owner is known, the money must be returned to him; otherwise it should be spent on the poor without any intention of earning reward.

If the two can no longer be told apart, then according to the school of Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) the unlawful wealth, by being mixed with the lawful, enters into his ownership, and zakat will therefore be necessary on the mixed wealth. Since this view is the easier one and is also of greater benefit to the poor, the jurists have adopted it.

Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ولو خلط السلطان المال المغصوب بماله، ملكه فتجب الزكاة فيه ويورث عنه، لأن الخلط استهلاك إذا لم يمكن تمييزه عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله

. وإلا فلا زكاة كما لوكان الكل خبيثاً، كما في النهر عن الحواشي السعدية. (الدر المختار: ۲/۲۹۰، سعيد).
وفي الشامي: (كما لوكان الكل خبيثاً) في القنية: لوكان الخبيث نصاباً لا يلزمه الزكاة، لأن الكل واجب التصدق عليه فلا يفيد إيجاب التصدق ببعضه. (شامي: ۲/۲۹۱، سعيد).

Al-Fatāwā al-Bazzāziyyah states:

لو بلغ المال الخبيث نصاباً لا يجب فيه الزكاة، لأن الكل واجب التصدق. (الفتاوى البزازية على هامش الهندية: ۲/۸٦).

For further detail see: Imdād al-Fatāwā: 2/14; Imdād al-Aḥkām: 2/3; Kifāyat al-Muftī: 4/256; Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/283; Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah: 3/523; and Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 7/637.
