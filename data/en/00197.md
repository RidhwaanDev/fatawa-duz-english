### TITLE
The reality of the spiritual pledge of allegiance, and whether it is a yogic practice

### QUESTION
I am the disciple of a pious elder. Before this my condition was that I prayed sometimes and left the prayer at other times. My tongue was in the habit of abuse, I lied constantly and swore false oaths, and I recited the Qur'an only occasionally in Ramadan. I made no distinction at all between lawful and unlawful income. I showed no respect to my elders, and I was constantly quarrelling with my neighbours and treating them badly. After I gave the pledge, all these sins and faults were gradually put right — something my acquaintances have noticed too. I was granted regularity in prayer, and, all praise to Allah, my heart engages in the prayer as though I am standing right before Allah. When I go to sit with my shaykh, my past sins come to mind, tears come, and I am given the strength to repent. I believe all this is a blessing of the pledge.

But a gentleman told me that this business of a spiritual guide and disciple is the way of the yogis and the Buddhists, who have people do little that is positive and much that is negative — in fact everything they teach is negative: do not do this, do not do that. They simply leave a man limp and paralysed. In short, he said, there is nothing good in this path, and it is not established from the Book and the sunnah either. What is established from the Prophet ﷺ is the pledge of Islam, by which he made disbelievers into Muslims; he did not take a pledge from Muslims.

I could not answer this gentleman. I feel the benefit of being a disciple myself, but I have nothing at hand with which to reply to him. Please give me an answer.

### ANSWER
Say to that gentleman that he should read Sūrat al-Fatḥ, in which Allah says:

﴿إن الذين يبايعونك إنما يبايعون الله﴾

— and so on to the end of the verse. Then, a few verses later, at the beginning of the third section, He says:

﴿لقد رضي الله عن المؤمنين اذ يبايعونك تحت الشجرة﴾

— and so on to the end of the verse. Here the pledge was taken from believers — indeed from Companions of the highest rank (may Allah be pleased with them), among whom were men who had already accepted faith in Makkah and had endured great hardship for the sake of Islam, who are counted among the first Emigrants, and who took part in the battles at the side of the Messenger ﷺ throughout. This pledge was not for accepting Islam: they had been Muslims long before, and their Islam was very strong.

And let him read Sūrat al-Mumtaḥinah, in which Allah says:

﴿يا أيها النبي إذا جاء ك المؤمنات يبايعنك على إن لا يشركن بالله شيئاً ولا يسرقن ولا يزنين ولا يقتلن أولادهن ولا يأتين ببهتان يفترينه بين أيهديهن وأرجلهن ولا يعصينك في معروف فبايعهن﴾. (الآية: ۱۲).

In this noble verse Allah commanded that a pledge be taken on six matters, and all of them are negative. If you reflect, you will see that the sixth of them takes in everything positive: that they will not disobey him ﷺ in anything right. That means obeying him in every command. In form it is a negation, but in reality it is the greatest affirmation of all. Besides this, taking a pledge from certain Companions on some particular matter is also established. The men of religion who take a pledge are not following the yogis and the Buddhists; they are following the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. They have a person repent explicitly of a few major sins, and by holding him back from every act of disobedience they move him towards obeying the Messenger of Allah ﷺ — just as is plainly stated in the hadith:

. رضي الله عنه. ﷺ. متفق عليه مشكاة المصابيح ۱۳، كتاب الإيمان)

This is the method of the pledge among all the masters of the spiritual path — Chishtī, Qādirī, Naqshbandī and Suhrawardī alike. Through it a great many people have had their hearts purified and have gained a connection with the order; base traits are removed and noble traits granted.

(والله الموفق لما يحب ويرضى) والله أعلم

(From Ḥaḍrat Muftī Maḥmūd Ḥasan Gangohī, may Allah fill his resting place with light)
