### TITLE
The ruling on video calls on a mobile phone

### QUESTION
FaceTime (face time) is a video call (video call) programme by means of which people can see one another face to face and speak. Is it permissible to use a programme of this kind or not? Likewise, what is the ruling on video call programmes such as Skype (skype)?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, if the video call is free of the things forbidden by the Sharīʿah — for example looking at a non-maḥram, the other party being immodestly covered, and so on — then its use is in itself permissible, because the image that appears on the screen is a reflection of the real form and is not a picture. Nowadays, however, this programme mostly consists of frivolities and forbidden things, and so one should try to keep away from it.

In Taqrīr Tirmidhī, Ḥaḍrat Muftī Taqī Ṣāḥib says:

The reason for this is that a picture is that which is fixed and settled upon something علی صفۃ الدوام. Hence if that image is not fixed and settled upon something علی صفۃ الدوام, then it is not a picture but a reflection. A live broadcast image, therefore, is a reflection and not a picture. For example, suppose a man is two miles away from here and has a mirror with him, and through this mirror he is viewing the scene here. It is obvious that this man, seated two miles away, is seeing in the mirror a reflection of what is here; he is not seeing a picture, because this reflection is not fixed and settled علی صفۃ الدوام in any place. In exactly the same way, in the case of a live telecast the particles of a person's form are transmitted by means of electrical particles and are then displayed by means of a screen. This image is therefore closer to a reflection than to a picture. (تقریر ترمذی، جلدِ دوم، ص ۳۵۱) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
