### TITLE
Sending zakat money from one place to another

### QUESTION
The jurists have written that it is mildly disliked (makrūh tanzīhī) to send zakat wealth from one place to another. In the light of that ruling, representatives travel abroad to collect donations for religious schools and also collect zakat money — does the dislike apply in their case or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case described, this ruling of the jurists is not as general as it appears; there are exceptions to it. If there are relatives in the other place, or the people there are in greater need, or the zakat is sent to God-fearing and religious scholars, to righteous people, to students and the like, then it is quite in order and there is no dislike in it — indeed it is better. Consider these statements of the jurists:

قال في البحر الرائق: وعدم الكراهة في نقلها للقريب للجمع بين أجري الصدقة والصلة وللأحوج لأن المقصود منها سد خلة المحتاج فمن كان أحوج كان أولي وليس عدم الكراهة منحصراً في هاتين لأنه لو نقلها إلى فقير في بلد آخر أورع وأصلح كما فعل معاذ رضي الله تعالى عنه لا يكره ولهذا قيل: التصدق على العالم الفقير أفضل، كذا في المعراج. (البحر الرائق: ۲/۲٦۹، ط: دار المعرفة).
وقال في الدر: وكره نقلها إلا إلى قرابة

أو أحوج أو أصلح أو أورع أو أنفع للمسلمين أو من دار الحرب إلى دار الإسلام أو إلى طالب علم

(الدر المختار: ۲/۳۵٤، سعيد).
(وكذا في تبيين الحقائق: ۱/۳۰۵، ط: إمدادية، وفتاوى الشامي: ۲/۳۵٤، سعيد، والفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۹۰، ومجمع الأنهر: ۱/۲۲٦، باب في بيان أحكام المصرف).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

If sending or transferring zakat from one city to another were disliked in every case, then consider that the Prophet ﷺ used to send his own collectors out to various settlements on his behalf, and ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) also made arrangements for zakat to be brought in from other cities. If a person's own close relatives are in another city, sending it there is not disliked; in the same way, if more religious people are in another place, sending it there is not disliked. These rulings are found in the books of fiqh: al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq, Shāmī, al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah and Majmaʿ al-Anhur. So if people send zakat from far away to religious schools where their relatives are studying, where the need is greater and where there are more religious people, there is no dislike in it at all. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 9/584, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah). See further: (Islāmī Fiqh: 1/474).
