### TITLE
Transplanting an organ taken from an animal

### QUESTION
May the organs of animals be fitted to a disabled or sick person when there is a need, or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
If some organ of a person has become useless and a replacement is needed for its function to continue in the future, then the use of non-animal materials to meet that need is permissible and correct. Likewise it is permissible to use organs of those animals "whose flesh is lawful to eat according to the Sharia and which have been slaughtered in the manner laid down by the Sharia."

In the same way, if there is a strong danger of loss of life or loss of a limb and a substitute for the required organ can be obtained only from animals whose flesh is unlawful to eat, or whose flesh is lawful but which have not been slaughtered in the Sharia manner, then in such a case the use of the organs of these animals — whether not lawful to eat, or lawful to eat but not slaughtered — is also permissible and correct when there is a need.

And if there is no severe danger of loss of life or of a limb, the use of parts of the pig is not permissible; though in a case of dire compulsion its use too is permissible.

قال في الفتاوى الهندية: وقال محمد رحمه الله: ولا بأس بالتداوي بالعظم إذا كان عظم شاة أو بقرة أو بعير أو فرس أو غيره من الدواب إلا عظم الخنزير والآدمي فإنه يكره التداوي بهما فقد جوز التداوي بعظم ما سوي الخنزير والآدمي من الحيوانات مطلقاً من غير فصل بينما إذا كان الحيوان ذكياً أو ميتاً وبينما إذا كان العظم رطباً أو يابساً وما ذكر من الجواب يجري على إطلاقه إذا كان الحيوان ذكياً لأن عظمه طاهر

وأما إذا كان الحيوان ميتاً فإنما يجوز الانتفاع بعظمه إذا كان يابساً ولا يجوز الانتفاع إذا كان رطباً. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳۵٤). وكذا في المحيط البرهاني: ٦/۱۱۷، الفصل التاسع عشر في التداوي).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وما طهر به أي بدباغ طهر بذكاة على المذهب. (الدر المختار: ۱/۲۰۵، سعيد).

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

والحاصل أن ذكاة الحيوان مطهرة لجلده ولحمه إن كان الحيوان مأكولاً وإلا فإن كان نجس العين فلا تطهر شيئاً منه وإلا فإن كان جلده لا يحتمل الدباغة فكذلك لأن جلده حينئذٍ يكون بمنزلة اللحم وإلا فيطهر جلده فقط

(فتاوى الشامي: ۱/۲۰۵، سعيد).

Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

Three forms of replacing human organs. One form, which has been in use since ancient times, is that a substitute for a human organ is sought from minerals or plants and so on, and by technical skill is made serviceable and useful — such as artificial teeth, an artificial hearing aid and the like. The second form is that this purpose is served by the organs of animals; this too has been in use since ancient times. Both of these forms are, from every point of view — religious, worldly, personal and social — free of risk and free of harm. Man uses all the creatures of the earth, minerals, plants and animals, for his own benefit, and for that benefit he may cut and trim them, pound and grind them, indeed burn them and use them by methods of chemical decomposition. (Jawāhir al-Fiqh, vol. 7, pp. 51–53).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states: fitting an animal's eye to a human being is permissible. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 8/236).

See further: (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith, vol. 1; Naʾe Masāʾil awr ʿUlamāʾ-e Hind ke Fayṣle, p. 143; and ʿAṣr-e Ḥāḍir ke Pechīdah Masāʾil kā Sharʿī Ḥal, p. 158).
