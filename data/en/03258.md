### TITLE
An examination of the verses found in some marginalia on the Sirājiyyah

### QUESTION
The following verses are written in the margins of some copies of the Sirājiyyah:

شخصے بسفر رفت از و ماند بسے مال

وارث دو نفر داشت یکے عم و دِ گر خال

خالش پسرِ عم وعمش پسرِ خال

اے مفتی آفاق چہ فتوی است دریں حال؟

That is: a man went away on a journey (meaning that he died) and left a great deal of wealth behind him. He has two heirs, one his paternal uncle and the other his maternal uncle. His maternal uncle is the son of his paternal uncle, and his paternal uncle is the son of his maternal uncle. O mufti of the world, what is the ruling in this case? That is, who is entitled to his wealth in the sharia?

### ANSWER
The case described may be pictured as follows. Aḥmad son of ʿAmr married Fāṭimah, the wife of his paternal uncle, and ʿAbd al-Qayyūm was born to them. Karīm likewise married Ḥabībah, the wife of his paternal uncle, and Qāsim was born to them. Maḥmūd married his paternal cousin, and ʿAbd al-Ṣabūr was born to them. ʿAbd al-Ṣabūr son of Maḥmūd then died.

ʿAbd al-Qayyūm is both his maternal uncle and the son of his paternal uncle. He is his maternal uncle because ʿAbd al-Ṣabūr's mother is Zaynab, and ʿAbd al-Qayyūm and Zaynab are half-brother and half-sister through their mother, since the mother of both of them is Fāṭimah. That he is the son of his paternal uncle is obvious.

Qāsim is his paternal uncle through the mother alone and the son of his maternal uncle. He is his paternal uncle through the mother alone because ʿAbd al-Ṣabūr's father Maḥmūd and Qāsim are half-brothers through their mother, since the mother of both of them is Ḥabībah. He is the son of his maternal uncle because ʿAbd al-Ṣabūr's mother is Zaynab daughter of Fāṭimah, while Qāsim's father is Karīm son of Fāṭimah; so Qāsim's father is ʿAbd al-Ṣabūr's maternal uncle, and Qāsim is thus the son of his maternal uncle.

Now, in this case, ʿAbd al-Qayyūm is the heir, because he is a full paternal cousin, even though he is also a maternal uncle. Qāsim receives nothing, because he is a paternal uncle through the mother alone and a maternal uncle, and such relatives belong to the dhawū al-arḥām (relatives who are neither fixed-share heirs nor residuaries).
