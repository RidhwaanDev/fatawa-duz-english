### TITLE
Placing the fingers on the eyes during the adhan purely as a remedy

### QUESTION
If a person places his fingers on his eyes during the adhan as a remedy and to relieve discomfort, without regarding it as a sunnah, is there room for that or not?

### ANSWER
Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

There is no firm evidence for kissing the two thumbs, so doing it in the belief that it earns reward is a claim without proof. Some people, however, do it as a practice for warding off eye disease; in that case it is permissible, like other such practices and amulets. But this means that no one who leaves it may be reviled or blamed: whoever wishes to do it may do it, and whoever does not, need not.

And in another place it says:

Some pious elders have described this act as a practice for warding off eye disease. That is not a matter of sharia. If someone does it believing that doing so keeps his eyes from hurting, he is free to do so. (کفایت المفتی: ۳/۵۷)

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

It is reported from some of the early scholars that this is a tested remedy for sore eyes. To do it as an act of worship, believing it to be a guided sunnah, is baseless and indeed an innovation, so it must be left. Yes, if someone does it for the purpose of treating sore eyes, in a way that gives no one else the impression that it is a sunnah or earns reward, that is fine. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۳/۱۶۰، باب البدعات و الرسوم، جامعہ فاروقیہ)

A detailed discussion of the reports concerning the kissing of the two thumbs may be found in al-Maqāṣid al-Ḥasanah, p. 384.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
