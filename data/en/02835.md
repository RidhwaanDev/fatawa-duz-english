### TITLE
Boys' names and their meanings

### QUESTION
Boys' names and their meanings.

### ANSWER
Letter alif:

Āṣif: a tasty plant whose buds are eaten mixed with salt or vinegar. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/126). It was the name of the minister of Sulaymān (peace be upon him), and it is used in the sense of "capable" and "one who retains." In terms of the Arabic word it means tasty and beloved.

Azhar: of white complexion, one whose face shines like the moon. (Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 404).

Usāmah: a lion. اسامۃ من اسماء الاسد. (Lisān al-Lisān: 1/30).

Aswad: possessing eminence and nobility, forbearing, wealthy, blessed, a chief, the banner on a mountain peak. (Tāj al-ʿArūs: 8; Bukhārī Sharīf: 1/17; and Fatḥ al-Bārī: 1/166).

Uways: beloved, a gift; al-aws means a gift. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/141; and Lisān al-ʿArab: 6/17).

Aḥmas: firm upon the religion. Ḥams means to be firm. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/375).

Aqraʿ: a hard and strong shield. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/1301).

Iyās: a gift. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 6/17).

Awrangzēb: the name of a king of the famous Mughal family who ruled with great splendour for more than fifty years. It means "the ornament of the royal throne."

Ayāz: the name of the slave of Maḥmūd Ghaznawī. In origin it is Iyās, in the sense of despair; then people changed the sīn into zāʾ, and the hamzah took a fatḥah because of frequent use.

Ghiyāth al-Lughāt states: Ayās, with a fatḥah, is the name of the slave of Sultan Maḥmūd Ghaznawī, and the undotted sīn here is a substitute for the dotted zāʾ — from Burhān. (Ghiyāth al-Lughāt, p. 60).

Iyās: it means despair, and its sense is the brave and courageous man of confronting whom people despair.

Ilyās: this too contains the word yaʾs, and its meaning too is the brave man before whom people are helpless. In the margin of Bukhārī, on the authority of al-Tawshīḥ and al-Kirmānī, it is written:

إلياس بهمزه قطع مكسورة إفعال من قولهم اليس الشجاع الذي لا يفر وقيل بهمزه وصل وهو ضد الرجاء.

(margin of Bukhārī Sharīf: 1/543, no. 7).

Umbāras: this word is in origin Humā-ras; then people corrupted it into Umbāras. The humā is an imaginary bird, and whoever's head it passes over becomes a king or a fortunate man. That is, "he reached the humā bird," meaning the man became fortunate.

Arslān: a lion. (Fīrōz al-Lughāt).

Letter bāʾ:

Bilāl: generous; water; everything with which the throat is moistened. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/180; and Lisān al-ʿArab: 11/64).

Bakr: a young camel; one who walks swiftly. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/176).

Barāʾ: free of faults. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/156).

Barīr: the fruit of the pīlū tree. ثمر الاراك عامة. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 4/55; and al-Ṣiḥāḥ: 2/512).

Badīl: noble, generous. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/154; and al-Ṣiḥāḥ: 4/1339).

Baṣrah: a soft whitish stone. (Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 59; and Lisān al-ʿArab: 4/67).

Pardil Khān: brave, courageous. (Lughāt-e Saʿīdī, p. 128).

Bābak: the name of a king who killed Ḍaḥḥāk; and it is in the sense of law and constitution.

Bābar: the tiger, and the name of a famous king of the Mughal family.

Banār: in origin it is in the sense of banārah, that is, "may you remain safe."

Banāras: one who attains safety.

Binyāmīn: in origin it is بُنَيَّ, and بُنَيَّ is in origin ابني, that is, "my son," and amīn means trustworthy — so, "my trustworthy son." Or in origin it is بُنَيّ, that is, "dear child," and amīn means trustworthy; the sense would then be: "a dear, trustworthy child."

Another good meaning of it is that yāmīn means fortunate, so the sense would be "a fortunate son."

Bahrām: the planet Mars, and the name of a just king of Iraq. (Ghiyāth al-Lughāt).

Parwēz: victorious. (Lughāt-e Kishwarī, p. 84).

Letter tāʾ:

Tamīm: complete and perfect; of full stature. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 12/69; and al-Ṣiḥāḥ: 4/1562).

Taymūr: steel, fine iron. (Ghiyāth al-Lughāt, p. 135; and Lughāt-e Kishwarī, p. 120).

Letter thāʾ:

Thumāmah: a plant with long branches. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 2/223).

Thaʿlabah: one who overawes the enemy. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 2/215).

Thamīr: fruitful. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/222).

Letter jīm:

Jadhlān: happy. (Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 113; and Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/243).

Juʿayl: a supervisor; a gift and bounty; it is the diminutive of juʿl. (Tāj al-ʿArūs: 28/210).

Jarīr: a rein; one who puts a rein on falsehood, and one who draws people towards the truth with a strong rope. Al-jarīr: a rope, the rope by which an animal is led. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/249; and Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 116).

Letter ḥāʾ:

Ḥafī: kind; al-ḥafī means gentle and kind. In the Noble Qurʾān: انه كان بي حفياً. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/358; and Lisān al-ʿArab: 14/187).

Ḥannān: merciful. Al-ḥanān: a mother's tenderness, love and affection, pity, kindness. In the Noble Qurʾān: وحناناً من لدنا. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/385; and Lisān al-ʿArab: 13/128).

Ḥamīm: alert; الحميم means a close friend. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/380). الحميم القريب الذي توده ويودك. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 12/135).

Ḥashīm: dignified; al-ḥishmah means modesty, bashfulness, dignity. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/343; and Lisān al-ʿArab: 12/135).

Ḥumrān: saffron, gold. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/374; and Lisān al-ʿArab: 4/215).

Ḥamzah: a lion. (Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 197).

Ḥafṣ: a lion cub. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/355).

Ḥanẓalah: the colocynth; bitter as colocynth towards falsehood. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/383; and Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 202).

Ḥuṣayn: a protected place; al-ḥaṣīn means protected, fortified. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/348).

Ḥarmalah: ḥarmal is a plant which is called kālā dānah, in Persian ispand and in Pashto spēlanay. Women and ordinary people place it on burning coals and use it to clear away foul odours from the air, to give fragrance, and for protection from the evil eye. In the bazaars the malangs fumigate the shops with it and collect some money. The tāʾ is for the singular. Likewise ḥarmalah is a short garment which is thrown around the neck over the shoulders and the back and projects in front. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/332; and Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 169).

Letter khāʾ:

Khaṣīb: verdant. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 1/355; and Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt, p. 204).

Khuzaymah: one who strings pearls. Khuzām is also a fragrant flower. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/436).

Khadīj: to be cut off from evil; one who keeps apart from bad people and bad deeds.

Khamīm: praiseworthy. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 19/12; and Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/477).

Khubayb: swift-paced. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 341).

Khirrīt: an expert. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 1/30; and Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/421).

Letter dāl:

Darīr: bright. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 4/281; and Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt, p. 234).

Dilhām: brave; a lion. (Al-Munjid fi'l-Lughah, p. 223; and Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/541).

Dukayn: dust-coloured; refined. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 13/157; and Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd).

Dilfigār: one with a wounded heart, that is, a lover. Figār means wounded, and dilfigār is not correct in the sense of "beloved." (Ghiyāth al-Lughāt, p. 377; and Lughāt-e Kishwarī, p. 351).

Durwēsh: if it is with a ḍammah on the dāl, then it is in the sense of a God-fearing and righteous Sufi. It was originally durr-āwēzish and was later shortened; it would mean pearls and precious stones, that is, hanging a rosary in the hands. Since the Sufis always had a rosary in their hands and their devotees would provide them with rosaries of precious stones, this name came to be given. Ghiyāth al-Lughāt states: Durwēsh, with a ḍammah on the dāl — it should be said that in this form it is a compound of dur, which means a pearl, and wēsh, which was originally wāsh, formed from wash, which is a word of comparison. (Ghiyāth al-Lughāt, p. 207).

Darwēsh: if it is with a fatḥah on the dāl, then it means one who hangs upon a door, that is, a righteous and pious beggar at the door of Allah Most High; and if "door" is taken in the general sense, then a beggar who keeps clinging to people's doors and asking. See: (Ghiyāth al-Lughāt, p. 206; and Lughāt-e Kishwarī).

Letter dhāl:

Dhakwān: alert and fragrant. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 1/448; and Al-Munjid, p. 353).

Dhuwayb: of lofty dignity. (Tāj al-ʿArūs: 2/449; and Al-Munjid, p. 347).

Dhābil: slender. (Al-Munjid, p. 349; and Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 309).

Letter rāʾ:

Rukānah: one who gives strength and support. (Lisān al-Lisān: 510; and Al-Munjid, p. 405).

Rummān: pomegranate — رمان: انار ۔ (لسان العرب: ۱/۵۱۵، و تاج العروس: ۳۲/۲۹٤).

Rabīʿ: the season of spring. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/593).

Raḥīl and Rāḥīl: swift in travelling. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 11/278; and Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/608).

Ramīz: alert. Al-ramīz: honoured and revered, of noble descent, intelligent. (Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt, p. 304). والرميز: العاقل الثخين. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 5/257).

Rahīb: awe-inspiring. Al-rahīb and al-marʿūb: fearsome; a lion. (Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt, p. 318). الرهبة: الخوف والفزع. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 1/436).

Rayyān: verdant and flourishing; one who waters; one who quenches thirst. (Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt, p. 326).

Ruwayd: a gentle, pleasant breeze. Al-rawd: a wind, a softly blowing wind. الرود الريح اللينة الهبوب. (Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 381).

Rābigh: prosperous. ربغ ربغاً القوم في النعيم، to live a life of ease. (Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt, p. 276). وعيش رابغ رافع أي ناعم. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 8/426).

Rashīq: of cheerful temperament and tall stature. Rashuqa rashāqatan al-ghulām: to be of handsome stature. (Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt, p. 294; and Lisān al-ʿArab: 10/117).

Rayḥān: every fragrant plant. (Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt, p. 322). الريحان جنس من النبات طيب الرائحة من الفصيلة الشفوية، وكل نبت طيب الرائحة. (Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 381).

Razīn: dignified. Razuna razānatan: to be serious, to be dignified; the masculine adjective is razīn. (Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt, p. 291).

Rifāʿah: elevated. الرَفاعة والرِفاعة والرُفاعة من الصوت — loudness and strength of voice. (Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt, p. 343).

Rājū: rāj is in the sense of kingship and rule, and the wāw comes for relation; so the meaning would be: one possessing kingship, independent. (Fīrōz al-Lughāt).

Letter zāy:

Zuhayr: shining. Zahara zuhūran: السراج والقمر والوجه، to shine, to be bright. (Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt, p. 349). Al-zahrah: نور كل نبات والجمع زهر. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 4/331).

Zubayd: a gift; butter. Al-zabd: a gift, a present. It comes in the hadith: انا لانقبل زبد المشركين. الزُبد, butter. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/695). And in Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ (388): ما يستخرج من اللبن بالمخض.

Zurārah: one who spreads blossoms. Al-zirr: blossoms, the bud of a plant. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/703; and Lisān al-ʿArab: 3/193).

Zufar: brave, a great chief. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/709). يقال للرجل الشجاع: زفر. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 4/325).

Zaʿīm: a chief. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/707; and al-Ṣiḥāḥ: 4/1576).

Zarrāf: swift. (Al-Ṣiḥāḥ: 3/1130; Lisān al-ʿArab: 9/126; and Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/704).

Zayd: one who advances. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/730).

Zarīr: very intelligent, and of cheerful temperament. (Al-Ṣiḥāḥ: 2/577; Lisān al-ʿArab: 4/323; and Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/703).

Zarwalī: walī has many meanings, but the meaning well known in common usage is a friend of Allah Most High. So Zarwalī means a golden walī, that is, a wealthy walī, like ʿUthmān al-Ghanī. Not all wealth is blameworthy; rather lawful wealth, in which the rights of Allah and the rights of His servants are discharged, is not detestable but beloved. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ supplicated for blessing in the family and wealth of Ḥaḍrat Anas (may Allah be pleased with him), and it is also reported: نعم المال الصالح للرجل الصالح.

Or the sense is that walī who is as precious and beloved as gold. It is also possible that this was in origin Ḍarb-e ʿAlī and people corrupted it into Zarwalī, since Ḍarb-e ʿAlī is famous in connection with the killing of Marḥab the Jew at Khaybar, and the name was given with that in view.

Letter sīn:

Saḥbān: one who draws forcefully, that is, one who draws people towards the religion. الجراف، يجرف كل ما مر به. (Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 418).

Sulayk: a path; one who brings a person to his goal. Also the chick of the chakor, which is a beautiful bird whose beautiful eyes are well known. سلكاً، وسلوكاً، والمكان والطريق. (Al-Munjid, p. 347). والسلك: فرخ القطا وقيل فرخ الحجل. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 10/442).

Suwaybiṭ: fresh and green; a plant. قال ابوعبيد: السبط النَّصِي مادام رطباً فإذا يبس فهو الحلي. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 7/309).

Sāriyah: night rain. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/766; and al-Ṣiḥāḥ: 5/1896).

Suḥaym: a plant; iron, that is, strong as iron. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/752; and Lisān al-ʿArab: 12/282).

Salīṭ: eloquent of tongue, clever. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 7/320; and al-Ṣiḥāḥ: 3/950).

Simāk: a boy of lofty rank. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 10/443; and al-Ṣiḥāḥ: 4/1306).

Sandar: brave. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/809).

Sufyān: swift as the wind in works of good. (Al-Ṣiḥāḥ: 5/1897; and Lisān al-ʿArab: 14/389).

Sarzamīn Khān: the choicest part of the land; and Khān is a title of the Pashtuns, the Baloch and the Turks. (Lughāt-e Saʿīdī, p. 410).

Sarfarāz: honoured and exalted. (Lughāt-e Kishwarī, p. 245).

Sikandar: a famous king; and in the Roman language it is in the sense of head and chief. (Lughāt-e Kishwarī).

Sūrāj: independent, a man of sound opinion. (Fīrōz al-Lughāt).

Letter shīn:

Shamīm: magnificent and fragrant. المرتفع. (Tāj al-ʿArūs: 32/475, Dār al-Hidāyah). ما يشم، والمرتفع. (Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 495, Kutub Khānah Ḥusayniyyah).

Shahīd: a witness. من قتل في سبيل الله، ومن يؤدي الشهادة، وفي تنزيل العزيز "ولا يضار كاتب ولا شهيد. (Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 497).

Shaybān: white-bearded, dignified. الاشيب والشاب: المبيض الرأس. (Lisān al-ʿArab: 7/251, Dār Iḥyāʾ al-Turāth, Beirut; and Al-Munjid, p. 410).

Sharīd: one who leaves his people and his town for the sake of the religion and migrates. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/853).

Shurayḥ: one who explains.

Shaqīq: a full brother. الأخ من الأب والأم، والمثيل. (Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 488).

Shamʿūn: one who lights the lamp. اشمع السراج، أي سطح نوره. (Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, p. 402).

Shubrumah: a particular tree that is beneficial in epidemic diseases.

شجر ذو شوك يقال انه ينفع من الوباء

وفي حديث ام سلمة رضي الله عنه انها شربت الشبرم فقال انه حار جار، والشبرمة: السنورة. (تاج العروس).

Shakīl: handsome. (Al-Ṣiḥāḥ: 4/1418; Lisān al-ʿArab: 11/357; Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/881; and Lughāt-e Kishwarī, p. 279).

Shamshīr Khān: a sword. Sham means a claw and shīr is well known, that is, the claw of a lion — a sword is like the claw of a lion. (Lughāt-e Saʿīdī, p. 460).

Shamrōz Khān: this is in origin Shāh-e Imrōz, that is, the king of the present age. And Khān is used among the Turks, the Baloch, the Kashmiris, the Pashtuns and some of the Banū Isrāʾīl in the sense of a chief and a commander.

Shamar: a water-tank; the cream that comes upon milk. (Lughāt-e Saʿīdī, p. 459).

Shammās Khān: a servant; a guide and leader of the fire-worshippers. (Lughāt-e Saʿīdī, p. 459).

Shamshād: a very strong, straight and beautiful tree; the beloved is likened to it, so it is in the sense of "beloved."

Shīrōyah: like a lion, brave. (Ghiyāth al-Lughāt, p. 303; and Lughāt-e Kishwarī, p. 286).

Shīrāz: (1) a famous city of Iran which was renowned for learning and letters; Saʿdī and Ḥāfiẓ Shīrāzī both belonged to this city. (2) Yoghurt whose water has been drained off, that is, fine yoghurt. (3) It is also used for the Shīrāzī pigeon, in which case the masculine form is Shīrāz.

Ṣunān: brave. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/947).

Letter fāʾ:

Letter qāf:

Qatādah: a hard tree. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 2/1275).

Quṣayy: far from faults, and high in rank. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1323).

Qayyāsh: no good meanings can be made of this name. It was in origin Qayyās, that is, intelligent and understanding, or one who draws one point out of another; then people corrupted it into Qayyāsh. And Allah knows best.

Qizilbāsh: red head, that is, one with a red cap; this was the mark of the soldiers of the well-known Shīʿī Ismāʿīl Ṣafawī.

Letter kāf:

Kahmas: a lion. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 2/1432).

Letter lām:

Labīd: one who keeps to a corner, who stays at home, who works steadfastly in one place. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 2/1447).

Luqmān: one who walks upon the highroad of Islam; wealthy. اللقم: الطريق الواضح. (العجم الوسيط، ص ۸۳۵)، اللقم: معظم الطريق ووسطه. (Tāj al-ʿArūs: 33/429).

Layyān: prosperous. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 2/1517).

Lājbar: this is in origin lājward, which is a precious stone that is ground and used in making designs; then it was corrupted into Lājbar. Or Lājbar means one who possesses honour and dignity.

Letter mīm:

Muṣʿab: a chief. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/924).

Misṭaʿ: eloquent of tongue. (Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, 429; and Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 2/768).

Malang: one who is heedless of the things of the world, even of his own clothing and the like; it is used for one who has renounced the world.

Muḥammad Nabī: this is in origin Muḥammad Nabīh. Nabīh means one of eminence and loftiness; then people corrupted it and began to say and write Muḥammad Nabī.

Mihrān: mihr means love, and also the sun; and alif-nūn comes for the plural in Pashto and Persian, as in Ṭālibān and ʿĀlimān. So Mihrān would mean a gathering of many suns or of many loves. Mihrān is also the name of a medicine. (Fīrōz al-Lughāt; and Ghiyāth al-Lughāt).

Nīmrōz Khān: this was in origin Nīmrōz Khān; Nīmrōz was a part of Sijistān, so it means the commander of a region.
