### TITLE
A study of the minarets of mosques

### QUESTION
Since when have there been minarets on mosques, what is their purpose, and are they not an innovation?

### ANSWER
Our respected teacher Mawlānā Sarfarāz Khān (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote in Rāh-e Sunnat what may be summed up as follows: minarets are in fact built so that the call to prayer may be given from them and people may hear the call far and wide.

Imam Abū Dāwūd (may Allah have mercy on him) devoted a chapter of his own to this:

"باب الأذان فوق المنارة". (أبو داود شريف: ۱/۷۷).

And Abū Barzah al-Aslamī رضي الله عنه, who died in the year ٦۵, and others say:

"من السنة الأذان في المنارة والإقامة في المسجد". (نصب الراية للعلامة الزيلعي: ۱/۲۹۳، كتاب الصلاة، بيروت، ومصنف ابن أبي شيبة: ۱/۲۲٤).

It is an established principle of hadith scholarship that when "sunnah" is used without qualification, the sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ is meant. (Rāh-e Sunnat, p. ۳۰۵).

In al-Iṣābah fī Tamyīz al-Ṣaḥābah, Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in his account of the Companion Maslamah ibn Mukhallad:

وقال ابن السكن: وهو أول من جعل على أهل مصر بنيان المنار. (الإصابة في تمييز الصحابة: ٦/۹۲، حرف الميم).

Since a minaret is not an end in itself, but a mark by which a mosque is recognised and, in former times, a means of carrying the call to prayer over a distance, it is not an innovation either — particularly as it is something established from the Companions ث (may Allah be pleased with them).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
