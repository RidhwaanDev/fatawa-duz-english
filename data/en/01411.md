### TITLE
Stoning the pillars on behalf of women because of overcrowding

### QUESTION
If, in view of the excuse of overcrowding, women do not perform the stoning themselves but their menfolk or someone else performs it on their behalf, does any defect occur in their Hajj? And does an expiatory sacrifice (dam) become necessary?

### ANSWER
Hajj is an act of worship in which the sharia has permitted the whole Hajj to be performed by a deputy in cases of valid excuse, incapacity and compulsion. It follows all the more strongly that deputising for the stoning is valid on account of a genuine excuse or incapacity. In the view of the jurists, however, deputising is valid only for the sick and the disabled, and this is determined essentially by two things: (1) a disabled or sick person who does not even have the strength to throw the pebbles; (2) a person who is able to throw the pebbles but for whom walking to the Jamarāt is extremely difficult and unbearable — and nowadays there is no way of moving about within the Jamarāt complex except on foot.

Apart from this, deputising for the stoning merely because of overcrowding is not valid. The reason is that, keeping in view the latitude the jurists have allowed in the times of stoning, the elderly, the weak and women can select a suitable time and comfortably carry out the stoning themselves.

Consider what Ghunyat al-Nāsik states:

(تنبيه) قد تبين مما قدمنا أنهم جعلوا خوف الزحام عذراً للمرأة ولمن به علة أو ضعف في تقديم الرمي قبل طلوع الشمس أو تأخيره إلى الليل لا في جواز النيابة عنهم لعدم الضرورة فلو لم يرموا بأنفسهم لخوف الزحام تلزمهم الفدية، والله سبحانه وتعالى أعلم. (غنية الناسك في بغية المناسك، ص۱۰۰، فصل في الترتيب بين الجمار الثلاث، ط: إدارة القرآن).

Zubdat al-Manāsik states:

Ruling: It is not permissible for a woman to have the stoning performed on her behalf by a deputy merely because of overcrowding, unless there is some other excuse, such as illness, on account of which she cannot perform the stoning herself. What has been conceded to her on account of overcrowding is the concession of delay, whether in the stoning of Jamrat al-ʿAqabah or in the stoning of any other day; and in the case of illness both men and women are permitted [to appoint a deputy]. So it is not permissible for a woman to have someone else stone on her behalf simply because of the press and crush of the crowds, and if she does not go and stone herself, a penalty will be due from her. (Zubdat al-Manāsik, p. 184).

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states:

On this matter all of the contributors are agreed that when a person is under compulsion or disabled it is correct to appoint another person as a deputy for the stoning of the Jamarāt. In defining the limits of a valid excuse, most of them have stated explicitly that the limit is that the disabled person is unable to perform the prayer standing and does not possess the capacity or strength to reach the place of stoning on his own; such a person is regarded as excused in the eyes of the sharia. Likewise all of them agree that mere overcrowding will not count as an excuse permitting a deputy, and that men and women are equal in this ruling.

In another place it states:

Since the honourable scholars do not accept overcrowding as an excuse at all where deputising in the stoning is concerned, the necessary implication is that in their view a situation created by overcrowding will likewise not be an excuse that makes deputising in the stoning permissible. It can therefore be said that the excuse which permits a deputy in the stoning is confined and restricted to this alone: inability to perform the stoning.

Accordingly these scholars say that although the place of stoning the pillars is limited, the time is broad; so by taking advantage of that breadth of time one can avoid the distress of the crowds. If the recommended and permitted time cannot be observed, the weak and women will be allowed to stone before sunrise or at night after sunset. In other words, in their view overcrowding is an excuse to the extent that stoning during the disliked time becomes valid without any dislike attaching to it — but there is no room for a deputy in any case. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 13/23 and 55).

A second view:

About twenty-one of the contributors hold that this too can be an excuse permitting a deputy: when overcrowding produces a situation in which, for a person suffering from any illness, or for an elderly and weak man or woman who because of that weakness lacks the strength to fend off the crowd, there arises a strong likelihood of being crushed, or of falling and breaking a limb, or of being trampled, and so of suffering such severe injury as is normally unbearable — then such overcrowding will also be an excuse, and it will be permissible for such people to appoint a deputy for the stoning. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 13/56).

For more, see: (Khayr al-Fatāwā: 4/158, and Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband: 6/554, Bāb al-Jināyāt).

The problem of overcrowding, however, belonged to an earlier time, when accidents occurred frequently and the Jamarāt were like a scene of the Day of Judgement in miniature. In our own time the argument from overcrowding is unacceptable, because the Saudi authorities have now divided the Jamarāt into several levels and have fixed a route in and out for each level, on account of which there is generally no crush and one can perform the stoning easily and return. Deputising merely because of overcrowding will therefore not be valid, and so in the case asked about an expiatory sacrifice will be necessary.

Yes, if the crowding is unbearable and there is a danger of death or injury, then deputising will be valid — as happened last year, 1436 AH, when the disorder in the crowd proved a message of death for hundreds of people.
