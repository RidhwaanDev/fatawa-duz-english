### TITLE
Whether drawing blood with a syringe breaks the ablution

### QUESTION
If blood is drawn out with a syringe, does the ablution break or not?

### ANSWER
If the aim is to draw and pull out blood with the syringe, the ablution is broken by it. A parallel case is the following ruling from the books of fiqh.

القُراد إذا مص عضو إنسان فامتلأ دماً إن كان صغيراً لا ينقض وضوءه كما لو مصت الذباب أو بعوض وإن كان كبيراً ينقض، وكذا العلقة إذا مصت عضو إنسان حتى امتلأت من دمه انتقض وضوءه كذا في محيط السرخسي. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۱، وكذا في الدر المختار: ۱/۱۳۹، والمحيط البرهاني: ۱/٤۲، الفصل الثاني في بيان ما يوجب الوضوء).

That is: when ticks suck at a limb of a person and become full of blood, then if they are small ones the ablution is not broken — as with flies, mosquitoes and the like — but if the tick is a large one the ablution is broken. Likewise, when a leech sucks at a limb of a person until it is full of his blood, his ablution is broken.

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

Question: Blood is drawn out by means of a syringe; does this break the ablution or not?

Answer: If the blood drawn out is enough to flow, the ablution is broken.

Kabīrī states:

إذا فصد وخرج منه دم كثير ولم يتلطخ رأس الجرح فإنه ينقض. (شرح منية المصلي ص ۱۲۹).

In earlier times the instrument for bleeding was the cupping horn; in our own age the syringe is that same bleeding instrument in a changed form. Blood is also drawn out by means of leeches, and the same ruling applies to that.

وكذا ينقضه علقة الخ. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 4/267). In short, if the blood drawn out with a syringe is enough to flow, the ablution is broken.
