### TITLE
The ruling on celebrating birthdays and wedding anniversaries

### QUESTION
Is it permissible or not to celebrate a wedding anniversary, as well as the birthdays of children?

### ANSWER
In our times the celebration of birthdays is included among the particular customs of non-Muslims; then, because a great many absurdities and extravagances and the like are bound up with it, it is necessary to abstain from it. Yes, Ḥaḍrat Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him) did grant permission for such celebration, and that was because in his time these things did not exist.

Consider what is recorded in Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah:

There appears to be no harm in a birthday celebration for the sake of keeping a record of the age of children, and it is also correct, once the year is completed, to feed people a meal for the sake of Allah. (فتاوی رشیدیہ، ص ۵۵٤، اسلامی کتب خانہ)۔

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

Celebrating a birthday is not a religious occasion; it is merely a memorial of a reckoning and a date. All these frivolities undertaken for it are pure futility and fall under binding oneself to what is not binding. (کفایت المفتی: ۹/۸٤، دار الاشاعت)۔

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah records:

To hold a function and to celebrate on the completion of a full year from birth is not an Islamic teaching; it is the way of others, and one ought to keep away from it. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۷/۳٦۰، جامعہ محمودیہ طبع قدیم)۔

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

The manner of celebrating birthdays that is current (cutting a cake, for example) is not necessary; rather it is fit to be abandoned, for resemblance to others is entailed by it. However, expressing joy and giving thanks to God is not forbidden. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ٦/۳۲۰، طبع قدیم)۔

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā records:

Celebrating a birthday is a repugnant custom and abandoning it is obligatory. The real birthday observance is that on such occasions one should take account of one's life and reflect upon one's deeds — whether they are carrying one towards Paradise or towards Hell. (احسن الفتاوی: ۸/۱۵۵)۔

Āp ke Masāʾil states:

The custom of celebrating birthdays is one instituted by the English. (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ۸/۱۲٦)۔

Kitāb al-Fatāwā states:

The birthday celebration is an unlawful practice. Neither did the Messenger of Allah ﷺ ever celebrate his own birthday or that of his children, nor did the Companions, nor the pious predecessors who came after them. It is a gift of Western culture; cutting a cake and lighting and extinguishing candles are the influences of other nations. Therefore one should keep away from such customs and usages. (کتاب الفتاوی: ٦/۲۸۵)۔

The Noble Prophet ﷺ has forbidden adopting a resemblance to other nations:

عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: "من تشبه بقوم فهو منهم". (سنن أبي داود: ۲/ ۵۵۹).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
