### TITLE
The kinds of crow according to modern research

### QUESTION
The kinds of crow according to modern research.

### ANSWER
According to modern research there are six kinds of crow in all:

(1) The nutcracker. This is found in North America, Asia and Europe. Its principal food is grain, and it also eats small creatures.

(2) The magpie. This eats a mixed diet of insects, birds' eggs, leaves, fruit and the like.

(3) The scrub jay. This eats only plants and leaves.

(4) The jackdaw. This is found in Europe and eastern Asia and eats a mixed diet.

(5) The raven. This is found in Canada and North Africa; its food consists of the leaves of trees, fruit, small birds and the like.

(6) The rook. This is met with in Europe. (Derived from Encarta.)

Of the kinds mentioned above, those whose food is leaves, plants, fruit and the like will be lawful to eat; while those that eat a mixed diet will be disliked to eat, like the free-roaming hen. One kind among them, which is included under the jay, eats only carrion, and by keeping company with predatory animals eats the flesh of their prey; eating it is therefore altogether unlawful.

A useful point: what the jurists call the ʿaqʿaq is the magpie.
