### TITLE
The ruling on the RCS contract

### QUESTION
Is the RCS contract correct or not? The nature of the RCS contract is as follows.

A buyer wishes to buy goods on credit. The seller tells him that a company, RCS, will buy the goods for him. The shopkeeper accordingly gives the buyer a form; the buyer fills it in and faxes it to the RCS company. If the application is approved, the RCS company gives the buyer a card, and the buyer buys the goods with that card. The company pays the money directly to the shopkeeper, and for three months it takes no interest from the buyer, who pays the money to the company. The question is: is it correct for the shopkeeper to arrange such a service or not?

### ANSWER
If the reality of the transaction is that the company buys the item from the shopkeeper and then sells that same item to the person in need at a higher price — that is, the company sells it on credit at a higher price — then this transaction is permissible in the sharia. But an objection arises here: the owner or manager of the company does not take possession of the item, so how can it be permissible for him to sell it before taking possession?

The answer: There are two possible ways in which it may be permissible. (1) The first is that the company should appoint the shopkeeper himself as its agent to take possession — that is, the owner should say: "You are holding on my behalf the item I have bought from you; now sell that same item on to the person in need at such-and-such a price."

See what Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

ولو اشتري من إنسان كراً بعينه ودفع غرائره وأمره بأن يكيل فيها ففعل صار قابضاً سواء كان المشتري حاضراً أو غائباً لأن المعقود عليه معين وقد ملكه المشتري بنفس العقد فصح أمر المشتري لأنه تناول عيناً هو ملكه فصح أمره وصار البائع وكيلاً له وصارت يده يد المشتري. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/۲٤۷، فصل في حكم البيع، سعيد).

No objection can be raised here that one and the same person cannot be both seller and buyer, because the shopkeeper is not the buyer or an agent to purchase, but an agent to take possession.

(2) The second way in which it may be permissible is that the buyer himself be appointed as the company's agent to take possession. Against this the objection arises that the buyer's first possession, as an agent, is possession as a trust, and after that comes possession by purchase, which is also called possession by exchange and possession under liability; and in the view of the jurists possession as a trust cannot take the place of possession under liability.

The answer: There is nothing unlawful in possession as a trust taking the place of possession under liability. The meaning of the jurists' statements is rather that if the item is destroyed before the buyer deals with it, he does not owe compensation; instead the loss is the company's. Once he deals with it, however, possession as a trust automatically becomes possession under liability and the buyer becomes responsible.

As for stipulating that if the debt is not paid within three months a certain percentage will be charged on top, this is an interest-based dealing; and a loan is not rendered defective by a defective condition.

See what "al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq" states:

وما لا يبطل بالشرط الفاسد القرض

بأن قال أقرضتك هذه المئة بشرط أن تخدمني شهراً مثلاً فإنه لا يبطل بهذا الشرط، وذلك لأن الشروط الفاسدة من باب الربا وأنه يختص بالمبادلة المالية، وهذه العقود كلها ليست بمعاوضة مالية فلا تؤثر فيها الشروط الفاسدة، ذكره العيني

وفي البزازية: وتعليق القرض حرام والشرط لا يلزم. (البحر الرائق: ٦/۱۸۷، باب المتفرقات من كتاب البيوع، كوئته).

وكذا في تبيين الحقائق: ٤/۱۳۳. والفتاوى البزازية على هامش الفتاوى الهندية: ٤/٤۲٦).

So one should make every effort to pay within three months; if he does not pay within three months, he incurs the grave sin of paying interest. If, however, such an agreement has been entered into by mistake, the buyer must keep clear of interest, and it is necessary for him to pay the money within the three months. That is to say, the shopkeeper should avoid collecting any interest, and if there is an interest-based dealing between the buyer and the company, the shopkeeper should stay completely clear of collecting it.

It is also possible for the company to appoint as its agent to take possession someone other than the seller and the buyer.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
