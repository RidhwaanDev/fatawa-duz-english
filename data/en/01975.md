### TITLE
An objection raised against current Islamic banking and its answer

### QUESTION
Some people object to the following bank transaction:

A man wants to buy a car but does not have the whole amount with him. The interest-free bank therefore buys the car itself and hires it out to the customer for a long term — say from three to five years. When fixing the rent the bank keeps in view that within the three years it should recover its cost together with some profit. After that it sells the car to the hirer for a nominal price, or gives it to him as a gift without any price.

Some people raise objections to this transaction. One of them is that, since the car is sold or gifted to the hirer after the hire, this is ṣafqah fī ṣafqah (one transaction inside another), which is forbidden in a hadith; is such a transaction therefore impermissible?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, because it has become the established custom, there is room for a contract of "صفقة في صفقة". Because of people's customary dealing and usage the contract will not become defective, since customary dealing is attached to consensus.

A second answer is that in the paperwork the two contracts are made separately, and for that reason it is permissible.

The various definitions of "صفقة في صفقة":

قال الامام الترمذي رحمه الله: وقد فسر بعض أهل العلم قالوا: بيعتين في بيعة:

(۱) أن يقول: أبيعك هذا الثوب بنقد بعشرة وبنسيئة بعشرين، ولا يفارقه على أحد البيعين، فإذا فارقه على أحدهما، فلا بأس، إذا كانت العقدة على واحد منهما، قال الشافعي رحمه الله: ومن معني ما نهي النبي ﷺ عن بيعتين في بيعة:

(۲) أن يقول: أبيعك داري هذه بكذا، على أن تبيعني غلامك بكذا، فإذا وجب لي غلامك وجبت لك داري، وهذا تفارق عن بيع بغير ثمن معلوم، ولا يدري كل واحد منهما على ما وقعت عليه صفقته. (ترمذي شريف: ۱/۲۳۳، باب ما جاء في النهي عن بيعتين في بيعة).

Ḥakīm al-Ummah Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

"قال في نور الأنوار: وتعامل الناس ملحق بالإجماع وفيه ثم إجماع من بعدهم أي بعد الصحابة رضي الله عنهم من أهل كل عصر".

From this we learn that customary dealing, like consensus, is not confined to any particular age. What is a pillar of consensus, however, must be present in it as well — that is, the scholars of that time must not object to it. In this way the jurists have argued from customary dealing for the permissibility of many new particular cases.

(2) If someone sells the trees after the fruit on them has become fit for use, Imam Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him) says about leaving the fruit on the trees that it is permissible to let it stay on them, and his evidence is custom, usage and juristic preference. See:

(3) Al-Muḥīṭ al-Burhānī states:

ولو قال: أعتق عبدك عني بألف درهم، فأعتق، فإنه يعتق عن الآمر ويلزمه المال عن الآمر استحساناً. (المحيط البرهاني: ٤/۲۸۱، كتاب العتاق، فصل في المتفرقات، رشيدية).

وفي البدائع: ولو قال لا خر: اعتق عبدك عني على ألف درهم فأعتق فالولاء للآمر لأن العتق يقع عنه استحساناً

أن الأمر بالفعل أمر بما لا وجود للفعل بدونه كالأمر بصعود السطح يكون أمراً بنصب السلم والأمر بالصلاة يكون أمراً بالطهارة ونحو ذلك ولا وجود للعتق عن الآمر بدون ثبوت الملك فكان أمر المالك بإعتاق عبده عنه بالبدل المذكور أمراً بتمليكه منه بذلك البدل ثم بإعتاقه عنه تصحيحاً لتصرفه كأنه صرح بذلك فقال: بعه مني واعتقه عني ففعل

لأن الملك في البيع الصحيح لا يقف على القبض، بل يثبت بنفس العقد فصار المأمور بائعاً عبده منه بالبدل المذكور ثم معتقاً عنه بأمره وتوكيله. (بدائع الصنائع، كتاب الولاء، ٤/۱٦۰، سعيد).

وفي درر الحكام: كأنه قال: بع عبدك عني بألف وكن وكيلي بالإعتاق. (درر الحكام شرح غرر الأحكام، أكثر مدة الحمل، ٤/٤۳۳).

In short, this contract — that is, an agency to set a slave free — contains within it an agency to sell, which is a case of one transaction inside another; yet in spite of this the jurists have ruled it permissible on the ground of juristic preference.

(4) The author of al-Hidāyah says:

وكذا لو سلطا المرتهن على بيعه لأنه توكيل بالبيع وهما يملكانه. (الهداية: ٤/۵۲۹، كتاب الرهن).

In the passage above there is an agency to sell within a contract of pledge, and the jurists have ruled it permissible.

(5) A sale made on the condition of free servicing is permissible, and that is a hire stipulated within a sale. Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muftī Taqī Ṣāḥib says:

If a condition contrary to what the contract requires is attached to a contract of sale, but in the custom of traders that condition counts as part of the contract, then attaching such a condition is permissible. For example, many goods are sold in the market today where the seller says that he will service the item free for one year. It is obvious that providing this free service is not part of what the contract itself requires, but because it has become customary the condition is permissible. So if the buyer stipulates, "I am buying it on condition that you service it free for a year," the contract will not become defective because of that condition. (تقریر ترمذی: ۱/۱۰۸).

Note these words of Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) about "صفقة في صفقة":

Question: In the light of the apparent meaning of

"نهى عن صفقة في صفقة"

some things seem impermissible even though they are very widespread among people of every sort. For example, repairing a watch, where the broken part is taken out and a sound part fitted: the part is a sale and the fitting is a hire. (2) Having a bed made without supplying the string oneself: the string is a sale and the weaving is a hire. (3) Taking water from a water-carrier: once he has drawn the water from the well into his own vessels it becomes his property, so there is a sale of the water and a hire for bringing it, and it is also a sale of what one does not have. (4) Giving jewellery or a ring to be set: the stones are a sale and the setting is a hire, و غیر ذلک من المعاملات الرائجۃ.

Answer: Because of customary dealing, which is widespread with no one objecting to it and is a kind of consensus, all these transactions are permissible. So the general text is a general ruling from which certain cases have been excluded, just as the jurists allowed it in dyeing and tailoring, where the dye and the thread belong to the craftsman and there is a hire in it as well. و ہذا ظاہر جداً فقط، واللہ اعلم ۔ (امداد الفتاوی: ۳/٦۳، ٦٤).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muftī Taqī Ṣāḥib says:

The position of the Ḥanafīs is that in ordinary circumstances attaching a condition to a contract makes the contract defective. There are, however, three kinds of condition that are permissible and do not make the contract defective: first, a condition that agrees with what the contract requires; second, a condition that suits the contract, such as a condition of pledge, of guarantee, or of transfer of a debt; and third, a condition that has become established by custom and usage.

If, in a sale with a right of redemption, the condition of redemption is made within the body of the contract, some Ḥanafī jurists have ruled this permissible too, and the author of al-Nihāyah gave a fatwa to that effect. ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) reported from ʿAllāmah Zaylaʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) that its meaning is this: the sale will be valid and it will be lawful for the buyer to benefit from the item; but since the sale carries the condition that whenever the seller returns the price the buyer must sell it back to him, it will not be permissible for the buyer to sell the item on to anyone else. Zaylaʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) held this to be the view on which fatwa is given, and ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) reports from al-Nahr that in our lands practice follows this view which Zaylaʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) preferred. He then says that this ruling of permissibility is perhaps based on the fact that the condition has become customary. Most Ḥanafī jurists, however, did not rule permissible the case in which the condition of redemption is made within the body of the contract. Muftī Taqī Ṣāḥib then set out evidence from Shāmī, al-Muḥīṭ al-Burhānī, Fatāwā Qāḍīkhān, Jāmiʿ al-Fuṣūlayn, Sharḥ al-Majallah and other books of fiqh. See: (غیر سودی بینکاری، ص ۲٤۲ ۔ ۲٤۷).

The second way in which "صفقة في صفقة" is permissible:

If the two contracts are made separately, the transaction will still be valid.

(That is, as long as one contract is not a condition for the other, the transaction is sound.)

The author of al-Hidāyah has mentioned a few examples:

(1) If someone says, "I sell five books for two hundred rand," and the buyer says, "I have bought three books for 120 rand," this is not valid, because in that case the seller would need to make two contracts for a single item sold. But if the seller says, "I sell five books for two hundred rand, and I sell each book for forty rand," and the buyer buys three books, this will be permissible, because the wording has been repeated.

Al-Hidāyah states:

وليس له أن يقبل في بعض المبيع ولا أن يقبل المشترى ببعض الثمن لعدم رضاء الآخر بتفرق الصفقة إلا إذا بين كل واحد لأنه صفقات معني. (الهداية: ۳/۱۹).

وفي الكفاية: قوله وليس له أن يقبل في بعض المبيع، وإذا أوجب البائع البيع في شيئين أو ثلاثة وأراد المشتري أن يقبل العقد في أحدهما دون الآخر فهذا على وجهين إن كانت الصفقة واحدة فليس له ذلك وإن كانت متفرقة فله ذلك وهذا لأن الصفقة إذا كانت واحدة فالمشتري بقبول العقد في أحدهما يريد تفريق الصفقة على البائع وفي ذلك ضرر بالبائع لأن العادة فيما بين الناس أنهم يضمون الرديء إلى الجيد في البياعات وينقصون شيئاً عن ثمن الجيد لترويج الرديء بالجيد فلو ثبت خيار قبول العقد في أحدهما فالمشتري يقبل العقد في الجيد ويترك الرديء على البائع فيزول الجيد عن ملك البائع بأقل من ثمنه وفيه ضرر بالبائع وقال القدوري رحمه الله: إلا أن يرضي البائع في المجلس نحو أن يقول بعتك هذا العبد بخمسين فيقول المشتري قبلت في نصفه فيرضي به البائع

ويكون ذلك من المشتري في الحقيقة استئناف إيجاب لا قبولاً فإذا رضي به البائع في المجلس يصح. (الكفاية: ۵/٤٦۲، رشيدية).

ʿAllāmah Ibn al-Humām (may Allah have mercy on him), discussing this in Fatḥ al-Qadīr, has written that if the price is divided according to the parts of the goods it is permissible, since each share will be known; but if the division is by value — for example, the contract is made over two slaves or two garments — it is not permissible, because of the uncertainty. If the seller says "بعتك هذين العبدين هذا بمائة وهذا بمائة" and the buyer accepts one of them, he has ruled it permissible in some instances. In al-Jāmiʿ al-Ṣaghīr it says that for it to be permissible the word of sale must be repeated, for example: "بعتك هذين العبدين بعتك هذا بمائة وبعتك هذا بمائة". Fatḥ al-Qadīr states:

إلا أن يرضي الآخر بذلك بعد قبوله في البعض ويكون المبيع مما ينقسم الثمن عليه بالأجزاء كعبد واحد أو مكيل أو موزون، فإن كان مما لا ينقسم إلا بالقيمة كثوبين وعبدين لا يجوز وإن قبل الآخر

فلوكان بين ثمن كل منهما فلا يخلو إما أن يكون بلا تكرار لفظ البيع أو بتكراره، ففيما إذا كرره فلاتفاق على أنه صفقتان فإذا قبل في أحدهما يصح مثل أن يقول بعتك هذين العبدين بعتك هذا بألف وبعتك هذا بألف أو اشتريت منك هذين العبدين اشتريت هذا بألف واشتريت هذا بألف كذا في موضع. (فتح القدير: ٦/۲۵۵، ۲۵۷، دار الفكر).

(2) If someone gives a money changer one dirham and says to him, "Give me copper coins for half of it and, for the other half, half a dirham less a grain," then in the view of the two companions the contract is permissible in the first half and impermissible in the second because of interest; while in the view of Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) the whole of it is void, because the contract is one, and when part of it is void the rest becomes defective as well, on account of the strength of the defect. But if he repeats the word "give" and says, "Give me copper coins for half of it, and give me half a dirham less a grain for the other half," then by agreement the contract is valid as far as the copper coins are concerned, because the word "give" has been repeated. Remember that a grain is 620 milligrams.

قال في الهداية: ومن أعطي صيرفياً درهماً وقال أعطني بنصفه فلوساً وبنصفه نصفاً إلا حبة جاز البيع في الفلوس وبطل فيما بقي عندهما وعلي قياس قول أبي حنيفة رحمه الله بطل في الكل ولو كرر لفظ الإعطاء بأن قال: أعطني بنصفه كذا فلساً وأعطني بنصفه الباقي نصفاً إلا حبة فالحكم أن العقد في حصة الفلوس جائز بالإجماع هو الصحيح. (الهداية مع الحاشية: ۳/ ۱۱۰، ۱۱۱).

The answer to the hadith:

There are three answers to this hadith. The first is that the prohibition was based on custom, and when the custom changed the ruling changed with it. The second is that it is a general text from which certain cases have been excluded. The third is that here two separate contracts are made. Of these answers, the first two need explaining.

The explanation is that texts are of three kinds. (1) The first kind: those hadiths that are based on custom. The rule for these is that when the custom changes, the ruling of the text changes. For example, wheat and barley were known as goods sold by volume, and this was the custom until some time ago; now the custom has changed and both are sold by weight, so they will now be counted as goods sold by weight.

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) and Shaykh Muṣṭafā al-Zarqā have explained this further. Note their words:

وعن الثاني اعتبار العرف مطلقاً ورجحه الكمال وخرج عليه سعدي أفندي استقراض الدراهم عدداً وبيع الدقيق وزناً في زماننا يعني بمثله وفي الكافي الفتوى على عادة الناس بحر، وأقره المصنف. (قوله مطلقاً) أي وإن كان خلاف النص، لأن النص على ذلك الكيل في الشيء أو الوزن فيه ما كان في ذلك الوقت إلا لأن العادة إذ ذاك كذلك وقد تبدلت فتبدل الحكم

(قوله ورجحه الكمال) حيث قال عقب ما ذكرنا: ولا يخفي أن هذا لا يلزم أبا يوسف رحمه الله لأن قصاراه أنه كنصه على ذلك وهو يقول: يصار إلى العرف الطارئ بعد النص بناء أن تغير العادة يستلزم تغير النص، حتى لوكان ﷺ حياً نص عليه وتمامه فيه،

وملخصه: أن النص معلول بالعرف فيكون المعتبر هو العرف في أي زمن كان ولا يخفي أن هذا فيه تقوية لقول أبي يوسف رحمه الله فافهم. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۵/۱۷٦، مطلب في أن النص أقوى من العرف، سعيد. وفتح القدير: ۷/۱۵، دار الفكر).

وقال مصطفى أحمد الزرقا: خلافاً لا بي يوسف رحمه الله الذي يعتبر المقياس المتعارف فيهما مطلقاً في كل زمن بحسبه، ويتبدل مقياس التساوي بتغير العرف تبعاً له حيث يعلل النص بالعرف الذي كان قائماً وقت وروده، فلا يكون اتباع العرف عند أبي يوسف رحمه الله مخالفاً للنص، بل يراه هو الموافق للنص، وأن الثبات على المقياس القديم الذي ورد في النص هو المخالف للنص، فهو يعتبر هذا النص نصاً عرفياً، بمعني أنه ذكر فيه المقياس الذي عينه النص، لأنه كان هو المتعارف حين وروده النص، ولوكان المتعارف مقياساً آخر لورد النص بذلك الآخر، لأن مقاييس الكميات تتبع الأعراف، ولتنظر رسالة "نشر العرف فيما بني من الأحكام على العرف" لا بن عابدين رحمه الله. وقد أوضحت هذه المسألة في كتابي المدخل الفقهي العام. (حاشية شرح القواعد الفقهية، ص ۲۲۱، تحت القاعدة: "العادة محكمة").

The second example is that Rasūlullāh ﷺ said about a stray camel that one should not disturb it but leave it alone, since it has its own water-skin and its own hooves — that is, one should not take it up as lost property. Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), however, says that the hadith is based on the custom of that time. In later times the custom changed and people no longer left camels loose, so a man may take hold of a loose camel in order to protect it and return it to its owner. This detail in the hadiths of the Book of Lost Property is hidden from no student.

The third example is this: if a man comes upon a shepherd's sheep and goats and is in need of milk, and the shepherd is not there, he should call out three times; if the shepherd does not answer, he may draw as much milk as he needs and drink it. Likewise, if he comes to an orchard and does not find the owner, he should call out three times; if the owner does not answer, he may pick some fruit and eat it — but he should not carry it away in his lap. Besides other answers, the commentators on hadith have also given this answer: that this hadith reflects the custom and habit of that time, when a guest who came could take these things to the extent of his need. As ʿAllāmah ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

والثالث: أن ذلك محمول على ما إذا علم طيب نفوس أرباب الأموال بالعادة أو بغيرها. (عمدة القاري: ۹/۱۷٤، دار الحديث ملتان).

Muftī Taqī Ṣāḥib has written the same. See:

أن هذه المسائل تدور على العرف والعادة وكانت عادة أهل الحجاز والشام المسامحة في مثل هذا، بخلاف البلاد الأخرى. (تكملة فتح الملهم: ۲/٦۲۷).

Later the custom changed, and now a guest may not take these things without permission.

قال العلامة العيني رحمه الله: وقال جمهور العلماء وفقهاء الأمصار، ومنهم الأئمة أبو حنيفة رحمه الله ومالك رحمه الله والشافعي رحمه الله وأصحابهم: لا يجوز لأحد أن يأكل من بستان أحد ولا يشرب من لبن غنمه إلا بإذن صاحبه. (عمدة القاري: ۹/۱۷٤، دار الحديث ملتان).

(2) The second kind: those texts and hadiths in which something is forbidden because of an evil in the thing itself. Such a thing remains forbidden in every situation. Interest, gambling, and contracts involving uncertainty and deception belong to this class.

(3) The third kind: those hadiths in which something is forbidden because it leads to quarrels and disputes. If no quarrel arises, the prohibition does not remain and there will be room for the thing. One example of this is in Bukhārī, volume one, page 292: people used to sell fruit before it ripened, and sometimes various diseases would strike the fruit, so that the buyer would drag his feet over paying the price while the seller demanded the full amount. When disputes of this kind began to come before Rasūlullāh ﷺ, he said that his advice was that they should not sell before the fruit ripened. This shows that the prohibition of selling fruit on the understanding that it will be left on the tree is because quarrels arise, and that it was said by way of advice; it was not an absolute prohibition. One narration in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī reads:

واشتري نافع بن الحارث داراً للسجن بمكة من صفوان بن أمية على إن عمر رضي الله عنه رضي بالبيع فالبيع بيعه وإن لم يرض عمر رضي الله عنه فلصفوان أربع مائة دينار. (رواه البخاري: ۱/۳۲۷).

That is, Nāfiʿ ibn al-Ḥārith bought a house in Makkah from Ṣafwān ibn Umayyah to serve as a prison, on the condition that if ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) approved of it, the purchase would be his, and if he did not approve, it would be Nāfiʿ's own. This is a conditional sale and a case of one transaction inside another. The commentators have given several answers to it, but Ḥaḍrat Shāh Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Fayḍ al-Bārī:

وقد علمت أن الفساد إذا كان لا جل مخافة النزاع لا يسري إلى العقد إذا لم يرفع أمره إلى القضاء أما إذا كان لكونه معصية فيلزم حينئذٍ والمذكور في الحديث من النحو الأول. (فيض الباري: ۳/۲۲۳).

Commenting on this, Mawlānā Badr-e-ʿĀlam Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

وفي جامع الفصولين: من اشتري حزمة من الحطب له أن يشترط حمله إلى البيت وفي الهداية: أن ما تعارف الناس عليه من الشرائط تتحمل في البيوع. قلت: لأنه لا تفضي إلى النزاع. (۳/۲۲۳).

Imam Tirmidhī (may Allah have mercy on him) gave three explanations of one transaction inside another. I leave the third aside, since it consists of pure interest and is plainly void and unlawful.

The first explanation is that Zayd says, "This item is a hundred dirhams for cash and two hundred dirhams on two months' credit," and ʿAmr says, "I accept," without settling on one of the two. This transaction is now open to dispute: the buyer will say, "I shall pay two hundred after two months," while the seller says, "I want 100 dirhams now."

The second explanation is the one related from Imam Shāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him), which Shāh Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) mentioned in al-ʿUrf al-Shadhī:

نقل صاحب المشكاة عن الخطابي رحمه الله تفسير بيعتين في بيع مثل ما ذكر الترمذي رحمه الله عن الشافعي رحمه الله وهو المختار وهو تفسير أبي حنيفة رحمه الله في كتاب الآثار انتهى، ذكره في باب النهي عن بيعتين. (العرف الشذي على هامش الترمذي: ۱/۲۳٤)

The gist of this explanation is that Zayd says, "ʿAmr, I sell you such-and-such a house of mine for a hundred thousand rand, on condition that you sell me your special horse for fifty thousand." ʿAmr accepts. As it happens, ʿAmr's horse dies. Now Zayd says, "I will not hand over my house," or, "I will hand it over for a hundred and twenty thousand, since I did not get the horse I wanted." Plainly this is a quarrel and a dispute. But in those cases where by custom no dispute arises, or where no dispute arises in the cases mentioned, there will be room for the transaction.

In the books of legal theory and fiqh the wording "أعتق عبدك عني بألف درهم" occurs. It contains the purchase of the slave from the person addressed for a thousand and then an agency to set him free; but since there is no dispute in it, it is permissible.

In the same way, in those transactions which Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) declared permissible in answer to the questioner, no dispute arises in customary practice, and it was for that reason that he ruled them permissible.
