### TITLE
Turning the chest or the back towards the Kaʿbah during ṭawāf

### QUESTION
If a person turns his chest or his back towards the Sacred House during ṭawāf, does any defect necessarily arise in the ṭawāf or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
If the whole ṭawāf was performed with the chest or the back turned towards the Sacred House, the ṭawāf is strictly disliked and it must be repeated; if it is not repeated, an expiatory sacrifice will be necessary. If only part of the ṭawāf was performed in this way, that too is strictly disliked and that much must be repeated; if it is not repeated, however, an expiatory sacrifice will not be necessary — rather he should pay the amount of ṣadaqat al-fiṭr for each circuit, and if the whole comes to the value of a sacrifice he should reduce it somewhat. In future it is necessary to be careful in this matter.

Consider what Sharḥ al-Lubāb states:

وأما ما في الكبير من أنه ذكر في منسك الرومي عن السروجي وليس شيء من الطواف يجوز مع استقبال البيت إلا قبالة الحجر، انتهى. وهو غلط منه لأنه إنما ذكره السروجي عن الشافعية وقد صرح في الغاية ومنسك السنجاري، ولو استقبل البيت بوجهه وطاف معترضاً وجعل البيت عن يمينه ومشي القهقري أو مر معترضاً مستدبر البيت لا يبطل عندنا

ولا يخفى أن ما نقل عن السروجي يمكن حمله على ما يوافق المذهب بأن يقال: معني لا يجوز يحرم لتركه الواجب

والحاصل أن وجوب التيامن يفيد أن من أتي بخلافه من الصور المذكورة المخالفة للتيامن في الهيئة والكيفية يحرم عليه فعله ويجب عليه الإعادة أو لزوم الجزاء. (شرح اللباب، ص۱٦۹، فصل في واجبات الطواف).

Ghunyat al-Nāsik states:

فصل وأما محرماته

وأداء شيء من الطواف مع استقبال البيت، قيل إلا قبالة الحجر الأسود في ابتداء الطواف خاصة. (غنية الناسك، ص ٦۷).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states:

If he kept the Sacred House before his chest, or turned his back towards it, and performed the ṭawāf sideways — that is, walking towards his right or his left side — or performed it in any other such way, his ṭawāf will be valid, and in our view such a ṭawāf counts for coming out of iḥrām; but a penalty will be due from him for omitting a necessary act. In short, apart from beginning the ṭawāf from the right side, all the forms mentioned above with regard to posture and manner are forbidden for him to perform, and repeating that ṭawāf is obligatory upon him; if he does not repeat it, a penalty becomes due. That is, repeating it is obligatory as long as he is in Makkah Mukarramah; and if he did not repeat it and went back to his family, an expiatory sacrifice is now due from him. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 4/172, the necessary acts of ṭawāf). (Likewise in Zubdat al-Manāsik, p. 121).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh also states:

Two general rules concerning ṭawāf: (1) If an obligatory (or necessary) ṭawāf, or a voluntary one (sunnah or supererogatory), is performed in a way that renders the ṭawāf defective, a penalty — that is, an expiatory sacrifice or a charitable payment — becomes due; and if he repeats that ṭawāf, the penalty lapses in every case by agreement.

(2) In those cases in which an expiatory sacrifice is due for the whole ṭawāf, a sacrifice is likewise due when the greater part of the ṭawāf is involved, because the greater part takes the place of the whole; and where a lesser part is involved, a charitable payment is due, since the violation is lighter — except in the ṭawāf of the ʿumrah, in which much and little carry the same ruling. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 4/538).

Note: Tuḥfah-yi Ḥujjāj, quoting Majmūʿah-yi Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi, records that if part of the ṭawāf was performed with the chest or back turned towards the Kaʿbah, the person is sinful and repentance is obligatory, but nothing further is due. (Tuḥfah-yi Ḥujjāj, p. 68, quoting Majmūʿah-yi Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi, unpublished).

In our own view, however, in the light of the passage of Sharḥ al-Lubāb quoted above and of the two rules just mentioned, the amount of ṣadaqat al-fiṭr ought to be due. This statement of Tuḥfah-yi Ḥujjāj is therefore, on the face of it, not something we can follow.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
