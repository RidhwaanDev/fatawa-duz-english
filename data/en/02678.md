### TITLE
The ruling on a watch with gold parts

### QUESTION
If someone has a watch whose strap has gold mixed into it, or some of whose parts are made of gold, will the use of such a watch be permissible or not?

### ANSWER
It is impermissible for men to use a watch made entirely of gold. But if the watch's strap has gold mixed into it, or some of its parts are of gold and the gold is the subordinate element, then its use will be permitted.

It comes in the noble hadith that when the Prophet's ﷺ blessed cup broke, he joined the broken place with silver. See what Bukhārī Sharīf states:

عن أنس بن مالك رضي الله عنه أن قدح النبي ﷺ انكسر فاتخذ مكان الشعب سلسلة من فضة قال عاصم: رأيت القدح وشربت فيه. (رواه البخاري، رقم: ۳۱۰۹).

وفي رواية قال: قدحاً كان للنبي ﷺ فيه ضبة فضة. (أخرجه الامام أحمد، رقم: ۱۲٤۱۱).

See what Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

The use of a gold watch will not be permissible at all, and most probably the use of a watch whose case is of silver and is plainly visible will not be free of dislikedness either, because the jurists declare the use of silver disliked in other articles of use as well, not only in the ring:

وكذا الأكل بملعقة الذهب والفضة وكذا الاكتحال بميل الذهب والفضة. (خلاصة الفتاوى: ٤ /۳۷۱، كتاب الكراهية)

However, if some part of it is of gold, it will be correct — and in the case of silver all the more so. Thus the jurists write:

ولا بأس بمسامير الذهب والفضة. (الهندية: ۵/۳۳۵)

In the same way, if the inner mechanism is made of gold or silver while the outer case is of iron, that too will be permitted. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/316).

Āp ke Masāʾil states:

Question: There are gold parts fitted in a Rado watch; likewise a watch may have platinum gold fitted in it, which is a metal twice as costly as gold. Is it permissible to wear such a watch?

Answer: If there is gold in it, it is not used directly but only as something subordinate to the watch, so it is permissible. (Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal: 8/378, new edition).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

In these imported watches whose cases are made of gold or silver, the other metals are predominant and the gold or silver subordinate; so they do not come under the ruling of gold and silver but, like ordinary metals, fall under goods and possessions (صرح بہ الہدایہ و غیرہا). Their use is therefore permissible for men, and zakat is not due on them as it is on gold and silver. If, however, the watches are held for trade, then zakat will be due on them as on ordinary trade goods. As for the nib of a fountain pen, that too is most probably not of real gold, so it is permissible. (Imdād al-Muftīn: 2/815, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
