### TITLE
The ruling on writing the ṣalawāt when a person's name is Ghulām Muḥammad

### QUESTION
If a man's name is Muḥammad Ghulām, the ṣalawāt (the invocation of blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ) is neither written nor recited after the word Muḥammad, because the one meant here is not Rasūlullāh ﷺ but the man who bears the name. But if a man's name is Ghulām Muḥammad, then Muḥammad here does mean Rasūlullāh ﷺ; so why is the ṣalawāt not written in this case either? Similarly, when one says Durūd-e Ibrāhīmī, "peace be upon him" is not added with it; but when one says Durūd-e Ibrāhīm, why is "peace be upon him" not said with it?

### ANSWER
When the name of the Messenger ﷺ forms part of the name of a member of his ummah, the ṣalawāt is not said after it. If a man's name is Muḥammad Karīm, the ṣalawāt is not said, because someone else is meant by it and not Rasūlullāh ﷺ. And where the name is a possessive construction — for instance, if a man's name is Khādim al-Rasūl or Muṭīʿ al-Nabī — the ṣalawāt is still not said. First, because if it were written or recited, the impression might arise that it belongs to the first part of the construction. Second, because such a name is used night and day, and to say the ṣalawāt every time would be burdensome. Third, because in the best generations such possessive names were used and there was no need for the ṣalawāt — as with Āl al-Nabī, Āl Muḥammad and the like.

The ṣalawāt is not written because of the misapprehension that it might be taken to attach to someone other than the Messenger ﷺ. Consider the following evidence for this:

لا يجوز الصلاة على غير النبي لان الصلاة على غير الأنبياء قد صارت من شعار أهل الأهواء يصلون على من يعتقدون فيهم فلا يقتدي بهم في ذلك. (تفسير ابن كثير: ٦/٤۷۸، سورة الأحزاب: ۵٦).
