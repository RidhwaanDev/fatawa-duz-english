### TITLE
A second view on the journey of a menstruating woman

### QUESTION
A second view on the journey of a menstruating woman.

### ANSWER
On the question just discussed, one view is that the journey is reckoned from the place where she became pure, as was mentioned above. Other jurists, however, have written that what counts is the point at which the journey began, not the place where she became pure; on that view the woman in question will shorten her prayers in Durban. Consider what ʿAllāmah Shurunbulālī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

قال في مختصر الظهيرية: الحائض إذا طهرت وبينها وبين المقصد أقل من ثلاثة أيام تصلي أربعاً هو الصحيح، قلت: ولا يخفي أنها لا تنزل عن رتبة الذي أسلم فكان حقها القصر مثله. (حاشية الشرنبلالية على الدرر: ۱/۱۳۲).

وقال في المحيط البرهاني: وفي متفرقات الفقيه أبي جعفر

وأما الحائض إذا طهرت في بعض الطريق قصرت الصلاة، لأنها مخاطبة. (المحيط البرهاني: ۲/٤۱).

Al-Jawharah al-Nayyirah states:

(قوله والحيض يسقط عن الحائض والصلاة) فيه إشارة إلى أنها وجبت عليها الصلاة ثم سقطت وهذه المسألة اختلف فيها الأصوليون وهي أن الأحكام هل هي ثابتة على الصبي والمجنون والحائض أم لا؟ فاختار أبو زيد الدبوسي أنها ثابتة والسقوط بعذر الحرج، قال: لأن الآدمي أهل لوجوب الحقوق عليه ألا تري أن عليه عشر أرضه وخراجها بالإجماع و عليه الزكاة عند الشافعي وكلام الشيخ بناء على هذا. (الجوهرة النيرة: ۱/۳٤، إمدادية ملتان).

(وكذا في البناية شرح الهداية: ۱/٦۳٤، مكتبه رشيديه، كوئٹه).

قال في الشامية: وأجاب في نهج النجاة بأن مانعها سماوي بخلافه، أي وإن كان كل منهما من أهل النية بخلاف الصبي، لكن منعها من الصلاة ما ليس بصنعها فلغت نيتها من الأول، بخلاف الكافر فإنه قادر على إزالة المانع من الابتداء فصحت نيته. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۱۳٥، سعيد).

From the passages of these works of fiqh — Ḥāshiyah Shurunbulāliyyah, the Mutafarriqāt of al-Faqīh Abū Jaʿfar, al-Jawharah and others — it emerges that a menstruating woman's journey begins from her own town and not from the moment she becomes pure. This view is evidently the easier and the better one, because a menstruating woman and a non-Muslim are both of age and legally accountable, and so both are competent to form the intention of travel.

As for what ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) quotes from Nahj al-Najāh — that a menstruating woman's excuse is an impediment sent from heaven, so that by Allah's decree she is not qualified to pray and her intention of praying is void — the answer is that the intention meant here is not the intention to pray, which would indeed be void, but the intention to cover the distance, and in this a non-Muslim and a menstruating woman are equal. Besides, treating a menstruating woman as a traveller from the beginning of the journey is easier and more reasonable. The intention of a child, by contrast, is defective because he has not reached puberty, and so the rulings of travel begin for him from the time he reaches puberty.

To put it another way, a child was not legally accountable and has only now become accountable, whereas a non-Muslim and a menstruating woman are both of age; it is true that neither of them is accountable for prayer, but both are competent to form the intention of travel. Moreover, the very fact that a menstruating woman's excuse comes from heaven and that she is not at fault also calls for a menstruating woman and a woman in post-natal bleeding to be treated as travellers from the beginning of the journey, so that they are given the concession of shortening the prayer and of leaving out the emphasised sunnahs, and are not burdened with praying in full. This would amount to a kind of juristic preference.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
