### TITLE
The ruling on a recreational game

### QUESTION
Some brothers of our area — among whom were also the imāms of the mosque, those in charge, and men of the Tablīgh — all joined together and arranged a breakfast in a playing field for the Muslim men of our settlement. The purpose of it was to create love among the people. Everyone bought a ticket so that the cost of the food and so forth could be met, and to inform people of this programme an announcement was put up on the notice board of the mosque. The children also played football and cricket, wearing clothing that covered the ʿawrah of the body. Is such a programme permissible or not, given that the purpose is only to create love and to strengthen the bond of the public with the mosque? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
If the local scholars and religious people see no harm in it and consider there to be a benefit in it — that the bond of the public with the mosque will grow stronger and there will be advantage in it — and if the limits of the Sacred Law are adhered to, then such a game and programme will be permissible and correct in the Sacred Law.

As for the matter of those attending having to buy a ticket, the ruling on it is this: an invitation to a meal is a gratuity, and a condition of a ticket has been laid down for it, while every person is free in his own choice as to taking that ticket. Hence this transaction comes under the ruling of a conditional gratuity (tabarruʿ mashrūṭ) and will be permissible and correct.

In Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah it is said:

In cricket, hockey and the like there is permission in view of a sound purpose; all of these are not simply forbidden. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 19/534, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

Those games from which some religious or worldly benefits may be obtained are permissible, provided they are played with the intention of those very benefits and there is no intention of mere sport and amusement. But it is not permissible to stipulate any payment or prize upon the outcome of the contest.

For example, the game of ball, from which bodily exercise is obtained; or games with the staff and the like; or the wrestling of wrestlers and so forth, which can assist in the strength needed for jihād. In the same way riddle-contests, poetry-contests, educational cards and the like — to hold a contest of winning and losing is permissible, but to fix any sum as payment upon it is not permissible; rather, it is unlawful. (Jawāhir al-Fiqh: 2/357, "Beneficial Games").

In Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah it is said:

If it is played for exercise, for training in jihād and for maintaining good health, it is correct; but covering the ʿawrah and observing the other limits of the Sacred Law is necessary, and no disruption should occur, through absorption in it, in the injunctions of the Sacred Law — the prayer, the congregation, and so forth. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 19/533, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).
