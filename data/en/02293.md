### TITLE
The ruling on taking impermissible assets as a pledge

### QUESTION
If a bank took shares as a pledge, and these shares are shares in an impermissible company, answers are sought to the following questions:

(1) May Al Baraka Bank take such impermissible shares as a pledge or not?

(2) If the debtor did not repay the loan, may Al Baraka Bank sell these impermissible shares?

(3) Is the profit obtained from these impermissible shares impermissible for the pledgee as well? بینوا و لکم الاجر الجزیل ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, shares that are wholly impermissible must be given away in charity; it is not sound, for either the pledgor or the pledgee, to take them as a pledge or to derive benefit from them.

Sharḥ al-Majallah states:

المادة: ۷۰۹ ــ يشترط أن يكون المرهون صالحاً للبيع فيلزم أن يكون موجوداً مالاً متقوماً مقدور التسليم في وقت الرهن. فلا يجوز رهن ما ليس موجوداً وقت العقد

ولا رهن الميتة والدم، لانعدام ماليتهما، ولا رهن صيد الحرم والإحرام،

ولا رهن الخمر والخنزير من مسلم سواء كان العاقدان مسلمين أو أحدهما مسلماً، لانعدام مالية الخمر والخنزير في حق المسلم. (شرح المجلة: ۳/۱٤۲، لمحمد خالد الأتاسي).

The upshot of this passage is that things which are not valued property and are not fit to be used by a Muslim may not be taken as a pledge either. Impermissible assets and assets acquired through interest are all unfit for a Muslim to benefit from; rather they must be given away in charity. There is therefore no room for taking them as a pledge.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
