### TITLE
The ruling on fasting on the fifteenth of Shaʿbān

### QUESTION
Is fasting on the fifteenth of Shaʿbān recommended or not? If it is, from where is it established?

### ANSWER
Keeping a fast on the fifteenth of Shaʿbān is recommended, and it is established by hadith. The hadith is weak, but there is room to act upon a weak hadith in matters of merit so long as one does not hold the act to be a sunnah. The details of the conditions for acting upon a weak hadith may be seen in the treatise

"الجزء اللطيف في الاستدلال بالحديث الضعيف"

Consider this hadith:

عن علي بن أبي طالب رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: "إذا كانت ليلة النصف من شعبان فقوموا ليلها وصوموا نهارها، فإن الله ينزل فيها لغروب الشمس إلى سماء الدنيا، فيقول: ألا من مستغفر لي فأغفر له، ألا مسترزق فأرزقه، ألا مبتلي فأعافيه ألا كذا، ألا كذا حتى يطلع الفجر. (رواه ابن ماجة: ص ۹۹، باب ما جاء في ليلة النصف من شعبان. والبيهقي في شعب الإيمان: ۳/۳۷۸/۳۸۲۲، ما جاء في ليلة النصف من شعبان).

In the whole treasury of hadith there is only this one hadith from which the fast of the fifteenth of Shaʿbān is derived. The hadith scholars have said a great deal about this hadith and have even written independent treatises on it; yet for all that, it cannot be called a fabrication.

Consider what the eminent hadith scholar Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ḥabīb al-Raḥmān al-Aʿẓamī says:

The hadith reported in Ibn Mājah about the fast of the fifteenth of Shaʿbān is not a fabrication; no expert in hadith has called it a fabrication. To argue from the wording of Tuḥfat al-Aḥwadhī that this hadith is fabricated is sheer ignorance. Among the narrators of this hadith there is indeed Abū Bakr ibn Abī Sabrah, and it is certainly said of him that he used to invent hadiths; but that does not establish that the hadith under discussion is one of his inventions and is fabricated. It is not permissible to declare a hadith fabricated merely because the chain contains some narrator who used to invent hadiths. All that follows from this is that the hadith is weak in its chain. (Majallah al-Maʾāthir, pp. 69–70, 1995 CE).

He has also firmly refuted those who call it a fabrication. For further detail see: (Majallah al-Maʾāthir, pp. 68–74, 1995 CE).

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

المرغوبات من الصيام أنواع أولها صوم المحرم، والثاني صوم رجب، والثالث صوم شعبان

(الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۲۰۲).

Islāmī Fiqh states:

Keeping a fast on the fifteenth of Shaʿbān, and keeping six fasts in the month of Shawwāl, are also sunnah. Fasting on the fifteenth of Shaʿbān, worshipping on the fifteenth night, and going to the graveyard and praying for the forgiveness of the dead are all mentioned in the hadith. (Islāmī Fiqh: 1/417).

Muftī Taqī Ṣāḥib writes: the explicit statements of the scholars on the recommendedness of the fast of the fifteenth of Shaʿbān are as follows.

The Hanafi scholars:

• The Sage of the Community and Reviver of the Faith, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may his secret be sanctified), says: Keeping a fast on the fifteenth of Shaʿbān is recommended. (Zawāl al-Sunnah: p. 10).

• The Grand Muftī of Pakistan, Muftī Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him), also counted it among the sunnah practices of the fifteenth of Shaʿbān — that is, fasting on that day is recommended.

• ʿAllāmah Quṭb al-Dīn, the hadith scholar of Delhi (may Allah have mercy on him), also counted the fast of the fifteenth of Shaʿbān in the chapter on optional fasting in his commentary on Mishkāt Sharīf, Maẓāhir-e Ḥaqq: 2/364.

The Mālikī scholars:

وندب صوم يوم النصف من شعبان (كذا في شرح الصغير علي أقرب المسالك للشيخ الدردير المالكي. (۱/٦۹۲، باب الصوم).

That is, Shaykh al-Dardīr al-Mālikī (may Allah have mercy on him) held the fast of the fifteenth of Shaʿbān to be recommended.

The Ḥanbalī scholars:

• Shaykh al-Mardāwī al-Ḥanbalī wrote in his book

"الانصاف"

that Shaykh Ibn al-Jawzī wrote in

"المستوعب"

that of the fasts of Shaʿbān the fast of the fifteenth of Shaʿbān is the most emphasised.

• Ibn Rajab al-Ḥanbalī (may Allah have mercy on him) also said that the fast of the fifteenth of Shaʿbān has been enjoined in particular. (Summarised from the treatise Shab-e Barāʾat kī Ḥaqīqat: pp. 74–77, by Muftī Taqī ʿUthmānī Ṣāḥib, may his shade endure).

• Furthermore, sound narrations exist concerning the three days of every month, that is the ayyām al-bīḍ, and on their basis too the fast of the fifteenth of Shaʿbān will be held recommended.

• ʿAllāmah Shurunbulālī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote in Marāqī al-Falāḥ that every fast for which a request and a promise of reward is established in the noble Sunnah is likewise recommended; and since a narration exists concerning the fast of the fifteenth of Shaʿbān, it will be recommended. Consider what Marāqī al-Falāḥ states:

ومنه (المندوبات) كل صوم ثبت طلبه والوعد عليه بالسنة الشريفة. (مراقي الفلاح: ص ۲۳۰).

The monthly Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, after discussing the above hadith in detail and setting out three reasons why it is not a fabrication, gave the following passage by way of summary. Consider it:

The upshot is that the fast of the fifteenth of Shaʿbān can neither be called obligatory or sunnah, nor can it be rejected outright as an innovation; rather it will be called recommended. (Monthly Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, p. 12, Shaʿbān 1434 AH corresponding to June 2013 CE).

See also: (Monthly Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband; and Mā Thabata bi'l-Sunnah, pp. 358–363, by Shaykh ʿAbd al-Ḥaqq, the hadith scholar of Delhi).
