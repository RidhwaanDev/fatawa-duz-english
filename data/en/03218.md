### TITLE
Research on a story from the childhood of Ḥaḍrat Dāniyāl (peace be upon him)

### QUESTION
I heard from a scholar an incident concerning Dāniyāl (peace be upon him): that when the qadis had given judgement that an innocent woman be stoned, and the king and everyone else were at their wits' end as to how to save her but could think of nothing, Dāniyāl (peace be upon him), who was a child at that time, proposed in a gathering of children a device by means of which the innocent woman was saved and the qadis were stoned. Is this story sound or not, and in which book is it found?

### ANSWER
This story has been mentioned by ʿAllāmah Ibn al-Qayyim (may Allah have mercy on him) (d. 751 AH) in his book al-Ṭuruq al-Ḥukmiyyah:

ثم حدثهم (علي رضي الله عنه): أن دانيال عليه السلام كان يتيماً، لا أب له ولا أم، وأن عجوزاً من بني إسرائيل ضمته وكفلته، وأن ملِكاً من ملوك بني إسرائيل كان له قاضيان. وكانت امرأة مهيبة جميلة، تأتي الملك فتناصحه وتقص عليه، وأن القاضيين عشقاها. فراوداها عن نفسها فأبت، فشهدا عليها عند الملك أنها بغت. فدخل الملك من ذلك أمر عظيم فاشتد غمه، وكان بها معجباً. فقال لهما: إن قولكما مقبول، وأجلها ثلاثة أيام، ثم ترجمونها. ونادي في البلد: احضروا رجم فلانة، فأكثر الناس في ذلك، وقال الملك لثقته: هل عندك من حيلة؟ فقال: ماذا عسى عندي؟ يعني وقد شهد عليها القاضيان. فخرج ذلك الرجل في اليوم الثالث، فإذا هو بغلمان يلعبون، وفيهم دانيال، وهو لا يعرفه، فقال دانيال: يا معشر الصبيان تعالوا حتى أكون أنا الملك، وأنت يا فلان المرأة العابدة، وفلان وفلان القاضيين الشاهدين عليها. ثم جمع تراباً وسيفاً من قصب، وقال للصبيان: خذوا بيد هذا القاضي إلى مكان كذا وكذا ففعلوا، ثم دعا الآخر فقال له: قل الحق، فإن لم تفعل قتلتك، بأي شيء تشهد؟ والوزير واقف ينظر ويسمع، فقال: أشهد أنها قد بغت، قال: متي؟، قال: في يوم كذا وكذا. قال: مع من؟ قال: مع فلان بن فلان. قال: في أي مكان؟ قال: في مكان كذا وكذا. قال: ردوه إلى مكانه، وهاتوا الآخر. فردوه إلى مكانه، وجاؤوا بالآخر، فقال: بأي شيء تشهد؟ قال: بغت. قال: متي؟ قال: يوم كذا وكذا. قال: مع من؟ قال: مع فلان بن فلان. قال: وأين؟ قال: في موضع كذا وكذا، فخالف صاحبه، فقال دانيال: الله أكبر، شهدا عليها والله بالزور، فاحضروا قتلهما. فذهب الثقة إلى الملك مبادراً، فأخبره الخبر، فبعث إلى القاضيين، ففرق بينهما، وفعل بهما ما فعل دانيال. فاختلفا كما اختلف الغلامان. فنادي الملك في الناس: أن احضروا قتل القاضيين، فقتلهما. (الطرق الحكمية في السياسة الشرعية: ۶۱).

Translation: Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him), after giving his judgement, related this story: Dāniyāl (peace be upon him) was an orphan and was being brought up by an old woman of the Children of Israel. The king of the Children of Israel in that age had two qadis. A beautiful woman used to come to the king; she would counsel him and relate various stories to him. Both the qadis had fallen in love with her. They tried to seduce her, but the woman refused; whereupon the two of them testified before the king that she had committed fornication. The king was greatly troubled and grieved to hear this, for he was very fond of that woman. In any case, the king said: Very well, your testimony is accepted; the woman is given three days' respite, after which she will be stoned. And he had it proclaimed throughout the city that all the townspeople should be present at the stoning of such-and-such a woman. Then the king summoned one of his trusted ministers and asked him: Have you any device for saving this woman's life? He replied: What device could I have, when two qadis have already testified against her? As it happened, on the third day the minister went out on some errand, and he saw some boys playing, and among them was Dāniyāl (peace be upon him). The minister did not recognise Dāniyāl (peace be upon him). Dāniyāl (peace be upon him) said: O boys, gather round; I shall be the king, and you shall be such-and-such a devout woman, and let so-and-so and so-and-so be the two qadis. Then he gathered some earth and also fashioned a sword of wood, and said to the boys: Take one of these qadis away to such-and-such a place. So they took him away. Then he called the other qadi and said to him: Tell the truth, or else you will be put to death. To what do you bear witness? (The minister was watching this whole affair.) The qadi said: I bear witness that this woman committed fornication. The king asked: When did she do it? The qadi said: On such-and-such a day. The king asked: With whom, and where did she do it? The qadi replied: With so-and-so, at such-and-such a place. The king said: Take him away and bring the other. So the first qadi was taken away and the second was brought forward, and the king put to him the same questions that he had put to the first. He gave answers entirely different from those of the first qadi. Dāniyāl (peace be upon him) raised the cry of takbīr and said: These two have borne false witness against this woman; so let all be present for the carrying out of their death sentence. The minister, who had been watching all this, went in haste to the king and related the whole affair to him. The king at once summoned the two qadis, separated them and questioned them, doing exactly as Dāniyāl (peace be upon him) had done. The two of them gave entirely different answers from one another. The king had it proclaimed among the people that all should be present for the carrying out of their death sentence; and so the two of them were put to death.

This story has likewise been mentioned by Dāwūd ibn ʿUmar al-Anṭākī, known as al-Akmah (d. 1008 AH), in his book تزيين الأسواق في أخبار العشاق (1/115), and by Zaynab bint ʿAlī (d. 1332 AH) in her book الدر المنثور في كتاب طبقات ربات الخدور (1/254); and they have written that this story is found in the Torah, in the Book of Daniel.
