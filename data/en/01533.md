### TITLE
Ruling on a ṭalāq written in a manner that is not customary

### QUESTION
Zayd was sitting on a chair with a table in front of him. On the table he wrote his wife's name and then wrote three ṭalāqs, saying in writing: "Three ṭalāqs to my wife Zaynab." Afterwards he said that there had been no quarrel between them and that he had not written it in anger; he was only joking. Has ṭalāq taken place or not?

### ANSWER
Writing a ṭalāq in a manner that is not customary does not bring a ṭalāq into effect unless it is intended; if it is written with the intention of ṭalāq, then the ṭalāq does take effect. In the case in question, since there was no intention of bringing a ṭalāq into effect by the words written in jest, no ṭalāq has taken place.

Radd al-Muḥtār states:

ومستبين غير مرسوم كالكتابة على الجدران وأوراق الأشجار أو على كاغذ لا على الوجه المعتاد فلا يكون حجة إلا بانضمام شيء آخر إليه كالنية. (فتاوى الشامي، مسائل شتي: ٦/۷۳۷).

Majmūʿah Qawānīn Islāmī states: A ṭalāq by a legible but non-customary writing will take effect only where the husband says that his intention was to divorce his wife. Radd al-Muḥtār states:

وإن كانت مستبينة لكنها غير مرسومة إن نوى الطلاق يقع وإلا لا.
(مجموعہ قوانین اسلامی، ص ۱۳۰) ۔
