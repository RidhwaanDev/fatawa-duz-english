### TITLE
Finding something valuable inside goods one has bought

### QUESTION
These days grain, foodstuffs and the like come packed by the companies and are sold in the shops. If, after buying such a bag, the purchaser finds something valuable in it — a gold coin, for example — whose will it be? Is it to be returned to the company, or does it belong to the seller, or may the purchaser keep it himself? بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
In the case described, the seller and the seller's own supplier should be asked about it. If they say they know nothing of it, or say that it is not theirs, then it comes under the ruling of lost property (luqṭah): the finder must look for the owner and hand it over to him. If the owner is not found, then the purchaser may use it if he is himself in need; otherwise he should give it away in charity.

Consider what Fatāwā Shāmī states:

وفي التاتارخانية عن الينابيع: اشترى داراً فوجد في بعض الجدار دراهم. قال أبوبكر: إنها كاللقطة. قال الفقيه: وإن ادعاه البائع رد عليه، وإن قال ليست لي فهي لقطة. (فتاوى الشامي: ٤/ ۲۸۵، كتاب اللقطة، سعيد).
