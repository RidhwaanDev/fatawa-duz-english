### TITLE
Accepting a gift out of unlawful wealth

### QUESTION
If interest money comes into the hands of a non-Muslim, does it enter his ownership or not? If a non-Muslim presents a gift out of interest money to a Muslim, is the Muslim permitted to accept it or not? And what is the ruling if a Muslim gives a gift out of interest money?

### ANSWER
If interest money comes to a Muslim, the Muslim does not become its owner; on the contrary, he must return it. If the owner of the money is known, it should be given back to him, and if the owner is not known it should be given away in charity without any intention of earning reward — it should be given away in charity in the sense of freeing oneself from a burden and a calamity. This money may then be given, permissibly and correctly, to a deserving poor or needy person who does not possess the zakat threshold. But accepting a gift out of interest money is not permissible or correct.

See what ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

لو رأي المكاس مثلاً يأخذ من أحد شيئاً من المكس ثم يعطيه آخر ثم يأخذ من ذلك الآخر آخر فهو حرام. (فتاوى الشامي: ۵/۹۸، مطلب الحرمة تتعدد، سعيد).

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) also says:

ويردونها على أربابها إن عرفوهم وإلا تصدقوا بها، لأن سبيل الكسب الخبيث التصدق إذا تعذر الرد على صاحبه. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۳۸۵، كتاب الكراهية، فصل في البيع، سعيد).

ʿĀlamgīrī states:

ولا يجوز قبول هدية أمراء الجور لأن الغالب في مالهم الحرمة إلا إذا علم أن أكثر ماله حلال بأن كان صاحب تجارة أو زرع فلا بأس به لأن أموال الناس لا تخلو عن قليل حرام فالمعتبر الغالب، وكذا أكل طعامهم كذا في الاختيار شرح المختار. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳٤۲).

Yes, if it is not known for certain that the wealth is unlawful, or it is mixed and the greater part of it is lawful earnings, then there will be room to accept the gift.

Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him), after quoting passages from the books of fiqh, says:

From these texts it is clear that where a person's income is mixed, lawful with unlawful, what counts is the greater part. If the greater part is lawful, it is permissible to accept his gift and his hospitality. But if, with regard to a particular gift or meal, it becomes known for certain that something unlawful is mixed into it, then accepting it is unlawful and eating it is unlawful too — though in Imam Abū Ḥanīfah's (may Allah have mercy on him) view there is mixing and absorption here, so on his view there is room for it, as will be seen from some of the reports. And if the greater part of the income is unlawful, then accepting a gift or hospitality is unlawful; unless, of course, he informs one that this particular food or gift is from lawful means, in which case it is permissible. (Imdād al-Aḥkām: 4/399).

For further detail see: (Shāmī: 5/98; Imdād al-Aḥkām: 4/397–400; and Fatāwā ʿUthmānī: 3/395).

If, however, interest money comes into a non-Muslim's hands, the non-Muslim becomes its owner and it will be permissible and correct to accept a gift from him, so long as he has not committed something that is forbidden and repugnant by reason itself, such as fraud, theft, robbery and the like. Yes, an Islamic government will not permit non-Muslims to deal in interest within the abode of Islam.

The real basis of this question is whether non-Muslims are addressed by the subsidiary laws of the sharia or not, and on this there is a difference of opinion among the jurists. Some scholars hold that in the first instance they are addressed only with regard to faith and punishments, and are not addressed with regard to transactions, while others say that non-Muslims are addressed with regard to transactions as well. In any case, non-Muslims do own the interest money; this is what appears to be correct.

For a detailed account of the evidence see: (Abwāb al-Ribā, chapter two, "Uses of interest money").
