### TITLE
Compelling the appointed imam to lead tarawih

### QUESTION
An imam refuses to lead the tarawih prayer, saying, "I have always led it elsewhere; I will arrange someone else for you." Can he be compelled to lead it? And if the imam says, "I alone will lead it," is that his right?

### ANSWER
The appointed imam has the greater right to lead tarawih. But if he has to lead it somewhere else, he will not be compelled. If, however, the people of the mosque ask him to lead the tarawih, he ought to agree, since this too is a form of leading the prayer.

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

واعلم أن صاحب البيت ومثله إمام المسجد الراتب أولى بالإمامة من غيره مطلقاً. (الدر المختار: ۱/٥٥۹، سعيد)

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states:

Question: Bakr has been appointed imam of a mosque and is a ḥāfiẓ of the Qur'an. Zayd is also a ḥāfiẓ, and he has been leading tarawih in that mosque for a long time. Bakr now says, "I have been appointed imam, so the right to lead tarawih belongs to me," while that ḥāfiẓ says, "It is my long-standing right." Whose right is it?

Answer: In the case described, once Bakr has been appointed imam, the right to lead tarawih belongs to him as well. (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: ٤/۲۸۲، دار الاشاعت)

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

The right to lead tarawih belongs to the imam. If he cannot lead it, or if he gives permission, it should be handed over to another ḥāfiẓ. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ٤/٤۲٥) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
