### TITLE
Donating blood

### QUESTION
In this country there is an organisation called the South African National Blood Service, which asks people of sound health to donate blood for the treatment of the sick. After the blood has been drawn it is stored in bottles and, as the need arises, is introduced into a patient's veins. This treatment is highly successful and there is a very great need for it nowadays. The organisation has therefore made a request that the guidance of the sharia be obtained concerning the donation of blood. So will such a donation of blood be permitted in the sharia or not?

### ANSWER
At a time of need and necessity it is permissible and sound to be treated with human blood, and from this it necessarily follows that there will also be permission to give blood at a time of need. Since blood is always needed in a hospital, donating blood is for this reason sound.

Fatāwā al-Shāmī states:

قال في النهاية وفي التهذيب يجوز للعليل شرب البول والدم والميتة للتداوي إذا أخبره طبيب مسلم أن فيه شفائه ولم يجد من المباح ما يقوم مقامه وإن قال الطبيب: يتعجل شفاؤك به فيه وجهان. (فتاوى الشامي: ۵/۲۲۸، سعيد). (وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳۵۵).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

To introduce one human being's blood into the body of another human being for the purpose of treatment, where his recovery by means of it is established according to a skilled physician, is permitted.

لم يبح الإرضاع بعد مدته لأنه جزء آدمي والانتفاع به لغير ضرورة حرام. (الدر المختار)

From the wording of the Durr al-Mukhtār we learn that to benefit from the parts of a human being without a need is forbidden — that is, if there is a need it can be permitted.

وهذا لأن الحرمة ساقطة عند الاستشفاء كحل الخمر والميتة لعطشان والجائع، رد المحتار

۔ (کفایت المفتی: ۹/۱۵٤، دار الاشاعت) ۔

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

At a time of severe necessity the operation of transfusing blood in order to save a life is permissible; but the buying and selling of blood is not permissible. If blood cannot be obtained free of charge and there is dire compulsion, then there is scope for buying it, and the seller will be sinful. (احسن الفتاوی: ۸/۲۷۳) ۔

See further: (جواہر الفقہ: ۷/٤٤ ۔ ۵۰), and (معارف القرآن: ۱/٤۲۰), and (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ٦/۲٦۲), and (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ۹/۱۷۵) ۔

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

Nowadays blood banks are established in place after place, where blood is regularly bought and sold. The scholars have permitted the transfusion of blood for treatment where there is a need, but they have not permitted its sale.

A blood bank is at present a necessity. The position is that a person may at any time fall prey to a fatal illness and may come to need blood; and then not every person's blood is suited to every person's body — rather it is necessary that the blood group be the same as regards its constituents, without which the body does not accept another's blood. A blood bank keeps different types of blood separately in advance, from which blood suited to the patient's condition can readily be taken. That voluntary blood donors should be available in good time for every patient is in the first place difficult enough, and more difficult still is that their blood should also be compatible with the patient. For this reason such banks have become a medical necessity, and under

"الضرورات تبيح المحظورات"

permission for them ought to be given. (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۱/۳۳۵، ط: نعیمیہ دیوبند) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
