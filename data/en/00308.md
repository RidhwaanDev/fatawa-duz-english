### TITLE
The ruling on clothes washed at the dry cleaners

### QUESTION
Every kind of garment is put into dry cleaning, pure ones and impure ones alike. Do clothes become pure when they are cleaned in this way? Also, clothes are washed in petrol over and over again; if impure clothes were washed in it first, will the clothes washed in it afterwards become pure?

### ANSWER
What we have ascertained about dry cleaning is that chemicals such as petrol are used in it, and these are made purely for washing and cleaning clothes. Impurity is therefore removed by them, and clothes of every kind, whether pure or impure, become pure.

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Impure splashes that have fallen on a garment can also be removed by petrol; have it washed with petrol and it will become pure. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 17/135).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

It is valid to wash clothes and the like with petrol, or to remove any perceptible impurity by means of it, because water is not required — any flowing liquid is enough. Al-Hidāyah states:

ويجوز تطهيرها بالماء وبكل مائع طاهر يمكن إزالتها به كالنحل وماء الورد.

(Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/86).

Niẓām al-Fatāwā states:

From this it also emerges that, since tumbling and shaking clothes in petrol removes their stains and marks and leaves them clean, and since the petrol is not absorbed into the cloth but evaporates, and once it has evaporated no trace of the impurity — colour, smell or taste — remains but has gone entirely, one has to say that it was the petrol itself that removed the impurity. Purification simply means the removal of impurity, whether that comes about through a change in the substance itself, as when wine turns into vinegar and the vinegar is then reckoned pure; or merely through evaporation, as when impure cotton becomes pure by being carded; or through washing with water; or through washing with any pure liquid. This is exactly what happens here, so on this ground too there is no need to rule that the clothes must be purified again.

Those who are certain that their clothes are impure, however — for instance, they saw the impurity fall on the garment or saw it on the garment themselves — should purify them before handing them over to be washed in petrol; or else, as a precaution, it is better for them to purify the clothes themselves once they come back. Likewise, if a solid impurity still leaves its substance on the garment after it comes out of the machine, that must be washed off, and without this the garment will not be called pure. It is a separate matter that, out of piety, a person may purify such cleaned clothes himself in the manner laid down by the sharia; but that cannot be given as the formal ruling. (Muntakhabāt-i Niẓām al-Fatāwā: 1/30–32).

From these passages it is clear that petrol removes impurity. There remains the question that this petrol, after being used once, is filtered to take out the impure particles and is then cleaned, so that it has become like used water. The answer is that used water is itself pure, though it does not purify from ritual impurity, and it can still remove actual physical impurity.

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

Used water is pure. Ablution and the ritual bath may not be performed with it, but it does purify from actual physical impurity: that is, if an impure item is washed with it, the item becomes pure. Radd al-Muḥtār states:

وحكمه انه ليس بطهور للحدث بل لخبث على الراجح.

(Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 2/40).

In short, the cautious course is that after the clothes come out of the dry-cleaning machine, plain water should be poured over them and they should be wrung out.
