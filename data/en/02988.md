### TITLE
The means of making the religion dominant and the ways of establishing the Islamic caliphate

### QUESTION
What are the means by which the religion is made dominant? How is the Islamic caliphate to be established? In our times, what is the best substitute for the Islamic caliphate? Further, what is the difference between the Islamic caliphate and what stands in its place today? An explanation of the following hadith is also required:

عن جنادة بن أبي أمية قال: دخلنا على عبادة بن الصامت رضي الله عنه وهو مريض قلنا: أصلحك الله حَدِّث بحديث ينفعك الله به سمعته من النبي ﷺ، قال: دعانا النبي ﷺ فبايعناه فقال: فيما أخذ علينا أن بايعنا على السمع والطاعة في منشطنا ومكرهنا وعسرنا ويسرنا وأثرة علينا وأن لا ننازع الأمر أهله إلا أن تروا كفراً. بينوا بالتفصيل توجروا بالأجر الجزيل.

### ANSWER
There are six means and methods for making the religion dominant: (1) proof and argument (ḥujjah); (2) daʿwah; (3) al-madāris wa'l-jāmiʿāt al-dīniyyah, the religious schools and colleges; (4) maḥw al-adyān al-bāṭilah, the effacement of the false religions; (5) al-ḥukūmah al-islāmiyyah, that is, a strong Islamic government; (6) al-khilāfah al-islāmiyyah, the Islamic caliphate.

(1) Dominance through proof: this is the dominance of Islam in the light of clear arguments. There is no need to expound this section, because in every age even non-Muslims acknowledge the superiority and eminence of Islam in the light of proofs. Those who work in this field, whether they are scholars or not, are such that non-Muslims lay down their weapons before their true arguments and shy away from coming before them. To this day the Christians have neither been able to understand, nor to explain, the philosophy of one being three and three being one.

(2) Dominance through daʿwah: that people come to the religion as a result of daʿwah and tablīgh is an open reality. Whether this work is done among non-Muslims or among Muslims, the result of both is that mosques, madrasas and Islamic colleges become filled; an irreligious environment is turned into a religious one; and where it is neglected, a dangerous situation arises.

"الإيمان يمان والحكمة يمانية" (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ٤۳۸۸)

That is, faith belongs to the people of Yemen, and sagacity too belongs to the people of Yemen.

In the view of this humble servant, a good explanation of this is that the faith which is contagious and which illumines the hearts of others belongs to the people of Yemen; for wherever the scholars and merchants of Yemen went in earlier times, they took the mission of calling to Islam along with their trade and their learning, and they conquered the hearts of their opponents with the sword of daʿwah. The Konkan and Kerala in India, then the Maldives, Sri Lanka, Indonesia and Malaysia, and in Africa as far as Somalia, Kenya and Mozambique — all the regions of the Shāfiʿī school appear to be the fruit of the labours of those sagacious men of Yemen.

In my view the khānqāhs too were centres of daʿwah and tablīgh in earlier times. The khānqāhs of Shaykh ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Jīlānī (may Allah have mercy on him), Shaykh Muʿīn al-Dīn Ajmerī (may Allah have mercy on him), Khwājah Quṭb al-Dīn Bakhtiyār Kākī (may Allah have mercy on him), Ḥaḍrat Niẓām al-Dīn Awliyāʾ (may Allah have mercy on him), Ḥaḍrat Mujaddid Alf-e Thānī (may Allah have mercy on him), Ḥaḍrat Ḥājī Imdād Allāh (may Allah have mercy on him), Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) and other elders were centres for the reform of the common people and for carrying daʿwah among them; the details of this cannot be set out here.

(3) The religious madrasas: the services of the religious madrasas and their striving for the dominance of Islam are before us all. When political movements against the non-Muslim government in undivided India did not succeed, our senior scholars (may Allah have mercy on them) laid the foundations of Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, Maẓāhir ʿUlūm Saharanpur, Nadwat al-ʿUlamāʾ, Shāhī Murādābād and other madrasas, and through the blessing of these very madrasas thousands upon thousands of madrasas were established. Indeed, the Barelwīs and the Ahl-e Ḥadīth ghayr muqallids (those who reject following a school of law) learnt this work from our senior scholars (may Allah have mercy on them) and adopted their very method.

These madrasas have brought about the intellectual, mental, outward, inward and spiritual reform of countless people, and now, with an excellent system, they are teaching and training hundreds of thousands of children throughout the world, providing for everything from the maktabs up to the memorisation of the Qur'an, and from memorisation up to the dars-e niẓāmī and the courses of specialisation; to set out the details of this would be to labour the obvious.

(4) The effacement of the other religions: the other religions will come to an end and Islam alone will remain, and this is the complete form of dominance. This work will take place in the last age, at the descent of Ḥaḍrat ʿĪsā (peace be upon him) and in the time of Ḥaḍrat Mahdī (may Allah be pleased with him). The descent of Ḥaḍrat ʿĪsā (peace be upon him) is one of the major signs of the Last Day. At that time every person of the Book will believe in him and will become a Muslim in the true sense. The details of this are found in the hadiths.

(5) A strong Islamic government: when one or several firmly established Muslim governments are actively at work, filled with the zeal of faith, and are equipped with the warmth of faith together with means, resources and weaponry, this too is a sign of the dominance of Islam. A strong Islamic government is one which possesses the means along with the strength and zeal of faith. Allah Most High has made this world a world of means, and it is therefore through means that one reaches one's goal. Yes, a saintly miracle or a prophetic miracle is a separate matter; it does not occur always but only now and then, and it is not within our control, whereas the means are within our control. It should be remembered that adopting means is not contrary to reliance upon Allah. Reliance is an act of the heart, whereas the means are the actions of the limbs. Reliance means to take up the means and then to hand the outcome over to Allah Most High. The prophets (peace be upon them) had reliance of the highest degree, yet they also adopted means.

التوكل: الاعتماد على الله تعالى مع رعاية الأسباب وضده التعطل.

A hadith states:

ما أكل إنسان طعاماً خيراً من أن يأكل من عمل يده وإن نبي الله داود عليه السلام كان يأكل من عمل يده ۔

(صحیح البخاری، رقم: ۲۰۷۲)

That is, the best food is that which a man eats from the earning of his own hand; Ḥaḍrat Dāwūd (peace be upon him) used to eat from the earning of his own hand. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ was asked: we take medicine, we have ourselves treated, we use incantations, we take care and abstain from harmful things — is this contrary to the divine decree? He said: this too is part of the divine decree.

Allah Most High says:

يا أيها الذين آمنوا خذوا حذركم. وقال: وابتغوا من فضل الله، وقال: وأعدوا لهم ما استطعتم من قوة. وقال: فأسر بعبادي ليلاً إنكم متبعون. وقال: يا بني لا تقصص رؤياك على إخوتك. وقال: لا تدخلوا من باب واحد وادخلوا من أبواب متفرقة

In all these verses there is a command to adopt means. If someone objects that, since both the one who relies upon Allah and the one who does not adopt the means alike, no difference remains between them, the answer is that there is indeed a difference between the two: if the means fail, the one who relies upon Allah does not grieve, whereas the other does; and when the work is accomplished through the means, no pride is produced in the one who relies upon Allah, and when it is not accomplished he does not complain — for the means sometimes fail. Moreover, the one who relies upon Allah adopts even weak means, because he has complete certainty in the power of Allah Most High, that the one who does everything is Allah Most High.

The scholars say that the means are of four kinds: (1) Certain worldly means, such as food for hunger. (2) Certain means relating to the Hereafter, such as ṣalāh and fasting for salvation. Both of these are binding and necessary. (3) Probable means, such as employment for one's sustenance; it is binding upon people of middling and weak state to adopt them, though those who are perfected may leave them, since they are able to be patient if straitened circumstances come upon them. (4) Imaginary, satanic means: such as the fulfilment of some purpose by spreading a sheet over a grave, lighting lamps and building domes, which is merely a whispering of Satan and has no connection with reality. Imaginary means must be abandoned.

The point is that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ himself adopted means. He wore two coats of mail, one over the other. At Mount Uḥud he stationed fifty archers at the place of danger. He appointed men for his own protection. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ foretold the Islamic naval fleet, which is a means of victory; he saw it in a dream and expressed his delight at it. The interpretation of that dream appeared in the time of Ḥaḍrat ʿUthmān (may Allah be pleased with him) through Ḥaḍrat Muʿāwiyah (may Allah be pleased with him): he built a naval fleet, and under his command the fleet reached Cyprus. In that same expedition Ḥaḍrat Umm Ḥarām fell from her mount and was martyred; the Prophet ﷺ had foretold Umm Ḥarām's participation in this jihad. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ sent ʿUrwah ibn Masʿūd and Ghaylān ibn Salamah to the Syrian city of Jurash so that they might see and learn about the dabbābah (the tank) and the manjanīq (the catapult); for this reason these two were unable to take part in the Battle of Ṭāʾif. (البدایہ و النہایہ: ٤/۳٤۸)

And on the advice of Ḥaḍrat Salmān (may Allah be pleased with him) the manjanīq was used at the Battle of Ṭāʾif. By naming one sūrah of the Noble Qur'an Sūrat al-Ḥadīd, the importance of iron was made clear. Concerning Ḥaḍrat Dāwūd (peace be upon him) He said:

و ألنا له الحديد أن اعمل سابغات وقدر في السرد

Concerning Ḥaḍrat Sulaymān (peace be upon him) He said:

يعملون له ما يشاء من محاريب وتماثيل وجفان كالجواب

Concerning Dhū al-Qarnayn He said:

آتوني زبر الحديد

And on the advice of Ḥaḍrat Salmān al-Fārisī (may Allah be pleased with him) a trench was dug around Madīnah.

Yes, if there is spiritual strength and the strength of good deeds, then sometimes the work is accomplished with weak means and sometimes even without means. And when some of those engaged in tablīgh say that one must have certainty that things come about from Allah and that nothing comes about from other than Allah or from the means, the meaning of it is that while adopting the means one's certainty should rest upon the being of Allah Most High, that it is He who brings the means to their intended destination. This is the very meaning of the saying, "the heart with the Beloved, the hands at work." And if He wills, He will make the means fail, as the fire of Nimrod failed, as the water failed to drown Ḥaḍrat Mūsā (peace be upon him), and as the plan to destroy Ḥaḍrat Yūsuf (peace be upon him) by means of the well failed. They speak of removing one's certainty from the means, not of abandoning the means, so that the means may not be taken as the objective and so that a person may make changes in his life and go out in the path of Allah Most High. In short, when there is an Islamic government, the dominance of the truth will be perfectly evident through it.

I have read in the history of Burma that in the time of Ayub Khan in Pakistan, when Pakistan and Bangladesh were one, oppression of the Muslims began, whereupon Ayub Khan sent the message: would you like our army to set out from Dhaka in the morning and take its afternoon meal in Rangoon at the time of ʿaṣr? As soon as this message arrived, the oppression stopped. At that time there was, admittedly, no Islamic government, but the unity and military strength of Pakistan was formidable. I have read this incident in the history of the atrocities of Burma.

(6) The Islamic caliphate: an Islamic government is a small thing, whereas the caliphate is a very great thing; this is why the two have been mentioned separately. The noble scholars and authors have written a great deal on the subject of the caliphate: Ibn Khaldūn (may Allah have mercy on him) in the introduction to his history; Shāh Walī Allāh (may Allah have mercy on him) in Izālat al-Khafāʾ ʿan Tārīkh al-Khulafāʾ; Ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī (may Allah have mercy on him) in volume one, p. 480; ʿAllāmah al-Bayḍāwī (may Allah have mercy on him) in his tafsīr; al-Rāghib (may Allah have mercy on him) in Mufradāt al-Qurʾān; Ibn Manẓūr al-Ifrīqī in Lisān al-ʿArab; ʿAllāmah al-Ālūsī (may Allah have mercy on him) in Rūḥ al-Maʿānī; ʿAbd al-Bāqī Ḥaqqānī in Islām kā Niẓām-e Siyāsat; Wahbah al-Zuḥaylī (may Allah have mercy on him) in Ḥuqūq al-Insān fī al-Islām; al-Māwardī in al-Aḥkām al-Sulṭāniyyah; and Muftī Taqī Ṣāḥib in the Takmilah Fatḥ al-Mulhim. All of them have written on the meaning of the caliphate and its details. The commentators of the Qur'an have written the meaning and definition of the caliphate under the verse:

وإذ قال ربك للملائكة إني جاعل في الأرض خليفة

I quote two definitions of the caliphate. ʿAllāmah al-Māwardī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written in al-Aḥkām al-Sulṭāniyyah, الباب الأول في عقد الإمامة:

الإمامة موضوعة لخلافة النبوة في حراسة الدين وسياسة الدنيا (الأحكام السلطانية، ص ۳)

That is, the imamate is the caliphate of prophethood, whose purpose is the guarding of the religion and the governance of the world, that is, the supervision of worldly affairs and their proper conduct.

ʿAllāmah al-Taftāzānī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written a very clear definition in Sharḥ al-Maqāṣid:

رئاسة عامة في أمر الدين والدنيا، خلافة عن النبي ﷺ (شرح المقاصد: ۲/۲۷۲، ط: دار المعارف النعمانية)،

That is, the caliphate is that international Islamic government which, as the deputy of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, protects both the religion and the worldly life of the people.

From this it is known that there will be one Khalīfat al-Muslimīn for the whole world. Yes, ʿAllāmah ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Baghdādī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written in Uṣūl al-Dīn, p. 274, that if two states are so far from one another that they cannot reach one another to render assistance, then there may be two caliphs.

لا يجوز أن يكون في الوقت الواحد إمامان، واجبي الطاعة إلا أن يكون بين البلدين بحر مانع من وصول النصرة أهل كل واحد منهما إلى الآخرين فيجوز حينئذٍ لا هل كل واحد منهما عقد الإمامة لواحد من أهل ناحية،

From this it is known that in a state of severe need and utter compulsion there can be two caliphates. It should be remembered that the Christian non-Muslims learnt the concept of the caliphate from the Muslims: formerly their centre of unity was England and now it is Washington; and they did not rest until they had put an end to our Ottoman caliphate.

No Muslim disputes that the caliphate is beneficial and profitable. Who does not know that when the Rightly Guided Caliphate, and after it the caliphate of the Banū Umayyah, was established in the world, the serious-minded class among the non-Muslims of East and West enjoyed the fruits of the caliphate with heart and soul, while the satanic class trembled at the very thought of the caliphate? The cool shade of the Ṭūbā tree of the caliphate was spread over the heads of Muslims and non-Muslims alike. Then, in the time of Ḥaḍrat Muʿāwiyah (may Allah be pleased with him), when the frontiers of Islam had reached Morocco in the west, Africa in the south, and Khurāsān, Turkestan, Samarqand and Bukhārā in the north, Ḥaḍrat Muʿāwiyah (may Allah be pleased with him) came to fear that if he did not nominate his son after him and departed from this world, there would be a grave danger of the caliphate being torn to pieces and of turmoil and strife. So, in order to save the caliphate, and having regard to his son's capability, he nominated his son for the caliphate, and most of the Muslims accepted this decision. Ibn Khaldūn writes in the Muqaddimah:

وكذلك عهد معاوية رضي الله عنه إلى يزيد خوفاً من افتراق الكلمة بما كانت بنو أمية لم يرضوا بتسليم الأمر إلى من سواهم، فلو قد عهد إلى غيره اختلفوا عليه. (مقدمۃ ابن خلدون: ۱/۱۰٦)،

And Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

وكان رأي معاوية رضي الله عنه في الخلافة تقديم الفاضل في القوة والرأي والمعرفة على الفاضل في السبق إلى الإسلام والدين والعبادة. (فتح الباري: ۷/۳۲٤).

Those who live in non-Muslim countries and talk of the caliphate are not even as salt in the flour; neither do the ordinary Muslims nor the people of binding and loosing recognise them. Besides, aʿūdhu billāh comes before bismillāh: that is to say, the claimants of the caliphate ought first to call to account those non-Muslim powers which brought the Islamic caliphate to an end. Yet these gentlemen live comfortably beneath their umbrella; and if these people were to try to establish the caliphate by force, they are a mere zero against the armed forces of the Muslim countries. They can create disorder; they cannot establish a caliphate.

A worthy substitute for the caliphate for the Muslims residing in non-Muslim countries:

Today is the age of the vote, and the ruler is elected through elections. Leaving aside whether this method is correct or not, this is the method that is current. Therefore the path to establishing a caliphate appears to be this: that we, and those gentlemen who claim the caliphate, should establish mosques, maktabs, religious madrasas, Islamic colleges, the work of daʿwah and tablīgh, and khānqāhs throughout the world, so that people's outlook may become Islamic; thereafter, in every country, rulers who are standard-bearers of Islam should be elected; and after that, all those rulers together should elect one Khalīfat al-Muslimīn. May Allah Most High accept us all for the work of mosques, maktabs, religious madrasas, Islamic colleges, daʿwah and tablīgh, and the reform of souls.

The first method of establishing the Islamic caliphate: the general pledge of allegiance.

The first method of the caliphate is the general pledge of allegiance. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ did not write down the caliphate in the name of Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him), but he gave indications; for example:

مروا أبابكر فليصل بالناس. (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ٦٦۴)، ويأبي الله والمؤمنون إلا أبابكر. (مسلم، رقم: ۲۳۸۷)

A woman said: if I do not find you, to whom should I turn? He said: to Abū Bakr.

(مسلم، رقم: ۲۳۸٦) اقتدوا بالذين من بعدي أبو بكر وعمر. (ترمذي، رقم: ۳٦٦۲)

After the demise of the Prophet (may Allah bless him and grant him peace), a difference arose between the Muhājirīn and the Anṣār at the Saqīfah of the Banū Sāʿidah. From the address of Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) the Anṣār understood the matter and withdrew from the caliphate — as though the Muhājirīn had convinced the Anṣār — and Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) placed this responsibility upon Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him). The Anṣār and the Muhājirīn pledged allegiance at his hand. Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him) also pledged allegiance the following day — and in one narration after six months — that is, he pledged allegiance twice. At the Saqīfah of the Banū Sāʿidah the representatives of the whole community were present and they pledged allegiance; then the next day the general pledge of allegiance took place in the mosque. In one narration Saʿd ibn ʿUbādah (may Allah be pleased with him) also pledged allegiance. In this way the pledge of allegiance to Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) was completed, and Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) confronted internal and external tribulations during the period of his caliphate and extended the frontiers of Islam. (تاریخ الخلفاء للامام جلال الدین السیوطی رحمه الله، ص ۵۸، قدیمی کتب خانہ)۔

The second method is nomination: as Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) nominated Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) after him. This nomination came after every kind of consultation and after ascertaining the general opinion. In the consultation some persons mentioned the sternness of Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him), and Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) gave a satisfying reply to it. In Tārīkh al-Ṭabarī it is recorded that some said:

ما أنت قائل لربك إذا سألك عن استخلافك عمر علينا وقد تري غلظته،

Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) said:

إذا لقيتُ الله ربي فسألني قلت: استخلفت على أهلك خير أهلك، طبري (۲/ ۳۵۵، دار الكتب العلمية، بيروت)، طبقات ابن سعد

It states in it that Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) looked out towards the people and said:

إني عهدت عهداً أفترضون به؟ فقال الناس: قد رضينا يا خليفة رسول الله، (۳/۱۹۲و ۲۰۰، دار صادر بيروت)،

That is: I wish to make a bequest concerning a certain person; will you be pleased with it? The people said: we are entirely pleased, O Caliph of the Messenger of Allah! In the books of history it is written that Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) said: I am appointing no relative of mine, but ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him),

فاسمعوا وأطيعوا،

The people said:

سمعنا وأطعنا.

ʿAllāmah Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) has written in Minhāj al-Sunnah (۱/۵۳۰):

كذلك عمر رضي الله عنه لما عهد إليه أبوبكر رضي الله عنه إنما صار إماماً لما بايعوه وأطاعوه ولو قدر أنهم لم ينفذوا عهد أبي بكر رضي الله عنه ولم يبايعوه لم يصر إماماً.

And the sources of history show that, in appointing Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar, Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq did not make his own opinion alone the basis; rather he took counsel with the senior men of the Muhājirīn and the Anṣār. Ibn Saʿd has written this in the Ṭabaqāt:

عن أبي سلمة بن عبد الرحمن وإبراهيم بن الحارث التيمي وعبد الله البهي

أن أبابكر عند استخلاف عمر شاور عبد الرحمن بن عوف وعثمان وسعيد بن زيد وأسيد بن الحضير وغيرهم من المهاجرين والأنصار ث. (الطبقات الكبري: ۳/۱۹۹، دار صادر، بيروت)،

Ibn ʿAsākir in Tārīkh Dimashq (٤٤/۲٤۸) and Ibn al-Jawzī in Manāqib ʿUmar have also written of Abū Bakr's taking counsel.

The third method is shūrā: that the rightful caliph entrusts the right of selection to a body of men who possess the capability and the qualities for the caliphate, and that this body, after ascertaining the general opinion, selects one man. Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) formed a shūrā of Ḥaḍrat ʿUthmān (may Allah be pleased with him), Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him), Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf (may Allah be pleased with him), Ḥaḍrat Ṭalḥah (may Allah be pleased with him) and Ḥaḍrat Zubayr (may Allah be pleased with him). He did not include Ḥaḍrat Saʿīd ibn Zayd (may Allah be pleased with him), who is one of the ten given the glad tidings of Paradise, because he was the cousin of Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him). He appointed Ibn ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) as an adviser, on the understanding that he would not become caliph, and he said that if three were on one side and three on the other, then the one on whose side Ḥaḍrat Ibn ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) was should be made caliph; and if they were not content with that, then the side on which ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf (may Allah be pleased with him) was. Then at the end three eminent men remained: ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, ʿUthmān and ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with them all). Then ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (may Allah be pleased with him) said: I shall not become caliph; give me the authority to decide. So Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (may Allah be pleased with him) spent three days ascertaining the general opinion, and all the people gave preference to Ḥaḍrat ʿUthmān (may Allah be pleased with him). ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (may Allah be pleased with him) and Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him) pledged allegiance at the hand of ʿUthmān (may Allah be pleased with him), and then all the people pledged allegiance.

What is the difference between the true caliphate and what stands in its place? All Muslims are agreed upon the Rightly Guided Caliphate; that was the true caliphate. After it, the caliphate of the Banū Umayyah served the Muslims and extended the frontiers of Islam, conquering Spain, Sindh, India, the Punjab and some parts of China. After that the ʿAbbāsid caliphate also served Islam; then the Ottoman caliphate made conquests and raised high the name of Islam. All these caliphates were rightful. Under the paper caliphate of this age there is neither any Muslim country nor any non-Muslim country; rather these people are themselves subjects bound by the laws of non-Muslim countries. The difference between the true caliphate and what stands in its place is therefore like the difference between a man and a woman.

عن جنادة بن أبي أمية قال: دخلنا على عبادة بن الصامت رضي الله عنه وهو مريض قلنا: أصلحك الله حَدِّث بحديث ينفعك الله به سمعته من النبي ﷺ، قال: دعانا النبي ﷺ فبايعناه فقال: فيما أخذ علينا أن بايعنا على السمع والطاعة في منشطنا ومكرهنا وعسرنا ويسرنا وأثرة علينا وأن لا ننازع الأمر أهله إلا أن تروا كفراً بواحاً عندكم من الله فيه برهان. (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۷۰۵۵، كتاب الفتن).

Ḥaḍrat ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit (may Allah be pleased with him) was ill. We went to him and said to him: may Allah Most High grant you benefit; narrate to us a hadith which will be of benefit to you and which you heard from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. He said: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ called us and we pledged allegiance at his hand, and he took from us a pledge of obedience: that we would obey the leader whether we were pleased or displeased, whether in hardship or in ease, or when someone else was given preference and precedence over us; and that we would not quarrel and dispute with those in authority unless we saw open disbelief.

The noble scholars have written that where the sinfulness and immorality of the imam is confined to his own person, no armed struggle should be waged against such a Muslim ruler; but an imam or ruler who is involved in sinfulness and immorality that extends to others, who promotes disbelief, enforces laws of disbelief and holds Islam to be of little account — armed revolt against him is permissible, provided that one has the power to remove him, that there is no danger of some greater corruption, and that a worse imam than he is not to be expected after his removal. واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم بالصواب۔
