### TITLE

Some rulings relating to muḍārabah

### QUESTION

(1) If the rabb al-māl (the investor) lays down a condition upon the muḍārib (the working partner) that he must trade for cash, may he then trade on credit?

### ANSWER

He may not.
Al-Mabsūṭ states:

ولو دفعه إليه مضاربة على أن يشتري بالنقد ويبيع فليس له أن يشتري إلا بالنقد لأن هذا تقييد مفيد في حق رب المال. (المبسوط للإمام السرخسي رحمه الله: ۲۲/٤٤، باب ما يجوز للمضارب ومالا يجوز).

Question (2): Can a muḍārabah contract be concluded with a non-Muslim?
The answer: This is a commercial transaction, and sharing the same religion is not a requirement for a commercial transaction. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ himself entered into dealings with non-Muslims.
Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

وإسلامه ليس بشرط أصلاً فتجوز الإجارة والاستئجار من المسلم والذمي والحربي المستأمن لأن هذا من عقود المعاوضات فيملكه المسلم والكافر جميعاً كالبياعات. الخ. (بدائع الصنائع: ٤/۱۷٦، كتاب الإجارة، سعيد).

Question (3): I gave a muḍārib one hundred thousand rand to trade with, but he is himself a trader as well and also takes funds from other people on a muḍārabah basis. Is this correct — that is, is it valid for him to mix the funds together or not?
The answer: He may mix them with the permission of the owner, that is, the rabb al-māl. If permission has not been given explicitly, customary permission is also sufficient, because this is what the prevailing custom is.
Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ولا يملك المضاربة والشركة والخلط بمال نفسه إلا بإذن. وفي رد المحتار: قوله والخلط بمال نفسه أي أو غيره كما في البحر الا أن تكون معاملة التجار في تلك البلاد أن المضاربين يخلطون، ولا ينهونهم فإن غلب التعارف بينهم في مثله وجب أن لا يضمن كما في التاتارخانية. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۵/٦٤۹، كتاب المضاربة، سعيد).

Question (4): I live in Lenasia and the place of business is in Johannesburg, and I am a muḍārib on behalf of Zayd. It is roughly 23 to 25 kilometres from Lenasia to Johannesburg, and I have to make the journey daily. May I take the cost of the car?
The answer: When the muḍārib is travelling outside his own city for the work of the muḍārabah, he may take his expenses and his food and drink in accordance with custom and usage.
It appears from the statements of the jurists that if he is able to reach home at night, then maintenance and the like are not payable out of the muḍārabah capital; but if the journey is such that it is difficult to reach home at night, then he may take maintenance and other such expenses out of the muḍārabah capital.
See: (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/۵، ۱۰٦، سعید ۔ و الفتاوی الہندیۃ: ٤/۳۱۲ ۔ و فتاوی الشامی: ۵/٦۵۷) ۔
But the whole matter turns on custom, and in our custom the midday meal is generally the owner's responsibility when a person goes outside the city for work, even though he returns home at night. Likewise, if a person uses his own car and so on for the business, then the petrol and similar costs also come out of the business funds. Indeed, in many places the owner himself provides a company vehicle. So these matters all turn on custom.
واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
