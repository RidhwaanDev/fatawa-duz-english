### TITLE
Zakat on white gold

### QUESTION
What is the reality of white gold? Is zakat obligatory on it or not?

### ANSWER
It is an accepted fact that pure gold is not fit for making jewellery unless some other metal is mixed into it. In yellow gold, copper and silver are generally mixed in, whereas in white gold palladium is mixed in, which gives it a white colour instead of a yellow one; otherwise it is gold just the same. The rulings of gold will therefore apply to it, and zakat will be obligatory on white gold too.

If, however, the other metal predominates and it can easily be separated, then zakat will be obligatory on the gold. But if the separation of the other metal is difficult or impossible, then in the light of the well-known and standard books of jurisprudence zakat is not obligatory — though it is better to pay it as a precaution. See what al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq records:

وإن غلب الغش فليس كالفضة كالستوقة فينظر إن كانت رائجة أو نوى التجارة اعتبرت قيمتها فإن بلغت نصاباً من أدنى الدراهم التي تجب فيها الزكاة، وهي التي غلبت فضتها وجبت فيها الزكاة وإلا فلا، وإن لم تكن أثماناً رائجة، ولا منوية للتجارة فلا زكاة فيها إلا أن يكون ما فيها من الفضة يبلغ مائتي درهم بأن كانت كثيرة ويتخلص من الغش، لأن الصفر لا تجب الزكاة فيها إلا بنية التجارة، والفضة لا يشترط فيها نية التجارة فإن كان ما فيها لا يتخلص فلا شيء عليه؛ لأن الفضة فيه قد هلكت كذا في كثير من الكتب، وفي غاية البيان الظاهر أن خلوص الفضة من الدراهم ليس بشرط بل المعتبر أن تكون في الدراهم فضة بقدر النصاب. (البحر الرائق: ۲/۲۲۸).

وللمزيد راجع: (الجوهرة النيرة: ۱/۱٤۹، إمدادية ملتان، والفتاوى الهندية: ۳/۲۱۹، وبدائع الصنائع: ۲/۱۷، سعيد، وتبيين الحقائق: ۱/۲۷۹، وفتح القدير: ۷/۱۵۲، دار الفكر).

Bihishtī Zēwar states:

If gold or silver is not pure but has some alloy in it — tin mixed with silver, for example — then see whether there is more silver or more tin. If there is more silver, its ruling is the ruling of silver; that is, if it reaches the quantity stated above, zakat is obligatory. And if there is more tin, it will not be regarded as silver, and the ruling that will be given below for brass, copper, iron, tin and similar goods is its ruling. (بہشتی زیور، زکوٰۃ کا بیان، تیسرا حصہ: ۲۳۹، مسئلہ نمبر ۷، دار الاشاعت) ۔

ʿIṭr-e Hidāyah records that gold and silver can easily be separated. Consider the passage:

From the observations and experience of goldsmiths, plain-workers and refiners — indeed even of those who are not craftsmen at all — it is established, well known and widely recognised that when new rupees are melted down and the other customary devices and refining processes are applied to them, their silver becomes entirely separate from the base alloy mixed into it. In many books of jurisprudence it is expressly stated, in connection with numerous rulings, that the silver of coins such as these (دراهم غالبۃ الغش) can be separated, and that the rulings of zakat, of usury and of currency exchange are applied to that silver. Of course, as Shaykh Ibn al-Humām has stated expressly and as the principles require, in those heavily adulterated coins in which the silver — as in a plating — is in such a very small quantity that it does not separate out but is lost, it will certainly not be taken into account. In the books of the Shāfiʿīs it is stated that the silver in a heavy plating too is taken into account, because it can be separated; and since the principles of the Ḥanafīs bear witness to this, some Ḥanafī scholars too have distinguished between a light and a heavy plating and have said that if the plating of silver or gold is so thick that it can be separated, then it too must be taken into account. Thus Radd al-Muḥtār (p. 329) states:

ونقل الخير الرملي نحوه من المحيط ثم قال: وأقول: يجب تقليد المسألة بما إذا لم تكثر الفضة أو الذهب المموه أما إذا كثر بحيث يحصل منه شيء يدخل في الميزان بالعرض على النار يجب حينئذٍ اعتباره ولم أره لأصحابنا لكن رأيته للشافعية وقواعدنا شاهدة به فتأمل.

At all events, whether or not a distinction is drawn between a light and a heavy plating and between adulterated coins resembling them, it is expressly transmitted that from most heavily adulterated coins — which, like the new rupees, contain even more silver than a heavy plating — pure silver can be separated, and that the silver in them is taken into account; and there is no report to the contrary. It would be established even so, since the basis of consideration is the possibility of separation. (Even though one may not be able to benefit from that silver at present, in the future one can melt it down and benefit from it whenever one wishes — as is expressly stated in Fatḥ al-Qadīr (۲/۲۱٤، دار الفکر):

وإن لم يخلص فلا شيء عليه لأن الفضة هلكت فيه إذ لم ينتفع بها حالاً ولا مآلاً فبقي العبرة للغش "

The real determining factor is the consideration due to the two currencies, and the possibility of separation is established by observation and experience and is well known and recognised. (عطر ہدایہ، جدید ص: ۳۵٤ ۔ ۳۵٦) ۔ (فتاوی دار العلوم زکریا: ۳/۱۰۹، ط: بمبئی) ۔
