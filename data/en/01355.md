### TITLE
The ruling on zakat for the flour brought into bakeries and factories

### QUESTION
In bakeries and hotels, foodstuffs are prepared and sold. To produce these items there are stocks of materials — bags of flour, for instance, bags of sugar and salt, and other things used to decorate cakes and the like. Will zakat be due on these stocks at the end of the year or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
The jurists have laid down a general principle in this matter: tools and materials whose effect does not remain in the manufactured article and which are not visible in it — machines, for example, or the salt in food — are not subject to zakat; whereas tools and materials whose effect does remain in the article, and whose constituents are present in it — car parts, sesame, flour and so on — are subject to zakat. Keeping this principle in view, zakat will be due, once the year has passed, on stocks of flour and on the ingredients applied to the top of a cake for decoration. On whether zakat is due on bags of sugar and salt, however, there are two opinions:

(1) In view of the principle mentioned above, zakat will not be due, because their constituents dissolve away and are no longer visible. کما فی البحر: ۷/۳۰۳، و المحیط: ۲/۳۹۳، وغیرہما من الکتب الفقہیہ ۔

(2) The wording of the author of Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ shows that zakat will be due, for he counted the fat used in tanning as merchandise and ruled that zakat is due on it, even though the substance of the fat does not remain but turns into oil, and even that dries up after a while. Caution likewise requires that zakat be due on stocks of sugar and salt.

An objection arises here, namely that the jurists have recorded the view that zakat is not due in the case of salt. The answer is that in earlier times salt had no price and no standing: a great deal of it could be had for a trifling sum, and more often than not it was obtained free of charge. That is why the jurists did not count it as merchandise.

Consider what Malik al-ʿUlamāʾ ʿAllāmah Abū Bakr al-Kāsānī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ:

وأما الأجراء الذين يعملون للناس نحو الصباغين والقصارين والدباغين إذا اشتروا الصبغ والصابون والدهن ونحو ذلك مما يحتاج إليه في عملهم ونووا عند الشراء أن ذلك للاستعمال في عملهم هل يصير ذلك مال التجارة روى بِشر بن الوليد عن أبي يوسف أن الصباغ إذا اشتري العصفر والزعفران ليصبغ ثياب الناس فعليه فيه الزكاة والحاصل أن هذا على وجهين إن كان شيئاً يبقى أثره في المعمول فيه كالصبغ والزعفران والشحم الذي يدبغ به الجلد فإنه يكون مال التجارة لأن الأجر يكون مقابلة ذلك الأثر وذلك الأثر مال قائم فإنه من أجزاء الصبغ والشحم لكنه لطيف فيكون هذا تجارة، وإن كان شيئاً لا يبقي أثره في المعمول فيه مثل الصابون والأشنان والقلي والكبريت فلا يكون مال التجارة، لأن عينها تتلف ولم ينتقل أثرها إلى الثوب المغسول حتى يكون له حصة من العوض بل البياض أصلي للثوب يظهر عند زوال الدرن فما يأخذ من العوض يكون بدل عمله لا بدل هذه الآلات فلم يكن مال التجارة. (بدائع الصنائع: ۲/۱۳، سعيد).

Al-ʿInāyah states:

وقوله وآلات المحترفين، قيل: يريد بها ما ينتفع بعينه ولا يبقى أثره في المعمول كالصابون والحرض وغيرهما كالقدور وقوارير العطار ونحوها لكون الأجر حينئذٍ مقابلاً بالمنفعة فلا يعد من مال التجارة.

وأما ما يبقى أثره فيه كما لو اشتري الصباغ عصفراً أو زعفراناً ليصبغ للناس بالأجر وحال عليه الحول فإنه تجب فيه الزكاة إذا بلغ نصاباً لأن المأخوذ من الأجر مقابل بالعين. (العناية على الهداية بهامش فتح القدير: ۲/۱٦٤، ط: دار الفكر).

(وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۷۲، وفتح القدير: ۲/۱٦٤، ط: دار الفكر، وفتاوى الشامي: ۲/۲٦۵، سعيد، والبحر الرائق: ۲/۲۲٦، ط: دار المعرفة، والمحيط البرهاني: ۲/٤۳٦، التراث العربي).

In the light of the passages quoted above it becomes clear that whatever leaves a lasting effect in the finished article, so that the dye or the like that has been applied has a share in the wage received, is subject to zakat once the year has passed. Now the effect of flour is present in a cake, and its price covers both the flour and the making of it; bags of flour are therefore subject to zakat, and so are the materials used for decoration.

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states: industrial tools and materials are of two kinds. The first are those employed to carry out some task, whose effect does not remain in the article; the second are those that are fitted into it just as they are. Some tools for repairing a car, for instance, are meant only to put things right, and the workman uses them to that extent alone; the machines in large factories are of this same nature. Other items, however, are kept precisely so that they may be fitted into the car when the need arises.

Of these two kinds, items of the first are not subject to zakat; this includes machines and the industrial tools of watchmakers, carpenters, blacksmiths, motorcycle repairers, farmers and the like. Items of the second kind are subject to zakat; this includes the saleable parts of watches, radios, motorcycles and so forth, since they have the standing of merchandise. Accordingly the jurists write as follows, treating items of the first kind as a basic necessity (ḥājat aṣliyyah) and items of the second kind as subject to zakat. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil 1/209)

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states: it emerges from the explicit statements and fatwas of the jurists and the great muftis that printing equipment and other tools whose effect does not remain in the article produced are not themselves subject to zakat, whereas tools whose effect does remain in the article are subject to zakat. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith 7/620)
