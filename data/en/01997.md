### TITLE
The ruling on taking payment for each new printing

### QUESTION
An author gave his book to a printer to print and stipulated with him: "After printing the book you will give us two hundred and fifty copies; the remaining copies are yours, and you may sell them at whatever price you wish." Is this transaction permissible or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, if the author granted the printing right to this printer permanently, then these two hundred and fifty copies have become the price of the manuscript and the printer has acquired the printing right in perpetuity. But if he specified a single occasion, then these two hundred and fifty copies are the payment for the printing right on that one occasion. The printing right is among those rights for which payment may be taken, the details of which have already been given.

And if the author made no mention of a single occasion or of perpetuity, then custom is what counts in the matter; and according to my information, in such a case only a one-time printing right is granted. The author therefore also has the right to have the book printed elsewhere a second time; and if he has it printed again by the earlier printer, he may then make a fresh agreement and take copies of the book from him once more in accordance with that agreement.
