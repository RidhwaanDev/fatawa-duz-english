### TITLE
Holding the working partner liable for a loss

### QUESTION
Ḥāmid and Maḥmūd started a business together. It was written in the agreement that Maḥmūd would put in 500,000 rupees as capital and that Ḥāmid would do the trading; that of the profit earned Maḥmūd would take 75 per cent and Ḥāmid would receive 25 per cent; and that if any loss occurred Maḥmūd alone would bear it. They had been trading under this agreement for two years. Some time ago a number of people bought goods from Ḥāmid and then disappeared without paying for them. Maḥmūd is now demanding the loss from Ḥāmid, even though the agreement states the opposite, and Ḥāmid had been trading properly and to the full extent of his ability, with no shortcoming on his part. What is the ruling of the sharia now?

### ANSWER
In a muḍārabah contract the working partner is a trustee with regard to the money he receives from the owner of the capital, and with regard to dealing in it he is also an agent — that is, he is the representative of the one who has put up the capital. As a trustee it is binding upon him to safeguard the capital. But if a loss happens to occur in that capital, or it is destroyed, the working partner is not answerable for it. If, however, it is established that he destroyed the property deliberately, then he will be answerable for it; and he will likewise be answerable if he has acted against the conditions laid down by the owner of the capital.

Keeping the above ruling of the sharia in view, the ruling in the case in question is that the loss will first be made good out of the profit earned. If no profit was made, or the loss is greater than the profit, and Ḥāmid was guilty of no shortcoming in the business, then Ḥāmid is not answerable for the loss; Maḥmūd will bear it.

Furthermore, even if the agreement had placed the condition of bearing the loss upon the working partner, he would still not be answerable for it, nor would the muḍārabah become defective; rather, the condition itself would be void.

See what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

(ويملك المضارب في المطلقة) التي لم تقيد بمكان أو زمان أو نوع (البيع)

(بنقد ونسيئة متعارفة). وفي رد المحتار: قوله بنقد ونسيئة ولو اختلفا فيهما فالقول للمضارب في المضاربة. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۵/٦٤۸، كتاب المضاربة، سعيد).

Al-Hidāyah states:

وما هلك من مال المضاربة فهو من الربح، دون راس المال، فإذا زاد الهالك على الربح فلا ضمان على المضارب لأنه أمين. (الهداية: ۳/۲٦٦).

(وكذا في الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۵/٦۵٦، سعيد).

Al-Hidāyah states:

وغير ذلك من الشروط الفاسدة لا يفسدها ويبطل الشرط كشرط الوضيعة على المضارب. (الهداية: ۳/۲۵۸. وكذا في الدر المختار: ۵/٦٤۸).

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

In the case described the muḍārabah contract is sound, but the whole of the loss will fall upon the one who gave the money; none of it will fall upon the working partner, the one who does the work.

وفي رد المحتار: قوله بطل الشرط كشرط الخسران على المضارب.

(Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām: 2/33).
