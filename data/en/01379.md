### TITLE
The ruling on fasting while undergoing dialysis in Ramadan

### QUESTION
If a sick person has his blood cleaned during Ramadan — the procedure called dialysis — is his fast broken by it or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
For a fast to be broken, one of the things that break a fast must reach the cavity of the stomach or the cavity of the brain through a natural opening. In the case asked about, dialysis cleans the blood in the blood vessels: the blood passes out of the vessels into a machine, is cleaned there, and then re-enters the vessels. On that basis there is no reason for the fast to be broken.

Consider what dialysis actually is and what its kinds are.

When a person's kidney stops working, the job of cleaning the blood is carried out by an artificial machine. This is called dialysis. There are two kinds of dialysis:

(1) Haemodialysis. In this the patient's blood circulates outside his body. First, by means of an operation, a tube is inserted into the blood vessels of the body; through this tube the body's blood passes out into the machine, where it is cleaned by a special kind of filter and then re-enters the body through a second tube. The machine, in effect, does the work of a kidney: it removes the spoiled and harmful matter from the blood. This process is called haemodialysis, and it is the method in general use.

(2) Peritoneal dialysis. To clean out the impurities, a liquid medicine (which comes in the form of glucose or of other substances) is introduced through a tube, by way of the abdominal membrane, into the cavity of the belly that lies beside the stomach and the intestines. This cavity is used as the filter, in that the liquid medicine remains in it for a while and absorbs the harmful matter, and is then drawn out through another tube along with the spoiled blood. This procedure is called peritoneal dialysis.

(Taken from the internet: the website "Medical News Today", where an article entitled "What is Dialysis" was published on 29 June 2015 under the supervision of a doctor, "Christian Nordqvist".)

For a fast to be broken, it is a condition that something enter the cavity of the belly. Consider this investigation into what the cavity of the belly is.

There are two kinds of cavity in the human belly. (1) The cavity of the stomach and the intestines, called in English the alimentary canal.

(2) The cavity that lies below the chest and in which the stomach and the intestines are set — that is, the cavity surrounding the stomach and the rest. In English it is called the abdominal cavity.

Which of these cavities is meant when we speak of something entering and breaking the fast is explained by Dr Mufti ʿAbd al-Wāḥid:

What is meant by "the cavity" (that is, the cavity of the belly) is the cavity of the stomach and the intestines — the alimentary canal. The word "belly" is also applied to the cavity below the chest in which the principal organs are set, such as the stomach, the intestines, the bladder, the womb and the kidneys — the cavity called the abdominal cavity — but that is not what is meant here. Although I have not come across an explicit statement on this point, the following passages support taking the first sense, the alimentary canal:

(1) أما الحقنة والوجور فلأنه وصل إلى الجوف ما فيه صلاح البدن. (فتاوى قاضي خان على هامش الهندية: ۱/۲۱۰).

(Administering an enema, or pouring drops into the mouth, breaks the fast, because something that benefits the belly has reached the cavity.)

(2) وما وصل إلى الجوف أو إلى الدماغ عن المخارق الأصلية كالأنف والأذن والدبر بأن استعط أو احتقن أو أقطر في أذنه فوصل إلى الجوف أو إلى الدماغ فسد صومه. (بدائع الصنائع: ۲/۹۳).

(If something reaches the cavity of the belly or the cavity of the brain through a natural opening such as the nose, the ear or the back passage, the fast is broken.)

(3) ولو أقطر في إحليله لم يفطر عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله وقال أبو يوسف رحمه الله يفطر

فكأنه وقع عند أبي يوسف رحمه الله أن بينه وبين الجوف منفذاً ولهذا يخرج منه البول ووقع عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله أن المثانة بينهما حائل والبول يترشح منه. (الهداية: ۱/۲۲۰).

(If a man puts drops into his urethra, the fast is not broken according to Imam Abū Ḥanīfah, may Allah have mercy on him, whereas according to Imam Abū Yūsuf, may Allah have mercy on him, it is broken. In effect, Imam Abū Yūsuf held that there is a passage between the bladder and the cavity of the belly, so that urine travels from the cavity of the belly into the bladder through that passage; whereas Imam Abū Ḥanīfah held that the bladder is a barrier between the cavity of the belly and the urethra, and that urine seeps through it drop by drop.)

Had the second meaning of "cavity" — the abdominal cavity — been intended, there would have been no disagreement between Imam Abū Ḥanīfah and Imam Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on them), since the bladder undoubtedly lies within it, and anything entering the bladder would have counted as entering the cavity of the belly. But since a disagreement did arise, we know that what the jurists mean by the cavity of the belly is the cavity of the stomach and the intestines. (Marīḍ o Muʿālij ke Islāmī Aḥkām, pp. 148–149).

Mufti Muhammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him), investigating the meaning of "cavity", says:

For a fast to be broken it is necessary that the fast-breaking substance reach the cavity of the brain or the cavity of the belly through a natural opening. Reaching the cavity of just any organ, or the cavity of the blood vessels (the arteries and the veins), does not break the fast. The statements of the jurists bear out this claim in two ways — nearly, indeed truly:

First, the jurists did not say that putting medicine on a wound breaks the fast without qualification; they restricted it to a wound that reaches the body cavity or one that reaches the brain, because it is from these two kinds of wound that the medicine reaches inside the cavity of the brain or the cavity of the belly. Otherwise medicine reaches inside the cavity of the blood vessels from other kinds of wound as well.

Secondly, among the particular rulings that the jurists accept there are many in which medicine or the like did reach a cavity of the body in a general sense, but which were not held to break the fast because it did not reach the cavity of the brain or the cavity of the belly. For instance, putting medicine or oil into a man's urethra does not break the fast, by the agreement of the three imams.

Even if the medicine reaches the bladder, it still does not break the fast according to the Greatest Imam and Imam Muhammad (may Allah have mercy on them). Imam Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him), who holds that reaching the bladder does break the fast, holds this only because he understood that there is a passage between the bladder and the stomach through which the medicine gets across; otherwise he too does not hold that merely reaching the bladder itself breaks the fast.

Likewise, if a person pours water into his ear the fast is not broken — as is stated explicitly in al-Durr al-Mukhtār and al-Khulāṣah — even though the ear too is a cavity. Likewise, if someone ties a grape or the like to a thread and swallows it, and then draws it back out before it reaches the stomach, the fast is not broken.

In sum: if getting something into any cavity of the body whatsoever broke the fast without dispute, then, since the urethra is itself a cavity, the bladder is a cavity all the more, and the throat is a cavity as well, getting something into them would break the fast without dispute. From this we know that a fast-breaking substance reaching just any cavity of the body does not break the fast; what is meant is specifically the cavity of the brain and the cavity of the belly. (Imdād al-Muftīn, volume two, p. 411, published by Dār al-Ishāʿat).

Mufti Rafīʿ says in al-Maqālāt al-Fiqhiyyah:

يتحصل من المسائل والجزئيات التي ذكرها الفقهاء في فساد الصوم أن الجوف المعتبر في نفسه عند الحنفية والمالكية هي المعدة والحلق والأمعاء، أما الأجواف الأخر التي توجد في باطن الجسم الإنساني، فما كان له مسلك إلى أحد هذه الثلاثة. بحيث إذا وصل شيء من الخارج إلى هذا الجوف وصل إلى أحد هذه الثلاثة عادة، إما مباشرة وإما بواسطة جوف آخر. فهو أيضاً جوف معتبر تبعاً لها، فيأخذ حكمها، وما لا يكون كذلك، فليس بجوف معتبر عندهم، لا أصالة ولا تبعاً، فإذا وصل إلى الجوف المعتبر شيء من المفطرات من منفذ معتبر وصولاً معتبراً فسد الصوم. (المقالات الفقهية، ص۸۵، الفصل الثالث في بيان الجوف).

See further: Imdād al-Fatāwā: 2/174–178, and Khayr al-Fatāwā: 4/74.

In short, neither of the two kinds of dialysis described above breaks the fast, since nothing enters the cavity of the stomach through them. In the second kind, peritoneal dialysis, however, if the liquid medicine strengthens the person's body so that he has no need of food and drink, then it is not free of dislikedness.

A note: according to Shaykh Ibn Bāz (may Allah have mercy on him), the procedure of dialysis does break the fast, as stated in Fatāwā al-Lajnah al-Dāʾimah (10/189–190). Shaykh al-ʿUthaymīn drew a distinction in this matter, holding that if the liquid medicine strengthens the body it breaks the fast, and otherwise not, as recorded in Fatāwā Nūr ʿalā al-Darb. But we do not agree with their rulings; in our view neither of these breaks the fast.
