### TITLE
Why the supplications of Muslims go unanswered, and the story of the conquest of Sindh

### QUESTION
In some non-Muslim countries there is endless oppression. I have heard from the scholars that the supplication of an oppressed person is never turned away. The oppressed keep supplicating, yet in spite of this no calamity falls upon the oppressors. Do these supplications go to waste? بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
In the matter asked about, there are certain reasons why a supplication is not answered at once. Moreover, the acceptance of a supplication takes various forms: sometimes a great calamity is averted by it, sometimes it is stored up for the hereafter, and so on. There are also certain conditions for a supplication to be accepted, and if any one of them is missing the supplication will not be accepted. As for the rest, Allah has promised help and support to the oppressed, but He has fixed no particular time for the fulfilment of that promise.

Consider the following causes and conditions relating to the acceptance of supplication:

1. Sometimes the oppressors are given a respite, and during that respite their oppression and cruelty pass all bounds; and help is delayed in order to test the Muslims and for certain other benefits.

Consider what Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib states in Maʿārif al-Qurʾān:

Out of His mercy and His far-reaching wisdom, Allah, glorious is His majesty, often gives criminal nations a respite, and this respite sometimes becomes very long, so that the boldness of the rebellious grows and the prophets face redoubled distress. (Allah) has said:

﴿حتي إذا استيأس الرسل وظنوا أنهم قد كذبوا جاء هم نصرنا فنجي من نشاء ولا يرد بأسنا عن القوم المجرمين﴾ [يوسف: ۱۱۰]

That is, He gave long respites to the disobedient among earlier communities, so much so that, when no punishment came upon them for a long stretch of time, the prophets despaired, thinking that within the time which they had themselves fixed in their minds for Allah's general promise of punishment no punishment would come upon the unbelievers and the truth would not prevail; and it became their strong impression that their own understanding had erred in fixing a time for the divine promise, since Allah had not stated any definite time and they had settled on a period from particular indications. In that very state of despair Our help reached them الخ. (Maʿārif al-Qurʾān: 5/149).

In another place Allah says:

﴿الٓمٓ، أحسب الناس أن يتركوا أن يقولوا آمنا وهم لا يفتنون، ولقد فتنا الذين من قبلهم فليعلمن الله الذين صدقوا وليعلمن الكاذبين﴾ [العنكبوت: ۱، ۳)،

In connection with the occasion of the revelation of this verse an incident is related which shows that Allah delays His help for certain wise purposes.

عن خباب بن الأرت قال: شكونا إلى رسول الله ﷺ وهو متوسد بردة له في ظل الكعبة فقلنا: ألا تستنصر لنا ألا تدعونا؟ فقال: قد كان من قبلكم يؤخذ الرجل فيحفر له في الأرض فيجعل فيها فيجاء بالمنشار فيوضع على رأسه فيجعل نصفين، ويمشط بأمشاط الحديد ما دون لحمه وعظمه فما يصده ذلك عن دينه، والله ليتمن هذا الأمر حتى يسير الراكب من صنعاء إلى حضر موت لا يخاف إلا الله والذئب على غنمه ولكنكم تستعجلون. (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ٦۹٤۳).

A report in Sunan al-Tirmidhī says that the supplication of an oppressed person is certainly answered, although a delay is possible for certain wise purposes: ولو بعد حين. (Sunan al-Tirmidhī, no. 3598).

2. Sometimes non-Muslim countries oppress the Muslims of one place while giving the Muslims in their own country every comfort, and because of the heartfelt goodwill of those Muslims the oppressors remain protected in this world, or protected for a time. For example, it has come in a hadith that whoever clothes a Muslim remains under Allah's protection as long as any shred of that cloth remains on his body. Sunan al-Tirmidhī states:

عن حصين قال: جاء سائل فسأل ابن عباس رضي الله عنه، فقال ابن عباس رضي الله عنه للسائل: أتشهد أن لا إله إلا الله؟ قال: نعم، قال: أتشهد أن محمداً رسول الله؟ قال: نعم، قال: وتصوم رمضان؟ قال: نعم، قال: سألت وللسائل حق، إنه لحق علينا أن نصلك، فأعطاه ثوباً ثم قال: سمعت رسول الله ﷺ يقول: ما من مسلم كسا مسلماً ثوباً إلا كان في حفظ من الله ما دام منه عليه خرقة. هذا حديث حسن غريب من هذا الوجه. (سنن الترمذي، رقم: ۲٤۸٤).

3. The acceptance of supplication takes various forms. It may be that if, in place of the oppressors who are committing the oppression, the Muslims were given rule, they would turn out to be even greater oppressors than those men; so being saved from a greater oppressor can itself be one form of a supplication's being answered. Consider the report in Musnad Aḥmad:

عن أبي سعيد رضي الله عنه أن النبي ﷺ قال: ما من مسلم يدعو بدعوة ليس فيها إثم ولا قطيعة رحم إلا أعطاه الله بها إحدى ثلاث؛ إما أن تعجل له دعوته، وإما أن يدخرها له في الآخرة، وإما أن يصرف عنه من السوء مثلها، قالوا: إذا نكثر، قال: الله أكثر. (مسند الامام أحمد: ۱۷/۲۱۳/۱۱۱۳۳). (وكذا في المستدرك للحاكم، قال الحاكم: هذا حديث صحيح الاسناد الا ان الشيخين لم يخرجاه عن على بن على الرفاعي: ۱/٦۷۰/۱۸۱٦).

4. Sometimes, for certain wise purposes, the seizing of the oppressor is held back for the hereafter, and in this world the oppressor goes on enjoying himself. Allah says:

﴿ولا تحسبن الله غافلاً عما يعمل الظالمون إنما يؤخرهم ليوم تشخص فيه الأبصار﴾ [إبراهيم: ٤۲]،

In this verse there is consolation for the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and for every oppressed person, and a threat of severe punishment for the oppressor: oppressors and criminals should not become carefree because Allah gives them rope, nor should they suppose that Allah has no knowledge of their crimes and that this is why, in spite of their crimes, they are flourishing and no punishment or calamity comes upon them. On the contrary, everything they are doing is before Allah's eyes; but out of what His mercy and wisdom require, He is giving them rope. (Maʿārif al-Qurʾān: 5/259).

5. Sometimes supplications are not answered because the conditions of supplication are not fulfilled. A few of these conditions are the following:

(1) That one's food, drink, clothing and the like be lawful and wholesome. A report in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim states:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: أيها الناس، إن الله طيب لا يقبل إلا طيباً، وإن الله أمر المؤمنين بما أمر به المرسلين، فقال: ﴿يا أيها الرسل كلوا من الطيبات واعملوا صالحاً، إني بما تعملون عليم﴾ [المؤمنون: ۵۱]، وقال: ﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا كلوا من طيبات ما رزقناكم﴾ [البقرة: ۱۷۲]، ثم ذكر الرجل يطيل السفر أشعث أغبر يمد يديه إلى السماء يا رب يا رب، ومطمعه حرام ومشربه حرام وملبسه حرام وغذي بالحرام، فأني يستجاب لذلك. (صحيح مسلم: ۲/۷۰۳. ۱۰۱۵).

(2) That one keep enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong. Sunan al-Tirmidhī states:

عن حذيفة بن اليمان رضي الله عنه عن النبي ﷺ قال: والذي نفسي بيده لتأمرن بالمعروف ولتنهون عن المنكر أو ليوشكن الله أن يبعث عليكم عقاباً منه ثم تدعونه فلا يستجاب لكم. هذا حديث حسن. (سنن الترمذي، رقم: ۲۱٦۹).

(3) That one keep supplicating always, adopting the means and waiting for Allah's help, and never grow despondent or lose hope. Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī states:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه أن رسول الله ﷺ قال: يستجاب لا حدكم ما لم يعجل، يقول: دعوت فلم يستجب لي. (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ٦۳٤۰).

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim states:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه عن النبي ﷺ أنه قال: لا يزال يستجاب للعبد ما لم يدع بأثم أو قطيعة رحم ما لم يستعجل قيل: يا رسول الله! ما الاستعجال؟ قال: يقول: قد دعوت، وقد دعوت فلم أر يستجيب لي فيستحسر عند ذلك ويدع الدعاء. (صحيح مسلم، رقم: ۲۷۳۵).

6. Sometimes the Muslims are punished for their heedlessness and their abandonment of the means, and the supplication is not answered — just as, if a man does not marry and keeps praying for children, his prayer will not be answered. In the same way, the oppressed keep supplicating, but the Muslim rulers do not adopt the means of repelling the oppressor and punishing him, and the oppressed go on being crushed under oppression. For example, when the subjects of Raja Dahir oppressed and plundered the Muslims of Iraq, the oppressed supplicated and appealed to Ḥajjāj ibn Yūsuf, the governor of Iraq; and he, together with the supplications of the oppressed, put the means to work and rooted the oppressors out. فاعتبروا يا أولي الأبصار.

Ḥajjāj ibn Yūsuf answering, from Iraq, the cry of a Muslim woman of Sindh:

Consider the details of the incident below.

Some Muslim merchants had died while travelling on the island of Sarandīp. Their orphaned children and widowed women had been left behind on that island, and the raja of Sarandīp considered them the best means of turning the favour and kindness of Ḥajjāj ibn Yūsuf al-Thaqafī and of the caliph al-Walīd ibn ʿAbd al-Malik towards himself. Hearing time and again of the conquests of the Muslims, the raja of Sarandīp was already overawed and was looking for some means and device by which to display his submissiveness. So he seated the orphaned children and the widows, with honour and respect, in his own special ships along with his trusted men and dispatched them to Ḥajjāj. He also sent very costly gifts and presents for Ḥajjāj and the caliph al-Walīd, and he hoped that these orphans and widows would surely speak well of him to Ḥajjāj.

The ships set out from Sarandīp and, sailing close to the coast, headed for the Persian Gulf, so that from there they might come ashore and reach Ḥajjāj at Kufa with the gifts and presents. On the way a storm of contrary winds drove these ships into Daybul, the port of Sindh. There the soldiers of the raja of Sindh, named Dahir, plundered the ships and imprisoned those aboard. When Ḥajjāj learned of this state of affairs he wrote to the raja of Sindh that those ships were on their way to him, that he should punish the robbers as they deserved and send him the people of the ships along with the stolen travel goods. From there the raja wrote Ḥajjāj a most arrogant and unreasonable reply. Ḥajjāj first sent ʿAbdullāh Aslamī towards Sindh with an army of six thousand; ʿAbdullāh reached Sindh and was martyred while fighting the army of Raja Dahir, and this expedition failed. The second time Ḥajjāj appointed a commander named Budayl; he too, with an army of six thousand, reached Budayl, but while fighting against Raja Jayb he fell from his horse and was martyred. On hearing this news Ḥajjāj was grieved still more.

The third time he sent Muḥammad ibn Qāsim — who was also his son-in-law, a young man of twenty-six or twenty-seven — towards Sindh with an army of six thousand Syrians. Syrian soldiers were sent with Muḥammad ibn Qāsim on this occasion because Ḥajjāj suspected that the Iraqi and Persian soldiers were in collusion with the idolaters. Muḥammad ibn Qāsim first conquered the province of Makrān, which the idolaters had occupied, and drove them out. Then he reached Daybul and laid siege to the city. He had a trench dug in front of his forces and drew up the ranks of the champions of Islam in good order, and mangonels too were set up at suitable places. In the middle of the city, in a very large temple, there was an idol of the Buddha; over the splendid building of the temple a very large tower had been built, and on the turret of the tower a very large red flag was fixed. When it was brought down with a mangonel, the courage of the people of the city collapsed. The Muslims then attacked the city with zeal and fervour and captured it. Next he advanced towards Nayrūn and Brahmanābād, and the ruler there made peace. Now Muḥammad ibn Qāsim pressed on towards Sindh, where Raja Dahir was. With Raja Dahir there had taken refuge not only Persians but also many Arabs who had rebelled against the caliph of the day or against the officers of the caliphate and had fled and taken shelter with Raja Dahir in Sindh; for this reason too an attack on Sindh was unavoidable. Raja Dahir was on the bank of the river Indus with the other rajas and a mighty army. The encounter took place in Sindh. In front of the idolaters' army elephants stood drawn up in ranks, and Raja Dahir himself was in the middle of the ranks, mounted on a white elephant, commanding the army. A bloody battle was fought between the two sides, and in the end Raja Dahir was killed and Muḥammad ibn Qāsim (may Allah have mercy on him) gained the victory. After this Muḥammad ibn Qāsim began to conquer the cities of Sindh one after another, until the whole country of Sindh and Multan was conquered at his hands. During the conquest of Sindh, Ḥajjāj's attention remained fixed entirely on Muḥammad ibn Qāsim; he called for reports daily and sent Muḥammad ibn Qāsim instructions daily.

Muḥammad ibn Qāsim proved himself a most kind and merciful conqueror towards the people of Sindh and a ruler who cherished his subjects. Examples of the tolerance, forbearance, generosity and liberality shown by this young victor can very rarely be found in the history of Islam. (Derived from Tārīkh-e Islām: 2/170; Tārīkh-e Millat: /602; and Islāmī Hind kī ʿAẓmat-e Rafta, p. 107).

Futūḥ al-Buldān states:

فحدثني منصور بن حاتم قال: داهر والذي قتله مصوران ببروص. (فتوح البلدان، ص ٤۱۹).

That is, Manṣūr ibn Ḥātim states that at Bharuch a memorial was set up bearing pictures of both Raja Dahir and the man who killed him. (Islāmī Hind kī ʿAẓmat-e Rafta, p. 111).
