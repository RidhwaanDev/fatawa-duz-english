### TITLE
Dealing with a company that trades in interest

### QUESTION
A person orders a machine through a company. Until the money is paid to the company, the company charges rent for the machine. The company, however, deals in interest with a bank: it takes money from the bank at interest, buys machines and the like with it, and then pays interest on that money. The question is whether it is permissible to buy a machine from this company or not, given that the purchasers pay no interest at all and this is purely the company's own private affair.
بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, if Zayd bought the machine for, say, one hundred thousand rand through the company, and the company paid the hundred thousand, and the company will now recover one hundred and twenty thousand in instalments, then this is interest, even if it is called rent. But if the company bought the machine for one hundred thousand and sold it to Zayd for, say, one hundred and twenty thousand on deferred payment, then this is a cost-plus sale and it is permissible.

And if the machine remains the property of the company while Zayd goes on paying it a fixed rent every month, and after a set period Zayd either returns the machine, or the company sells it to him at a reduced price or makes him a gift of it, there is room for this arrangement as well.

As for the company's interest-based business, that is its own doing and does not affect the purchase; for a non-Muslim company in non-Muslim countries buys machines with money obtained from interest, and we cannot compel such companies to follow the rulings of Islam, since there is no Islamic government there. Moreover, in this arrangement the Muslim is not directly involved in the dealing in interest, and he has no connection with the interest at all. There is therefore room for dealing with such a company.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
