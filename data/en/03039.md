### TITLE
The ruling on forbidding the cutting of trees on a mountain

### QUESTION
There is a mountain near a village on which various trees are growing. The men in charge of the village have placed a ban on cutting the trees on this mountain, but some people cut them secretly and say that these are among the things freely permitted, so the ban is unwarranted. Is it permissible to cut the trees in spite of the ban, or not?

### ANSWER
Since the people have accepted the men in charge of the village as their leaders, they now stand in the position of a ruler; and by the sharia it is binding and necessary upon a Muslim to obey a lawful ruler, so long as he does not order something contrary to the sharia. In matters contrary to the sharia, of course, they are not to be obeyed.

On this basis it will not be permissible to cut trees on the mountain that has been placed out of bounds. Consider the following parallels to this question:

(1) The principal of a madrasah: obeying him is binding and necessary, because in one respect he is like an officer and a ruler — so much so that if he is not obeyed he has the right to impose a discretionary punishment.

(2) The Noble Prophet ﷺ forbade hunting in a particular part of Madīnah al-Munawwarah and cutting the trees there. In the view of the Ḥanafīs what is meant by this is not a sanctuary in the technical sense; rather, he forbade it for a particular benefit. This shows that a ruler may forbid something otherwise permitted on grounds of benefit.

(3) In the view of the Ḥanafīs the sanctuary of Madīnah al-Munawwarah is not a sanctuary in the technical sense, but only in the linguistic sense. Hunting there and cutting trees there are all permissible; but if the ruler imposes a ban for some benefit — for instance, to preserve the beauty and adornment of the city — then obeying it is necessary.

باب حرم المدينة

عن أنس رضي الله تعالى عنه عن النبي ﷺ قال: المدينة حرم من كذا إلى كذا لا يقطع شجرها ولا يحدث فيها حدث فمن أحدث فيها حدثاً فعليه لعنة الله والملائكة والناس أجمعين

(رواه البخاري، رقم: ۱۸٦۷).

وقال الثوري وعبد الله بن المبارك وأبو حنيفة وأبو يوسف ومحمد: ليس للمدينة حرم كما كان لمكة فلا يمنع أحد من أخذ صيدها وقطع شجرها وأجابوا عن الحديث المذكور بأنه إنما قال ذلك لا لأنه لما ذكروه من تحريم صيد المدينة وشجرها بل إنما أراد بذلك بقاء زينة المدينة ليستطيبوها ويألفوها

وذلك كمنعه من هدم آطام المدينة وقال: إنها زينة المدينة على ما رواه الطحاوي

عن نافع عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه قال: نهي رسول الله ﷺ عن آطام المدينة أن تهدم، وفي رواية لا تهدموا الآطام فإنها زينة المدينة. وهذا إسناد صحيح. (عمدة القاري: ۷/۵٦۹، دار الحديث، ملتان).

Muntakhabāt al-Fatāwā states:

The principal of a madrasah stands in the place of the sultan with respect to the sultan's second attribute — the substance of which is the management of the general religious affairs of the Muslims. (Muntakhabāt Niẓām al-Fatāwā, p. 98, Iṣlāḥī Kutub Khānah, Deoband).
