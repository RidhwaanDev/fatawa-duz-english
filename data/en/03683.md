### TITLE
The ruling on taking part in a competition and receiving a prize from a company with an unlawful business

### QUESTION
Can Muslims take part in horse racing under the following conditions?

1. One has to buy one's own personal horse.

2. One has to go to the (NHA), that is, the horse-racing authority, and register the horse.

3. One has to keep a trainer to exercise the horse.

4. The trainer will select the horse for a competition, deciding in what grade of race it can run; then the entry fee is from 90 rand up to 40 thousand rand.

5. If the horse succeeds in coming between numbers one and five, a prize is given. This prize is sometimes fixed by way of gambling, and is sometimes from a third-party company. Now the matters to be enquired about are as follows:

Is permission given to take part in a horse race merely by paying the entry fee without laying a wager, or not? And if a Muslim takes part and is successful, is it correct to receive the prize from the company or not, given that nowadays most non-Muslim companies are involved in unlawful business? بینوا بالبرہان توجروا عند الرحمن ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, taking part in a horse race by paying an entry fee and receiving a prize from a third-party company upon being successful is permissible, because in exchange for the entry fee the right is obtained to race the horse in the place they have prepared, and in return for it the facilities of watching the contest and of sitting there and so forth are also obtained. Therefore the prize that will be received will not be regarded as being in exchange for the entry fee. Then, this prize is not given by the competitors themselves; rather it is from a third party who has not taken part in the competition. Yes, if the competitors have laid a wager among themselves, then this prize will come under gambling and will be impermissible.

Receiving a prize from non-Muslim companies is permissible. The reason is that whatever wealth non-Muslims acquire by carrying out interest-based dealings, void and defective sales and the like, or by selling wine and pork, enters into their ownership, and Muslims may accept that wealth from them by way of a gift. Yes, if they acquire it through theft, usurpation, robbery and the like, then taking it is not permissible; and companies do not do this.

However, if this prize is given by a Muslim and the greater part of his business is unlawful, then it is not permissible to take that wealth from them as a prize. If the prize has been received, then, if the owner is known, it is necessary to return it to the owner; otherwise it should be given away in charity without the intention of reward. And if the greater part of the Muslim company's business is lawful, then taking it and using it is permissible.

Kindly consider the proofs and references given below in order:

قال في البحر الرائق: والمسابقة بالفرس والإبل والأرجل والرمي جائزة، لقوله: لا سبق إلا في خف أو نعل أو حافر وأذن رسول الله ﷺ لسلمة بن الأكوع أن يسابق رجلاً كان لا يسابق أبداً فسبقه سلمة بن الأكوع وقال الزهري: كانت المسابقة بين أصحاب رسول الله ﷺ بالخيل والركاب والأرجل ولأن الغزاة يحتاجون إلى رياضة خيلهم وأنفسهم، وحرم شرط الجعل من الجانبين لا من أحد الجانبين

ولو قال واحد من الناس لجماعة من الفرسان أو للاثنين فمن سبق فله كذا من مال نفسه أو قال للرماة من أصحاب الهدف فله كذا جاز لأنه من باب التنفيل فإذا كان للتنفيل من بيت المال كالسلب ونحوه يجوز فما ظنك بخالص ماله. (تكملة البحر الرائق: ۸/۵۵۵، مسائل شتي، ط: دار المعرفة).

وقال في البدائع: وكذلك ما يفعله السلاطين وهو أن يقول السلطان لرجلين من سبق منكما فله كذا فهو جائز لما بينا أن ذلك من باب التحريض على استعداد أسباب الجهاد خصوصاً من السلطان فكانت ملحقة بأسباب الجهاد. (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/۲۰٦، سعيد).

(وكذا في فتاوى الشامي: ٦/٤٠٣، سعيد، والفتاوى الهندية: ٦/٤٤٥، وتبيين الحقائق: ٦/٢٢٧، ط: إمداديه، ملتان).

It is permissible and correct to accept unlawful wealth from the disbelievers as a gift or a prize. Consider: it is in Al-Hidāyah:

وإذا باع المسلم خمراً وأخذ ثمنها، وعليه دين، فإنه يكره لصاحب الدين أن يأخذه وإن كان البائع نصرانياً فلا بأس به. (الهداية: ٤/٤٧٠).

ʿAllāmah al-Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him) states in Al-Mabsūṭ:

وأما الخمر والخنزير فقد قيل: الحرمة بخطاب خاص في حق المسلمين وهو قوله تعالى: ﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا إنما الخمر والميسر﴾ [المائدة: ۹۰] إلى قوله تعالى: ﴿فهل أنتم منتهون﴾ [المائدة: ۹۱]، وقيل: ليس من ضرورة الحرمة سقوط المالية والتقوم فالمال قد يكون حراماً وقد يكون حلالاً وإنما تنبني المالية على التمول وهم يتمولون ذلك. (المبسوط: ٥/٣٩، إدارة القرآن).

Muntakhabāt-e Niẓām al-Fatāwā records:

Non-Muslims also carry on interest-based business and interest-based dealings among themselves; they also carry out many void and defective sales; they also make and sell wine and take its price; they also sell pork and take its price; and then they give this money to Muslims as wages, as prizes and so forth. Despite it being known that all this money is unlawful, taking this money is regarded in the Sharīʿah as permissible and not disliked, so long as it is not certain that this thing which he is selling is the very same thing which he brought by stealing it or by robbery, or that the price which he is giving is the very same money which, for example, he brought by stealing it or by robbery — that is, if it is the very item of theft, robbery and so forth, then it is not permissible. In short, taking all of this and using it remains correct. Now the question arises as to why the ruling of permissibility applies. There are several reasons for it, and several Sharʿī principles are its basis: for example, (a) by the change of ownership there is, in terms of the ruling, a change of the very substance; or (b) the principle of mixing and consumption, which is agreed upon. In the case of a department whose people of authority are purely non-Muslim or are mixed, and where the supreme authority is in the hands of a non-Muslim, and it on its own account carries on interest-based business and thereby earns a profit and obtains some amount, and then gives it as a gratuity to some Muslim employee, taking it will remain permissible, and, entering under the heading of a prize, it will be counted as wholesome and lawful. (منتخباتِ نظام الفتاوی، ص ٤٦٨، ط: اصلاحی کتب خانہ) ۔ For more, consider: (امداد الاحکام: ٤/٤٩٠) ۔

It is not permissible to accept a gift of unlawful and interest-based wealth from a Muslim. Consider: ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) states:

لو رأي المكاس مثلاً يأخذ من أحد شيئاً من المكس ثم يعطيه آخر ثم يأخذ من ذلك الآخر آخر فهو حرام. (فتاوى الشامي: ٥/٩٨، مطلب الحرمة تتعدد، سعيد).

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) states:

ويردونها على أربابها إن عرفوهم وإلا تصدقوا بها، لأن سبيل الكسب الخبيث التصدق إذا تعذر الرد على صاحبه. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/٣٨٥، كتاب الكراهية، فصل في البيع، سعيد).

ʿĀlamgīrī states:

ولا يجوز قبول هدية أمراء الجور لأن الغالب في مالهم الحرمة إلا إذا علم أن أكثر ماله حلال بأن كان صاحب تجارة أو زرع فلا بأس به لأن أموال الناس لا تخلو عن قليل حرام فالمعتبر الغالب، وكذا أكل طعامهم كذا في الاختيار شرح المختار. (الفتاوى الهندية: ٥/٣٤٢).

Yes, if it is not known for certain whether this is unlawful wealth, or it is mixed and the greater part is of lawful earning, then there will be room to accept the gift. Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him), after transmitting the passages of fiqh, states:

From these texts it is known that in the case of a person whose income is mixed of lawful and unlawful, what is predominant is to be taken into account. If the predominant part is lawful, then accepting his gift and hospitality is permissible; but if, with regard to a particular gift or food, it becomes known for certain that something unlawful is mixed in it, then accepting it is unlawful and eating it too is unlawful — though in the view of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) there is mixing and consumption, and for that reason there is room upon his statement, as will appear from some narrations. And if the predominant income is unlawful, then accepting the gift and the hospitality is unlawful. Yes, if he informs one regarding that food and gift that it is lawful, then it is permissible. (امداد الاحکام: ٤/٣٩٩) ۔
