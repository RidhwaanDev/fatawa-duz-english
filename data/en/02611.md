### TITLE
The ruling on wearing a sari

### QUESTION
Is it permissible for Muslim women in South Africa to wear the sari, which is the dress of the Hindu community, or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
The scholars and the senior authorities (may Allah have mercy on them) have differed about the sari. The upshot is that in a society where this dress is common there is room for it, provided that it covers the whole body properly; while in a society where it is not common but is regarded simply as the dress of the Hindus, it should not be worn. In South Africa this dress is not common but is peculiar to the Hindus, so such dress should be avoided — the more so as it does not cover the body properly. Consider the narration of Muslim Sharīf:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ صنفان من أهل النار لم أرهما قوم معهم سياط كأذناب البقر يضربون بها الناس ونساء كاسيات عاريات مميلات مائلات رؤوسهن كأسنمة البخت المائلة لا يدخلن الجنة ولا يجدن ريحها وإن ريحها ليوجد من مسيرة كذا وكذا. (رواه مسلم، رقم: ۲۱۲۸، باب النساء الكاسيات عاريات).

Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr says in Al-Tamhīd:

وأما معني قوله "كاسيات عاريات" فإنه أراد اللواتي يلبسن من الثياب الشيء الخفيف الذي يصف ولا يستر فهن كاسيات بالاسم عاريات في الحقيقة. (التمهيد: ۱۳/۲۰٤).

قال الإمام النووي رحمه الله: وقيل معناه: تستر بعض بدنها وتكشف بعضه إظهاراً للزينة لجمالها ونحوه. (شرح صحيح مسلم:).

قال الإمام البغوي رحمه الله في شرح السنة: وقيل: هن اللائي يسدلن الخمر من ورائهن، فتنكشف صدورهن، فهن كاسيات بمنزلة العاريات إذا كان لا يستر لباسهن جميع أجسامهن. (شرح السنة: ۱۰/۲۷۲).

Mullā ʿAlī al-Qārī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Mirqāt:

وقيل: يسترن بعض بدنهن ويكشفن بعضه إظهاراً لجمالهن وإبرازاً لكمالهن وقيل: يلبسن ثوباً رقيقاً يصف بدنهن وإن كن كاسيات للثياب عاريات في الحقيقة قال الطيبي رحمه الله: أثبت لهن الكسوة ثم نفاها لان الحقيقة الاكتساء ستر العورة فإذا لم يتحقق الستر فكأنه لا اكتساء. (مرقاة المفاتيح: ۱۱/۹۵).

Ibn al-Jawzī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Kashf al-Mushkil:

والثاني: أنهن يكشفن بعض أجسامهن فهن عاريات أي بعضهن منكشف. (كشف المشكل من حديث الصحيحين: ۱/۱۰۳۱، ط: الرياض).

Mawlānā ʿĀshiq Ilāhī (may Allah have mercy on him) says, commenting on this hadith:

One form of having cloth on the body and yet being naked is this: that there is only a little cloth on the body while most of it — and especially those limbs which modest women conceal from strange men — remains uncovered, as is the fashion in Europe (and in some cities of Asia, such as Bombay, Calcutta, Rangoon and Singapore), where the dress worn is a shirt or frock reaching to the knees, with either no sleeves at all or sleeves so short that they extend only a few inches from the shoulder, and with a neck so wide and open at the front and the back that half the back and half the chest can be seen. (فصل الخطاب فی مسئلۃ الحجاب، ص ۱۰۷، ط: ادارۃ المعارف) ۔

The gist of the statements of the commentators on hadith is that one meaning of "clothed yet naked" is that part of the body is uncovered and part is covered, so that the covering of what must be concealed is not complete. This meaning is found in the sari, for most of the body is covered while some parts of it — the chest, the stomach, the back and the forearms, for instance — remain uncovered.

Mawlānā Muḥammad Yūsuf Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

If the sari is worn in such a way that it covers the whole body, there is no harm in it. But nowadays scarcely one woman in a thousand wears a sari covering the whole body in this manner; and since the veiling required by the Sharīʿah cannot be achieved in a sari, it is not permissible for a woman to go out wearing a sari alone. (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ۸/۳٦٦ طبع جدید۔ و کفایت المفتی: ۹/۱٦۰، دار الاشاعت)۔

Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh says:

Since the sari is no longer the distinctive dress of non-Muslims — indeed in the eastern and southern regions of India Muslim women too commonly wear the sari — Muslim women may wear it. But to use a blouse that leaves the back and the stomach uncovered is a most sinful thing, and is contrary to modesty and shame as well. (کتاب الفتاوی: ٦/۹۱)۔

The gist of Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh's words is that where there is no resemblance to non-Muslims and the body is also fully covered, there will be room for it, and otherwise not. But Mawlānā Muḥammad Yūsuf Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) said that such a woman would scarcely be one in a thousand. Then, in our common usage, the sari is generally regarded as the dress of the Hindus. Muslim women should therefore avoid such dress.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
