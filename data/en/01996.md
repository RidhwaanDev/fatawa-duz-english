### TITLE
The ruling on taking payment for copyright

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to take payment for copyright or not? It generally takes three forms. (1) A publisher or press draws up a project of work on some subject and engages the services of scholars for it, a fee being agreed for them which is paid in return for that authorship and compilation, arrangement and research, or other scholarly work. This method is current mostly among internationally renowned presses and publishers.

(2) The author gives the publisher the right of publication of the book, but an agreement is made between them that on the printing of every new edition of the book the publisher will pay the author a fixed sum. This is called royalty.

(3) The author sells the right of publication of the book outright to the publisher or press in return for a substantial sum. In this way all rights of printing and publishing the book become attached to the publisher or press rather than to the author. In these forms, however, the publisher is bound both morally and legally to refrain from any kind of alteration, deletion or addition in the original book. Which of these forms is permissible in the Sharīʿah and which is impermissible?

### ANSWER
It is permissible to take payment for copyright. Of the forms mentioned in the question, the ruling on the first is that there is no dispute about its permissibility; its ruling is the same as that of payment for leading the prayer and for teaching. Moreover, the work will be the property of the publisher or press, since it has paid the wage for that work.

Concerning (2) and (3) there has previously been disagreement among the people of learning. At first most scholars called it impermissible, but in our own time the inclination of most scholars is towards permissibility, and the right of authorship and compilation is generally acknowledged as the author's right on the following grounds.

(1) In present-day custom, "the right of authorship" and the like are dealt with as property; its sale and purchase is current in the market on a large scale. And people's established practice has a large part in a thing's being counted as property, the details of which have already been given.

(2) The jurists have permitted the taking of a wage for teaching the Qurʾān, hadith, fiqh and the like, and the reason they have given for this is present here too — for example, on the basis of benefit, in that if this were not done the whole enterprise of teaching and learning would suffer. Likewise, if this right were not granted to the author despite his immense labour and toil, it would discourage him, for the author expends a great deal of his time and his mental and intellectual energy on this work; and the work of preserving and propagating the religion, and of research, could also suffer. This, then, is a benefit that cannot be disregarded.

(3) This is a right of priority, which is recognised in the sight of the Sharīʿah. A narration in Abū Dāwūd Sharīf states:

من سبق إلى ما لم يسبقه إليه مسلم فهوله". (رواه البيهقي في سننه الكبرى: ٦/۱٤۲، كتاب إحياء الموات. وأبو داود: ۲/٤۳۷، باب في اقطاع الأرضين).

(4) For a thing to be deemed property it must be capable of being secured, that is, fit to be safeguarded, and the securing of the rights in question is accomplished legally by means of registration.

(5) A further condition of property is that it be capable of being benefited from, and ample benefit is derived from this right.

(6) Giving these rights legal standing requires the expenditure of capital as well as labour and toil; they may therefore be made a means of earning.

See further: (Ḥuqūq awr un kī Kharīd o Farokht, pp. 208–215; Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/178–184; Fiqhī Maqālāt: 1/223–227; ʿIṭr-e-Hidāyah, pp. 343–344; and Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith, volume three).
