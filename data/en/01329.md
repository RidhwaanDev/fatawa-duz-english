### TITLE
Food served by the bride's family after the marriage contract

### QUESTION
Is it recommended, merely permissible, or an innovation for the bride's family to serve food after the marriage?

### ANSWER
If, while remaining within the limits of the sharīʿah, the bride's family wish of their own accord to feed the guests who come, that is a permissible matter. However, the feasts held nowadays on the occasion of weddings involve a great deal of ostentation and extravagance, which is entirely contrary to the temperament of the sharīʿah. Moderation in this is therefore absolutely necessary, or else it is more appropriate to bring the practice to an end altogether.

Consider what Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

The food that the bride's family serve to the wedding party is, in itself, permissible — provided it is unpremeditated or is given out of need, for example when the wedding party has come from out of town, and provided there is no extravagance in the food, no showing off or display, and no binding adherence to custom. (کفایت المفتی: ۵/۱۵۳، دار الاشاعت)۔

In another place it is recorded:

It is not obligatory, nor a sunnah, nor recommended for the bride's family to serve food to the wedding party or to the extended family. If they serve food of their own accord, without making it binding, it is permissible; and if they do not serve it, there is no obligation upon them. (کفایت المفتی: ۵/۱۵۷)۔

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

It is correct that the walīmah is to be held by the bridegroom or his guardians. But as for the people who come as guests to the house of the bride's family, whose purpose is to take part in the wedding and who have also been invited — where, after all, are they to go and eat? Feeding one's guest is a command of the sharīʿah, and the Prophet ﷺ emphasised it. However, a walīmah from the bride's side, in competition with the bridegroom's side, is not established. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۲/۱٤۲، مبوب و مرتب)۔

Kitāb al-Fatāwā states:

On the occasion of a marriage there is room for the bride's family also to offer hospitality, so long as there is no compulsion or pressure. At the marriage of his daughter Fāṭimah (may Allah be pleased with her), the Messenger of Allah ﷺ slaughtered a goat and invited the Muhājirīn and the Anṣār to it; and when the men had finished eating, food was sent to the blessed wives (may Allah be pleased with them) so that the women who came might eat there. This narration is reported in detail from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with them), and it covers four pages of Muṣannaf ʿAbd al-Razzāq. Thus this serving of food is not a sunnah, nor was it a general custom in the age of the Companions, so it is not appropriate to turn it into a custom either — although there is room for it. (کتاب الفتاوی: ٤/۴۱۷، نکاح میں دعوت اور ولیمہ کے احکام)۔

Consider the narration of Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) in Muṣannaf ʿAbd al-Razzāq:

(1) عبد الرزاق عن يحيي بن العلاء البجلي عن عمه شعيب بن خالد عن حنظلة بن سمرة بن المسيب عن أبيه عن جده عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنه قال: كانت فاطمة تذكر لرسول الله ﷺ فلا يذكرها أحد إلا صد عنه، حتى يئسوا منها فلقي سعد بن معاذ رضي الله عنه علياً فقال: إني والله ما أري رسول الله ﷺ يحبسها إلا عليك

. إلى قوله فانطلق علي رضي الله عنه، فقال: يا رسول الله متى تبنيني؟ قال: الثالثة إن شاء الله، ثم دعا بلالاً، فقال: يا بلال إني زوجت ابنتي ابن عمي، وأناْ أحب أن يكون من سنة أمتي إطعام الطعام عند النكاح، فأت الغنم، فخذ شاة، وأربعة إمداد أو خمسة، فاجعل لي قصعة لعلي أجمع عليها المهاجرين والأنصار، فإذا فرغت منها فآذني بها، فانطلق ففعل ما أمره، ثم أتاه بقصعة، فوضعها بين يبديه، فطعن رسول الله ﷺ في رأسها، ثم قال: ادخل علي الناس زفة زفة

حتى فرغ الناس، ثم عمد النبي ﷺ إلى ما فضل منها فتفل فيه، وبارك، وقال: يا بلال! احملها إلى أمهاتك، وقل لهن: كلن وأطعمن من غشيكن

(مصنف عبد الرزاق: ۵/٤۸٦. ٤۸۹، رقم ۹۷۸۲، باب تزويج فاطمة رضي الله تعالى عنها. ويحيي بن العلاء البجلي متروك).

In this narration the transmitter Yaḥyā ibn al-ʿAlāʾ al-Bajalī is abandoned, so this narration is not reliable.

(2) Some scholars have argued from the narration of the marriage of Umm Ḥabībah (may Allah be pleased with her): that the Prophet ﷺ wrote a letter to the Negus asking him to conduct his marriage to Umm Ḥabībah; that Khālid ibn Saʿīd acted as agent for the marriage; that the Negus performed the marriage; and that food was then eaten in that very gathering.

Consider what al-Mustadrak of al-Ḥākim states:

ثم أراد أن يقوموا فقال: اجلسوا فإن سنة الأنبياء عليهم الصلاة والسلام إذا تزوجوا أن يؤكل الطعام على التزويج، فدعا بطعام فأكلوا. (المستدرك للحاكم: ٤/۲٦/٦۷۷۰، ذكر أم حبيبة رضي الله عنه. والاستيعاب لا بن عبد البر: ۱/٦۲٦، أم حبيبة بنت أبي سفيان رضي الله عنه).

Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

قلت: وليس ذلك بوليمة، بل هو طعام التزويج، ويلتحق به ما تعارفه المسلمون من نثر التمر ونحوه في مجلس النكاح. (إعلاء السنن: ۱۱/۱۲).

But the Grand Muftī, Ḥaḍrat Muftī Kifāyat Allāh (may Allah have mercy on him), took it to be the walīmah meal, as has already been mentioned.

In sum, if the bride's family feed the guests something as a mark of honour, that is fine, but it is not a sunnah.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
