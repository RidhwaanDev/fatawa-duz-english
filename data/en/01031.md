### TITLE
Paying a poor man's debt from zakat through an agent

### QUESTION
Suppose a man is entitled to zakat because of his heavy debts, but if zakat is given to him he will squander it. May this man appoint an agent to pay his debt on his behalf?

### ANSWER
In the case described, if the poor man appoints an agent, and the agent takes the zakat money and pays off the debt on his behalf, this is permissible and the zakat is discharged. And if he has not appointed anyone as his agent but has merely given permission, the zakat is still discharged; it is as though the creditor took possession on the poor man's behalf and then put the money in his own pocket.

Consider what Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

ولو قضي دين حي فقير إن قضي بغير أمره لم يجز لأنه لم يوجد التمليك من الفقير لعدم قبضه وإن كان بأمره يجوز عن الزكاة لوجود التمليك من الفقير لأنه لما أمره به صار وكيلاً عنه في القبض فصار كان الفقير قبض الصدقة بنفسه وملكه من الغريم. (بدائع الصنائع: ۲/۳۹، ركن الزكاة، سعيد).

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

ولو قضي دين الفقير بزكاة ماله إن كان بأمره يجوز وإن كان بغير أمره لا يجوز. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۹۰، الباب السابع في المصارف، وكذا في الشامي: ۲/۳٤۵، سعيد).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

If a poor man's debt is paid out of zakat funds with his permission, that is permissible; it is not necessary to tell him that the money comes from zakat. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/250).

Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil states:

If a man is very heavily in debt, and there is a danger that if zakat money is given to him to pay off the debt he will use it up himself and not pay the debt, then the wealthy man should obtain the poor debtor's permission to pay his debt and settle the poor debtor's debt from his own zakat money. In that case both the poor man's debt and the wealthy man's zakat are discharged. (Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil: p. 113, Nuʿaymiyyah).
