### TITLE
The sharia ruling on funds received by Arabic madrasahs

### QUESTION
Arabic madrasahs receive obligatory charities as well as non-obligatory ones, that is, donations and the like. What is the ruling on this money? Does it leave the ownership of its owner or not? And once a year has passed, will zakat be due on it or not? Further, when is the zakat of the owners discharged — after the money has been spent, or once the administrators have taken possession of it?

### ANSWER
Three kinds of funds generally come into madrasahs:

(1) Donations, gifts, voluntary charities and the like, that is, funds in which transfer of ownership is not obligatory.

(2) Zakat, vows, expiations and the like, that is, funds in which transfer of ownership is obligatory.

(3) Funds which the donor earmarks for a particular purpose — a particular room, for example, or a particular floor covering.

The ruling on each of them is as follows:

(1) In donations too, that is, in funds in which transfer of ownership is not obligatory, the principals of madrasahs and their deputies are agents and representatives. But whatever rule of expenditure has been laid down and settled, within the bounds of the sharia, in consultation with the men of authority, it remains necessary to spend only in accordance with that rule. If there are no men of authority, or there are but no clear rule can be found regarding a particular item of expenditure, then the practice of the institution's earlier men of learning, integrity and responsibility will be examined and followed. The principal and the madrasah staff cannot act on their own opinion.

(2) As for zakat and similar funds, in which transfer of ownership is obligatory, in these the principals of madrasahs are the agents of the donor in one respect, and so the principal's taking possession is not in every respect the taking of possession by the person entitled. For this reason it will be necessary to give this money to students or to those entitled to zakat by way of transfer of ownership; it is neither correct nor permissible to spend it on any other purpose without such a transfer. This money should also be kept permanently separate from funds in which transfer of ownership is not obligatory. Moreover, in this money these people are also the agents and representatives of the students; so once the donors have handed it over they cannot take it back, nor will zakat be due on it after a year has passed, nor can it be said that once a large amount has been received it is impermissible to receive any more, nor will any person entitled to zakat be reckoned wealthy on account of it. Then the donors themselves differ: some state explicitly that the money is to be spent on the students, while others give it for the madrasah without any such stipulation. In the first case the transfer of ownership to the students must be kept in view; in the second it will be enough to have ownership transferred, without any difficulty, to any person in the madrasah who is entitled to zakat.

(3) As for funds which the donor designates for a particular purpose, the ruling is that in these the principal of the madrasah is simply the agent and representative of the donor. Such funds must therefore be kept entirely separate in madrasahs so that they are not mixed with other money or consumed, and it remains obligatory to spend them in accordance with the instruction and wish of the donor.
