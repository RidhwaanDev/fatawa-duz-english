### TITLE
Selling animals by weight

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to sell animals by weight or not? For an animal sometimes makes itself heavy and sometimes light, so that it is difficult to establish its full weight — yet this is the current practice. Kindly inform us of the ruling of the sharia and earn a great reward.

### ANSWER
With regard to selling animals by weight, one passage from the author of al-Hidāyah suggests that it is not permissible:

قال في الهداية: لأن الحيوان لا يوزن عادة ولا يمكن معرفة ثقله بالوزن لأنه يخفف نفسه مرة ويثقل أخري. (الهداية: ۳/۸۳، باب الربوا).

That is, by general custom animals are not sold by weight, and weighing cannot establish the animal's bulk either, because an animal sometimes makes itself light and sometimes makes itself heavy.

In the present age, however, it is the general custom to sell animals by weight; and not knowing the exact bulk is a slight uncertainty. A slight uncertainty in trade that is not "مفضية إلى النزاع" can be borne with and does not invalidate the contract — as appears from the words of Shāh Ṣāḥib, that an uncertainty which is not "مفضية إلى النزاع" does not invalidate the contract.

Consider the words of Shāh Ṣāḥib (1352 AH):

قلت: إن الناس يعاملون في أشياء تكون جائزة فيما بينهم على طريق المروءة والإغماض، فإذا رفعت إلى القضاء يحكم عليها بعدم الجواز وذلك لأن العقود على نوعين: نحو: يكون معصية في نفسه، وذا لا يجوز مطلقاً، ونحو آخر: لا يكون معصية وإنما يحكم عليه بعدم الجواز لا فضاء ه إلى المنازعة فإذا لم تقع فيه منازعة جاز. (فيض الباري: ۳/۲۸۹، كتاب الوكالة).

ʿAllāmah Akmal al-Dīn al-Bābartī (may Allah have mercy on him) (786 AH) says that in goods subject to the law of interest, uncertainty about the quantity invalidates the contract, because of the possibility of interest, whereas in goods not subject to the law of interest it does not invalidate it.

قال في العناية: وهذا إنما يستقيم إذا لم تكن الأعراض ربوية، أما إذا كانت ربوية فجهالة المقدار تمنع الصحة لاحتمال الربا. (شرح العناية على هامش فتح القدير: ٦/۲٦۰، دار الفكر).

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

If the buyer and the seller are agreed on buying and selling an animal by weight, then buying and selling the animal by weight, whether for cash or for goods of another kind, are both permissible, provided that the rate per kilogram has been settled and that the price of the animal has been fixed after weighing it. This works as follows: a buyer needs, say, a goat. He goes to the trader and picks out one goat from among the goats, and the trader tells him that the rate for this goat is fifty rupees a kilogram; he then weighs the goat in front of the buyer and tells him that it is, say, twenty kilograms. If the buyer now accepts it, the sale is concluded, and a purchase and sale carried out in this way is permissible in the sharia. (جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام: ۱/۱۱۸، از مفتی احسان اللہ صاحب) ۔

The former Grand Mufti of Pakistan, Ḥaḍrat Muftī Walī Ḥasan Ṭonkī (may Allah have mercy on him) (1415 AH), says:

In the present age the view of Imam Muhammad (may Allah have mercy on him) is the better one, because nowadays animals too have become goods sold by weight: chickens and the like are sold by weight. For the real purpose of weighing is that an unknown thing should not be sold and that the weight should be known at the time of the sale. (درس الہدایہ: ۲۸۳) ۔

Muftī Niẓām al-Dīn Ṣāḥib says:

If in selling chickens in this way (by weight) it is the chickens themselves that are meant to be bought and sold — that is, the chickens are made the object of the sale and not merely their meat — then, since the actual object of the sale (the chickens) will be known, specified and present, this sale will remain permissible. (منتخباتِ نظام الفتاوی: ۱/۲٤۵) ۔

Muftī Rashīd Ṣāḥib Ludhiyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) (1421 AH) says:

A chicken's breathing makes no appreciable difference to its weight, so this is a slight uncertainty that does not lead to dispute. Since selling chickens in this way has become the general custom, there is no possibility of dispute in it, and therefore this sale is permissible. (احسن الفتاوی: ٦/٤۹۷) ۔

Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil states:

It is permissible and correct to buy and sell fish, chickens, goats, buffaloes and the like alive by weight in exchange for money. مستفاد از اوجز المسالک: ۵/۱۰۵، (ایضاح المسائل، ص ۱۵۸) ۔

The sum of the matter is that three objections arise concerning the sale of animals by weight:

Objection (1): Animals are not sold by weight but by number.

Answer (1): In the time of the early generations they were not sold by weight; in our own day they have become goods sold by weight, as is clear from the passages of the fatwa works quoted above.

Objection (2): Animals sometimes make themselves light and sometimes heavy.

Answer (2): The answer is that this is a slight uncertainty which is not "مفضية إلى النزاع". In goods subject to the law of interest such uncertainty is indeed harmful, but here it is not a case of selling a thing for its own kind.

Objection (3): The weight is unknown.

Answer (3): This uncertainty is removed by weighing in the presence of the two contracting parties, and thereby any dispute is done away with.
