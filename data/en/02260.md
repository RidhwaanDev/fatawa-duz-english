### TITLE
How to earn profit on Islamic principles

### QUESTION
If a bank or a company takes money from people in accordance with Islamic principles, earns a profit on that money, and pays the profit to the people whose money it is, how should this be done?

### ANSWER
The company or bank should take the money from people on a muḍārabah basis. Those who place their money with it will be in the position of the owner of the capital, while the company or bank will be designated the working partner — provided that they actually carry on trade. The profit will be divided between them according to a particular agreed ratio: for instance, 40 per cent for the owner of the capital and 60 per cent for the working partner, that is, the bank. Now, it is obvious that people do not all deposit and withdraw their money on the same dates, so in this case the profit should be divided on the basis of "الحساب الیومی", an accounting based on the daily product (Daily Product Basis): each person is given the average share of the profit calculated according to the number of days his money remained in the bank.

See what al-Hidāyah states:

المضاربة عقد يقع على الشركة بمال من أحد الجانبين ومراده الشركة في الربح وهو يستحق بالمال من أحد الجانبين والعمل من الجانب الآخر ولا مضاربة بدونها. (الهداية: ۳/۲۵۷، كتاب المضاربة).

In earning profit, however, it is essential to observe the following basic principles of partnership and muḍārabah.

(1) It is not permissible in the sharia to fix the profit as a proportion of the capital. The correct sharia method of fixing profit is to fix a percentage of whatever profit is actually made.

(2) The partners may settle whatever ratio of profit they wish by mutual agreement; the profit need not be divided in proportion to the capital. A partner who has stipulated that he will not work, however, may not receive a share of the profit greater than the proportion of his capital.

(3) Different rates may be settled for different partners where the profit is concerned, but this is not permissible where loss is concerned: the loss will in every case be in proportion to the capital. The jurists express this as follows:

"الربح على ما اصطلحوا عليه، والوضيعة بقدر رأس المال".

For further detail see: (Islām awr Jadīd Maʿīshat o Tijārat; Islāmī Fiqh, volume two; and Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil, volume five).

An objection and its answer:

An objection is raised against "الحساب الیومی", namely that dividing the profit in this way is an approximate division and not a division of the capital's actual profit, and that the danger in it is that some part of Zayd's actual profit may pass to ʿUmar.

The answer: in a partnership the partners' funds are mixed together as undivided shares, so when the profit is divided the actual profit of each is not looked at; rather, the total profit earned from the total capital is divided. Otherwise it would be equally possible that no profit at all was earned from Zayd's capital and that the profit came only from ʿUmar's capital. In the sharia, therefore, an exact division is not required; an approximate one is sufficient.

For the details see: (Islām awr Jadīd Maʿīshat o Tijārat, by Ḥaḍrat Muftī Taqī Ṣāḥib ʿUthmānī, may his shade endure).
