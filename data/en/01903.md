### TITLE
The ruling on selling a television

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to sell one's own television or not? And is the money obtained from it lawful or not?

### ANSWER
The noble sharia has declared the use of instruments of amusement and play, singing and music-making, musical instruments and the like to be a cause of sin, and this is the predominant use to which a television is put. Keeping a television in the house is therefore not correct. The money obtained from selling one is nevertheless lawful, and money earned from repairing one is lawful as well. If, however, one wishes to sell a television, it should be sold to a non-Muslim, since some scholars have declared the sale of a television to be impermissible.

See what Mawlānā Mujībullāh Nadwī says:

Instruments that were made from the outset only for amusement and play or for singing and music-making — such as the tablah, the sitar, the harmonium and so on — are impermissible, and buying and selling them is impermissible too. As for the tape recorder and the radio, they are used for singing and music, yet in themselves they were not made for that. The television and the video, however, are built so that people may also see pictures of the person speaking, or of the singers or the players; and it is obvious that pictures of women who are not one's close relatives are also viewed and displayed on them. For this reason keeping one, buying one and selling one cannot be permissible in any way, however widespread it may become.

ولو أمسك في بيته شيئاً من المعازف والملاهي كره ويأثم وإن كان لا يستعملها

(Islāmī Fiqh: 2/388).

Mufti Rashīd Aḥmad Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Aḥsan al-Fatāwā:

They bear a complete resemblance to the singing slave-girl; indeed, they are themselves singing slave-girls. So, according to the stronger view, selling and repairing videos and televisions is strictly disliked; and even if the weaker view that it is only mildly disliked were adopted, taking up this trade would still be strictly disliked. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 6/543).

Certain other scholars say that selling a television is permissible and that the income from it is lawful as well, though taking it up as a trade is not good. This latter view appears to be the stronger one.

See what Shāmī states:

قوله معزياً للنهر قال فيه من باب البُغاة وعلم من هذا أنه لا يكره بيع مالم تقم المعصية به كبيع الجارية المغنية والكبش النطوح والحمامة الطيارة والعصير والخشب ممن يتخذ منه المعازف وأما في بيوع الخانية من أنه يكره بيع الأمرد من فاسق يعلم أنه يعصي به مشكل. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۳۹۱، فصل في البيع، سعيد).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Mufti Taqī Ṣāḥib says:

The sets fitted at airports are also televisions, but they are used as monitors or as closed-circuit screens, and that is a permissible use of them. So in itself the sale of a television is not unlawful. Even so, one cannot advise anyone to take up selling them. It is like what you have read about the cupper's earnings, concerning which the Prophet ﷺ said,

"كسب الحجام خبيث"

yet he did not call it impermissible. It is permissible in the sharia, but along with that he said that it is not a good trade. Since a television is mostly used for impermissible purposes, taking up its sale as a trade is not good, and no Muslim should be advised to do so. But to call it outright unlawful, with the result that the income from it becomes unlawful, is not correct. (Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil: 4/14).

Fiqh al-Muʿāmalāt states:

The ruling of the sharia on this matter is that, since the predominant use of the television is by impermissible means, and since it at present involves countless religious and worldly harms and corruptions, the basic ruling is precisely this: that it is neither permissible to keep a television in the house nor permissible to buy and sell it. Even so, in the present age a permissible use of it is also possible — for example, using it to view the movement of inanimate things such as buildings, sites, parks and seas, or scenes and pictures of sunrise and sunset and the like; or using it for checking baggage and the like, and for announcing aircraft timetables and other announcements; or using it in security arrangements and so forth. Therefore, if a television is sold to someone who is buying it for the permissible purposes mentioned above, the sale is permissible and its price is without doubt lawful. But if the television is sold to a person about whom it is most likely that the buyer will use it for impermissible purposes, then selling it to him is not permissible and is sinful, since it involves assisting in sinful acts; and in that case the price received is lawful but with dislike attaching to it.

في خلاصة الفتاوى (۳/۱۰۰): وبيع الغلام الأمرد ممن يعلم أنه ممن يعصي الله يكره لأنه إعانة على معصية"

There is also another permissible way of selling a television, namely that all its parts be separated and those parts sold; this method too is correct. (Fiqh al-Muʿāmalāt: 1/76).

See also further: (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/237. Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 16/129, arranged by chapters. Taqrīr-e Tirmidhī, vol. 1, 223).
