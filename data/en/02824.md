### TITLE
The ruling on keeping the name Abrār al-Ḥaqq

### QUESTION
A man has named his son Abrār al-Ḥaqq, that is, the abrār of Allah — whereas a single person is not abrār but barr. Is it correct, then, to call one man abrār or not?

### ANSWER
A plural word is sometimes used of a single person by way of intensification, or as a good omen, as though that person were equal to a whole company — just as the word ummah is used for Ibrāhīm (peace be upon him) in the Noble Qur'an.

قال الله تعالى: ﴿إن إبراهيم كان أمة قانتاً لله حنيفاً﴾

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) gave the meaning of ummah as جامع الکمالات. (Maʿārif al-Qurʾān: 5/405).

Imam al-Qurṭubī states:

والأمة الرجل الجامع للخير. (تفسير قرطبي: ۱۰/۱۹۷).

Tafsīr al-Khāzin states:

سمي إبراهيم عليه السلام أمة لأنه اجتمع فيه من صفات الكمال وصفات الخير الأخلاق الحميدة ما اجتمع في أمة، ومنه قول الشاعر:
ليس على الله بمستنكر

٭

أن يجمع العالم في واحد
(تفسير الخازن: ٤/۱۲۲، ط: دار الفكر).

See further:

(تفسير النسفي، وروح المعاني: ۱٤/۲٤۹، وتفسير الفخر الرازي: ۱۰/۱۳٦، وتفسير السمرقندي: ۲/۲۵٤).

In the same way Kaʿb ibn Mātiʿ al-Ḥimyarī is called al-Aḥbār. Aḥbār is the plural of ḥibr, and a ḥibr is a God-fearing scholar; he was so called because his piety was equal to that of many God-fearing men.

See:

(سير اعلام النبلاء: ۳/٤۸۹، وتهذيب الكمال: ۲٤/۱۸۹، و ۱۹۲).

Likewise ʿUbaydullāh Aḥrār is called Aḥrār. Aḥrār is the plural of ḥurr, and it means one who is free from the love of the world.

His name was Khwājah Naṣīr al-Dīn ʿUbaydullāh Aḥrār. He was born on 4 Ramaḍān al-Mubārak 8066 AH, corresponding to 1490 CE, in Uzbekistan, and he died on Monday 26 Rabīʿ al-Awwal. He was an elder of the Naqshbandī order.

Likewise Khwājah Niẓām al-Dīn is called Awliyāʾ. See: (Bazm-e Ṣūfiyyah, p. 218).
