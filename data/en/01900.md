### TITLE
A study of selling non-foodstuffs before taking possession

### QUESTION
Traders sometimes order certain goods from abroad and sell them before they arrive. Is doing this permissible or not?

### ANSWER
In the view of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), selling movable goods before taking possession of them is impermissible. It is, however, correct for a person to make a promise with the buyer and then sell the goods once they have arrived; or the person who ordered the goods may appoint someone in the other country as his agent to take possession, and once that agent has taken possession he may sell the goods on — though in that case the buyer will have the option of inspection.

In the view of Imām Mālik (may Allah have mercy on him) and Imām Aḥmad (may Allah have mercy on him), however, selling non-foodstuffs before taking possession is permissible and correct; and where the difficulty is widespread, there appears to be room to give the ruling on that view.

The hadith master of our age, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Sayyid Muḥammad Yūsuf Banūrī (may Allah have mercy on him), writes:

The senior scholars of our own age (may Allah have mercy on them) have resolved the difficulties surrounding the annulment of marriage in just this way, and the later Ḥanafīs did the same in the question of the missing husband. It will, however, be necessary to avoid combining positions in a way that no one school allows, and the deliberate pursuit of concessions must not be made an aim. For example, among the questions of transactions there is selling before taking possession, in which the whole trading class is caught up today. The situation should now be examined and fully assessed: if this difficulty is genuine, if present-day society is truly hard pressed, and if there is no other way out, then the ruling will be given according to the Mālikī school, namely that the impermissibility of selling before taking possession is confined to foodstuffs. On this question the Ḥanbalī school is like the Mālikī school, and the hadith explicitly mentions food alone:

"نهي رسول الله ﷺ عن بيع الطعام قبل أن يستوفيه". (سنن)

Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) and Imām al-Shāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) prohibited the rest of the goods by analogy with food. (Bayyināt, Rabīʿ al-Thānī 1383 AH / September 1963, under the heading Fikr-o-Naẓar, pp. 4–5; quoted from Ghayr Sūdī Bankārī by Ḥaḍrat Mufti Taqī ʿUthmānī Ṣāḥib, p. 291).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

Regarding a defective sale, this too should be borne in mind: if the defect in the sale arose because of a right of the sharia itself and involves some sinful matter, then in every case it is a cause of sin. But if it arose out of the fear of a dispute, and no dispute actually arises, then although the sale will be defective in terms of judicial ruling, it will be correct and valid in terms of a person's conscience before Allah. On this point the following statement of Mawlānā Anwar Shāh Ṣāḥib is eye-opening:

إن من البيوع الفاسدة ما لو أتي بها أحد جازت ديانة وإن كانت فاسدة قضاءً وذلك لأن الفساد قد يكون لحق الشرع بأن اشتمل العقد على مآثم فلا يجوز بحال وقد يكون الفساد لمخالفة التنازع ولا يكون فيه شيء آخر يوجب الإثم فذلك إن لم يقع فيه التنازع جاز عندي ديانة وإن بقي فاسداً قضاءً لارتفاع علة الفساد وهي المنازعة ويدل عليه مسائلهم في باب المضاربة والشركة فإنها ربما تكون فاسدة مع أن الربح يكون طيباً وراجع "الهداية" ونبه الحافظ ابن تيمية رحمه الله في رسالته على أن من البيوع ما لا يقع فيه النزاع فتكون تلك جائزة فإذا أدخلتها في الفقه وجدتها محظورة لأن أكثر أحكام الفقه تكون من باب القضاء والديانات فيها قليلة وإنما يصار إلى القضاء بعد النزاع فإذا لم يقع النزاع ولم يرفع الأمر الي القاضي نزل حكم الديانة لا محالة فيبقي الجواز. (فيض الباري: ۳/۲۵۸).

For this reason it is the view of the present writer that the prohibition of "selling before taking possession" rests on a right of the servant, not on a right of the sharia, and that its defectiveness is in terms of judicial ruling, not in terms of conscience before Allah. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/213–214).
