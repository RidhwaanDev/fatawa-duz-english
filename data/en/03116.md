### TITLE
A brief proof that the kurta with side slits is closer to the Sunnah

### QUESTION
In Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā, volume seven, you have written a detailed and fully argued ruling concerning the kurta that is closest to the Sunnah. Praise be to Allah, I have looked at it, but the long discussion was beyond my grasp. I therefore request you to tell me briefly which kurta is closest to the Sunnah, and to give its brief proof as well, so that it may be understood quickly and there be no need to read the lengthy discussion. The long discussion I leave to those with a taste for it and an aptitude for research.

### ANSWER
In Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā, volume seven, we wrote precisely this: that the kurta with a slit is closer to the Sunnah. The proof of it is that a shirt has a gore (dikhrīṣ), and the definition of dikhrīṣ is as follows:

الدخريص: الشَّقُّ الذي يفعل في قميص الحي ليتسع في المشي. (التعريفات الفقهية، ص ۱۷۷).

Here شَقّ is with a fatḥah, just as in the definition of jayb:

الجيب: الشَّقُّ النازل على الصدر. (التعريفات الفقهية، ص ۱۷۷).

In the noble Qur'an Allah Most High says:

﴿علي جيوبهن﴾

In explaining this, Shaykh Abū al-ʿAbbās Aḥmad ibn Muḥammad al-Shādhilī al-Fāsī writes in his celebrated commentary "البحر المديد":

الجيب: وهو شَقُّ القميص من ناحية الصدر. (البحر المديد: ۵/۱۰٦، ط: دار الكتب العلمية، بيروت).

The jurists too have used the word shaqq in defining jayb. See the following:

قال في البحر الرائق: والمراد بالجيب الشق النازل على الصدر. (البحر الرائق: ۲/۱۷۵).

وقال في مراقي الفلاح: وهو (الجيب) الشق النازل على الصدر. (مراقي الفلاح مع الطحطاوي، ص ۵۷۷، ط: قديمي).

وقال في فتح القدير: يراد بالجيب الشق النازل على الصدر. (۲/۱۱۵، ط: دار الفكر).

وفي فتاوى الأزهر: ولا جيب، وهو الشق النازل على الصدر لأنه لحاجة الحي. (فتاوى دار الافتاء المصرية: ۵/٤۵۹).

Here shaqq means a slit or an opening; it does not mean that the collar is closed by attaching an extra piece of cloth. The very same word has been used with regard to the shirt as well.

Some people read shiqq in the statement of the jurists with a kasrah on the shīn and take it to mean an extra piece. It should be borne in mind that shiqq with a kasrah on the shīn carries two meanings. (1) Hardship. Allah Most High says:

لم تكونوا بالغيه إلا بشِق الأنفس

that is, you could not have reached it save with hardship to yourselves. Likewise it has come in the hadith of Umm Zarʿ that a woman said:

وجدني في أهل غنيمة بشق

that is, my husband found me among a few sheep and goats, in a state of hardship.

(2) The second meaning is "a piece". It comes in the hadith:

اتقوا النار ولو بشق تمرة،

that is, guard yourselves against the Fire, even if it be with a piece of a date. As for shaqq with a fatḥah on the shīn, it means to tear and to split, and this meaning is well known:

ألم تروا إلى الميت إذا شق بصره

that is, do you not see the dead man when his eyes become fixed and open? And in:

إذا السماء انشقت، اقتربت الساعة وانشق القمر

it is this same meaning that is intended.

Now, on the face of it, in the statement of the jurists:

الدخريص هو الشق الذي يفعل في القميص

the word is with a fatḥah on the shīn, because in a definition clear words ought to be used; it is not preferable to use a word bearing two meanings, and shiqq has become such a word, whereas shaqq in the sense of a slit is not ambiguous. Moreover, the word:

يفعل

is also a proof of this, for had the meaning been "a piece", it would have said:

يوضع

or:

يخاط

And if some scholars insist that it is shiqq with a kasrah on the shīn, even then this does no harm to the well-known kurta, for shiqq would then mean the extra piece that is slit open. Whether it is shiqq with a kasrah or shaqq with a fatḥah, the sense of splitting is present in it; shiqq is used for hardship precisely because hardship, as it were, splits a person apart. It is for this reason that the gore with a slit is what is well known in the shirts of our elders.

The upshot of the above discussion is that in the shirt there is a slit on both sides, on account of which there is ease and comfort in walking and in sitting down and standing up; and this shirt, in our view, is closer to the Sunnah. As for the round one, which is called the Arab jubbah, its use too is permissible and correct, and not impermissible. واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم بالصواب۔
