### TITLE
Zakat on a company's reserve fund

### QUESTION
After trading for a year, a company works out its annual profit and keeps back part of it as a reserve, so that any future loss can be made good, and the rest of the profit is divided among the shareholders. The question is whether zakat is due on this reserve that the company has kept back — that is, will the shareholder pay zakat on it or not — given that it is not in his possession and he has no right to dispose of it?

### ANSWER
In the case described, zakat on the part of the profit that the company has kept back as a reserve is also due from the shareholder, because it has the standing of a strong debt. Further, if the company has put the reserve back into its capital and distributed the rest of the profit, then, since in a trading share company zakat is due on both the capital and the profit, the shareholders will pay the zakat on the capital along with the zakat on the profit.

Consider what Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states:

In the light of the rulings and principles of fiqh, zakat is due on the value of the capital and the profit as they stand at the time the zakat is paid, since in trade goods zakat is due on both the capital and the profit. The market price is not taken into account here. If, however, a person's business is precisely the buying and selling of shares, then zakat is due according to the market price of those shares at the time the zakat is paid. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 6/251, Idārat al-Qurʾān wa al-ʿUlūm al-Islāmiyyah).

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

أما القوي فهو الذي وجب بدلاً عن مال التجارة كثمن عرض التجارة من ثياب التجارة وعبيد التجارة أو غلة مال التجارة ولا خلاف في وجوب الزكاة فيه. (بدائع الصنائع: ۲/۱۰، سعيد).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

Suppose a partner's original stake in the partnership was one hundred rupees. Part of it went into buildings and equipment, and no zakat is due on that; part of it went into trade, and zakat is due on that together with its profit — whether the whole of that profit came to the partner, or some of it was distributed and the remainder added to the capital. For example: of the hundred rupees, twenty go into buildings and equipment and eighty into trade; on that eighty there is a profit of fifteen rupees, of which ten go to the partner and five are put back into the capital. Zakat is then due on 95 rupees. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 2/21).
