### TITLE
Zakat on money deposited for the Hajj

### QUESTION
A man in Pakistan or India deposited money for the Hajj in Jumādā al-Thāniyah, and his Hajj application was approved. He had still not set out for the Hajj by Ramadan — and Ramadan is the month in which his zakat falls due — and he then travelled in Dhū al-Qaʿdah. Is zakat binding on that money in Ramadan or not? And does it make a difference whether the Hajj is the obligatory one or a voluntary one?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, zakat is binding on whatever money he received back after Ramadan. Whatever the government kept for the necessary costs of the Hajj is exempt from zakat; that is, no zakat is binding on it.

Consider what Radd al-Muḥtār states:

إذا أمسكه لينفق منه كل ما يحتاج به فحال الحول وقد بقي معه منه نصاب فإنه يزكي ذلك الباقي وإن كان قصده الإنفاق منه أيضاً في المستقبل لعدم استحقاق صرفه إلى حوائجه الأصلية وقت حولان الحول، بخلاف ما إذا حال الحول وهو مستحق الصرف إليها. (شامي: ۲/۲٦۲، سعيد).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

There is no zakat on the money handed over for travel fares and for the fees of the guide and the like. As for the surplus which will be returned to him in the form of currency, zakat is obligatory on however much of it is still left on the first of Ramadan, but not on what has already been spent.

Some muftis hold that zakat is obligatory on the whole amount; see Khayr al-Fatāwā: 3/474, and elsewhere.

But when a man deposits money for the Hajj and receives in return the right to the Hajj arrangements, that money has passed out of the pilgrim's ownership. Zakat will be due on whatever is returned to him, but once money has left his ownership there is no zakat on it. The case is like that of a man who obtains the right to use a canal for irrigating his field and pays money in return: that money has left his ownership. Al-Hidāyah holds the sale of water rights permissible; and if a man pays another money in return for a right of way, that money has left his ownership. Or again, if a year's rent is paid in advance under a lease, that money too has left his ownership. Or in a contract of manufacture (istiṣnāʿ) a man places an order for shoes: even though they are not yet made, once he has paid the price the money has left his ownership. This case resembles a lease in that the government stands in the position of the hired party and this man in the position of the hirer; and if in a lease the wage is paid in advance, that wage passes out of the hirer's ownership.

Consider what al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

ثم الأجرة تستحق بإحدى معانٍ ثلاثة: إما بشرط التعجيل، أو بالتعجيل، أو باستيفاء المعقود عليه، فإذا وجد أحد هذه الأشياء الثلاثة، فإنه يملكها كذا في شرح الطحاوي. (الفتاوى الهندية: ٤/٤۱۳، كتاب الإجارة، في بيان متى تجب الأجرة).

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

لأنهما لما شرطا تعجيل البدل لزم اعتبار شرطهما لقوله عليه السلام "المسلمون على شروطهم" وملك الآجر البدل حتى تجوز له هبته والتصدق به والإبراء عنه. (بدائع الصنائع: ٤/۲۰۳، فصل في حكم الإجارة، سعيد).

The government has therefore become the owner of that money, and no zakat is due from the pilgrim on it. In this matter we follow Muftī Rashīd Aḥmad, the author of Aḥsan al-Fatāwā.

Kitāb al-Fatāwā states:

The fares for the Hajj journey and the unavoidable expenses of staying in Makkah al-Mukarramah fall within a person's basic needs, and zakat is not necessary on them. Money over and above this, which the pilgrim spends on the Hajj journey as he himself sees fit, does not fall within basic needs, and zakat will be necessary on it. (کتاب الفتاوی: تیسرا حصہ ص۲۷٤)۔
