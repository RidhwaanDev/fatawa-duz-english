### TITLE
Taking water from a mosque or madrasah to one's home

### QUESTION
What is the ruling on taking water from a mosque or a madrasah home? Aḥsan al-Fatāwā records that it is impermissible; what standing does that have?

### ANSWER
Taking water from a mosque or a madrasah home is permissible where the endowers installed the supply for the general benefit of the public and gave the people of the neighbourhood permission to draw water from it. If the endowers set it aside for the mosque alone, then carrying it off is not correct; but if permission has been given, it is correct. As a general rule people do take water from the mosque well and the endowers have that in mind when they make the endowment. Likewise, where the water comes in from outside, permission is normally given to take as much as one needs.

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

If the washroom lies within the boundaries of the mosque, it is not permissible for the general public to use it; only the imam, the muezzin and those employed in the service of the mosque may use it. But if it was installed for the general benefit of the public before the land was fully endowed for the needs of the mosque, then everyone is permitted to take water from it, provided the mosque is not soiled and the worshippers are not disturbed or troubled by it. (احسن الفتاوی: ٦/٤٤۷)۔

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

The water of the tank is reserved for the mosque. It is not correct to give the people of the neighbourhood permission to draw water from it; it is also a cause of disputes. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ٦/۹۰)۔

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

If there is a well or a tap in the mosque, may its water be used only by the worshippers for ablution for prayer, or may other people — the residents of the neighbourhood — also use it for their necessary household needs?

The water of such a well may also be used for purposes other than prayer, but care is necessary. That is, if the well stands on the floor of the mosque, it must be seen to that the mosque floor is not soiled with impurity. Further, it is not correct to use the mosque's bucket and rope; if the person who provided the bucket and rope gave general permission, then it is correct. The mosque's tap should not be used so heavily or so roughly that it soon breaks down. And if it was installed out of the mosque's own income, it should not be used for anything beyond the needs of prayer. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ٦/۱٦۰)۔

The same work says in another place:

The people of the neighbourhood may take water from this tap, but they should use it carefully, and if it breaks down they should also have it repaired. It should not happen that the residents draw the water while the cost of repairs falls on the mosque. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۵/۱۷۸)۔

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

Question: Out of caution and for the sake of cleanliness, the trustee of the mosque reserves the mosque's well for the worshippers; the general public of the neighbourhood are not permitted to carry water to their homes, and the water vessels they bring are smashed. Please inform us of the ruling of the sharia.

Answer: It is permissible to forbid this in order to keep the mosque clean and pure, so long as there is another well nearby or some other arrangement for water by which the needs of the public can be met. If there is no other well or arrangement for water, then forbidding them is unlawful; and smashing their pots is oppression and unlawful. (امداد الفتاوی: ۲/۷۱۵)۔

Al-Fatāwā al-Bazzāziyyah states:

وحمل ماء السقاية إلى أهله إن كان مأذونا للحمل يجوز وإلا فلا. (الفتاوى البزازية: ٦/۳۷۲).

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

ويجوز أن يحمل ماء السقاية إلى بيته ليشرب أهله كذا في قاضي خان. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۳/۳۰٦).
