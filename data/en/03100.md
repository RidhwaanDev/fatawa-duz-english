### TITLE
The ruling on using the stem cells of an embryo

### QUESTION
A man suffers from epilepsy and is also afflicted with other mental and physical illnesses. Nowadays a new form of treatment has come to light which is called stem cell therapy. The method of treatment is that stem cells are introduced into the patient's body, and there is strong hope that these cells will make up for the defective cells inside; and these cells are obtained from an embryo.

Now what needs to be established is whether this method of treatment is permissible or not. بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, if in obtaining the stem cells there is no fear of any harm to the life of the embryo, neither at present nor in the future, then some scholars have granted permission to use its stem cells by way of treatment. But if in obtaining the cells there is fear of any harm or injury to the embryo, either at present or at a later time, then it is not permitted in anyone's view, and it is necessary to keep away from it altogether.

Consider the following necessary points regarding stem cells.

An introduction to embryonic stem cells: Among the questions that have come to light as a result of the advance of science is the question of stem cells. The embryonic stem cell is in reality the ʿalaqah. Four or five days after the establishment of pregnancy, the compound drop takes, in the earliest stages of its development, a form of the ʿalaqah which is called a blastocyst; and the cells of this blastocyst are what are called stem cells. Scientists hold about it that it has the capacity to become a complete human being and that within its limited sphere it also takes in oxygen. (adapted from the article of Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh, جدید فقہی مباحث: ۲۰/۱٦۳)۔

The sources from which stem cells are obtained: According to scientific research, stem cells can be obtained from the following sources. (1) From an embryo one or two weeks old. (2) From an aborted foetus. (3) From the remaining clots of a test-tube baby. (4) From the blood of the umbilical cord of a newborn child. (5) From bone marrow. (6) From hair follicles. (7) From the fat cells beneath the skin. (adapted from the article of Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh, جدید فقہی مباحث: ۲۰/۱٦٤)۔

The status of the stem cell: (a) Some say that embryonic stem cells hold the rank of a living being possessed of a soul, and parallels for this are available in various ways in the words of the jurists.

In Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith (۲۰/۳۰۷) Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Wadūd writes in his article:

The jurists have strictly forbidden the wasting of semen, and ʿAllāmah Shams al-Aʾimmah al-Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him) has said in his celebrated work al-Mabsūṭ that so long as the drop that has reached the woman's womb has not gone bad, the capacity for life remains within it; therefore, in the case of destroying it, it is treated as a living person and compensation for it becomes due — just as when a man in the state of iḥrām breaks the egg of a game animal, the same penalty becomes due upon him as would be due for killing a game animal. (المبسوط: ۲٦/۸۷)۔

In the same way, the Noble Messenger ﷺ forbade the Companions to castrate themselves. It is plain that there is a reason for the prohibition: in its outcome it would mean the cutting off of the human race, and in destroying a priceless favour of Allah it would mean increasing the wrath of Allah. يمنع في خصاء الآدميين۔ (الاحکام السلطانیۃ)۔

Likewise it is stated in al-Hidāyah, the well-known book of Ḥanafī jurisprudence, that if a man strikes someone on the spine so that his reproductive fluid is cut off, the full blood money becomes due upon him, because by this a whole category of benefit — procreation and the continuation of the race — has been brought to an end.

ومن ضرب صلب غير ه فانقطع ماء ه تجب الدية لتفويت جنس المنفعة. (الهداية: ٤/٤۸۹).

In another place (۲۰/۳۹۱) Muftī ʿAbd al-Raḥīm Qāsimī writes in his article:

According to the research of the scientists, if the embryonic stem cell has the capacity to become a complete human being and within its limited sphere also takes in oxygen, then it will be worthy of respect like the existence of a living being possessed of a soul.

ʿAllāmah al-Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

ثم الماء في الرحم ما لم يفسد فهو معد للحياة فيجعل كالحي في إيجاب الضمان بإتلافه كما يجعل بيض الصيد في حق المحرم كالصيد في إيجاب الجزاء عليه بكسره. (المبسوط: ۲٦/۸۷).

ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes, quoting from the jurist ʿAlī ibn Mūsā:

فإن الماء بعد ما وقع في الرحم مآله الحياة فيكون له حكم الحياة كما في بيضة صيد الحرم، ونحوه في الظهيرية. (فتاوى الشامي: ۳/۱۷٦).

According to the research of the above-mentioned scholars, in their view the use of stem cells will be impermissible.

But some do not accept it as having the ruling of a living being possessed of a soul, nor do they count it as worthy of respect.

Dr Ẓafar al-Islām Aʿẓamī, quoting the wording of Imām Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ (may Allah have mercy on him), writes:

قال أبو بكر: قوله تعالى: من مضغة مخلقة ظاهره يقتضي أن لا تكون المضغة إنساناً كما اقتضي ذلك في العلقة والنطفة والتراب وإنما نبهنا بذلك على تمام قدرته ونفاذ مشيته حين خلق إنساناً سوياً معدلاً بأحسن التعديل من غير إنسان وهي مضغة والعلقة والنطفة التي لا تخطيط فيها ولا تركيب ولا تعديل للأعضاء فاقتضي أن لا تكون المضغة إنساناً كما أن النطفة والعلقة ليستا بإنسان وإذا لم تكن إنساناً لم تكن حملاً فلا تنقضي بها العدة إذ لم تظهر فيها الصورة الإنسانية. (أحكام القرآن: ۵/۵۷).

From the above wording it is learnt that the ʿalaqah and the muḍghah in which there is no shaping, composition or forming of the limbs is not a human being; and when it is not a human being, the term pregnancy will not be applied to it either, let alone the waiting period coming to an end by it. (جدید فقہی مباحث: ۲۰/۵۹)

Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh writes:

The truth is that it cannot be given the rank of a real human being, because as yet it is an ʿalaqah and a muḍghah in which growth does take place but there is no system of respiration. For this very reason the jurists have permitted the termination of a pregnancy before sixteen weeks. ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

قال يباح لها أن تعالج في استنزال الدم ما دام الحمل مضغة أو علقة ولم يخلق له عضو وقدروا تلك المدة بمائة وعشرين يوماً وإنما أباحوا ذلك لأنه ليس بآدمي. (رد المحتار: ۱/۲۷۲). (جديد فقهي مباحث: ۲۰/۱٦٤).

The two views of the scholars regarding the use of embryonic cells as treatment:

Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh writes: Two aspects deserve attention in this connection. The first is whether obtaining the cells from the embryo will prove fatal to it. The second is that preparing an organ from these cells means that it is for the fulfilment of a need that will arise in the future; and as far as benefiting from human parts by way of treatment at a time of need is concerned, the opinion of some people of knowledge is that it is forbidden absolutely, while even in the view of those who have permitted it this permissibility is conditional upon certain conditions, one of which is that the need be actually present. Therefore taking cells from an embryo in order to manufacture an organ does not appear to be correct. (جدید فقہی مباحث: ۲۰/۱٦۵)۔

Mawlānā Zubayr Aḥmad Qāsimī writes in his article:

It is difficult to say that the embryonic stem cell is at present a living thing possessed of a soul and worthy of respect as such, even though it may have the capacity to become a complete human being in the future, because the ruling of the Sharīʿah applies to the present and actual state, not merely to a potential and expected state of affairs. This embryonic cell is in no way regarded as alive in ordinary eyes and in common usage, nor is any sensation or movement felt in it as in a living being possessed of a soul. Therefore it can neither be called a living being possessed of a soul, nor worthy of respect as such.

If a complete organ can be made from this embryonic stem cell, then to the extent necessary for that the embryonic stem may be taken both from a child being nurtured in the mother's womb and from an aborted foetus, and a useful organ may be made, so that a human being in need may benefit; and this act may be said to be of the kind covered by "خير الناس من ينفع الناس".

Yes, for obtaining embryonic cells from a child being nurtured in the mother's womb there will certainly be the condition that a strong likelihood exists that no harm or injury of any level will come to that child from it; otherwise it will be forbidden, in the light of "لا ضرر ولا ضرار في الإسلام".

And the use of this embryonic stem cell for the making of an organ cannot be called contrary to respect and honour: firstly because it is at present not a living thing, and secondly because of the goodness of the aim and intention. Keeping in view the essential sense of the meaning of honour and respect, if an act may in some circumstances be contrary to honour and respect, yet if that same act is done in another manner it is difficult to call it contrary to honour and respect. Therefore, in our view, with the above condition and with the aim and intention — that is, the intention of fulfilling the need of a human being in need — permission may be given for this act. (جدید فقہی مباحث: ۲۰/۱۷۱۔۱۷۳)۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
