### TITLE
Paying zakat by money order

### QUESTION
If someone sends his zakat money by money order, is the zakat discharged or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, sending the zakat money to a poor person by money order discharges the zakat; that is, the zakat is discharged by handing the money over to the post office. Even if it is lost in transit, there is no obligation to pay it again.

Consider what Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

Zakat money may be sent by money order or by bank draft, since there is no other way of doing it. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ۵/۱٦٤).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

في الدر المختار مسائل متفرقة من كتاب الهبة: تمليك الدين ممن ليس عليه الدين باطل إلا في ثلاث: حوالة أو وصية وإذا سلطه أي سلط المملك غير المديون على قبضه أي الدين فيصح حينئذٍ ومنه ما لو وهبت من ابنها ما على أبيه فالمعتمد الصحة للتسليط.

From this passage — "ومنه ما لو وهبت" — we learn that where such authorisation is given, ownership actually passes. Otherwise the validity would not have been explained by the authorisation, for when possession is physically taken there is no doubt whatever about the validity of the gift, and it would then make no sense to give preference to the view that it is valid. This establishes that the authorisation is itself a transfer of ownership, even though it may be revoked before possession is taken, since the contract is not yet complete. So, since authorisation amounts to a transfer of ownership, and an intention of paying zakat made at the time of the transfer of ownership is sufficient, and sending a money order is undoubtedly such an authorisation, the intention made when the money order is despatched is sufficient. (امداد الفتاوی: ۲/۲۵، ادائے زکوٰۃ بذریعہ منی آرڈر).

Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil states:

If zakat money is sent to a poor person by money order, the zakat is discharged by handing it over to the post office with the intention of zakat. So if it is lost in transit after that, the zakat will not have to be paid a second time. (ایضاح المسائل: ۱۲۱).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
