### TITLE
The ruling on describing worship as play

### QUESTION
ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Mubārak (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote some verses in a letter to Fuḍayl ibn ʿIyāḍ (may Allah have mercy on him). The first of them is this:

يا عابد الحرمين لو أبصرتنا
لعلمت أنك في العبادة تلعب

"O worshipper of the two sanctuaries, had you but seen us, you would have known for certain that you are merely playing at worship." In this verse, calling worship "play" is on the face of it disrespect towards worship; and Ibn al-Mubārak (may Allah have mercy on him) is a person of very great standing, so how did he compose this verse? And where are these verses to be found?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, there are several ways of explaining the above verses, as set out below, on the basis of which no aspersion falls upon so eminent a figure as Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Mubārak (may Allah have mercy on him).

(1) Here "play" is used figuratively for what is easy. That is, worship on the prayer mat is easy in comparison with jihad, in which the struggle is greater; and where the struggle is greater, it is of greater help in raising a person's ranks. It is like the way people say in ordinary speech, "This is child's play for me," meaning that it is very easy for me.

(2) By using the word "play" the aim is to convey the abundance of the reward of jihad — that is, in comparison with acts of worship the reward of jihad is far greater, as the whole account indicates. For example, the second verse runs:

من كان يخضب خده بدموعه
فنحورنا بدمائنا تتخضب

That is, you dye your cheeks with your tears while we dye our throats with our blood; and it is obvious that the reward of shedding blood is many times greater than that of shedding tears. Likewise the next verse:

ريح العبير لكم ونحن عبيرنا
رهج السنابك والغبار الأطيب

That is, you go to your worship having applied fine perfumes such as ambergris and musk, whereas our musk and ambergris is the dust of the battlefield; and it is plain that the two cannot be equal.

In the same way, the fire of Hell not touching a body that is covered in the dust of jihad, and the martyr's attaining everlasting life — none of these excellences are attained by acts of worship alone.

Furthermore, the hadith which Fuḍayl ibn ʿIyāḍ (may Allah have mercy on him) had written down in reply to this letter also supports this meaning:

وأملي على الفضيل بن عياض رحمه الله: حدثنا منصور بن المعتمر عن أبي صالح عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه أن رجلاً قال: يا رسول الله! علمني عملاً أنال به ثواب المجاهدين في سبيل الله فقال: هل تستطيع أن تصلي فلا تفتر وتصوم فلا تفطر؟ فقال: يا رسول الله! أنا أضعف من أن أستطيع ذلك، ثم قال النبي ﷺ: فوالذي نفسي بيده لو طوقت ذلك ما بلغت المجاهدين في سبيل الله أو ما علمت أن فرس المجاهد ليستن في طوله فيكتب له بذلك الحسنات.

That is, between the reward of worship and the reward of jihad there is the difference of earth and sky, even though both are important in their own place.

(3) "Play" is an expression for a lesser degree of delight. That is, just as there is a certain delight in weeping and shedding tears at night, so too there is a delight in striking down the enemy on the field of jihad, and this latter delight is many times greater than the former. In the same way, a prayer performed on the field of jihad carries many times more delight than prayers performed elsewhere. This incident is recorded in a number of books:

(1) Al-Tārīkh of Ibn ʿAsākir. (۳۲/٤٤۹، ط: مکتبہ دار الفکر)۔

(2) Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr. (۱/٤۸۵، ط: مکتبہ دار السلام)۔

(3) Siyar Aʿlām al-Nubalāʾ. (۸/٤۱۲، ط: مؤسسۃ الرسالہ)۔

(4) Tārīkh al-Islām. (٤/۸۸۲، ط: دار الغرب الاسلامی)۔

(5) Ṭabaqāt al-Shāfiʿiyyah of al-Subkī. (۱/۲۸٦، ط: ہجر للطباعۃ و النشر و التوزیع)۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
