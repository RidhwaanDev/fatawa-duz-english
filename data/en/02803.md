### TITLE
The ruling on keeping the name Bandar

### QUESTION
A man is devoted to Saudi Arabian names and wants to name his son Bandar, since this is the name of a Saudi prince. His wife opposes it, saying that bandar (which in Urdu means "monkey") is a repulsive and ugly animal and that he should not keep this name. If this name is kept, what does it mean? بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
In the case in question such a name should not be kept; it is not appropriate, because as soon as one hears it the mind turns to the well-known animal, which is proverbial for its ugliness and repulsiveness. If someone insists on keeping the name, however, its good meaning would be an important and honoured person, and instead of ugliness the sense of beauty and comeliness would be taken from it.

Consider the research on the word bandar. Al-bandar means a harbour or a coastal city. A harbour holds an abundance of merchandise, which is very valuable, and value indicates that a man is held in honour; likewise a coastal city is generally beautiful and is a cause of the country's prosperity.

Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd records: al-bandar: a harbour, a large city, a commercial city.

Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt states: al-bandar: a harbour, a city on the sea shore, the place in a city where merchants lodge.

Al-Munjid records: a harbour, a coastal city, the place where merchants stay.

Al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ states:

البندر: موسي السفن في الميناء (فارسي) ويطلق الآن على البلد الكبير، يتبعه بعض القري.

Al-Mawrid states: bandar:

مدينة ساحلية، مركز تجاري

Bandar therefore means a beautiful city, or a harbour that supplies necessary goods; that is, a man called Bandar means one who supplies people's needs, and the sense of beauty is also drawn from it.

Bandargāh is a Persian word, compounded from band and dar. For the sake of lightness one dāl was dropped, and so it became bandar. Dar means a door, and in Persian gāh means a place.

The door is shut because of the valuable goods, and in order to protect the city from attack from outside.

Ghiyāth al-Lughāt states: bandar, with a fatḥah, is the place where boats are moored; it also means a city that lies on the shore of a river or sea and is surrounded by it, such as Surat and Bombay.

Fīrūz al-Lughāt states: a harbour, the place where ships anchor.
