### TITLE
The ruling on eating the duldul

### QUESTION
Is it permissible in the sharia to eat the flesh of the duldul (the large porcupine), or not? Most lexicographers have counted it as a variety of the qunfudh (the hedgehog). See:

قال في الصحاح: الدلدل عظيم القنافذ. (٤/۱٦۹۹)،

And the jurists have counted the qunfudh among the unlawful animals. Kindly inform us of the ruling of the sharia and earn a great reward.

### ANSWER
The large porcupine, which we call shakūnṛ in Pashto, has been written down as unlawful by Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Kifāyatullāh Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him). See: (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 9/141, Dār al-Ishāʿat, old edition). In some works of lexicography too, the duldul — which is the large one and is called shakūnṛ — and the small one, which is called qunfudh and which we call shīshke in Pashto, have both been counted as a single species, only the difference of large and small being indicated. Damīrī has quoted the statement of Jāḥiẓ that the difference between the qunfudh and the duldul is like the difference between the buffalo and the cow, or between the Arabian and the Khurāsānī camel. ʿAllāmah Damīrī writes:

الفرق بين الدلدل والقنفذ كالفرق بين البقر والجواميس والبخاتي والعراب والجرد والفار، وهو كثير ببلاد الشام والعراق وبلاد المغرب في قدر الثعلب القلطي وقال الإمام الرافعي: الدلدل على حد السخلة

وفي الوسيط: أنه كان يعده من الخبائث، وقال ابن الصلاح: هذا غير مرضي وكأنه لم يعرف ما الدلدل

(حيوة الحيوان: ۱/٤۸۱).

The general works of lexicography also count the two as one species: al-Muʿjam al-Wasīṭ, al-Ṣiḥāḥ, Lisān al-ʿArab and others likewise do not hold that there is any difference between the duldul and the qunfudh. So, according to what these authorities have written, both are unlawful in the view of the Ḥanafīs.

The older Afghan scholars, however, and present-day modern researchers, regard the two as separate species. They count the qunfudh among the crawling creatures of the earth and hold it to be unlawful, while they regard the duldul as a grass-eating animal and call it lawful.

In Miṣbāḥ al-Lughāt, moreover, duldul is rendered as sīhī (porcupine), and the qunfudh is described as a kind of mouse.

The Afghan scholars say that the following differences exist between the qunfudh and the duldul:

(1) The qunfudh eats filth and insects, whereas the duldul eats grass.

(2) The qunfudh is among the vermin of the earth, whereas the duldul is not.

(3) The qunfudh is a fanged predator, whereas the duldul is otherwise.

(4) The qunfudh has five canines, whereas the duldul has four teeth (so say experienced Afghan scholars; according to modern researchers the duldul has two teeth [Encarta]).

(5) The qunfudh drinks water like a dog, whereas the duldul drinks like a goat.

These differences make it perfectly clear that the qualities of unlawful animals are found in the qunfudh, while the duldul, by contrast, possesses the characteristics of lawful animals.

As for those who have declared the duldul to be the large variety of the qunfudh, it is possible that dulduls are of two kinds: one is the large variety of qunfudh, which is what the general lexicographers describe; (2) the second kind is the one whose characteristics have been mentioned above.

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Farīd Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) also used to give the fatwa that the duldul is lawful.

In one of the fatwas of Shaykh Ibn Bāz the following is mentioned about the duldul:

قد اختلف العلماء في حكمه فمنهم من أحله ومنهم من حرمه، وأصح القولين أنه حلال،

وهو يتغذى بالنبات كالأرنب والغزال وليس من ذوات الناب المفترسة. (فتاوى الشيخ عبد العزيز بن باز: ۲۳/۳۵).

Consider now the substance of what the modern researchers have written:

The duldul is an animal that lives on dry land; its food consists of the roots of plants, seeds and kernels, and the bark and rind of trees. (Encyclopaedia of Animals).

Encarta records: a plant-eating animal. It is counted among those animals that have two large teeth at the front; with these two long teeth it cuts through plants. It has no canines. (Encarta Encyclopaedia).

Britannica records: it generally comes out at night. It is a plant-eating animal. (Encyclopaedia Britannica).
