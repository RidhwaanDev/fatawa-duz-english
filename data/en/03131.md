### TITLE
The objection concerning the age of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) at her marriage and its consummation, and the answer

### QUESTION
The deniers of hadith and those who call themselves enlightened raise a number of objections against the hadith of the marriage of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā). A few of the important objections are these: that intercourse and conjugal relations of a man past fifty with a girl of nine is unreasonable and incomprehensible; therefore the age of ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) at the marriage cannot have been this, but must have been more.

A few of the arguments of the "enlightened" are as follows:

Argument (1): The argument from the revelation of Sūrat al-Najm and Sūrat al-Qamar.

Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah says:

لقد أنزل على محمد ﷺ بمكة وإني لجارية ألعب ﴿بل الساعة موعدهم والساعة أدهي وأمر﴾. (صحيح البخاري: ۲/۷۲۳/٤۸۷٦).

Presenting this hadith, some people argue that this verse is from Sūrat al-Qamar, and the revelation of Sūrat al-Qamar belongs to the early Makkan period, since it mentions the miracle of the splitting of the moon, and it is evident that this miracle belongs to the early period, because later the opposition of the disbelievers to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ became so severe that they confined him in the ravine of Abū Ṭālib, and this confinement was an event of the seventh year of prophethood.

They then say: secondly, Sūrat al-Najm and Sūrat al-Qamar have a close mutual connection, as the exegetes have acknowledged; therefore the revelation of the two (al-Qamar and al-Najm) must also belong to the same period. And that Sūrat al-Najm was revealed in the fifth year of prophethood is a certain matter; hence Sūrat al-Qamar too was revealed at about that time, so the revelation of these verses falls in the fifth or sixth year of prophethood. And Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) says that at that time she was a girl and used to play, and that she then heard these verses, understood them and even memorised them. From this it certainly appears that it is not reasonable for the age of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) to have been six or seven years at the time of the marriage in the tenth year of prophethood. (سیرتِ عائشہ رضی الله عنه، ص: ۳۵۸۔۳۵۹، ط: مکتبہ مدنیہ)

Argument (2): The prevalence of marrying minors in Arabia.

Before her marriage to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) had been betrothed to Jubayr ibn Muṭʿim (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu); and in Arabia the betrothal or marriage of girls of four or five was not customary. The fact that Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) had already been betrothed at that time shows that her age at that time was such as when girls are ordinarily thought of for betrothal or marriage. This too is a testimony to the fact that at the time of the marriage the age of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) was more than six years. (سیرتِ عائشہ، ص ۳٦۲)

Argument (3): The author of the Mishkāt writes:

وهي أكبر من أختها عائشة رضي الله عنه بعشر سنين،

Ḥaḍrat Asmāʾ (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) was ten years older than her sister Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā). (الاکمال فی اسماء الرجال الملحق بمشکوۃ المصابیح: ۲/۵۸۷، ط: قدیمی).

Dr Muḥammad Ramaḍān Abū Bakr Maḥmūd, who is an assistant professor at al-Azhar University in Egypt, sets out this objection in his book

رواية الصحيحين لعمر السيدة عائشة رضي الله عنه عند زواجها

as follows; consider the summary below:

The objectors claim that all the historical sources testify that Ḥaḍrat Asmāʾ (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) was ten years older than Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā), and that everyone has related without difference that the birth of Ḥaḍrat Asmāʾ (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) took place twenty-seven years before the migration to Madīnah. This confirms that the age of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) at the beginning of the prophetic mission was four years; from which it follows that at the time of the marriage, in the tenth year of prophethood, she was already fourteen, and the consummation took place a further three years and some months later, so that the age of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) at the time of the consummation was a full eighteen years, and this was her true age. (Summarised from رواية الصحيحين, p. 2.)

Argument (4): The ratio of the age of Ḥaḍrat Fāṭimah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) to the age of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā).

Consider the explanation of this objection in the writing of Shaykh Muḥammad Ramaḍān; the summary is as follows:

The objectors say that Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (raḥimahullāh) wrote in Al-Iṣābah that the birth of Ḥaḍrat Fāṭimah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) took place in the year of the building of the Kaʿbah, when the blessed age of the Prophet ﷺ was thirty-five years; and it is also agreed that Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) was five years younger than Ḥaḍrat Fāṭimah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā). From this it becomes clear of itself that the birth of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) took place in the year of the prophetic mission and that at the time of the migration she was a full thirteen years old, not nine.

رواية الصحيحين، ص ۲۳). بينوا توجروا.

### ANSWER
In the narration mentioned in the question there is mention of the consummation, or of being alone together, or of her being brought to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ; there is no mention of intercourse. Since the household of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was a religious university for the women of the ummah, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ needed a girl who was intelligent, alert, constantly present, and outstanding in memory and retention. These qualities were present in Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) in full measure, and this constant presence could not be brought about without marriage. He therefore married her at a young age and had her brought to him at a young age. It was for this reason that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ wished to remain with Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) as much as possible, so that the process of imparting and acquiring might continue; and it was for this reason that Ḥaḍrat Sawdah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) gifted her turn to Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā). Even in his final illness he kept longing to stay with Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā), to the extent that his passing also took place in the house of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā). Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) fully honoured the purpose of the Messenger of Allah's ﷺ marriage. Thus, of the Companions who narrated most — those from whom more than a thousand hadith are related — there are seven, and among them Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) stands fourth: 1. From Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu), 5,374. 2. From Ḥaḍrat Ibn ʿUmar (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu), 2,630. 3. From Ḥaḍrat Anas (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu), 2,286. 4. From Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā), 2,210. 5. From Ḥaḍrat Ibn ʿAbbās (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu), 1,660. 6. From Ḥaḍrat Jābir رضي الله عنه, 1,540. 7. From Ḥaḍrat Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu), 1,170 hadith are related.

The answer to argument number 1: Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Sayyid Sulaymān Nadwī Ṣāḥib (raḥimahullāh) writes in his paper "A research review of the age of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah al-Ṣiddīqah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā)" (p. 359):

How many baseless premises there are in this constructed argument! Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) speaks only of the revelation of a single verse and of her remembering it, and these people extend it to the whole of Sūrat al-Qamar — whereas everyone knows that in the Holy Qur'an sometimes a single verse was revealed, sometimes a few verses, sometimes a whole sūrah; and sometimes a single sūrah was completed by being revealed piecemeal over several years, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would say, "Place this verse in such-and-such a position." Therefore, until it is established that this verse was not revealed alone but that the whole of Sūrat al-Qamar was revealed at one time, the argument cannot be complete. Had the whole sūrah been revealed at once, why would Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah, instead of referring to Sūrat al-Qamar, refer to a single verse at its end?

Moreover, it is not necessary that two sūrahs which correspond to one another should have been revealed in the same period. Sūrat al-Nisāʾ and Sūrat al-Ṭalāq correspond closely, yet there is a difference of years in their revelation; and the same is the case with other sūrahs. Between Sūrat al-Anfāl and Sūrat al-Barāʾah there is such continuity and correspondence that Ḥaḍrat ʿUthmān (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) did not even write the basmalah as a separator between them; yet anyone can see that al-Anfāl relates mostly to the Battle of Badr, which was an event of the second year after the migration, while Sūrat al-Barāʾah relates to the period after the conquest of Makkah, which was an event of the ninth year.

To state the fifth year of prophethood as the definitive date for the revelation of Sūrat al-Najm is also not correct. The objectors take this period to be definitive because this is the sūrah which, while he was reciting it in Ramaḍān of the fifth year of prophethood, he — or (according to some exegetes) Satan — is said to have had the praise of the idols

تلك الغرانيق

interpolated into it (we seek refuge with Allah), and all, Muslims and idolaters alike, prostrated; and on hearing this the emigrants to Abyssinia, who had migrated in Rajab of the fifth year of prophethood, returned from Abyssinia in Shawwāl of the fifth year of prophethood — hence, they say, this sūrah was revealed in the fifth year of prophethood. But all the critics of hadith know that this account is entirely baseless. The recitation of Sūrat al-Najm and the prostration of all the disbelievers is mentioned in the sound hadith without any specification of the date, and without the passage about "tilka al-gharānīq" being in it, and without this incident being made the false cause of the return of the emigrants to Abyssinia. But that has no bearing on the objectors' argument; it would have a bearing only if that baseless portion were included, and that is not sound. Rather, if a ruling about a whole sūrah can be based on a few verses, then I say that it is impossible for Sūrat al-Najm to have been revealed in the fifth year of prophethood and for the Prophet ﷺ to have recited the whole sūrah at that time, because at the beginning of this sūrah there is mention of the spiritual scenes and sights of the Ascension, and the dates of the Ascension are the eleventh or twelfth year of prophethood. How, then, can it be possible that this whole sūrah was revealed and recited in the fifth year of prophethood?

As for stating that the time of the occurrence of the miracle of the splitting of the moon was early, on the ground that from the seventh year of prophethood he went into the ravine of Abū Ṭālib (to call it "confinement" is not correct, since coming and going and leaving were not blocked; it was only relations and buying and selling that were blocked) — could he not have shown this miracle in the ravine of Abū Ṭālib, or after leaving it, from the ninth year of prophethood up to the fourteenth? What necessity is there that if he could show this miracle he could show it only up to the fifth or tenth year of prophethood? There may be some other proof of it, but this is no proof at all.

Besides this, not one or two but a great many scholars of hadith and of the biography have fixed the date of the miracle of the splitting of the moon at five years before the migration, that is, the tenth year of prophethood
(دیکھو فتح الباری: ٦/۲٦٤، و قسطلانی: ۷/٤۰۷، و سیرتِ حلبی: ۳/٤۰۵،
و زرقانی بر مواہب: ۵/۱۲٤، و تاریخ خمیس دیار بکری: ۱/۲۹۸).
Perhaps some have confused the expressions "the fifth year of prophethood" and "five years before the migration" because of their resemblance, whereas there is a difference of about six years between the two. On the basis of these explicit statements, the revelation of Sūrat al-Qamar must be an event of at least the tenth year of prophethood, when the fifth year of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah's (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) age would be ending or her sixth year beginning. It is therefore no great matter that at that age, while playing, a single verse — and only one verse — should fall upon her ears and be remembered, especially for a sharp, intelligent girl with a strong memory. Therefore, by this argument too, to fix the time of the revelation of this sūrah — or, to put it more correctly, of this single verse — as the fifth or sixth year of prophethood is without proof. (سیرتِ عائشہ، ص ۳۵۹۔۳٦۲).

We content ourselves with just two Arabic passages. Consider what Ibn Ḥajar (raḥimahullāh) says in Fatḥ al-Bārī:

فذكر فيه حديث ابن مسعود وأنس وابن عباس في ذلك وقد ورد انشقاق القمر أي من حديث على وحذيفة وجبير بن مطعم وابن عمر وغيرهم فأما أنس وابن عباس فلم يحضرا ذلك لأنه كان بمكة قبل الهجرة بنحو خمس سنين وكان ابن عباس إذ ذاك لم يولد وأما أنس فكان ابن أربع أو خمس بالمدينة وأما غيرهما فيمكن أن يكون شاهد. (فتح الباري: ٦/٦۳۲، ط: دار نشر الكتب الإسلامية).

ʿAllāmah al-Qasṭallānī (raḥimahullāh) says:

وفي رواية عن ابن عباس عند أبي نعيم في الدلائل والفضائل فصار قمرين في زمان النبي ﷺ وابن عباس أيضًا لم يحضر ذلك لأنه كان بمكة قبل الهجرة بنحو خمس سنين، وكان ابن عباس إذ ذاك لم يولد لكن في بعض الطرق أنه حمل الحديث عن ابن مسعود، وانشقاق القمر من أمهات المعجزات وأجمع عليه المفسرون وأهل السُّنّة وروي عن جماعة كثيرة من الصحابة. (إرشاد الساري: ٦/۷٤، ط: المطبعة الكبرى الأميرية). (وكذا في تاريخ الخميس في أحوال أنفس النفيس: ۱/۲۹۸، والمواهب اللدنية بالمنح المحمدية: ۲/۲۵٤، ط: المكتبة التوفيقية).

The answer to argument number 2: We submit that in Arabia not only the betrothal and engagement of young girls was customary, but even the marriage of infant girls; indeed, a promise of marriage for a child still in the womb was also in vogue. See Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Kitāb al-Nikāḥ, Bāb fī tazwīj man lam yūlad — that in the age of ignorance even an unborn child was betrothed. If by the absence of the custom of marrying young girls in Arabia the age of ignorance is meant, then everyone knows that the events of the age of ignorance are not preserved such that you could say anything, negative or positive, about the Arabs of that era; and it is not known on what proof the claim of the absence of the custom in that era rests. And if the Arabia of the era of Islam is meant, then, apart from Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā), numerous examples of the marriage of young girls are found. By way of a handful as a sample of the load, a few examples are presented:

(1) The Companion Ḥaḍrat Qudāmah ibn Maẓʿūn (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) contracted marriage with the newborn daughter of Ḥaḍrat Zubayr رضي الله عنه on the very day she was born. (مرقاۃ ملا علی قاری حنفی: ۳/٤۱۷).

(2) The Messenger of Allah ﷺ himself married the young son of Ḥaḍrat Umm Salamah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā), Salamah رضي الله عنه, to the minor daughter of Ḥaḍrat Ḥamzah رضي الله عنه, the martyr of Uḥud. (احکام القرآن رازی حنفی: ۲/۵۵). Derived from (سیرتِ عائشہ رضی الله عنه، ص: ۳٦۲۔۳٦۳).

(3) Ḥaḍrat Muʿāwiyah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) married his daughter Hind to ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿĀmir at the age of nine. (تاریخ مدینۃ دمشق: ۷۰/۱۸۸، ط: دار الفکر).

(4) Hishām ibn ʿUrwah was married to Fāṭimah bint Mundhir, and at the time of the consummation Fāṭimah was nine years old. (تاریخ بغداد: ۱/۲۲۲، ط: دار الکتب العلمیۃ).

The answer to argument number (3): The author of the Mishkāt said in his treatise "Al-Ikmāl fī Asmāʾ al-Rijāl" concerning Ḥaḍrat Asmāʾ (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) that she was ten years older than Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā). He stated this with the words "it is said", and this is weak. Apart from him, the majority of historians and biographers have stated no limit at all, but have contented themselves with saying only that Ḥaḍrat Asmāʾ (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) was older in age than Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā). Thus Ibn al-Athīr writes in Usd al-Ghābah:

وكانت (أسماء بنت أبي بكر) أسن من عائشة رضي الله عنه وهي أختها لابيها

(۷/۷، ط: دار الکتب العلمیۃ). See further: (تاریخ دمشق: ٦۹/۹، ط: دار الفکر، و معرفۃ الصحابۃ لابی نعیم: ۵/۱۸۲/۳۷٦۹، دار الکتب العلمیۃ).

Yes, Imām al-Dhahabī (raḥimahullāh) used the expression

"بضع عشر"

in Siyar Aʿlām al-Nubalāʾ. Consider:

وكانت أسن من عائشة ببضع عشرة سنة. (سیر اعلام النبلاء: ۲/۲۸۸، ط: مؤسسۃ الرسالۃ).

And the word "biḍʿ" is used in Arabic for a number from three to nine. From this it is learnt that Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) was about eighteen years younger than Ḥaḍrat Asmāʾ, and that at the time of the consummation her age was nine years, which accords entirely with the sound narrations.

In some books of history, however, the fixing of ten years is related on the authority of ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Abī al-Zinād.

Consider: (سیر اعلام النبلاء: ۲/۲۸۹، و الاستیعاب فی معرفۃ الاصحاب: ۱/۱۸۵، و تہذیب الاسماء: ۱/۹۱۹).

But some scholars of hadith have declared ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Abī al-Zinād weak, and some have said of him

لا يحتج به

Consider: (الجرح و التعدیل: ۵/۲۵۳، و تہذیب التہذیب: ٦/۱۷۰، ط: دائرۃ المعارف،
و سیر اعلام النبلاء: ٤/٤۰۸، ط: دار الحدیث).

Therefore the statement of a weak narrator is no proof against sound narrations.

The answer to argument number (4): The objectors have taken only what suits their purpose. Otherwise Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (raḥimahullāh) has related several statements concerning the birth of Ḥaḍrat Fāṭimah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) in Al-Iṣābah: on the authority of al-Wāqidī, the year of the building of the Kaʿbah, that is, five years before the prophetic mission; and on the authority of Abū ʿUmar, the statement that her birth was in the forty-first year of the Prophet's birth. He then wrote his own opinion, that the birth of Ḥaḍrat Fāṭimah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) took place somewhat before the prophetic mission (by a year or a little more). See: (الاصابۃ: ۸/۲٦۳، دار الکتب العلمیۃ).

Other historians too have related this. By way of a handful as a sample of the load, consider a few references:

Thus Imām al-Dhahabī (raḥimahullāh) in Siyar Aʿlām al-Nubalāʾ (۲/۱۱۹، ط: مؤسسۃ الرسالۃ); Aḥmad ibn ʿAbdullāh al-Ṭabarī in Dhakhāʾir al-ʿUqbā (۱/۲٦); Muḥammad ibn Yūsuf al-Ṣāliḥī al-Shāmī in Subul al-Hudā wa'l-Rashād (۱۱/۳۷، ط: دار الکتب العلمیۃ); Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr in Al-Istīʿāb fī Maʿrifat al-Aṣḥāb (٤/۱۸۹۳، ط: دار الجیل); Ibn ʿAsākir in Tārīkh Madīnat Dimashq (۳/۱۵۷); Ḥāfiẓ al-Mizzī (raḥimahullāh) in Tahdhīb al-Kamāl (۳۵/۲٤۸، ط: مؤسسۃ الرسالۃ); and Shaykh al-ʿIṣāmī in Simṭ al-Nujūm al-ʿAwālī (۱/۲۱۷); and so on.

From all these books of history it appears that the birth of Ḥaḍrat Fāṭimah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) took place somewhat before the prophetic mission or after it, and the birth of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhā) took place four years after the prophetic mission. On this reckoning there is a difference of about five years between Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) and Ḥaḍrat Fāṭimah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā), and the blessed age of Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhā) at the time of the migration was nine years, which accords exactly with the sound narrations.
