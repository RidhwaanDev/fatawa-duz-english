### TITLE
Putting up a building on endowed land

### QUESTION
A piece of land has been endowed for a mosque, but there is no building on it, so no income is being earned from the land and no benefit is being had from it. It does not even cover its own expenses, such as tax, and the mosque does not have the means to build on it. The mosque's trustees are therefore worried. A man has told them that he would like to build a shop there with his own money — not as an endowment, but so that it remains his own property — and that he will pay a reasonable rent for the land and will also keep giving the mosque a portion of the income. Is there room for building in this way? And can a privately owned building be put up on endowed land?

### ANSWER
A derelict endowed property that cannot be put to any use may be built upon so as to make it usable; doing so is not merely permissible but preferable and better. But when the mosque does not have the means to make it usable, the mosque's trustees may give another person permission to build. That building will be his own personal property until he endows it, and a monthly rent will be collected from him. Beyond that, he may of his own free will give the mosque whatever portion of the income he wishes. This arrangement should, however, be put in writing with the signatures of both parties and kept safely in the mosque's file, so that with the passage of time his heirs do not lay claim to ownership, since there is a risk here of the endowment being lost.

Consider what Qānūn al-ʿAdl wa'l-Inṣāf states:

وإذا بني الواقف بناء في أرض الوقف، أو غرس فيها أشجاراً، فإن كان البناء والغراس من مال الوقف، أو كان من مال الواقف، وذكر أنه بناه أو غرسه للوقف، فإنه يكون وقفاً، وإن كان من ماله، ولم يذكر أنه للوقف يكون ما بناه أو غرسه ملكاً له. (قانون العدل والإنصاف، ص ۱٤۲، مادة: ۲۰۷، البناء والغراس في ارض الوقف).

Majmūʿah Qawānīn Islāmī states:

To close the door to any claim of ownership or to wrongful seizure, a tenant should not be given permission to put up a building of his own on endowed land. But if the trustee has given him permission to build a house of his own, or he has built one himself and the term of the lease has come to an end, then even if he is willing to pay the going rent and there is no strong likelihood of his seizing the property, the trustee is still not obliged to keep him on as a tenant. If there is no risk of harm to the endowed land, the trustee may even require him to put up the building; and if there is a risk of harm, he should not give him permission to build a house. As long as none of these arrangements is possible, the trustee should lease the endowed land together with the building to someone else, until the building falls apart and its material is returned to its owner. The rent obtained during that period will be divided between the owner of the building and the endowment, each according to his own share. (Majmūʿah Qawānīn Islāmī: 366–368, clause 40).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ففي المنية حانوت لرجل في أرض وقف فأبي صاحبه أن يستأجر الأرض بأجر المثل أن العمارة لو رفعت تستأجر بأكثر مما استأجره أمر برفع العمارة، وتوجر لغيره، وإلا تترك في يده بذلك الأجر. وفي الشامية: والحاصل أن مستأجر أرض الوقف إذا بني فيها ثم زادت أجرة المثل زيادة فاحشة فأما أن تكون الزيادة بسبب العمارة والبناء أو بسبب زيادة أجرة الأرض في نفسها ففي الأول لا تلزمه الزيادة لا نها أجرة عمارته وبنائه وهذا لو كانت العمارة ملكه

قوله إلا تترك في يده بذلك الأجر لأن فيها ضرورة بحر عن المحيط وظاهر التعليل تركها بيده ولو بعد فراغ مدة الإجارة، لأنه لو أمر برفعها لتوجر من غيره يلزم ضرره، وحيث كان يدفع أجرة مثلها لم يوجد ضرر على الوقف، فتترك في يده لعدم الضرر على الجانبين فلو مات المستأجر كان لورثته الاستبقاء أيضاً إلا إذا كان فيه ضرر على الوقف بوجه ما. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٤/۳۹۱، سعيد).
