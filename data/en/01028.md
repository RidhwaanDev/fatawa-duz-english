### TITLE
Giving zakat to the imams of mosques

### QUESTION
In our area people appoint an imam for their mosques and do not fix a salary for him. Instead, once the crop is harvested, they give him grain and other produce out of their zakat. If the imam is entitled to zakat, is the zakat discharged or not? Is this not a wage? May zakat be given as a wage?

### ANSWER
In the case described there are two ways in which zakat may be given. (1) If the mosque's imam is entitled to zakat, giving it to him as a poor man is valid — indeed it is better.

(2) The zakat may be given to the imam as payment for leading the prayers. In this case the zakat is not discharged, although the imam may lawfully take it as a salary.

Leaving aside the two situations above, the better and preferable course is to fix a wage for the imam, and then, since it is plain that he cannot manage on it, to give him charity and zakat as well.

Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

التصدق على الإمام الفقير أفضل، وفي الشامي: أي من الجاهل الفقير. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ۲/۳۵٤، سعيد).

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

لو نوى الزكاة بما يدفع المعلم إلى الخليفة ولم يستأجره إن كان الخليفة بحال لو لم يدفعه يعلم الصبيان أيضاً أجزأه، وإلا فلا. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۹۰، باب المصارف).

Fatāwā Farīdiyyah states:

When the imam of a mosque is poor, it is permissible to give him zakat; but it is not permissible when it is given as a wage or payment. If these obligatory charities are given to imams who are not well off in exchange for leading the prayers, the givers' obligation is not discharged. But if they are given on account of his leading the prayers and as a mark of gratitude for it, they are permissible beyond any doubt.

والمتعارف هو الإعطاء له على وجه الترحم، والصلة ولذا ينوي المعطون الثواب والتقرب إلى الله، والأجير لا يكون كذلك، وبالجملة أن منع الإعطاء مطلقاً خراب نظام أكثر المساجد

(Fatāwā Farīdiyyah: 3/537; and Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal: 3/401).

Fatāwā Muftī Maḥmūd states:

If it is well known and accepted that people give the imam zakat and ʿushr, and the imam leads their prayers with the aim that they will give him zakat, so that if they stopped he would give up the imamate and leave, then no lease contract has come about, either valid or defective, but the arrangement is certainly like a contract, since he will abandon the imamate if these people do not give him zakat. In this case the cautious course is that they first present the imam with something as a gift and afterwards give him zakat, ʿushr and the like; for then no contract of any sort has been made, and so the people are under no obligation to give him anything. The zakat will therefore not count as a wage, and its payment will be valid. And if no contract of any sort has been made — if he leads the prayers even when people give no zakat or give less, and he has merely made the imamate a means of receiving zakat and ʿushr, the whole matter being that he reckons that if he does not lead the prayers people will not give zakat and ʿushr — then giving zakat to such an imam is undoubtedly permissible and the zakat is discharged; there are many parallels to this in the books of fiqh. And if he leads the prayers purely for the sake of Allah, with no eye on receiving zakat and ʿushr, then giving and taking is permissible all the more. (Fatāwā Muftī Maḥmūd, Pakistan: 3/334–336; and Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm: 6/213, complete annotated edition).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
