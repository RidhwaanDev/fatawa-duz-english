### TITLE
How many steps the Prophet's pulpit had

### QUESTION
How many steps did the pulpit of the Prophet ﷺ have? If it had three, would it be permissible to build more than that, or would that be an innovation, or is it simply allowed?

### ANSWER
Radd al-Muḥtār (Shāmī) states:

ومنبره ﷺ كان ثلاث درج غير المسماة بالمسترح. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲ /۱٦۱، سعيد).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states: The pulpit of the Prophet ﷺ had three steps. Keeping to that number is preferable, though having fewer or more is also permissible. (احسن الفتاوی ٤ /۱۲۰)۔

From the passages quoted above it is clear that the Prophet's ﷺ pulpit had three steps, and that building more than three is also permissible; but it is better and more excellent to keep to the pattern of the Prophet's ﷺ pulpit.

Imdād al-Aḥkām states:

قال الحافظ في فتح الباري: إن في الأحاديث الصحيحة أنه ﷺ كان يستند إلى الجذع إذا خطب ولم يزل المنبر عن حاله ثلث درجات حتى زاده مروان في خلافة معاوية رضي الله عنه ست درجات من أسفله ثم ذكر سبب ذلك. (ص ۳۳۱ ج ۲).

وروى أبو داود عن تميم الداري أنه قال لرسول الله ﷺ: ألا أتخذ لك منبراً يا رسول الله يجمع عظامك قال: بلى فأتخذ له منبراً مرقاتين. (رقم: ۱۰۸۳). إسناده صحيح.

وقال سيدي مولاي خليل أحمد دام مجده (في شرحه قال الحافظ: وإسناده جيد) فاتخذ له منبراً مرقاتين أي درجتين وبين ما في الصحيح أنه ثلث درجات منافات.

قلت: الذي قال: مرقاتين لم يعتبر الدرجة التي كان يجلس عليها النبي ﷺ. (ص ۱۷۸ ج ۲).

This shows that the Prophet's pulpit had three steps in all, counting the step on which the Prophet ﷺ used to sit, and that the steps actually climbed were two. In short, there were two steps for climbing, and adding the sitting step to them makes three steps in all. As for what Shāmī says:

"ومنبره ﷺ ثلاث درج غير المسماة بالمستراح". (ص ۸٦۰/۱)

here mustarāḥ does not mean the sitting step. It is the back panel that is fixed behind the sitting step for one to lean against. What Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) means is that the mustarāḥ — that is, the back-rest — should not be counted among the three steps; the three steps are over and above it. So this statement does not contradict what is in Fatḥ al-Bārī and in ʿAynī. And if mustarāḥ were taken to mean the sitting step, as the second view holds, and this statement of Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) were regarded as contradicting Fatḥ and ʿAynī, even then Shāmī's statement could not be given preference, because he is reporting it without a chain of transmission, whereas Fatḥ and ʿAynī give the three steps on the authority of a sound chain. Moreover, in the science of narration the researcher ʿAynī ranks higher than ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him), and as for the rank of al-Ḥāfiẓ, it is "أظهر من الشمس". (امداد الاحکام: ۱۹۵، احکام المساجد)۔
