### TITLE
The limits within which a man may use his shar‘ī right of divorce

### QUESTION
A man has grown weary of married life and wishes to know at what stage he ought to make use of the right of divorce. بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the pure sharīʿah the bond of marriage and the tie of married life is not a temporary one; it is a bond of life and death. It is required that the tie be honoured and maintained till one's dying breath, for this tie is a firm means and instrument for passing a life of comfort, tranquillity, chastity and modesty. Moreover, divorce is not the first remedy for unpleasantness and for discord between husband and wife; rather divorce is the last stage in the effort to set matters right.

Accordingly, those elders of the family who possess understanding and good sense should be appointed as arbiters and an effort at reconciliation should be made, and various measures should be employed. Nevertheless, if no measure proves effective, and on account of the want of harmony the living together of the two becomes a calamity and a torment, then as the final measure the right of divorce may be used and release obtained.

Consider what ʿAllāmah al-Ālūsī al-Baghdādī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

أن الأصل في الطلاق هو الحظر لما فيه من قطع النكاح الذي تعلقت به المصالح الدينية والدنيوية والإباحة للحاجة إلى الخلاص. (روح المعاني: ۲/۱۳٦، القاهرة).

وفي تفسير آيات الأحكام: إن مرتبة الطلاق بعيدة عن مرتبة النكاح، إذ أن النكاح تترتب عليه منافع كثيرة، ويثمر ثمرات طيبة، فأما الطلاق فإنه يحل العقدة، ويفصم العروة، ويبطل ما بين الزوجين وأقاربهما من روابط وصلات، ولهذا قال بعض الفقهاء: إن الآية ترشد إلى أن الأصل في الطلاق الحظر، وإنه لا يباح إلا إذا فسدت الزوجية، ولم تفلح وسائل الإصلاح بين الزوجين. (تفسير آيات الأحكام للشيخ محمد علي، ص ۳، ط: المكتبة العصرية).

قال في البحر الرائق: وأما سببه فالحاجة إلى الخلاص عند تباين الأخلاق وعروض البغضاء الموجبة عدم إقامة حدود الله تعالى وشرعه رحمة منه سبحانه، وأما صفته فهو أبغض المباحات إلى الله تعالى، وفي المعراج: إيقاع الطلاق مباح وإن كان مبغضاً في الأصل عند عامة العلماء. (البحر الرائق: ۳/۲۳٦، كوئته). (وكذا في إحياء علوم الدين: ۲/۳۳٦).

Maʿārif al-Qurʾān mentions:

The real thrust of the Islamic teachings is that the matter and the contract of marriage should be for a whole lifetime, and that the occasion for breaking it and bringing it to an end should never arise at all; for the effect of the severing of this relationship does not fall upon the two parties alone. It leads to the ruin and destruction of offspring and progeny, and sometimes even to strife between families and tribes, and the whole of society is grievously affected by it. For this reason the teachings of the Qurʾān and the Sunnah have made full provision for removing from the path all those causes and reasons which may become a means of breaking this relationship. The directives mentioned in the Qurʾān and the Sunnah for every situation and every condition of the couple all amount to this: that this tie should grow ever firmer and firmer and should not be allowed to break. In the case of disharmony, directives were given first for mutual explanation and understanding, then for reproof and warning; and if the matter grows worse and even this does not answer, the teaching was given that a few members of the family itself should be appointed as arbiters and mediators to settle the matter.

But at times such situations also arise in which all efforts at setting matters right fail, and instead of the desired fruits of the marriage tie being obtained, the living together of the two parties becomes a torment. In such a state the ending of this marital tie is itself the path of relief and safety for both parties.

For this reason the sharīʿah of Islam did not do as some other religions have done, namely, that the marriage tie should remain indissoluble in every circumstance; rather it laid down a law of divorce and of annulment of marriage. The right of divorce it gave to the man alone, in whom, ordinarily, the capacity for thought, deliberation and forbearance is greater than in the woman. This right was not placed in the woman's hand, so that the being overcome by momentary impressions — which is greater in the woman than in the man — should not become a cause of divorce.

Yet the woman too was not left wholly deprived of this right, so as to be compelled to endure her husband's oppression and cruelty. She was given the right to present her case in the court of a shar‘ī judge and, by proving her complaints, to have the marriage annulled or to obtain a divorce. Then, although the man was given a free right of divorce, it was said in the first place that the use of this right is extremely hateful and detestable in the sight of Allah, and that permission for it exists only in a state of necessity. In the hadith the noble words of the Prophet are:

عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: أبغض الحلال إلى الله الطلاق. (رواه أبو داود، وابن ماجه، وصححه الحاكم، ورجح أبو حاتم إرساله).

That is, of lawful things the most hateful and detestable in the sight of Allah is divorce. The second restriction laid down was that this right should not be used in a state of rage and anger, or on account of some momentary and passing displeasure. (Maʿārif al-Qurʾān, volume one, p. 556)
