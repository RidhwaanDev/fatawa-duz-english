### TITLE
Prizes for solving a puzzle that requires an entry fee

### QUESTION
In some places an advertisement is placed saying that whoever sends in a solution together with a fee of, say, one rupee will be entered among those whose solutions are correct, and the prize will be given to the person whose name comes out by lottery or draw. The question is whether there is any room for taking part in such schemes or not, and whether it is permissible to take the prize if one does take part.

### ANSWER
If those running the scheme charge an entry fee, then it falls under gambling and is impermissible. There is no room for taking part in such schemes, and keeping away from them is obligatory and necessary. If someone has mistakenly taken part and received a prize, the whole sum must be given away in charity; there is no room for putting it to his own use.

See what the Noble Qur'an states:

﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا إنما الخمر والميسر والأنصاب والأزلام رجس من عمل الشيطان فاجتنبوه لعلكم تفلحون﴾. (المائدة، الآية: ۹۰).

The jurist Abū al-Layth al-Samarqandī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in his commentary on maysir:

وقال عطاء ومجاهد: الميسر القمار كله حتى لعب الصبيان بالجوز والكعاب. (تفسير السمرقندي: ۱/۲۰۳).

Al-Taʿrīfāt al-Fiqhiyyah states:

القمار مصدر قامر هو كل لعب يشترط فيه غالباً أن يأخذ الغالب شيئاً من المغلوب، وأصله أن يأخذ الواحد من صاحبه شيئاً فشيئاً في اللعب ثم عرفوه بأنه تعليق الملك على الخطر والمال في الجانبين. (التعريفات الفقهية، ص ۱۷۷، وجواهر الفقه: ۲/۲۲۷، وإمداد الفتاوى: ۳/۱٦۱).

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

وسمي القمار قماراً لأن كل واحد من المقامرين ممن يجوز أن يذهب ماله إلى صاحبه، ويجوز أن يستفيد مال صاحبه، وهو حرام بالنص. (رد المحتار: ٦/٤۰۳، كتاب الحظر والإباحة، سعيد).

Āp ke Masāʾil states:

The money paid along with the entry as a fee is paid out of a desire for more and in order to receive more, so it is interest; and since receiving it or not is uncertain, it is gambling. Interest and gambling are both unlawful. Whether the extra that is received comes in cash or in the form of a ticket, both are unlawful. The real purpose of these schemes is the lure of an extra sum; mental and intellectual improvement is not the purpose. In this way the habit and appetite for gambling are created. It is gambling in respectable dress. (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ٦/۲٦۱) ۔

Jawāhir al-Fiqh states:

Various forms of a puzzle are printed and an advertisement is placed saying that whoever sends in a solution together with a fee of, say, one rupee will be entered among those whose solutions are correct, and the prize will be given to the person whose name comes out by lottery or draw. This is open gambling, for a man stakes his one rupee of fee on a mere hope: either this rupee too is lost, or it brings him thousands of rupees. That is precisely what the sacred law of Islam calls gambling. (جواہر الفقہ: ۲/۳٤۹) ۔

Fatāwā ʿUthmānī states:

If any fee has been fixed for sending in the solution to a puzzle, then it is unlawful, because it falls under gambling. (فتاوی عثمانی: ۳/۳٤۱) ۔

If someone says that the fee is for entry and is not the price of the prize, Ḥaḍrat Muftī Maḥmūd Ḥasan Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) says of this:

Everyone knows that mere entry is not the object, such that a fee would be borne for it; the object is to obtain the sum of money which has been given the name "prize". And everyone also knows that the object of the one giving the prize is not merely to fill in a form, nor has he any particular interest of that kind; his object is to collect money by holding out the lure of a large prize. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱٦/٤٤۲، جامعہ فاروقیہ) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
