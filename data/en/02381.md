### TITLE
The ruling on the glands

### QUESTION
What is the ruling on the glands, which in Arabic are called "الغدة", and on the spinal cord, which is called "نخاع الصلب"? If they are cooked in a curry, will it be permissible to eat that curry or not?

### ANSWER
Concerning the glands, "الغدة", the jurists have written that eating them is strictly disliked according to the sounder position, and mildly disliked according to another position. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ disapproved of seven things in a lawful animal: the organ of generation, the two testicles, the private part [farj], the glands, the gall bladder (the bitter sac of bile attached to the liver, which assists in digesting fat), the bladder, and blood. Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) says that blood is unlawful, because a noble verse has come down concerning it, and that the remaining things are disliked, because sound nature finds them repugnant.

See what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

كره تحريماً وقيل تنزيهاً والأول أوجه من الشاة سبع

للأثر الوارد في كراهة ذلك. وفي رد المحتار: قوله كره تحريماً لما روي الأوزاعي عن واصل بن أبي جميلة عن مجاهد قال: كره رسول الله ﷺ من الشاة: الذكر والأنثيين والقبل والغدة والمرارة والمثانة والدم، قال أبو حنيفة رحمه الله: الدم حرام وأكره الستة

لأنه مما تستخبثه الأنفس، وتكرهه وهذا المعنى سبب الكراهية لقوله تعالى: ﴿ويحرم عليهم الخبائث﴾

الخ. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٦/۷٤۹، مسائل شتي. سعيد).

See further:

(البحر الرائق: ۸/٤۸، ط: كوئته، وبدائع الصنائع: ۵/٦۱، سعيد، والفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۲۹۰).

By "gland" is not meant the marrow of the bones, which ordinary people eat and the eating of which is expressly mentioned in the hadiths as well. What is meant is rather the sinewy lumps, which in English are called gland; while the marrow that is eaten is called marrow in English.

The gland — الغدة and الغددة — is one of the seven things and is strictly disliked. Concerning it the lexicographers write: a knot that forms in the body with fat around it; and likewise a knot of flesh that swells up because of some disease.

The marrow inside the bones, which is eaten, is called "مخ" and "لب" in Arabic, and in Urdu it is expressed as gūdā and maghz-i ustukhwān. See: (Fīrūz al-Lughāt, al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd and others).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Question: kindly explain what the gland is. Generally the gland is what comes out of the marrow-bone or out of the trotters — that is what it is called — and people commonly eat it with great relish. Tell us: is eating it lawful or unlawful?

Answer: when blood congeals and takes the form of a lump, that is called ghuddah, and the same thing is called ghudūd in Urdu. What comes out of the trotters and other bones is called galī, gūdah and gūd; eating that is correct. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 17/297, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

The spinal cord has been discussed in detail above. The upshot is that, because of the passage of ʿAllāmah Sayyid Aḥmad Ṭaḥṭāwī (may Allah have mercy on him), the position of mild dislike — that is, of its being contrary to what is preferable — is the sound one.

Cooking in a curry those things which are strictly disliked or unlawful to eat will not make the curry unlawful; rather, eating the curry is permissible and correct.

See what is recorded in Fatāwā Shāmī:

ذكر [أي صاحب القنية] أن الذكر أو الغدة لو طبخ في المرقة لا تكره المرقة. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۷٤۹، سعيد).

ʿAllāmah Rāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

قوله إنه لا يكره أكل المرقة واللحم، أي المطبوخ مع الذكر أو الغدة وعبارة القنية على ما ذكره في شرح الوهبانية: ذكر الشاة وغُدَدُها طبخا في اللحم لا تكره المرقة. (التحرير المختار: ٦/۳۵۷، سعيد).

Nafʿ al-Muftī wa al-Sāʾil states:

الاستفسار: ذكر الشاة إذا طبخ في المرقة، هل يكره أكلها؟

الاستبشار: نعم؛ ولا كراهة في المرقة. كذا في السراج المنير. (فتاوى اللكنوي، ص ۳۷۳، كتاب الحظر والإباحة، ما يتعلق بالأكل والشرب).

(وكذا في شرح منظومة ابن وهبان: ۲/۱۵۵، ط: ديوبند).
