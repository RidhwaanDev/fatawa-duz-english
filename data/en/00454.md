### TITLE
The imam turning round to face the worshippers

### QUESTION
In Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyya (2/170) it is written that it is better for the imam to turn towards the congregation from the right side. Does "the right" here mean the worshippers' right, or the right of the qiblah, which in the language of the jurists is the worshippers' left?

### ANSWER
Bear in mind that it is not correct to make any one side binding on oneself; rather, turning to either side should be regarded as correct. The narration of ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) states that whoever makes one side binding on himself — that is, the right — has given Satan a share of his prayer; the Prophet ﷺ used to turn and depart from the left as well as from the right. (مسلم شریف: ۱/۲٤۷، شرح مسلم: ۱/۲٤۷) Al-Bayhaqī's al-Sunan al-Kubrā records that he ﷺ prayed both barefoot and wearing sandals, and both standing and sitting, and that he would leave from the right side and from the left. (سنن کبریٰ ۔ ۲/۲۹٥) The same matter is found in Sunan Ibn Mājah (p. 66). This was also the practice of Anas (may Allah be pleased with him), and he objected to those who attached excessive importance to the right side. (بخاری شریف: ۱/۱۱۸)

Wāsiʿ ibn Ḥabbān relates: Ibn ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) was sitting leaning against a wall. When I had finished my prayer I turned to my left and came to him. He asked why I had not come from the right. I said that I had wanted to turn towards him. Ibn ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) said: you did well. Some people take the right side to be binding, but it is as you please — you may turn from the right or from the left. (مؤطا مالک ۱٥٥) The same is recorded in al-Tirmidhī. (۱/٦٦)

With all this, it is still better to give preference to the right. Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) says that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ usually turned to the right. (مسلم ۱/۲٤۷) Imam al-Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote in his commentary that the right is better, since this is what the hadiths generally indicate. (شرح مسلم: ۱/۲٤۷) Awjaz al-Masālik states that al-Ḥasan liked to turn to the right after the prayer. (اوجز: ۳/٤۹۷)

Now, which side is meant by "the right"? The passage in Radd al-Muḥtār shows that, according to Sharḥ al-Munyah, it is the worshipper's own right that counts:

في شرح المنية: أن انحرافه عن يمينه أولى وأيده بحديث في صحيح مسلم. (شامي: ۱/٥۳۱).

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim states:

عن البراء رضي الله عنه قال: كنا إذا صلينا خلف رسول الله ﷺ أحببنا أن نكون عن يمينه يقبل علينا بوجهه. (مسلم ۱/۲٤۷).

Marāqī al-Falāḥ states:

وإن شاء انحرف عن يمينه وجعل القبلة عن يساره، وهذا أولى. (مراقي الفلاح: ص ۱۱۷).

As for which side he should take if he then wishes to go home, Imam al-Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote that if the two sides are equal for him and he has no need of one in particular, the right is better; Ibn ʿĀbidīn quoted this and accepted it. (۱/۵۳۱) Aḥsan al-Fatāwā likewise states that the Prophet ﷺ preferred the right both in departing and in turning towards the congregation. (احسن الفتاوی: ۳/۳٦۸)

Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) said that the Prophet ﷺ would turn from the right side when he faced the congregation, but when he left for home he would take the left side, that is, the worshippers' left — "since the houses lay in that direction" — and this same side is the right of the qiblah and the left of the worshipper. His view, then, is that if someone wishes to perform the sunnah units at home, it is better for him to follow the example of the Prophet ﷺ and take the right of the qiblah, which is the worshippers' left. (إعلاء السنن: ۳/۱۸٦. ۱۹۱۳).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
