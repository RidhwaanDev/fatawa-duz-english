### TITLE
The meaning of the verse ﴿وجعل القمر فيهن﴾

### QUESTION
Can the moon be reached or not? And the verse

﴿وجعل القمر فيهن﴾

shows that the moon is in the heavens, whereas to all appearances it is far below them.

### ANSWER
Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

That man can reach the moon is not against the teaching of Islam. So if the American astronauts make this claim, there is nothing objectionable in the sharia in accepting it. The moon too is a creation of Allah, and it is Allah who gave them the understanding to reach it; this too is one of the wonders of Allah. Allah has given Satan, the accursed, even greater power than this, so that in an instant he moves from one place to another; it was Allah who gave him the power to reach the stars. In the same way Allah will give the Dajjāl astonishing power, so that he will travel the world in a few moments. Sayyidunā Sulaymān (peace be upon him) used to travel through the air, and one of his ministers, who possessed knowledge of the divine Book, showed the marvel of bringing the royal throne of Bilqīs and setting it before him in less than the blink of an eye. So if the American astronauts went to the moon and brought back sand and stones, what is there to wonder at, when even a bird — the hoopoe — and others have already done such things?

Most people say that reaching the moon is impossible because the moon is in the sky, and the angels keep watch there. As evidence they cite this verse of the Qur'an:

﴿تبارك الذي جعل في السماء بروجاً وجعل فيها سراجاً وقمراً منيراً﴾

and likewise:

﴿وجعل القمر فيهن نوراً وجعل الشمس سراجاً﴾

The verses above state that Allah has made the moon, the sun and so on in the heavens. But the sense of "the heavens" is very broad. In Arabic, whatever is above is called samāʾ (sky):

أريد بالسماء السحاب فإن ما علاك سماء (بيضاوي، ص ۲٦)

Mufradāt al-Qurʾān states: "سماء كل شيء أعلاه" — that is, the upper part of anything is called samāʾ. In the language and usage of the Qur'an, samāʾ is also applied to the atmosphere below the sky and to the clouds. The Qur'an says:

﴿وأنزلنا من السماء مائً﴾ و ﴿وأنزلنا من السماء ماء ً طهوراً﴾

Yet rain water plainly falls to the earth from the clouds, as the Holy Qur'an says:

﴿وأنزلنا من المعصرات مائً ثجاجاً﴾

that is, "We sent down pouring rain from the water-laden clouds." In these verses the word samāʾ is used for the clouds. In the same way the word samāʾ is used for that atmosphere and open space in which, according to verses 1 and 2 quoted above, the moon and the sun are found — that is, they hang suspended in that space. That is why it was said:

﴿جعل فيها سراجاً وقمراً منيراً﴾.

Tafsīr Ḥaqqānī states:

"السماء" is used in several senses. The horizon is called samāʾ, the cloud is called samāʾ, and the sky is called samāʾ. In origin the word is applied to whatever is above, whether it is a cloud or the sky. In this place — ﴿وأنزلنا من السماء مائً﴾ — the clouds are meant, because rain comes down from there, though it is also possible to understand the sky. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ۳/۲۲۵)

The findings of modern science:

Modern science has found that the moon, the stars and so on are below the sky, not above it. This is supported by the narration of Ḥaḍrat Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him):

إن النجوم قناديل معلقة بين السماء والأرض بسلاسل من نور بأيدي ملائكة"

That is: the stars hang suspended between the sky and the earth on chains of light, and these chains are in the hands of angels of light. (روح المعانی: ۳۰/ ۵۰، و تفسیر کبیر سورہ تکویر: ٦۷).

After this narration ʿAllāmah al-Ālūsī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes: it is clear that the stars and the like are not embedded in the heavens, as the ancient philosophers claimed; rather they are suspended in this space. (روح المعانی: ۳۰/ ۵۰).

This also supports the modern scientists, who hold that the stars and the like are held in place by the force of attraction:

وظاهر هذا أن النجوم ليست في جرم أفلاك لها كما يقول الفلاسفة المتقدمون، بل معلقة في فضاء ويقرب منه من وجه قول الفلاسفة المحدثين فإنهم يقولون بكونها في فضاء أيضاً لكن بقوي متجاذبة. (روح المعاني: ۳۰/۵۰ ا).

Ḥakīm al-Ummah Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

Some Muslim scholars have concluded that the sun and the stars are not fixed into the sky; rather they stand in empty space, and the very space in which they move in their orbits is their sphere. (تکمیل الیقین، ص ۲١۱).

Ḥaḍrat Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) says:

"الفلك السماء والجمهور على أن الفلك موج مكفوف تحت السماء تجري فيه الشمس والقمر والنجوم". (مدارك التنزيل: ۲/۷۸).

ومثله في تفسير روح المعاني: "موج مكفوف تحت السماء يجري فيه الشمس والقمر". (تفسير روح المعاني: ۱۷/٤۰).

وفي التفسير الكبير للرازي: قال عطاء: وذلك أنها في قناديل معلقة بين السماء والأرض بسلاسل من النور. (التفسير الكبير: ۳۱/٦۲).

Certain verses of the Qur'an convey that the moon and the rest are in the lower part of the sky. In Sūrah al-Ṣāffāt:

﴿إنا زينا السماء الدنيا بزينة الكواكب﴾

that is, "We have adorned the nearest sky with the stars." And in Sūrah Ḥā Mīm al-Sajdah and Sūrah al-Mulk:

﴿زينا السماء الدنيا بمصابيح﴾

In these verses the words ﴿السماء الدنيا﴾, "the nearest sky", deserve careful thought. What appears to be meant is the lower part of the sky, or the space below it: just as a lamp hangs from a ceiling and adorns it, so the moon, the sun and the stars are suspended below the sky, and the roof of the sky is adorned by them. Muslims should not be afraid of the advance of science, nor bewildered by it. Truth is never defeated. The more science advances, the more the beliefs of Islam and the miracles of the prophets and the wonders of the saints are supported and strengthened. (ماخوذ از فتاوی رحیمیہ: ۳/ ۲۲٦ ۔ ۲۲۹).

For further detail see: (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۵/ ۳۳۵، و احسن الفتاوی: ۱/ ٤۱)

On what the word "فيهن" in the noble verse refers back to, see:

﴿وجعل القمر فيهن نوراً﴾ منوراً لوجه الأرض في ظلمة الليل وجعله فيهن مع أنه في إحداهن وهي السماء الدنيا كما يقال زيد في بغداد وهو في بقعة منها. (روح المعاني: ۲۹/۷۵).

So "فيهن" means "في بعضهن", in some of them, and by "the nearest sky" what appears to be meant is the lower sky or the space below it. And if "فيهن" is taken to mean that the moon is among the heavens — just as the earth itself is among the heavens and the sky surrounds the moon — that too is correct, because fī also carries the sense of bayna (among), as in ﴿فادخلي في عبادي﴾ "أي بين عبادي". An example of this is that if a football were placed in the middle of seven rows of players sitting around it, it would be correct to say "كرة القدم في صفوف اللاعبين أي بين صفوفهن". In that case the moon would be in space with sky above and below it; or else samāʾ would mean the open atmosphere, that is, the moon hangs suspended in these expanses of space.
