### TITLE
The ruling on making a bequest in favour of Imām Mahdī (may Allah be pleased with him)

### QUESTION
A man made the following bequest during his illness: "I have 19 Krugerrands (a gold coin minted and sold in South Africa) put away. They are being kept for Imām Mahdī, so that when he comes he may spend them on some good work that benefits the Muslims." These 19 Krugerrands amount to less than one third of the deceased's wealth. Now that he has died, what should be done with them? To keep them until the appearance of Imām Mahdī and to deliver them to him is an extremely difficult matter, and over so long a period they may well be lost and never reach Imām Mahdī. Please, then, tell us the solution to this problem.

### ANSWER
In the case in question there are two ways of dealing with the above bequest:

1. Among the conditions of a bequest is that the legatee be certainly alive, or present in the form of an unborn child; and this condition is not met. On that account this bequest will not be executed, and the 19 gold coins will be added to the deceased's estate and divided among the lawful heirs according to their prescribed shares. Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وشروطها: كون الموصي أهلاً للتمليك

وكون الموصي له حياً وقتها تحقيقاً أو تقديراً يشمل الحمل الموصي له. (الدر المختار: ٦/٦۴۹، سعيد).

It is recorded in Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ:

وأما شرائط الركن فبعضها يرجع إلى نفس الركن

فهو أن يكون القبول موافقاً للإيجاب، فإن خالف الإيجاب لم يصح القبول لأنه إذا خالفه لم يرتبط فبقي الإيجاب بلا قبول فلا يتم الركن. (بدائع الصنائع: ۷/۳۳٤، سعيد).

Majmūʿah Qawānīn Islāmī states: The conditions of a bequest: (d) that the legatee exist, either in reality or in law, and that he still exist after the death of the testator; (e) that the legatee be known and clearly identified. (Majmūʿah Qawānīn Islāmī, p. 250). And Imām Mahdī (may Allah be pleased with him) is neither in existence now nor known.

2. The second way is that, keeping in view the deceased's underlying purpose, the bequest be executed out of one third of his wealth in such a manner that the money is put into a madrasah, a mosque, or some other good work, so that it takes the form of an ongoing charity and its reward keeps reaching the soul of the deceased. Consider what Ibn Mājah Sharīf states:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: إن مما يلحق المؤمن من عمله وحسناته بعد موته علماً علمه ونشره وولداً صالحاً تركه ومصحفاً ورثه أو مسجد اً بناه أو بيتاً لابن السبيل بناه أو نهراً أجراه أو صدقة أخرجها من ماله في صحته وحياته يلحقه من بعد موته. (سنن ابن ماجه، ص ۲۱، ۲٤۲). قال محمد فؤاد عبد الباقي: إسناده حسن.

It is recorded in Āp ke Masāʾil aur un kā Ḥal:

One kind of charity is ongoing charity, which continues even after a person has died. For example: there was a shortage of water somewhere and he had a well dug there; he had a rest-house built for travellers; he had a mosque built, or contributed a share towards a mosque; or he had a religious school built, or arranged for the food, clothing, books and so forth of the students at a religious school; or he bought copies of the Noble Qur'an and gave them to the children of a madrasah; or he bought the religious books that scholars need and gave them to them, and so on. For as long as the benefit of these things continues, the reward for them will keep reaching that person even after his death. (Āp ke Masāʾil aur un kā Ḥal: 5/214).

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah mentions:

Such a use should be made of this money of the deceased that it takes the form of an ongoing charity, so that reward keeps reaching the deceased; the dead are greatly gladdened by reward. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 5/500).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
