### TITLE
Verifying the hadith about the sun prostrating

### QUESTION
I would like to know the meaning of a hadith. Its substance is that the Prophet ﷺ asked Abū Dharr (may Allah be pleased with him) where the sun goes. He said, "I do not know." The Prophet ﷺ said that at sunset it prostrates beneath the Throne and then moves on, and that near the Resurrection it will be commanded to return, and it will return.

The difficulty with this hadith is that the sun does not set but merely becomes hidden from our sight; so what is the meaning of its going beneath the Throne and prostrating? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
This hadith is in Bukhārī Sharīf. Consider the wording of the hadith:

عن أبي ذر رضي الله تعالى عنه قال: قال النبي ﷺ لا بي ذر حين غربت الشمس أتدري أين تذهب قلت: الله ورسوله أعلم قال: فإنها تذهب حتى تسجد تحت العرش فتستأذن فيؤذن لها ويوشك أن تسجد فلا يقبل منها وتستأذن فلا يؤذن لها يقال لها: ارجعي من حيث جئت فتطلع من مغربها فذلك قوله تعالى: ﴿والشمس تجري لمستقر لها ذلك تقدير العزيز العليم﴾. (رواه البخاري: ۱/۴۵۴، باب صفة الشمس والقمر بحسبان، تحت أبواب بدء الخلق).

Four difficulties are raised about this narration. (1) The sun does not in fact set; it merely becomes hidden from our sight and appears elsewhere. (2) The sun, the heavens and the earth are all beneath the Throne, so what is the meaning of its going beneath the Throne? (3) Prostration requires stillness, whereas the sun is to all appearances in motion at every moment, which is how time passes. (4) Where does the sun have the intelligence to understand the address and speech mentioned above and then to prostrate?

Consider the following answers to these questions.

(1) By setting is meant its setting in the Arabian peninsula and becoming hidden from sight. (2) The soul of the sun goes near the Throne and falls in prostration; the sun too has a soul, though we do not know of it. (3) Its soul is in prostration while its body to all appearances moves on; and prostration is not incompatible with movement. (4) The sun has understanding and awareness, though we do not know of it — just as the trunk of the date palm wept out of separation from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and a stone greeted the Messenger of Allah ﷺ with peace. All these answers hold good in their own way, but the better answer is that by this hadith the Ummah of Muḥammad ﷺ has been taught that the sun, for all its size and its shining bulk, is under the dominion and power of Allah and is engaged in complete obedience to Him; and prostration here carries the sense of complete obedience.

This covers the answers to all the questions. In it the sun's complete obedience at the moment of its setting has been placed before us and made plain to all. The meaning of its going beneath the Throne is that it is under the dominion of Allah: it was under that dominion before and it is so still; after setting it has not passed into anyone else's dominion, but is in the dominion of Allah alone. And for complete obedience intelligence is not required: this whole universe is under the power of Allah.

Mawlānā Shabbīr Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote a treatise entitled Sujūd al-Shams. Its substance is contained in what has been written here. The treatise is not before us at present; it has been printed in his collected treatises (تالیفاتِ عثمانی از ص ۴۳۱ تا ص ۴۵٦، ط: ادارۂ اسلامیات لاہور)۔

(2) The reason the sun in particular is singled out in this hadith as carrying out its duty under the dominion and power of Allah is perhaps this: that many polytheists regard the sun as the greatest shining deity, and the spectacle of the rising and setting of the sun on the shores of seas and rivers is so captivating that many non-Muslims gather there and worship the sun. So the Messenger of Allah ﷺ made it clear that the sun is neither a deity nor a god, but a creature that moves at the command of Allah.

Mawlānā Rūmī, in the Mathnawī, brought out the folly of sun-worshippers admirably. He said that the sun ripens our crops, fruits and vegetables for us by giving them heat, so that we may eat wholesome food; Allah has made it our servant and our cook. So worshipping the sun is like a man beginning to worship his own servant — a master falling to the worship of his own servant. What a pity for such fools!

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
