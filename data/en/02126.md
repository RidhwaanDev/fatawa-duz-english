### TITLE
Prizes offered to the purchasers of a monthly magazine

### QUESTION
Reader's Digest is a monthly magazine which people buy by paying in advance. A draw is held among them, and whoever's name comes out receives a prize. Is this practice permissible or not? And is it permissible to accept the prize when one's name comes out?

### ANSWER
If the Digest was bought for a price, and the managers of the Digest said that they would give such-and-such an item as a prize to whoever's name came out, then there is no objection to it in the Sacred Law. Indeed it belongs to the category of a conditional gratuity — that is, "we shall confer a favour upon those who buy the Digest," which is then made conditional upon the draw, just as a student is granted admission on condition of paying the fee. No gambling is present in this draw, because in a gambling draw one person who has a right is deprived and another becomes entitled to the money, whereas here nobody has a right to anything: in return for their money they are entitled to the Digest, which they have already received.

Imdād al-Aḥkām states:

The question was put: is the following agreement permissible in the Sacred Law or not — that if a purchaser goes on buying goods of the current brands for a whole year he is given a rebate at the end of the year of, say, two or three rupees per hundred, but that if he buys from anywhere else even once during the year he is given nothing at all?

Answer: This agreement is permissible, because the commission per hundred that is given to the purchaser at the end of the year is a gratuity on the part of the seller; it is not a binding right of the purchaser. And it is permissible to make a gratuity subject to a condition. (Imdād al-Aḥkām: 3/386.)

Elsewhere he writes:

Question: Is it permissible to take an admission fee and a monthly fee from students in madrasahs or not?

Answer: It is permissible, because this is not a wage but a contribution, and a condition is permissible in a contribution, since no compulsion follows from it: whoever does not accept the condition has the option of not enrolling.

ودليله أنه عليه السلام قال لمن أضافه وعائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها، قال: لا، قال: فلا إذن، قال في الثالثة وعائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها قال: نعم

(Imdād al-Aḥkām: 3/606.)

The sense of the noble hadith is that a man wished to invite the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ made his going conditional upon ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her) going as well, and at the third time the host accepted this. The Messenger of Allah's ﷺ going was a gratuity, which he made conditional upon ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her) going.

(Imdād al-Aḥkām is a collection of the fatāwā of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him), written under the supervision of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him). In the monthly al-Balāgh for Jumādā al-Ūlā 1432 AH an article by Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muftī ʿAbd al-Shakūr Tirmidhī (may Allah have mercy on him) was published in which he said: in reality this Imdād al-Aḥkām is a supplement to Ḥaḍrat Thānawī's (may Allah have mercy on him) Imdād al-Fatāwā. al-Balāgh, p. 40.)

If there is no other objectionable feature in the arrangement described above, there is no harm in doing this.
