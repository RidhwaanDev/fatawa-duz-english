### TITLE
Mawlid gatherings and standing up during them

### QUESTION
The mawlid gathering and standing up in it, the fortieth-day gathering for a deceased person, and giving charity on the eve of Friday are all matters over which the sacred law is silent. Why, then, are they called innovations?

### ANSWER
Mawlid gatherings and the like are in themselves permissible. But when they are held to be necessary or a sunnah, or when set times are fixed for them on one's own authority, or greater reward is believed to attach to that fixed time, or those who do not take part are condemned, and so on, then they are ruled to be innovations.

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) quotes the definition of innovation in the following words:

ما أحدث على خلاف الحق المتلقي عن رسول الله ﷺ من علم أو عمل أو حال بنوع شبهة واستحسان وجعله ديناً قويماً وصراطاً مستقيماً. (حاشية ابن عابدين: ۱/۵٦۰، ط: سعيد، والبحر الرائق: ۱/۳٤۹، كوئته).

Mawlānā Shabbīr Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

Innovation means doing something that has no basis in the Book, the sunnah, or the generations that were testified to as good, and doing it in the belief that it is part of the religion and earns reward. (Rāh-e Sunnat, p. 79, quoting Ḥamāʾil Sharīf, p. 702).

Muftī Kifāyatullāh (may Allah have mercy on him) (d. 1372 AH) writes:

Innovation refers to those things whose basis is not established in the sacred law — that is, no evidence for them is found in the Noble Qur'an or the noble hadiths, and they did not exist in the time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, the noble Companions, the Successors, or those who followed them — and which are then done, or left undone, in the belief that this is a matter of religion. (Rāh-e Sunnat, p. 79, quoting Taʿlīm al-Islām, part four, p. 27).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وكل مباح يؤدي إليه (أي إلى اعتقاد السنية أو الوجوب) فمكروه. وفي رد المحتار: (قوله فمكروه) الظاهر أنها تحريمية لأنه يدخل في الدين ما ليس منه. (رد المحتار: ۲/۱۲۰، سعيد).

Fixing times and forms for acts of worship on one's own authority is an innovation.

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim states:

"لا تخصوا ليلة الجمعة بقيام من بين الليالي ولا تختصوا يوم الجمعة بصيام من بين الأيام إلا أن يكون في صوم يصوم أحدكم. (رواه مسلم: ۱/۳٦۱، قديمي).

Al-Iʿtiṣām states:

ومنها: التزام العبادات المعينة في أوقات معينة لم يوجد لها ذلك التعيين في الشريعة. (الاعتصام: ۱/۲۹، دار الكتب العلمية، بيروت).

Al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

ولأن ذكر الله تعالى إذا قصد به التخصيص بوقت دون وقت أو بشيء دون شيء لم يكن مشروعاً حيث لم يرد الشرع به لأنه خلاف المشروع. (البحر الرائق: ۲/۱۵۹، ط: الماجدية).

Mawlānā Sarfarāz Khān (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

A thing does not have to be bad in itself to be an innovation. Even with the great acts of obedience and worship which the sacred law has left unrestricted, to impose restrictions on them of one's own accord, or to change the manner in which they are done, or to tie them to particular times on one's own authority, is also innovation in the terminology of the sacred law, and Islamic law will not approve of it. (Rāh-e Sunnat, p. 118, chapter four).

Our senior scholars (may Allah have mercy on them) have stated plainly that a mawlid gathering and the like are in themselves permissible; the harm lies in the innovations and excesses that are found in them.

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

والاحتفال بذكرى الولادة الشريفة إن كان خالياً من البدعات المروجة فهو جائز، بل مندوب كسائر أذكاره ﷺ. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 6/312, Karachi).

Mawlānā Sarfarāz Khān Ṣafdar says:

Standing up for a respected elder who comes in person is correct in certain situations, provided there is no going to extremes in either direction; Imam al-Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him) and others have argued for this from the hadith "قوموا إلى سيدكم" (شرح مسلم: ۲/۹۵). But we must look at what the noble Companions did on such occasions, and which practice the noble Prophet ﷺ liked and which he disliked. Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) says:

لم يكن شخص أحب إليه من رسول الله ﷺ وكانوا إذا رأوه لم يقوموا لما يعلمون من كراهيته لذلك. (رواه الترمذي: ۲/۱۰۰، وقال: هذا حديث حسن صحيح، ومشكوة: ۲/٤۰۳، ومسند احمد: ۳/۱۵۱، والأدب المفرد، ص ۱۳۸).

For the noble Companions there was nobody dearer than the person of the Prophet ﷺ, yet when they saw him they did not stand up, because they knew that he disliked this standing.

This authentic hadith establishes that the Prophet ﷺ did not like people standing for him, and that is why the noble Companions, for all their love of him, did not stand. It is a strange thing that a practice which the Prophet ﷺ himself did not like, and which the Companions did not adopt despite their perfect love for him, should today be declared permissible and recommended — indeed called necessary and obligatory, with those who do not stand being branded unbelievers — when his coming to any mawlid gathering is not established by any evidence of the sacred law at all, and nobody sees him there. (Rāh-e Sunnat, p. 168, on standing at the mawlid, Maktabah Ṣafdariyyah).
