### TITLE
Removing a doubt about a vow to do something that is sinful in itself

### QUESTION
Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh Raḥmānī Ṣāḥib (may his shade endure) has written in Qāmūs al-Fiqh (۵/۱۸۳) that in a vow to do something sinful it is obligatory to abandon the sin and unlawful to fulfil the vow, but that paying the expiation of an oath is obligatory. Yet from the explicit statements of the jurists and the senior scholars given above it appears that expiation is not binding either. What is the answer to this?

### ANSWER
On the face of it Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā's wording is open to question, and in the light of the explicit statements of the jurists it does not appear to be correct. In all likelihood Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā's mind did not turn to the division of vows involving sin.

Consider Mawlānā's own words. Qāmūs al-Fiqh states:

The thing vowed may itself be a sin, as in a vow to drink wine. Fulfilling such a vow is unlawful and abandoning it is obligatory, and there is the consensus of the ummah upon this. However, as was mentioned before this, in such a case paying the expiation of an oath is obligatory. In our own day people make vows to hold mawlid gatherings; all of this falls under this ruling, and it is not permissible to fulfil it. Rather one should pay the expiation of an oath, and should repent of making such vows in future. (قاموس الفقہ: ۵/۱۸۳) ۔

In the passage quoted above Mawlānā has given an example of a sin that is sinful in itself, and has then recorded the ruling that expiation is obligatory; whereas in the light of the statements of the jurists the ruling appears to be the opposite. The correct and preponderant view, therefore, is that a vow to do something that is sinful in itself does not take effect at all, and that expiation is not obligatory either.
