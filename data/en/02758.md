### TITLE

The ruling on making coloured flowers and the like on the body

### QUESTION

It is obvious that tattooing (washm) is impermissible, in which the body is pricked with needles or other instruments. Nowadays, however, flowers and other designs are made on the body from outside with certain instruments; in this the body is not pierced, and after a few days it disappears of itself. What is the ruling on making such flowers and the like on the body? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER

In the case described, if needles are not inserted into the body, and no layer forms like a plaster which would prevent wuḍūʾ and ghusl from being valid, but it is only a colour, and resemblance to other nations has not been deliberately adopted in it, then in view of these conditions there is scope for it. As for washm, that is, tattooing the body, that is not permitted, because the prohibition of it has come in the aḥādīth.
Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī Sharīf has:
عن علقمة عن عبد الله قال: لعن الله الواشمات والمستوشمات

الخ. (رواه البخاري، رقم: ٤۸۸٦).
قال العلامة العيني رحمه الله: قوله الوشم وهو غرز إبرة أو مِسلة ونحوهما في ظهر الكف أو المعصم أو الشفة وغير ذلك من بدن المرأة حتى يسيل منه الدم ثم يحشي ذلك الموضع بكحل أو نورة أو نيلة

وهو حرام على الفاعل والمفعول بها

وقال النووي رحمه الله: قال أصحابنا الموضع الذي وشم يصير نجساً فإن أمكن إزالته بالعلاج وجبت إزالته

الخ. (عمدة القاري: باب ما آتاكم الرسول فخذوه. وكذا في شرح النووي على صحيح مسلم: ۲/۲۰۵، ومرقاة المفاتيح: ۸/).

ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:
والواشمة: التي تشم في الوجه والذراع، وهو أن تغرز الجلد بإبرة ثم يحشي بكحل أو نيل فيزرق. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۳۷۳، سعيد).

Qāmūs al-Fiqh states:
Among women there has been an ancient fashion of tattooing the body: with a needle a fine hole is made in some part of the body, and then collyrium or some coloured substance is applied to it, so that black dots, or dots of some other colour, appear. This was regarded as adding to beauty, and the practice still exists among certain tribes and peoples. This is what is called "وشم" in Arabic. (قاموس الفقہ: ۵/۲٦۸) ۔
Dr ʿAbd al-Ḥayy ʿĀrifī writes:
In the same way the practice of tattooing and of having oneself tattooed has been going on from ancient times; in Arabic it is called washm. Its method is that deep marks are made in the skin with a needle or something similar and collyrium or indigo is filled into them, and in this way pictures of animals and of other things are made on the body. Among the Hindus of India this practice is very common, and the true Messenger of Allah, our Master of Madīnah ﷺ, forbade this too and cursed the woman who does it. (خواتین کے لیے شرعی احکام، ص ٤٦۳) ۔ See further: (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ۸/۳٤٦) ۔
Tāj al-ʿArūs states:
والوشم كالوعد غرز الإبرة في البدن. (تاج العروس: ۹/۹٤).

Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd states: الوشم: the mark left by pricking with a needle and filling it with blue or green colour; tattooing; a change in the colour of the skin caused by a blow or the like. (القاموس الوحید: ۲/۱۸۵۵) ۔
From the commentaries on the aḥādīth mentioned above, from the statements of the jurists and from the statements of the lexicographers it becomes clear that if the body is pricked and colour is filled in, that is washm and is forbidden; but if only designs are made without any pricking, that does not come under the forbidden washm. Rather it resembles reddening the hands and feet with henna, and it is permitted. It is, however, necessary to avoid deliberate imitation of other nations.
