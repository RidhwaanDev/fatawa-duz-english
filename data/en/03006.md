### TITLE
The ruling on killing a fornicator without the order of a ruler

### QUESTION
In the well-known Canadian city of Montreal, a man who claimed to be a Muslim killed his three daughters because they had committed fornication, or because they had relationships with unrelated boys, and he gave this killing the name of a religious killing. Is this really in accordance with Islamic teachings or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
The actions of people cannot be made the standard of Islamic teaching, because on many occasions people's actions are contrary to Islamic teaching and bear not even a remote relation to Islam and the Islamic sharīʿah, even though those people may give them the name of Islam and religion. This incident is a fresh example of just that.

The basic condition for enforcing the sharʿī prescribed punishments is that there be an Islamic government and a sharʿī judge present. Without this, the prescribed punishments and retaliation cannot be carried out; otherwise, far from the sharīʿah being enforced, corruption will become widespread.

Consider what is stated in Fatāwā al-Shāmī:

فيشترط الإمام لاستيفاء الحدود. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۵٤۹، سعيد).

Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Islāmī ʿAdālat:

Where a matter is such that to place it in the hands of the general public would be a cause of discord and corruption, and where because of it there is the danger of such disorder as would lead to the destruction of life and property — whenever there is the apprehension of such a state of affairs arising in some matter, there the order of a ruler becomes necessary; for example, the prescribed punishments. (اسلامی عدالت، ص ۲۴۱، قاضی پبلشرز)۔

Moreover, there are still further conditions for stoning: (1) that it be established by four eyewitnesses; (2) that the person be muḥṣan — and for someone to be characterised by the quality of iḥṣān it is a condition that there be a valid marriage.

Then, after the conditions have been fulfilled, the prescribed punishment is carried out by the order of the judge, that is, a Muslim judge.

Al-Hidāyah states:

فالبينة أن تشهد أربعة من الشهود على رجل وامرأة بالزنا

وقالوا رأيناه وطيها في فرجها كالميل في المكحلة

ويبتدئ الشهود برجمه ثم الإمام ثم الناس. (الهداية: ۲/ ۵۰۷، ۵۰۹).

The upshot is that there are stringent conditions for establishing stoning, and by the slightest doubt the prescribed punishment lapses; and in Islamic history not a single case has occurred in which stoning was established by witnesses.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
