### TITLE
Liability when goods are lost before they reach the buyer

### QUESTION
Zayd bought a large quantity of goods from ʿAmr and told him to hand them over to a truck. ʿAmr did so, and on the way thieves made off with the goods. Is the compensation binding on the seller, or is the buyer responsible for it?

### ANSWER
In the sharia, liability does not pass merely by the sale being concluded and ownership being established; it passes only once the buyer has taken possession. The reality of possession and the particular form it takes are not laid down in the Qurʾān and hadith, so the matter turns on custom. Accordingly, as long as the buyer has not taken possession in the customary sense, the goods will be treated as still belonging to the seller, and if they are destroyed the seller is responsible. Yes, if possession had been obtained in the customary sense and the goods were destroyed after that, the buyer is responsible for them.

So, in the case in question, if the goods were dispatched at the buyer's instruction and the buyer also paid the cost of it, this will be counted as customary possession, and if the goods are lost the buyer is responsible for them. And if the buyer gave the instruction to dispatch and send them but the seller paid the cost and so on, possession is established in this case as well, and the buyer will be responsible if they are destroyed. But if the seller sent them of his own accord — the buyer neither instructed him nor paid the cost and so on — then the seller will be responsible if they are destroyed, because possession was not established.

See Radd al-Muḥtār, which states:

وحاصله أن التخلية قبض حكماً لو مع القدرة عليه بلا كلفة لكن ذلك يختلف بحسب حال المبيع،

قال أجمعوا على أن التخلية في البيع الجائز تكون قبضاً

اشتري وعاء لبن خاثر في السوق فأمر البائع بنقله إلى منزله فسقط في الطريق فعلي البائع إن لم يقبضه المشتري،

إلا أن يقول (المشتري): ادفعه إلى الغلام لأنه توكيل للغلام والدفع إليه كالدفع إلى المشتري. (رد المحتار: ٤/۵٦۲، ۵٦۳، مطلب في شروط التخلية، سعيد. وكذا في الفتاوى البزازية على هامش الهندية: ٤/٤۹۹. والفتاوى الهندية: ۳/۱۹).

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

ولا يشترط القبض بالبراجم، لأن معني القبض هو التمكين والتخلي وارتفاع الموانع عرفاً وعادة حقيقة. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/۱٤۸، سعيد).
وفيه أيضاً: وكذلك لو فعل البائع شيئاً من ذلك بأمر المشتري لأن فعله بأمر المشتري بمنزلة فعل المشتري بنفسه. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/۲٤٦).

Al-Mughnī states:

لأن القبض مطلق في الشرع فيجب الرجوع فيه إلى العرف. (المغني لا بن قدامة الحنبلي، ٤/۲۲۰).

ʿIṭr-e-Hidāyah states: Goods sent by rail or post and the like are regarded as being in the possession of the buyer who ordered them as soon as they are dispatched. If the buyer has written that certain goods should be sent by rail or as a postal parcel, and the owner sent them accordingly, then the seller is not responsible if they are lost on the way, because the seller handed them over to the buyer's agent (that is, the rail service or the post). And if the buyer did not order them but the seller sent them of his own accord, then this dispatch does not constitute possession on the buyer's part, and so if they are lost before reaching the buyer, the buyer is not responsible for them. (ʿIṭr-e-Hidāyah, p. 187, The rulings on parcels).
