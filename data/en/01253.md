### TITLE
Engagement and the customs that go with it

### QUESTION
What is the reality of an engagement in the sacred sharia, and what is the ruling on the customs that go with it?

### ANSWER
Before the marriage the young man may look at the young woman with the intention of marrying her. After that the two families may settle the match between themselves and a promise of marriage may be made; and if each side wishes to give some gifts in keeping with its means, it may do so out of affection and love. In the light of the Prophet's words "تهادوا تحابوا" there is every hope that the marriage will then be concluded and the wishes of both families fulfilled. There is also room for a short conversation at the time of looking at the young woman. This is what an engagement really is in the sacred sharia. An engagement is no more than a promise of marriage; it is not in reality a marriage, and so the rulings of marriage do not take effect.

Beyond this, all the other customs should be given up. Customs and usages differ from place to place. Among the customs of this country at the present time, a few that deserve mention are: sitting together on a stage all day and talking like husband and wife; shaking hands; putting rings on one another's fingers; decorating a hall and observing various rituals in it; and, on such occasions in particular, taking pictures with a video camera, and the mixing of unrelated men and women. Moreover, keeping up these customs often leaves the bride's family in financial straits, so that they have to take out a loan, whereas a hadith forbids taking a loan without need.

In short, it is necessary to give up these absurdities and, acting upon "الدین یسر", to save oneself as far as possible from falling into such troubles and hardships.

The evidence is as follows:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله تعالى عنه قال: جاء رجل إلى النبي ﷺ فقال: إني تزوجت امرأة من الأنصار، قال: فانظر إليها فإن في أعين الأنصار شيئاً. (رواه مسلم، مشكاة شريف: ۲/۲٦۸).
عن سهل بن سعد رضي الله تعالى عنه قال: ذكر لرسول الله ﷺ امرأة من العرب فأمر أبا أسيد أن يرسل إليها فأرسل إليها فقدمت فنزلت في أجم بني ساعدة فخرج رسول الله ﷺ حتى جاءها فدخل عليها فإذا امرأة منكسة رأسها فلما كلمها رسول الله ﷺ قالت: أعوذ بالله منك، قال: قد عذتك مني، فقالوا لها: أتدري من هذا؟ فقالت: لا، فقالوا: هذا رسول الله ﷺ جاء ك ليخطبك قالت: أنا كنت أشقي من ذلك. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۱٦۹).

Al-Ṭaḥṭāwī states:

قوله والنظر إليها قبله، أي فإنه مندوب، لأنه داعية للألفة فينظر إلى وجهها وكفيها وإن لم تأذن له هيأو وليها إذا علم أنه يجاب في نكاحها. (حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ۲/۵، كوئته)

Shāmī states:

قال في شرح الطحاوي: لو قال: هل أعطيتنيها؟ فقال: أعطيت، إن كان المجلس للوعد فوعد. (فتاوى الشامي: ۳/۱۱، سعيد).

Majmūʿah Qawānīn-e-Islāmī states:

It is permissible for a man to look at the woman he wishes to marry, and it is permissible for the woman as well to look at the man who has sent the proposal. The settling of a match is, in the sharia, a "promise of marriage", and it carries no legal binding force. Giving a ring by way of engagement, giving some money, presenting clothes, or giving any other gift is a sign of consent to the marriage; but no one can be compelled to marry on account of such an act. (مجموعہ قوانین اسلامی: ص۳٦، ۳۷).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

What is intended by an engagement is a promise of marriage. That is, the words spoken at the time of the engagement are words of promising marriage; concluding the marriage itself is not intended. This is why the marriage is performed later at a gathering arranged for that purpose. (کفایت المفتی: ۵/۵۱، دار الاشاعت).

Āp ke Masāʾil states:

The gist of the question is this: meeting one's fiancée, speaking to her on the telephone and the like, and going about with her, is not right; but what is the ruling if this is common in society and nobody thinks ill of it?

Answer: before the marriage the fiancé is a stranger, so before the marriage the ruling on the fiancé is the same as that on any unrelated man — it is not permissible for the woman to mix with him. That something has become customary in society is no proof; a wrong custom that goes against the sharia itself stands in need of reform. Nor is there any permission for close ties before the marriage: neither mixing together nor being alone together is allowed. Meeting and mingling before the marriage is in itself an immoral act. (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ۵/۳٤، ۳۵)

In Bahishtī Zewar, Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him), after describing the customs of engagement, writes:

In short, it is necessary to give up all these absurdities. A proposal of marriage can be conveyed by a single postcard or by word of mouth. The other side, after making the necessary enquiries for itself, may give its promise by a postcard or simply by word of mouth — and the engagement is done. (بہشتی زیور حصۂ ششم ص۲۲).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
