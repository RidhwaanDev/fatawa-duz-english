### TITLE
Divorce pronounced without referring it to the wife

### QUESTION
A man had a quarrel with his wife's brothers, and the cause of the quarrel was his wife. He spoke these words: "مجھ پر تین طلاق کے ساتھ مطلقہ ہے" — "she is divorced from me with three divorces". He did not name his wife or refer the words to her. Has divorce taken effect or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, when he says these words — "مجھ پر تین طلاق کے ساتھ مطلقہ ہے", "she is divorced from me with three divorces" — with regard to his wife, divorce has taken effect, because divorce is given to a wife and to no one else. If, however, the husband states on oath that he did not mean his wife, then divorce will not take effect.

See what al-Durr al-Mukhtār says:

قيد بخطابها لأنه لو قال: إن خرجت يقع الطلاق أولا تخرجي إلا بإذني فإني حلفت بالطلاق فخرجت لم يقع لتركه الإضافة إليها. وفي رد المحتار: والمفهوم من تعليل الشارح تبعاً للبحر عدم الوقوع أصلاً لفقد شرط الإضافة، مع أنه لو أراد طلاقها تكون الإضافة موجودة ويكون المعنى فإني حلفت بالطلاق منك أو بطلاقك، ولا يلزم كون الإضافة صريحة في كلامه، لما في البحر لو قال: طالق فقيل له من عنيت؟ فقال: امرأتي طلقت امرأته. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۳/۲٤۸، باب الصريح، سعيد).

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states:

For divorce to take effect it is not necessary that the words be referred to the wife in explicit terms. (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: جلد دوم: ص۵۰٤) ۔

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) has reproduced a treatise of Ḥaḍrat ʿAllāmah Kashmīrī (may Allah have mercy on him) entitled "حکم الانصاف فی الطلاق الغیر المضاف". See: (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: جلد دوم: ص۵۰۵. ۵۱۰، دار الاشاعت) ۔

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

When a husband utters the word "divorce" with regard to his wife, divorce takes effect by that too, even if he has not spoken a complete sentence ("I have divorced you"); that is nevertheless what he means. If, however, the husband says that he did not pronounce a divorce and did not utter the word with regard to his wife, then the husband's statement, on oath, will be accepted. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۲/۲۷٤، مبوب و مرتب) ۔

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

In these words of Zayd's that are mentioned in the question, the word "divorce" is explicit, but its being referred to the wife is not explicit. So if Zayd states on oath that he did not say these words to his wife, his statement and his oath will be accepted, and it will not be ruled that divorce has taken place. (کفایت المفتی: ٦/۵٤، دار الاشاعت) ۔
