### TITLE
Saying "The marriage is no longer intact"

### QUESTION
If a man, speaking to someone else about his wife, says, "My marriage with this woman is no longer intact", is a fresh marriage needed?

### ANSWER
This is among the allusive expressions. If he meant by it that the marriage no longer stands but has been broken, an irrevocable divorce has taken effect; but if he meant that some fault and trouble has arisen in the marriage although the marriage itself still stands, no divorce takes place.

It is recorded in Fatāwā Sirājiyyah that apart from the three allusive expressions

"اعتدي، اختاري، أمرك بيدك"

every other allusive expression requires an intention. "The marriage is no longer intact" is likewise among the allusive expressions. With some allusive expressions, however, a divorce takes effect without any intention when a discussion of divorce is going on — for example,

"أنت خلية، برية، بتة، بائن"

are of that kind.

وفيما عداها من ألفاظ الكنايات "أي ما عدا الثلاثة" يصدق، ولو قال: في مذاكرة الطلاق أحد هذه الألفاظ الثلاثة أوقال: أنت خلية أو برية أو بتة أو بائن أو حرام لم يصدق. (فتاوى سراجية: كتاب الطلاق، باب المتفرقات،).

According to the author of al-Baḥr, ʿAllāmah Ibn Nujaym al-Miṣrī (may Allah have mercy on him), and ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him), though, denying the marriage as it stands brings about a revocable divorce, کما مر آنفاً ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
