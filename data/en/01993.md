### TITLE
The ruling on selling goodwill and a trade mark

### QUESTION
Is it permissible in the Sharīʿah to sell a trade name (goodwill) and a trade mark, or not?

### ANSWER
In the present age, along with commercial development, the question of the trade name and the trade mark has arisen. A trader or a company manufactures goods and places its own name and mark upon what it produces, the purpose being that it should be recognised in the market that these goods belong to such-and-such a company; and this generates demand and renown. Because it produces fine goods, the company gradually attains a certain standing. Then, once the problem of ordinary people being deceived arose, the government began registering trade marks and goodwill, and traders were forbidden to use names and marks registered by others. If other people use such a name, from a business point of view this is a very grave uncertainty and deception, and a fraud upon purchasers. One of the important principles governing legal dealings in the pure Sharīʿah is that no act should be performed which becomes a cause of deception for others; registration is therefore entirely in accordance with the Sharīʿah. Furthermore, since this is a protected right and, on account of its renown, an economic interest has also become attached to it, it will be given the ruling of property and its sale and purchase will be permitted.

The jurists have given various definitions of māl (property).

Consider: ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

المراد بالمال ما يميل إليه الطبع ويمكن ادخاره لوقت الحاجة، والمالية تثبت بتمول الناس كافة أو بعضهم والتقوم يثبت بها وبإباحة الانتفاع به شرعاً

وفي البحر عن الحاوي القدسي: المال اسم لغير الآدمي خلق لمصالح الآدمي، وأمكن إحرازه والتصرف فيه على وجه الاختيار. (فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۵۰۱، مطلب في تعريف المال، سعيد).

The marginal notes on Majmaʿ al-Anhur state:

والمراد بالمال عين يجري فيه التنافس والابتذال. (الدرر المنتقي بهامش مجمع الأنهر: ۲/۳).

ʿAllāmah Abū Bakr al-Kāsānī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

منها أن يكون مالاً

وسواء كان المال عيناً أو منفعة عند عامة العلماء. (بدائع الصنائع: ۷/۳۵۲، كتاب الوصايا، سعيد. وكذا في كتاب الصلح، ٦/٤۲، سعيد).

In the passage quoted above, Malik al-ʿUlamāʾ ʿAllāmah Abū Bakr al-Kāsānī (may Allah have mercy on him) has accepted usufruct too as being property.

Shaykh Muṣṭafā al-Zarqā has taken a very broad view in defining property. Consider:

والتعريف الصحيح يمكن أن يستنبط من مجموع ما ذكره الفقهاء عن المال وخصائصه في مختلف المناسبات، فقد قالوا: إن المال اسم لغير الآدمي

الخ. (المدخل الفقهي العام: ۳/۱۱۵).

Shaykh Muṣṭafā al-Zarqā said: If a correct definition of property is framed with all the statements of the jurists before one, and with regard also to the characteristics of property mentioned by them on various occasions, then it will be said that property is a name for everything other than the human being.

Mawlānā Khālid Sayfullāh says that there are three basic elements required for something to be property: (1) the first is that it be lawful in the Sharīʿah; (2) the second is that the thing be capable of being benefited from; (3) the third element is that whatever thing has become current in customary usage as an object of sale and purchase is property. And it would be no exaggeration to say that all four schools of fiqh are agreed on this. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/171).

In Ḥuqūq awr un kī Kharīd o Farokht, Mawlānā ʿUmar ʿĀbidīn Qāsimī writes:

The upshot of this whole discussion is: (1) in the view of the majority, usufructs and rights may be bought and sold as well as property;

(2) if it is indeed necessary that the object of a sale be property, then reflection upon the statements of the jurists shows that rights and usufructs are themselves included within property. This is not only the position of the three imams; precedents for it exist among the Ḥanafīs as well.

See: (فتح القدير: ٦/٤۲۹، بيع الطريق وهبته، دار الفكر. والدر المختار: ٤/۱۳۲، سعيد. وفتاوى الشامي: ۵/۸۰، وصح بيع حق المرور الخ، سعيد. والعناية بهامش فتح القدير: ٦/٤۲۹، دار الفكر. والفتاوى الهندية: ۳/۱۳۱، ٦/۲۹٤. وتبيين الحقائق: ٤/۵۲، ملتان. ومنحة الخالق: ۵/۲۳٤. ۲۳۵، كوئته).

(3) Custom and usage carry fundamental importance in a thing's being property.

(4) There is no single fixed form of being capable of storage; a thing's being legally protected is also sufficient for it to be capable of storage. (Ḥuqūq awr un kī Kharīd o Farokht, by Mawlānā ʿUmar ʿĀbidīn Qāsimī, p. 141).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

Every person has the right to give his business a name; but if a person has named his business "ʿIṭristān" or "Gulshan-e-Adab" and his commercial interest has become attached to it, then another person no longer has the right to adopt that name. And since the acquisition of wealth and of trade in the future is intended by means of a particular name, payment for goodwill is permissible. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 3/120).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Taqī ʿUthmānī says:

The sale of rights ought to be permissible subject to the following conditions:

(1) that the right be established at present and not merely expected in the future;

(2) that the right be established for its holder in its own right — such as retaliation, inheritance and the like — and not established merely to repel harm, such as the right of pre-emption, the option of a party given a choice, and so forth;

(3) that the right be capable of being transferred to another;

(4) that it be capable of precise definition, that is, that it not entail uncertainty or ignorance of the object;

(5) that in the custom of merchants this right hold the standing of property and tangible goods in dealings. (Fiqhī Maqālāt: 1/192).

Some later scholars say that the fitting course is to take payment for relinquishing the right rather than for selling it, and they have drawn an analogy between this question and the question of نزول عن الوظائف (relinquishing a religious post for payment).

Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وفي الأشباه: لا يجوز الاعتياض عن الحقوق المجردة كحق الشفعة، وعلى هذا لا يجوز الاعتياض عن الوظائف بالأوقاف، وفيها في آخر بحث تعارض العرف مع اللغة: المذهب عدم اعتبار العرف الخاص، لكن أفتى كثير باعتباره، وعليه فيفتي بجواز النزول عن الوظائف بمال. (الدر المختار: ٤/۵۱۸، ۵۱۹، سعيد. وكذا في الأشباه: ۱/۲۸٦).

وفي "الفقه الحنفي في ثوبه الجديد": فالسؤال الآن: هل يجوز بيع الاسم التجاري أو العلامة التجارية؟

وظاهر أن الاسم والعلامة ليس عيناً مادياً، وإنما هو عبارة عن حق استعمال هذا الاسم أو العلامة، وهذا الحق ثبت لصاحبه إصالة بحكم الأسبقية والتسجيل الحكومي، وهو حق ثابت في الحال، وليس متوقعاً في المستقبل، وهو حق يقبل الانتقال إلى آخر، ولكنه ليس حقاً ثابتاً في عين قائمة، فعلي ضوء القواعد التي استخلصناها من كلام الفقهاء ينبغي أن يجوز الاعتياض عنه عن طريق التنازل دون البيع. وبهذا أفتي شيخ مشايخنا العلامة أشرف على التهانوى رحمه الله وقاسه على مسألة النزول عن الوظائف بمال، وحكي فيه عبارة ابن عابدين رحمه الله التي نقلها في مسألة النزول عن الوظائف. (الفقه الحنفي في ثوبه الجديد: ٤/۳۷، بيع الاسم التجاري والعلامة التجارية، دمشق).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

The name of a factory too resembles the right to a religious post, in that it is ثابت علی الاصالۃ (established in its own right) and not established for the repelling of harm; both are at present relative matters, and both are a means of acquiring wealth in the future. On this basis, then, there appears to be room for giving such a payment — though for the one who takes it, it is contrary to piety; yet in case of need this too will be permitted. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 3/119).

A passage of the author of al-Hidāyah also points towards this. Consider:

وإذا أقعد الخياط أو الصباغ في حانوته من يطرح عليه العمل بالنصف فهو جائز لأن هذه شركة الوجوه في الحقيقة فهذا (الخياط أو الصباغ) بوجاهته يقبل (أي العمل من الناس) وهذا (أي من يطرح عليه العمل) بحذاقته يعمل فينتظم بذلك المصلحة فلا تضره الجهالة فيما يحصل. (الهداية: ۳/۳۱۷، كتاب الإجارة).

That is to say: just as standing is a means of renown and of attracting custom, in return for which it is permissible to take a wage, that is, property, so too a trade mark and a trade name are a means of renown and of attracting custom; hence taking property in return for them too ought to be permissible.

In sum, when a trade mark and a trade name (goodwill) are duly registered by the government, then both selling them and taking payment for them are permissible and sound.

For further detail see: (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/ "The reality of property and the sale and purchase of rights", from 157 to 190; Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: vol. 3; Fiqhī Maqālāt, "The sale and purchase of abstract rights", 1/159 to 228; the treatise Ḥuqūq awr un kī Kharīd o Farokht by Mawlānā ʿUmar ʿĀbidīn Qāsimī; Niẓām al-Fatāwā: 2/314; and Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah: 6/67).
