### TITLE
The wisdom behind the obligation of the waiting period

### QUESTION
From the discussion above it becomes quite clear that there are two wisdoms behind the waiting period:

(1) Making certain that the womb is empty and free of pregnancy. This safeguards lineage.

(2) Giving expression to regret and grief at the severing of the marriage tie.

"Islāmī Qānūn Nikāḥ o Ṭalāq" records:

The wisdom of the waiting period is that the woman grieves at her separation from her husband and mourns his death. The second wisdom in the waiting period is making certain that the womb is empty — that is, being satisfied that nothing of the husband's semen remains in the woman's womb, so that if she marries again there is no fear of lineage being mixed and of doubt arising as to which man the child belongs to. (اسلامی قانون نکاح و طلاق، از مولانا یعقوب قاسمی صاحب، ص۱٦۷) ۔

See further: (کتاب الفتاوی: ۵/۱٤۰، ط: زمزم ۔ و اسلامی فقہ: ۲/۱۸۸، عدت کا مقصد) ۔

Objection: One difficulty comes to mind here. If the waiting period is binding because the blessing of marriage has been lost, then it ought to be binding when a divorce takes place before intercourse or seclusion as well; yet the statements of the jurists make it clear that there is no waiting period when the divorce comes before intercourse and seclusion. What is the answer to this?

### ANSWER
The answer to this objection is that before intercourse or seclusion the blessing of marriage has not become firmly established: the woman has not yet tasted its affection and love, so the blessing is not full and complete, and for that reason the waiting period is not binding either.

The learned Ibn al-Humām (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

وقد أفاد المصنف فيما سيأتي أنها أيضاً تجب لقضاء حق النكاح بإظهار الأسف عليه

بخلاف غير المتأكد وهو ما قبل الدخول لا يؤسف عليه إذ لا ألف ولا مودة فيه. (الهداية مع فتح القدير: ٤/۳۰۸، باب العدة، ط: دار الفكر).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
