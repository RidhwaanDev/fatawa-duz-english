### TITLE
Rabīʿat al-Raʾy's criticism of a Companion

### QUESTION
The statement "كل الصحابة عدول" is well known, yet I am surprised that a scholar of such high standing as Rabīʿah said something improper about a female Companion. His words are quoted in Ṭaḥāwī:

"لو أن بسرة رضي الله عنه شهدت على هذه النعل لما أجزت شهادتها". (شرح معاني الآثار: ۱/۵۸، باب مس الفرج هل يجب فيه الوضوء أم لا، فيصل).

Can the imams say such things about a Companion in order to strengthen their own argument? We are taught that abusing the Companions harms one's faith and is a sign of hypocrisy. What do the muftis say?

### ANSWER
Imam al-Ṭaḥāwī (may Allah have mercy on him) (۲۳۹. ۳۲۱هـ) has reported this criticism of Busrah (may Allah be pleased with her) from Rabīʿat al-Raʾy, namely that even if she were to testify about a shoe her testimony would not be accepted. The first point to note here is that Ḥāfiẓ al-Dhahabī, in Mīzān al-Iʿtidāl, quotes Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ about Rabīʿat al-Raʾy himself: "أنه تغير في الآخر". Abū Ḥātim Muḥammad ibn Ḥibbān (م۳۵٤هـ) also listed him in Kitāb al-Ḍuʿafāʾ. See: (الميزان: ۲/۲۳٤، ترجمة: ۲۷۵۳).

Hadith scholars in general consider Rabīʿah reliable, but it is quite possible that this remark came from him in his final period, once his memory had deteriorated. When that happens, anger can dominate a person's mind and the tongue sometimes runs out of control.

The second point is that the chain by which Imam al-Ṭaḥāwī (may Allah have mercy on him) reports this remark contains someone called Ibn Zayd, and it is not known who he is. Some say he is Yūnus ibn Zayd al-Aylī, a narrator of the two Ṣaḥīḥs; some say he is ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam, who is a weak — indeed a very weak — narrator; and some say he is Usāmah ibn Zayd. Such is the condition of the chain. The substance of the remark itself also suggests that the man must be ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam.

The third point is that statements of this kind are given a proper interpretation. For example, when the words "صدق أم كذب" are used with regard to the Companions, they carry the sense of "أصاب أم أخطأ". So what is meant here is that if Busrah (may Allah be pleased with her) reports a hadith, it should not be accepted without scrutiny; rather it should be examined in the way testimony is examined — did she hear exactly these words, or did she narrate the sense while the wording was something else? It may be that the words she heard were correct but she understood them according to her own mind, while their real meaning is different. For instance, touching the private part may here be an indirect reference to urinating, or "فليتوضأ" may be intended as a recommendation rather than an obligation. Apart from this, the narration about touching the private part is itself open to discussion because Imam al-Zuhrī reports it with "ʿan" (without stating that he heard it directly), and he was a mudallis (a narrator who conceals such gaps).
