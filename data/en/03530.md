### TITLE
The ruling on a fast when sour water comes into the fasting person's mouth

### QUESTION
A man got up in the morning and a little sour water came into his mouth. He swallowed this water while he was fasting, but it was not a mouthful — it was only a little. Has the fast been broken or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, water coming into the mouth takes the ruling of vomit; and moreover it was not a mouthful. On this basis no defect has entered the fast. The ruling regarding vomit is that the fast is broken in two cases only: 1. Vomit comes up of its own accord in a mouthful and the person deliberately swallows it while remembering that he is fasting. 2. A person deliberately vomits a mouthful. Apart from these two cases the fast is not broken. Consider the noble ḥadīth:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: "من ذرعه القيء وهو صائم فليس عليه قضاء ومن استقاء فليقض. قال الشيخ شعيب الأرناؤوط: إسناده صحيح. (أخرجه ابن حبان في صحيحه: ۸/۲۸٤/۳۵۱۸).

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

قوله وإن ذرعه القيء، أي غلبه وسبقه، والمسألة تتفرع إلى أربع وعشرين صورة، لأنه إما أن يقئ أو يستقئ وفي كل إما أن يملأ الفم أو دونه، وكل من الأربعة إما أن خرج أو عاد أو أعاده، وكل إما ذاكر لصومه أو لا، ولا فطر في الكل علي الأصح إلا في الإعادة والاستقاء بشرط الملء مع التذكر، شرح الملتقى. قوله وإن أعاده أي أعاد ما قاء الذي هو ملء الفم، قوله أو قدر الحمصة منه فأكثر أشار أنه لا فرق بين إعادة كله أو بعضه إذا كان أصله ملء الفم. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/ ٤۱٤، سعيد).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states: If a fasting person vomits, his fast is broken in two cases only. The first is that a mouthful of vomit came up of its own accord, without his intending it, and then he deliberately swallowed it back from inside his mouth while remembering that he was fasting — whether he swallowed all of it or swallowed a part of it, provided that what he swallowed was the amount of a chick-pea or more. In this case the fast will be broken by the agreement of all. The second is that he deliberately, of his own choice, vomited a mouthful while remembering that he was fasting; in that case his fast will be broken absolutely, in every situation, by the agreement of all. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 3/299).

Kitāb al-Fatāwā states:

Question: If a person vomits while fasting, or a mouthful of water comes up, will the fast be broken by that?

Answer: If a mouthful of vomit comes up of its own accord, without the person intending it, or water comes up — which is itself a form of vomiting — the fast is not broken by it. Yes, if a person deliberately swallows the vomit, or deliberately vomits and it is a mouthful, then in that case the fast will be broken. (Kitāb al-Fatāwā: 3/389).
