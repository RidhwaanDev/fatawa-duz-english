### TITLE
Zakat on house rent that has gone unpaid for several years

### QUESTION
The rent on a house that has been let out has not been received for several years and is still outstanding as a debt. Is the owner of the house liable for zakat on it? And once it is received, is zakat due for the past years as well?

### ANSWER
In the case described, there is no zakat on the rent money before it is actually received. Once it has been received and a year passes over it, zakat is due for that year only; it is not due for the years gone by.

Consider what al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

ولو آجر عبد ه أو داره بنصاب إن لم يكونا للتجارة لا تجب ما لم يحل الحول بعد القبض. (البحر الرائق: ۲/۲۰۸، كوئٹه).

Shāmī states:

لكن قال في البدائع أن رواية ابن سماعة أنه لا زكاة فيه حتى يقبض المائتين ويحول الحول من وقت القبض هي الأصح من الروايتين عن أبي حنيفة رحمه الله، ومثله في غاية البيان. (شامي: ۲/۳۰٦، باب زكاة المال، سعيد).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

Even so, if one looks at the definitions of a strong debt and a middling debt, it becomes plain that in these passages of the jurists what is meant by "wages" is the wages of a slave. For in both these categories of debt it is fundamental that the debt be the counter-value of property, and it is clear that in the Hanafi school it is only the service of a slave that has the standing of property. On this basis the wages of a free man count as a weak debt: employees own such money but do not have physical possession ("yad") of it. So zakat for the days gone by should not be obligatory on that money. Among the scholars of India, Mufti Muhammad Shafiʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) and Mufti Jamīl Aḥmad (may Allah have mercy on him) also preferred this view — that zakat for past days is not due on such money. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/216, Nuʿaymiyyah)

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states:

These statements of the jurists show that complete ownership requires both ownership and possession with the power of disposal. Complete ownership is also called, in other words, absolute ownership and full ownership. Thus Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ explains absolute ownership in exactly the terms just given for complete ownership: that the owner has both ownership of the thing and possession and disposal of it. The passage of Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ is this:

"منها الملك المطلق وهو أن يكون مملوكاً له رقبة ويداً". بدائع: ۲/۹، شامي: ۲/٤

(Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 7/535, Idārat al-Qurʾān wa al-ʿUlūm al-Islāmiyyah).

Elsewhere it states:

For zakat to be obligatory, the owner's ownership of the wealth must be complete. If the owner has complete ownership of the wealth, then zakat on it is obligatory upon him; otherwise not. Fatāwā ʿĀlamgīrī: 1/172; Shāmī: 1/5. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 7/536, Idārat al-Qurʾān wa al-ʿUlūm al-Islāmiyyah).

See also: Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/261.
