### TITLE
The form of offer and acceptance when buying and selling over the internet

### QUESTION
A man is sitting in South Africa and makes an offer and acceptance over the internet with a merchant in China. Will a sale be concluded by this offer and acceptance or not? Is it necessary for the session of the offer and acceptance of a sale to be one and the same or not? And if a marriage contract were transacted in this way, what is the ruling? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, buying and selling by means of the internet will have the ruling of a sale by writing, in this manner: he makes the offer over the internet in South Africa, and the person sitting in China reads the text over the internet and accepts. So when the text has arrived and he has opened the internet and read it, and at that moment accepted, the sale has been concluded — just as the jurists have written that in a sale by writing, the session in which the writing arrives is what counts. Consider what ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Fatāwā Shāmī:

ولا يتوقف شطر العقد فيه أي البيع على قبول غائب فلو قال بعت فلاناً الغائب فبلغه فقبل لم ينعقد اتفاقاً، إلا إذا كان بكتابة أو رسالة فيعتبر مجلس بلوغها. قال في رد المحتار: قوله إلا إذا كان بكتابة أو رسالة، صورة الكتابة أن يكتب أما بعد فقد بعت عبدي فلاناً منك بكذا فلما بلغه الكتاب قال في مجلسه ذلك اشتريت تم البيع بينهما، وصورة الإرسال أن يرسل رسولاً فيقول البائع بعت هذا من فلان الغائب بألف درهم فاذهب يا فلان وقل له فذهب الرسول فأخبره بما قال، فقبل المشتري في مجلسه ذلك

قلت: ويكون بالكتابة من الجانبين فإذا كتب اشتريت عبدك فلاناً بكذا فكتب إليه البائع قد بعت فهذا بيع كما في التتارخانية.

قوله فيعتبر مجلس بلوغها أي بلوغ الرسالة أو الكتابة قال في الهداية: والكتابة كالخطاب وكذا الإرسال حتى اعتبر مجلس بلوغ الكتابة وأداء الرسالة

(فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۵۱۲، سعيد).

It is mentioned in Naʾe Masāʾil awr ʿUlamāʾ-i Hind ke Fayṣle:

Over the internet too, if both contracting parties are present at the same time and acceptance is expressed immediately by the other party after the offer, the sale will be concluded, and in these forms the contracting parties will be considered to be in one and the same session.

If a person made an offer of sale over the internet and the other person was not present on the internet at that time, and afterwards he received the message of the one making the offer, this form will be a sale by means of writing and correspondence, and it is necessary that acceptance be expressed on his part at the very moment when the second person reads that offer.

But as far as marriage is concerned, for a marriage contract to be concluded it is necessary that the session of offer and acceptance be one and the same; and since over the internet the session has changed, the marriage will not be concluded. Yes, the correct form is that the girl should appoint someone as her agent over the internet, and then the agent should make the offer and acceptance on behalf of his principal in the marriage session.

Consider what is recorded in Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ:

وأما الذي يرجع إلى مكان العقد فهو اتحاد المجلس إذا كان العاقدان حاضرين وهو أن يكون الإيجاب والقبول في مجلس واحد حتى لو اختلف المجلس لا ينعقد النكاح. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/۳۲٦، سعيد).

وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ٦/٤۳۱، وخلاصة الفتاوى: ۲/۱۵، والبحر الرائق: ٦/۳۵۱.

It is mentioned in Kitāb al-Fatāwā:

In marriage it is necessary that the offer and acceptance be in one and the same session. In the forms mentioned in the question it is evident that the session of the one speaking, or of the one presenting his statement in writing, is separate, and the session of the one addressed is separate; therefore the offer and acceptance of marriage through these means of communication is not correct. However, a person may be appointed as an agent for the offer and acceptance, and he may conclude the marriage of his principal. (Kitāb al-Fatāwā: 4/306).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
