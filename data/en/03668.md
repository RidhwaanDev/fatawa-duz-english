### TITLE
Taking up the profession of ghost writing

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to take up the profession of ghost writing or not? Also, will the wealth earned from ghost writing be lawful or not? Ghost writing means that a man, by his craft, skill and experience, writes books of stories and the like, and then, under a contract, some other company publishes them under its own name and pays him his fee for it. The purpose of this is that the actual author should not receive the fame for it.

### ANSWER
Here there are two separate issues. (1) Writing such stories or books as contain fine moral qualities, instructive incidents and articles relating to the reform of society is permissible and correct. Yes, if there are matters that corrupt morals, or obscene and filthy topics, or things that spread indecency, then writing and publishing them is not permissible.

(2) As for taking a salary and a fee, the ruling of the Sacred Law is that the fee for those writings which it is correct to write is also permissible, and the fee for those writings which it is not permissible to write is likewise impermissible. The upshot will be that the ruling on ghost writing and on its fee will be built upon the subject matter — that is, if the subject matter is sound, the ruling of permissibility will apply; otherwise the ruling of impermissibility will apply. But if it is such subject matter as has no religious or worldly benefit, then it will fall under what is pointless and it will be better to avoid it. See the noble hadith, which states:

عن علي بن حسين قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: إن من حسن إسلام المرء تركه ما لا يعنيه. (سنن الترمذي، رقم: ۲۳۱۸).
أخرج أبو يعلى في مسنده: عن أبي سعيد الخدري رضي الله عنه، أن رسول الله ﷺ قال: حدثوا عني ولا حرج حدثوا عني ولا تكذبوا علي ومن كذب علي متعمداً فقد تبوأ مقعده من النار وحدثوا عن بني إسرائيل ولا حرج. (مسند أبي يعلي، رقم: ۱۲۰۹).
وفي تعليقاته لحسين سليم أسد: إسناده صحيح.
قال في الدر المختار: وحديث حدثوا عن بني إسرائيل يفيد حل سماع الأعاجيب والغرائب من كل ما لا يتيقن كذبه بقصد الفرجة لا الحجة بل وما يتيقن كذبه لكن بقصد ضرب الأمثال والمواعظ وتعليم نحو الشجاعة على ألسنة آدميين أو حيوانات ذكره ابن حجر. وفي الشامية: وفي لفظ لا حمد بن منيع عن جابر رضي الله عنه: حدثوا عن بني إسرائيل فإنه كان فيهم أعاجيب

فرق عليه الصلاة والسلام بين الحديث عنه والحديث عنهم. قوله بقصد الفرجة لا الحجة، الفرجة مثلثة التفصي عن الهمّ، قوله: لكن بقصد ضرب الأمثال، وذلك مقامات الحريري فإن الظاهر أن الحكايات التي فيها عن الحارث بن همام والسروجي لا أصل لها وإنما أتي بها علي هذا السياق العجيب لما لا يخفى على من يطالعها وهل يدخل في ذلك مثل قصة عنترة والملك الظاهر وغيرهما. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٦/٤۰۵، سعيد).

Maʿārif al-Qurʾān states:

In this age most young people are in the habit of looking at obscene novels, or tales made up of the doings of criminals, or obscene verses. All these things fall under this same category of unlawful diversion. Likewise, the study of the ideas of misguided people of falsehood is also impermissible for the common people, because it is a cause of misguidance for them; if scholars firmly grounded in knowledge look into them in order to reply to them, there is no harm. (Maʿārif al-Qurʾān: 7/23).

In Ḥalāl o Ḥarām, Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh Ṣāḥib writes:

If these fictional stories are instructive and full of good counsel, if they carry a sound purpose and are constructive, then they are not merely permissible but excellent. But if their purpose is to inflame base desires and to create moral anarchy, then it is obvious that they are a grave sin and impermissible. (Ḥalāl o Ḥarām, p. 234).

The fee for an impermissible act is likewise impermissible; accordingly, to hire out obscene novels and story books, and to take a fee for them, is not permissible. See: Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām records:

Some people keep various kinds of obscene novels and story books in their shop and earn an income by giving them out on rent. Firstly, literature containing obscene pictures, and likewise novels and tales made up of the doings of criminals, or obscene verses and so forth, and likewise the study of the ideas of the people of falsehood, are impermissible for the common people because they are a cause of misguidance; to supply books for this is a sinful act, since it is assistance in an impermissible work. (1/229).

In another place it is recorded:

Where the object of the contract is itself an act of disobedience — such as dancing, singing and playing music, fornication, theft, forgery, tale-bearing, making taʿziyahs, making idols, making pictures, distilling wine, and the promotion of polytheism, disbelief and unlawful works, and of innovations and sinfulness and licentiousness — this hire contract is by agreement impermissible and unlawful. Whatever fee or profit is obtained from carrying out these sinful works is filthy property, and it is obligatory to give it in charity without the intention of reward. (Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām, volume one, p. 204, and p. 110).

Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal records: The fee for an unlawful work is also unlawful. (6/40).
