### TITLE
The narration about the reward for going out in the path of Allah

### QUESTION
It is said of those who go out with the Tablīghī Jamāʿat that the reward for a single prayer of theirs is 49 crore (490 million). Please tell me whether there is any evidence for this. And if there is, does it mean that the prayer of someone out on tablīgh carries more reward than a prayer in the Sacred Mosque, since one prayer in the Sacred Mosque earns the reward of a hundred thousand?

### ANSWER
What these brothers say is not something peculiar to them; it applies generally. Whoever goes out in the path of Allah — for jihad, to seek knowledge, for Hajj and Umrah, or for any other religious purpose — deserves this reward. Nor is the merit taken from a single narration: the figure is arrived at by putting two hadiths together.

The first hadith:

"عن جماعة من الصحابة مرفوعاً: من أرسل بنفقة في سبيل الله ومن غزي بنفسه في سبيل الله وأنفق في وجه ذلك فله بكل درهم سبع مئة ألف درهم، ثم تلا هذه الآية والله يضاعف لمن يشاء". (رواه ابن ماجه باب فضل النفقة في سبيل الله، ص ۱۹۸).

قلت: إسناده ضعيف؛ فيه: الخليل بن عبد الله وهو مجهول.

وقال بشار عواد: إسناده ضعيف لأجل الخليل بن عبد الله الخ. (سنن ابن ماجه بتعليق بشار عواد: ٤/۵٦).

وبالجملة فالحديث ضعيف لكن يعمل به في فضائل الأعمال.

That is: whoever fights in Allah's path with his own person and spends even a single dirham in Allah's path will be given the reward of seven hundred thousand dirhams for every dirham. It follows that spending one dirham in the path of Allah is equal to spending seven hundred thousand dirhams.

A word of caution: there is a difficulty here. The hadith uses the word "غزي", which is used in the particular sense of attacking the enemy on the battlefield. Extending it to other meanings — calling people to Allah and tablīgh, seeking knowledge, and the like — does not appear to be appropriate. This objection was raised by Mawlānā Muftī Rashīd Aḥmad Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him).

Muftī Rashīd Aḥmad (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

Even if these two hadiths were accepted as fit to argue from, one of them states explicitly "من غزي بنفسه". There is therefore no room here to take "in the path of Allah" in a general sense and to make this merit cover the other branches of religious work as well. Rather, the merit belongs only to the man who actually fights — that is, to those in battle against the disbelievers. (احسن الفتاوی: ۹/۱۸۳).

Consider a few statements of the lexicographers. Al-Qāmūs al-Muḥīṭ states:

غزاه وغزواً أراده وطلبه وقصده والعدو: سار إلى قتالهم وانتهابهم، غزواً وغزواناً وغزاوة. (القاموس المحيط: ۱/۱٦۹۸، وكذا في المعجم الوسيط، ص: ٦۵۲).

وفي المحيط الأعظم (٦/۳۸): الغزو: السير إلى قتال العدو وانتهابه.

The hadith in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, however, shows that the word is sometimes used for something other than actual fighting:

"من جهز غازياً في سبيل الله فقد غزي ومن خلف غازياً في سبيل الله فقد غزي". (۱/۵۹۸)

The second hadith:

عن سهل بن معاذ عن أبيه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: إن الصلاة والصيام والذكر يضاعف على النفقة في سبيل الله ل بسبع مئة ضعف".

رواه أبو داود: (۱/۳۳۸، باب في تضعيف الذكر في سبيل الله عز وجل، ط: سعيد)، والحاكم (رقم: ۲٤۱۵)، وقال: صحيح الإسناد، ووافقه الذهبي، وأخرجه البيهقي في "الكبرى" (۹/۱۷۲).

قلت: إسناده ضعيف؛ فيه: زبان بن فائد المصري، قال أحمد: أحاديثه مناكير. وقال ابن معين: شيخ ضعيف. وقال ابن حبان: منكر الحديث جداً يتفرد عن سهل بن معاذ بنسخة كأنها موضوعة لا يحتج به. وقال الساجي: عنده مناكير. (تهذيب التهذيب: ۳/۲۷٤/۲۰٦۷).

(۲) وسهل بن معاذ بن أنس الجهني، قال ابن معين: ضعيف. قلت (ابن حجر): لا يعتبر حديثه ما كان من رواية زبان بن فائد عنه وذكره في الضعفاء فقال: منكر الحديث جداً. (تهذيب التهذيب: ٤/۲۳٤/۲۷٦۰، وتحرير التقريب، ج ۲).

وقال المناوي: وهو حديث ضعيف لضعف زبان بن فائد. (التيسير بشرح الجامع الصغير للمناوي: ۱/۵۹۲/۲٤۹۸).

وبالجملة فالحديث ضعيف لكن يعمل به في فضائل الأعمال.

That is: the reward of prayer, fasting and remembrance of Allah while out in His path is seven hundred times greater than the reward of spending in His path. Multiply that seven hundred thousand by seven hundred and you get 490 million, that is, 49 crore. This is the truth behind the report about 49 crore.

Furthermore, no comparison is intended between this reward of 49 crore and the reward of the Sacred Mosque. It is like saying that a certain person is the best of men: that does not require him to have become better and more excellent than the Companions, because no comparison between the two was meant. In the same way, no comparison is intended here between the reward mentioned and the reward of the Sacred Mosque.

Even so, if a comparison is drawn, then according to one narration the reward of the Sacred Mosque comes to far more than a hundred thousand. Mullā ʿAlī al-Qārī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote the following in his commentary on the Mishkāt, under one of the hadiths:

"قال رسول الله ﷺ: صلاة الرجل في بيته بصلاة أي تحسب بصلاة واحد وصلاته في مسجد القبائل بخمس وعشرين صلاة أي بالإضافة إلى صلاة في بيته لا مطلقاً وصلاته في المسجد الذي يجمع فيه بخمس مئة صلاة أي بالنسبة إلى مسجد الحي وصلاته في المسجد الأقصى بخمسين ألف صلاة أي بالنسبة إلى ما قبله وصلاته في مسجدي بخمسين ألف صلاة أي بالإضافة إلى ما يليه وصلاته في المسجد الحرام بمائة ألف أي بالنسبة إلى مسجد المدينة على ما يدل عليه سياق الكلام فيحتاج إلى ضرب الأعداد في بعض فإنه ينتج مضاعفة كثيرة". (مرقاة المفاتيح شرح مشكاة المصابيح: ۲/۲۲۸).

The Prophet ﷺ said that a man's prayer in his own house counts as one prayer — that is, it is reckoned as a single prayer; his prayer in the local mosque counts as 25 prayers, that is, compared with the prayer at home, not absolutely; his prayer in the congregational mosque counts as 500 prayers, again in relation to what came before it; his prayer in my mosque counts as 50,000 prayers in relation to what came before it; and his prayer in the Sacred Mosque counts as a hundred thousand prayers, that is, compared with the Prophet's Mosque. The figures must therefore be multiplied one by the other, and the result comes out very much larger.

The multiplication works out as follows:

At home: 1 (one).

The local mosque: 25.

The congregational mosque: 25 × 500 = 12,500.

Masjid al-Aqṣā: 12,500 × 50,000 = 625,000,000.

The Prophet's Mosque: 625,000,000 × 50,000 = 31,250,000,000,000.

The Sacred Mosque: 31,250,000,000,000 × 100,000 = 3,125,000,000,000,000,000.

مسجدِ حرام: ۰۰۰، ۰۰۰، ۰۰۰، ۳۱۲۵۰×۰۰۰، ۱۰۰=۰۰، ۰۰۰، ۰۰۰، ۰۰۰، ۰۰۰، ۳۱۲۵۰

So this hadith shows that if a person prays in his own house he receives the reward of one prayer; if he prays in his local mosque he receives twenty-five times that; if he prays in the congregational mosque he receives (25 × 500 = 12,500); if he prays in Masjid al-Aqṣā he receives (12,500 × 50,000 = 625,000,000); if he prays in the Prophet's Mosque he receives (625,000,000 × 50,000 = 31,250,000,000,000); and if he prays in the Sacred Mosque he receives (31,250,000,000,000 × 100,000 = 3,125,000,000,000,000,000).

In other words, the reward comes to 31 sankh and 25 qadam. Remember that a hundred thousand make one lakh, a hundred lakh make one crore, a hundred crore make one arab, a hundred arab make one kharab, a hundred kharab make one padam, a hundred padam make one qadam, and a hundred qadam make one sankh. Now judge for yourself how immense the reward of a single prayer in the Sacred Mosque is — an amount our minds cannot grasp. May Allah grant us all this reward. آمین.
