### TITLE
Using exaggerated titles for one's spiritual guide

### QUESTION
Some people use exaggerated titles for their spiritual guide. A few of them are given below:

"My shaykh, you are the self-disclosures of the Truth, glory be to Him; you are absolute light from head to foot. O absolute light, you are only not a prophet, and not a Companion in the technical sense; everything else you are. Before the pomp and splendour of the circling of your exalted soul, the souls of all the saints are utterly bowed low, without any proclamation from the pulpit of Baghdad. I am not saying this out of emotion; I am compelled from Allah's side. From after the ʿishāʾ prayer until the call to the fajr prayer, in a state of self-oblivion, Persian verses keep coming spontaneously upon my heart. You are, from head to foot, the self-disclosures of the Truth, the light of the Truth, absolute light, the light of the Master of the Messengers ﷺ. Ḥaḍrat Jī, in the eyes of this lowly one there is no one but you. By Allah, you are without parallel. At no moment of the day or night can this slave utter even a single word against the will of the Truth." (There are further expressions in the question; they have been left out because of their length.)

Kindly write a detailed and well-argued answer about these expressions and about the man who says them.

### ANSWER
In the situation asked about, the first point is that the man who says this will not be called a wrongdoer, nor one who has overstepped the limits of the sharia, because he himself admits that he is compelled from Allah's side and is in a state of self-oblivion. Words spoken in such a state are excused, and there will be no accountability for them. A hadith states:

لله أشد فرحاً بتوبة عبده حين يتوب إليه من أحدكم كان على راحلته بأرض فلاة فانفلتت منه وعليها طعامه وشرابه فأيس منها فأتي شجرة فاضطجع في ظلها قد أيس من راحلته فبينا هو كذلك إذ هو بها قائمة عنده فأخذ بخطامها ثم قال من شدة الفرح: اللهم أنت عبدي وأنا ربك أخطأ من شدة الفرح. (صحيح مسلم: ۲/۳۵۵).

The hadith above establishes explicitly that intense joy too can strip a person of his volition, and that in such a state the utterance of words of unbelief does no harm and does not bring about apostasy.

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah records that if someone is afflicted with pleurisy, or is fed something, and loses his reason and involuntarily utters words of unbelief, this does not amount to unbelief. And the words quoted above may at most be called an overstepping of the limits; they are not words of unbelief.

قال: من أصابه برسام أو أطعم شيئاً فذهب عقله فهذي فارتد لم يكن ذلك ارتداداً وكذا لوكان معتوهاً أو موسوساً أو مغلوباً على عقله بوجه من الوجوه فهو على هذا كذا في السراج الوهاج. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۲/۲۵۳)،

In this passage the words "أو مغلوباً على عقله بوجه من الوجوه" deserve attention: besides the well-known causes — intoxication, insanity, coercion and mental deficiency — advanced age, illness, calamity, overwhelming joy, overwhelming grief, an excess of love and the like can also become causes of the loss of reason or of the loss of volition, and on account of them the utterance of words of unbelief will not be held to bring about apostasy or the loss of faith.

An example of intense joy has been given in the hadith above. Now consider an example of overwhelming grief in the following incident.

On the occasion of the Treaty of Ḥudaybiyah, when the peace agreement had been completed, the Prophet ﷺ ordered the noble Companions to slaughter their sacrificial animals and to have their heads shaved. In their overwhelming grief the noble Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) did not carry out his noble command, even though he ﷺ repeated it again and again.

قال رسول الله ﷺ: لا صحابه قوموا فانحروا ثم احلقوا قال: فو الله ما قام منهم رجل حتى قال ذلك ثلاث مرات. (صحيح البخاري: ۱/۳۸۰، رقم: ۲۷۳۲، باب الشروط في الجهاد).

The fear of Allah too can become a cause of the loss of reason. A report in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī says that a man [who had never in his life done a single good deed] said: When I die, burn me and scatter half of the ashes on land and half in the sea.

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه أن رسول الله ﷺ قال: قال رجل لم يعمل خيراً قط: فإذا مات فحرقوه واذروا نصفه في البر ونصفه في البحر فو الله لئن قدر الله عليه ليعذبنه عذاباً لا يعذبه أحداً من العالمين فأمر الله البحر ليجمع ما فيه وأمر البر فجمع ما فيه ثم قال: لم فعلت قال: من خشيتك وأنت أعلم فغفر له. (صحيح البخاري: ۲/۱۱۱۷/۷۵۰٦).

In the hadith above, the words of that man, "لئن قدر الله عليه" (if qadara is taken as derived from qudrah, power), are without doubt among the words of unbelief; yet the answer he gave in the presence of Allah, the Lord of Might — "من خشيتك", "the dread and fear of You forced me to it" — saved him from unbelief and made him deserving of Allah's forgiveness.

Consider an example of being overpowered by an excess of love. After observing perfect justice among his pure wives, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

"اللهم! هذه قسمتي فيما أملك فلا تلمني فيما تملك ولا أملك". (سنن الترمذي: ۱/۲۱٦/۱۱٤۰).

The meaning is: O Allah, do not call me to account for what lies outside my control — that is, the love of the heart.

Yes, the forbidden kind of extravagant praise that springs from an excess of love is forbidden so far as it is voluntary; if it reaches the point of being involuntary, then the pen is certainly lifted from it. In the hadith "لا تطروني" there is indeed a prohibition of extravagant praise, but it is not necessary that the cause of such praise should always be an excess of love; ignorance, partisanship, misunderstanding and similar causes can also give rise to it. So a prohibition of extravagant praise cannot entail a prohibition of overwhelming love. In the same way, overwhelming love is not confined to involuntary extravagant praise. For this reason the prohibition of extravagant praise cannot be held to entail a prohibition of overwhelming love.

The hadith just mentioned plainly shows that overwhelming love sometimes creates an involuntary inclination towards the beloved, and that this inclination is not open to censure. Why? Because it is involuntary. And what made it involuntary? Overwhelming love. (Derived from Kifāyat al-Muftī: 1/65, published by Dār al-Nawādir).

The second point is whether these words are fit to be published or not. In this humble servant's view they should not be published. Sometimes certain words, even while they fall within the limits of the sharia, are not fit for publication because of the fear of discord. Imam al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) set up a chapter:

"باب من خص بالعلم قوماً دون قوم كراهية أن لا يفهموا"

and under it he cited a report from Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him).

وقال على رضي الله عنه: حدثوا الناس بما يعرفون أتحبون أن يكذب الله ورسوله. (صحيح البخاري: ۱/۲٤). وفي عمدة القاري: أي كلموا الناس بما يعرفون أي بما يفهمون والمراد كلموهم على قدر عقولهم وفي كتاب العلم لا دم بن أبي إياس عن عبد الله بن داود عن معروف في آخره ودعوا ما ينكرون أي ما يشتبه عليهم فهمه.
"أن يكذب" وذلك لأن الشخص إذا سمع ما لا يفهمه وما لا يتصور إمكانه يعتقد استحالته جهلاً فلا يصدق وجوده فإذا أسند إلى الله ورسوله يلزم تكذيبهما. (عمدة القاري: ۱/۲۸۹، ط: دار الحديث).

The upshot of the passage above is that one should not say every kind of thing in front of all and sundry. Many a time a sound statement becomes a cause of discord because it is understood wrongly. It follows that not every kind of statement should be published either; otherwise it will prove a cause of discord for someone of little understanding or of crooked understanding.

A report from Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) shows that not every matter is fit to be made public. The substance of the report is this: once the Prophet ﷺ left from among the noble Companions, and when a long time had passed the Companions grew anxious and got up to look for him. Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) says: I came to a garden where the Noble Prophet ﷺ was sitting. He said: "Abū Hurayrah, what has brought you here?" I told him what had happened. The Prophet ﷺ gave me his blessed sandals and said: "Beyond this garden, whoever you meet who bears witness sincerely that Allah is the true object of worship, give him the good news of Paradise." Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) met Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) and gave him the good news. When Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) heard it he pushed Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) back and complained to the Prophet ﷺ: "O Messenger of Allah, people will rely on this and sit back and do no deeds. They should be left as they are, so that they stay occupied with deeds." The Prophet ﷺ said: "Very well, leave them."

قال: يا رسول الله! بأبي أنت وأمي أبعثتَ أبا هريرة بنعليك من لقي يشهد أن لا إله إلا الله مستيقناً بها قلبه بشره بالجنة قال: نعم، قال: فلا تفعل فإني أخشى أن يتكل الناس عليها فخلهم يعملون، قال رسول الله ﷺ: فخلهم. (صحيح مسلم: ۱/٤۵).
قال الإمام النووي رحمه الله: وفيه جواز إمساك بعض العلوم التي لا حاجة إليها للمصلحة أو خوف المفسدة. (شرح النووي: ۱/٤٦).

The words of Imam al-Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him) show that if the spreading of certain non-essential branches of knowledge is held back for some benefit or out of the fear of discord, that is quite proper.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
