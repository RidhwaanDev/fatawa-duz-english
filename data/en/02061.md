### TITLE
Compulsory insurance

### QUESTION
It is the practice in companies nowadays that they deduct a fixed sum from the employees' salaries and deposit it into a life-insurance fund. The company deducts this sum of its own accord before the employee takes possession of his salary; the employee has no choice in the matter and is bound by the rules and regulations laid down by the company. If the employee dies, a very large sum is given to his heirs, which is far greater than the original amount. Is this sum permissible for the heirs or not? Also, what is the ruling on compulsory insurance of property and the like?

### ANSWER
In the present age, when obtaining employment without insurance — and, once one is employed, retaining that employment — has become all but impossible; and given also the financial losses that Muslims suffer through the disturbances that occur day after day; and given that under government law life insurance has been made compulsory in employment, without which either one is not entitled to the post at all or, if one is, one does not keep it for long: for these reasons insurance of this kind will be permitted in a situation of compulsion, and some scholars have stated this explicitly in their fatwas. One should, however, continue to regard it as evil in one's heart and to repent and seek forgiveness.

Further, in the form of insurance in which a person has himself deposited the money, it is not correct to put to one's own use whatever additional sum is received upon it; rather it should be given in charity to the poor without the intention of reward. But in the form of insurance in which the company deducts a portion of the salary without the employee having any choice and then pays an additional sum upon it, this is not interest, and the use of that money is permissible and valid.

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Insurance contains interest, and gambling as well; and since both of these are forbidden, insurance too is forbidden. But if a person is in such a place and such an environment that the protection of life and property is simply not possible without taking out insurance, or there is legal compulsion, then taking out insurance is valid. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 16/387, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

In another place it states:

Since it is hard to make a living without a business or a factory or a shop, and this legal requirement is imposed upon it, a man will be held excused, out of compulsion, in the matter of this life insurance. Nevertheless, whatever sum exceeds the amount he paid in he should give in charity to the poor and not put to his own use. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 16/390, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

All those forms of insurance in which insurance is compulsory under government law will be permissible — such as insurance for imports and exports in international trade — since in them the choice of the person insuring plays no part. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/124).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

To have a portion of one's salary deducted in this way and then to receive it in a lump sum is altogether permissible, even though some money is received along with it under the name of interest, because in reality it is not interest. That is because the portion of the salary that was not received never entered the employee's ownership; so that extra sum was not given in return for the use of something he owned, but is rather an initial gratuity, whatever the government may call it in its own terminology. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 3/149).

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states:

Although the government pays that additional sum under the name of interest, the definition of interest would apply only if we had ourselves deposited the money and then received a further sum upon it. Here, however, the government has itself deducted the money and deposited it without our choice, and it has not even come into our possession and ownership; and then it makes an addition to it. So whatever addition is made to that sum will not be an addition to something we own, but will be an initial gratuity and gift. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 4/485).

Niẓām al-Fatāwā states:

The money that the government compulsorily deducts in an official capacity under the name of insurance, and whatever it adds to it when paying it out, is not impermissible either; one may take it and spend it on one's own needs. (Niẓām al-Fatāwā: 2/236).

For more, see: (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 4/237; Fatāwā ʿUthmānī: 3/314; Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/435; and Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 7/25).

Consider some general evidences for the permissibility of compulsory insurance.

One of the very great principles of the sharia is ease and the removal of hardship:

قال الله تعالى: ﴿يريد الله بكم اليسر ولا يريد بكم العسر﴾ (سورة البقرة، الآية: ۱۸۵).
وقال تعالى: ﴿وما جعل عليكم في الدين من حرج﴾ (سورة الحج، الآية: ۷۸).

Among the accepted maxims of fiqh are the following:

"المشقة تجلب التيسير". (الأشباه والنظائر: ۱/ ۲۲٦).
"الضرورات تبيح المحظورات". (شرح المجلة، وقواعد الفقه، والأشباه،: ۱/ ۲۵۱).
"الحاجة تنزل منزلة الضرورة عامة أو خاصة". (الأشباه والنظائر: ۱/ ۲٦۷).
"وما حرم لذاته يباح للضرورة". (تفسير المنار لمحمد رشيد بن على رضا، ٦/ ۲۲۳).
"وما حرم سداً لذريعة يباح للحاجة". (تفسير المنار لمحمد رشيد بن على رضا، ٦/ ۲۲۳).
"إذا ضاق الأمر اتسع". (قواعد الفقه، ص ٦۲).
