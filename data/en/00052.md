### TITLE
Why different groups exist within the Muslim community

### QUESTION
These days there are many different groups — for example the Deobandis, the Barelwis, the people of the Tablighi Jamaat, and the people of the khānqāhs. Why is there no unity in the ummah today? How good it would be if the whole ummah came together on one platform and consulted about how to become one. Please explain how the ummah can become one.

### ANSWER
In reality this difference within the ummah is not a hardship but a mercy. On this subject, consider the noble words of ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz (may Allah have mercy on him), who is called "the second ʿUmar" and whose caliphate is reckoned equal to the Rightly Guided Caliphate:

"ما سرني لو أن أصحاب محمد لم يختلفوا لأنهم لو لم يختلفوا لم تكن رخصة"

That is: it would not have pleased me had the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ not differed with one another, because if they had not differed, no concession or latitude would have remained.

Fatāwā Shāmī also states that the disagreement of the jurists is a mercy, and the greater the disagreement, the greater the mercy.

When has there ever been a time when the scholars did not differ? From the beginning of Islam until today — indeed, from the very beginning of the world — difference has existed. Did the Lord of Glory Himself send down one and the same law to all the prophets (peace be upon them)? Certainly not. In the fundamentals of religion there was always unity; in the secondary matters there was always difference. Dāwūd (peace be upon him) and Sulaymān (peace be upon him) themselves differed over several judgements, yet despite that difference Allah praised them both. Allah says:

﴿ففهمناها سليمان وكلاً آتينا حكماً وعلماً﴾ (سورة الأنبياء).

Did Abū Bakr and ʿUmar not differ over the prisoners of Badr? Was there not a difference over fighting those who withheld zakat? Was there not a difference over sending out the army of Usāmah (may Allah be pleased with him)? Was there not a difference over the compilation of the Qur'an?

In short, the noble Companions (may Allah be pleased with them all) differed among themselves on a great many questions, and in the same way the imams and mujtahids who came after them differed on a great many questions. Whatever different groups of the people of truth exist in our own time — that is, those whose beliefs are sound — every one of them is a source of mercy for us, and all of them are a means of reaching Allah. How much we ought to thank Allah for this! If anyone imagines that the existence of these different groups is a form of discord, that is a serious misunderstanding. In reality they are all fruits of a single tree, namely the religion of Islam. So a person should adopt whichever group and way among the people of truth his heart is drawn to, or he should consult his trustworthy scholars and act on the advice they give. And if someone has the energy to take part in the Tablighi Jamaat, and to visit the khānqāh as well, and to support the madrasas as well, and to take part in jihad in the path of Allah as well, then that is light upon light. This can be understood by a simple example.

There are various ways of travelling from here to Durban. A man with his own car may go in it; he may travel by bus; and if he has greater means he may even go by aeroplane. Clearly the means differ, but the destination is one and the same. Understand the different groups in just this way: they are in fact different means, but our destination and our aim are one, namely the pleasure of Allah and the spread of the true religion.

Ḥaḍrat Shaykh al-Ḥadīth Mawlānā Muḥammad Zakariyyā (may Allah fill his grave with light) writes:

What is essential, however, is that the aim should be obedience to Allah and the declaring of the truth, and not unwarranted partisanship for one's own group, which is what is called bigotry and prejudice. There is no harm in difference; if it stays within proper principles it is even praiseworthy. What is blameworthy is to turn that difference into a quarrel and to make it a means of scattering and splitting the Muslims — and there is a plain distinction between the two. We are turning this difference, which was something good, into a disaster for ourselves and a cause of our own ruin. Ḥasan al-Baṣrī (may Allah have mercy on him), an eminent Successor, one of the famous jurists and one of the great Sufis, once in the heat of investigation let fall some words on the question of divine decree that went against the majority of the scholars. A great outcry arose and much was made of it; and after that, false statements began to be attributed to him. Ayyūb says that two kinds of people told lies about Ḥasan al-Baṣrī (may Allah have mercy on him). The first were those who belonged to the Qadariyyah sect: they wanted to give currency to their own opinion, so they made out that Ḥasan al-Baṣrī (may Allah have mercy on him) held their view. The second were those who bore him a personal grudge and circulated these sayings of his. Exactly the same thing happens in our own age: those who want to give currency to their own opinion attribute it to the senior figure of a group, and those who are opposed to them repeat these false sayings, so that the gulf of quarrelling and hostility keeps growing wider. Yet the proper station of a follower was this: that when he comes to feel devotion to one of the true scholars, and it is established that the man is a scholar who acts on his knowledge, he should act on his teachings. But among us, for all our claims of love and devotion, action is simply not there. The sum total of all our love is that, out of love for our own elder, we abuse other people's elders. Allah says in His Book:

﴿ولا تسبوا الذين يدعون من دون الله فيسبو الله عدواً بغير علم﴾ (سورة الأنعام).

The Noble Qur'an forbids even the reviling of other people's idols. (الاعتدال في مراتب الرجال، ص: ۲۱۲).

So instead of opposing anyone, we should keep concerning ourselves with correcting and reforming ourselves. There are, however, certain groups with whom our difference is over belief; as long as they do not come round to correct belief, the difference will remain. It is not that we bear such people any hatred or enmity; rather, we are dissatisfied with their beliefs and declare ourselves free of some of their practices.

In short, a person should join whichever group of the people of truth he is fully satisfied about, and if he can take part in all of them, that is better still.
