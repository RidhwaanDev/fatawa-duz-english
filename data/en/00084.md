### TITLE
Saying "peace be upon him" after the name of the Mahdi

### QUESTION
Is it correct to say "peace be upon him" after the name of the Mahdi, or not?

### ANSWER
One should not use "peace be upon him", because it involves resembling the Rāfiḍīs. One should use "may Allah be pleased with him" instead.

Al-Shāmī states:

والظاهر أن العلة منع السلام ما قاله النووي رحمه الله في علة منع الصلاة: أن ذلك شعار أهل البدع. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۷۵۳، سعيد).

ولا فرق بين السلام عليه وعليه السلام إلا أن قوله على عليه السلام من شعار أهل البدعة فلا يستحسن في مقام المرام.

(فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۳/ ٤۲۳ بحوالہ شرح فقہ اکبر ص ۲۰٤).

Ḥaḍrat Shāh ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz al-Muḥaddith al-Dihlawī (may Allah have mercy on him) said that saying "peace be upon him" has become a badge of the Rāfiḍīs, who say it because they hold him to be sinless; so this badge should be avoided. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱/۳۹٦).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muḥammad Yūsuf Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) used "may Allah be pleased with him" for the Mahdi in several places in Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal, and he noted that Imām Rabbānī Mujaddid Alf Thānī (may Allah have mercy on him) also referred to the Mahdi in these very words. (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ۱/۲۷۱).

If anyone has used "peace be upon him" for the Mahdi, it is because the expression was in such common use that no attention was paid to the question of resemblance. But now that saying "peace be upon him" for the Mahdi does amount to resembling the Shīʿah, and a hadith states:

"من تشبه بقوم فهو منهم". (أبو داود: ۲/۵۵۹)

— for this reason "peace be upon him" should not be used. And if the point is simply to pray for him, then why do they not say "peace be upon him" after the names of Abū Bakr and ʿUmar as well?
