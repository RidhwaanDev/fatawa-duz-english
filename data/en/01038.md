### TITLE
Spending zakat money on a madrasah's other needs after the device of transferring ownership

### QUESTION
Most of our madrasah's income is zakat, and both wealthy and needy students study here. The madrasah also needs Braille (Braille, Books) — the script used by blind people — and tapes. On top of this, blind people from other countries want to benefit from our services, and our aim is to serve as many blind people as we can. Please tell us of some permissible device by which we may convert zakat money into lillāh funds (general charity that is not zakat) and spend it on the madrasah's needs.

### ANSWER
The device of converting zakat into lillāh funds is not permissible without severe need. If we keep resorting to devices in zakat, the whole purpose of zakat will be lost. There is, however, one thing you can do to run the madrasah without any scheme or device. Fix a fee for the students, give the poor students zakat money — say 500 — and then collect from them, say, 400 rand for running the madrasah, for food, for tuition and so on. One person may hand it over and another, or the same person, may collect it elsewhere. The money collected in this way may then be spent on tapes or on any other purpose. It may not, however, be given to people outside, because the wealth of the madrasah should be spent on the madrasah itself.
