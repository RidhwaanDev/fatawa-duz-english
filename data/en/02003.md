### TITLE
Exchanging currencies of different kinds

### QUESTION
There are two ways of sending money somewhere. (1) One is that here in South Africa one gives a bank or some person a thousand dollars or eight thousand rand, and in return, after two or three days, a fixed agreed sum of rupees is collected in Karachi or Delhi. In this method the following defect apparently arises: in selling a medium of price for a medium of price, possession ought to be taken by both parties in the session of the contract, which is absent here.

(2) The second form is that fifty thousand rupees are sent from Karachi to Lahore through a bank or the like. That very money does not reach the destination; rather, an equivalent sum is paid there to the addressee through the bank. Some people have a doubt about this, namely that it is precisely the suftajah which the books of fiqh record as forbidden, and which is called a hundi or bill of exchange. What is the ruling of the sharīʿah on both forms?

### ANSWER
(1) In the first form, since rupees and the like are received in exchange for dollars, and the currencies of two countries have the ruling of two different genera, trading them with a difference in amount is permissible. Moreover, since this is a conventional medium of price and not a real one, mutual taking of possession in the session is also not necessary. Yes, possession by one side is necessary, so that a sale of one debt for another is avoided. The ruling that a conventional medium of price does not carry the ruling of a real medium of price has already been set out in detail.

(2) The second form is to all appearances a suftajah, and there is some detail in it. Suftajah is derived from suftan, which means to bore a hole and thread something through it. In former times, in order to protect dirhams and dinars from thieves, people used to bore a hole in a staff and the like, thread — that is, place — the coins into it, and then convey them; for this reason it is called suftajah. The reality of a suftajah is that a man gives someone a thousand dirhams as a loan and lays down the condition that he will collect a thousand dirhams from him, or from his agent, in such-and-such a city. The jurists have called this forbidden and disliked, because it falls under the ruling of

"كل قرض جر نفعاً فهو ربا"

— that is, by means of this loan the lender has secured safety of the road for his money, and for that reason it is forbidden. But there are two conditions in this. One is that it must be a loan: if it is given as a trust, then there is no harm, and if the trust is destroyed, it is the property of the owner of the trust that has been destroyed. The second condition is that he stipulates in the loan contract that "I am giving the loan on condition that you write to someone in such-and-such a city so that he will hand me this money there." If he gave it without any condition and the borrower then wrote to someone in the other city, it is not disliked.

Shaykh Asʿad Ṣāghirjī writes:

فشرط الكراهة أو عدم الجواز شيئان: الأول أن يدفع المال في بلده قرضاً لمن يكتب له، فلو دفعه أمانة لم يكره ولم يفسد. والثاني: أن يشترط عليه في عقد القرض أن يكتب له به إلى البلدة الأخرى فلو لم يشترط لم يكره، عن عطاء بن أبي رباح أن عبد الله بن الزبير رضي الله عنه كان يأخذ من قوم بمكة دراهم، ثم يكتب بها إلى مصعب بن الزبير رضي الله عنه بالعراق فيأخذونها منه، فسئل ابن عباس رضي الله عنه عن ذلك، فلم ير به بأساً، فقيل له: إن اخذوا أفضل من دراهمهم، قال: لا بأس إن اخذوا بوزن دراهمهم، وروي في ذلك عن علي رضي الله عنه.
فإن صح ذلك عنه، وعن ابن عباس رضي الله عنه فإنما أرادا والله أعلم إذا كان ذلك بغير شرط. "السنن الكبرى للبيهقي": (۵/۳۵۲، دار المعرفة، بيروت). (الفقه الحنفي وأدلته "فقه المعاملات، القسم الاول، حكم السفتجة، ص ۱۳٤).

Fatḥ al-Qadīr states:

والقرض بهذا الشرط فاسد ولو لم يكن مشروطاً جاز وصورة الشرط ما في الواقعات رجل أقرض رجلاً مالاً على أن يكتب له به إلى بلد كذا فإنه لا يجوز، وإن أقرضه بغير شرط وكتب جاز. (فتح القدير: ۷/۲۵۱، كتاب الحوالة، دار الفكر).

However, if a person gives dirhams to a bank or to someone as a loan and pays a fee for their transfer, and then stipulates in the contract that he will take the money in such-and-such a city, then even so it is permissible. This is because the benefit of safety of the road that is obtained (indeed, here the object is not safety of the road at all, but merely the delivery of the money) was not obtained by reason of the loan; rather a fee was paid in return for it and the bank became a hired agent. Generally a fee for the transfer is paid to the money-order service or to the bank, so there is no harm in this transaction. Mawlānā Fatḥ Muḥammad Laknawī (may Allah have mercy on him) declared it permissible in his marginal notes on Sharḥ al-Wiqāyah; the details of it are found in Takmilah ʿUmdat al-Riʿāyah: 2/119.
