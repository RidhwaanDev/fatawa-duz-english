### TITLE
The ruling on taking a fee for keeping someone else's money in one's account

### QUESTION
A man gave two hundred thousand rupees to another person and said: deposit this money in your account. He also told him: because of depositing it and using your account you will receive 50 thousand rupees as a fee; then keep withdrawing the money from the account little by little and giving it to me. Is such an arrangement correct or not?

### ANSWER
In some countries, because of temporarily difficult conditions, people cannot keep large sums of money in their own accounts — otherwise the money will be confiscated, and there will be embarrassment on top of that. For this reason a person will have to have his money kept in someone else's account, and that person will receive 50 thousand in return for safeguarding the money and will then withdraw it little by little and hand it over to him. This is a form of hire (ijārah), and the sharīʿah permits it. It should also be borne in mind that depositing money into an account and withdrawing it is a troublesome business in which a good deal of effort has to be expended: one has to stand in a queue. Accordingly, at the time of the contract, safeguarding the money, using the account, and the effort of depositing and withdrawing and so on should all be mentioned; taking 50 thousand in return for all of this is permissible and correct. Consider what is recorded in al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah:

المودِع إذا شرط الأجرة للمودَع على حفظ الوديعة صح ولزم عليه كذا في جواهر الأخلاطي. (الفتاوى الهندية: ٤/۳٤۲).

The jurists have defined ijārah in the following words. Consider what is stated in al-Qudūrī:

الإجارة عقد على المنافع بعوض ولا يصح حتى تكون المنافع معلومة والأجرة معلومة، لأن الجهالة في المعقود عليه وبدله يفضي إلى المنازعة كجهالة الثمن والمبيع. (القدوري مع الجوهرة:). (وكذا في المبسوط للإمام السرخسي رحمه الله : ۱۵/۱۳۵، ط: بيروت).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
