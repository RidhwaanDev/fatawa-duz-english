### TITLE
Expiation for a traveller who returns home and deliberately breaks his fast

### QUESTION
If a man sets out from home intending a journey of the distance recognised by the sharia while he is fasting, and then turns back part-way, without completing that distance, and breaks his fast, is expiation due from him or not?

### ANSWER
Juristic preference requires that no expiation be due from him. The reason for this is that the man had become a traveller in the eyes of the sharia, and while travelling a traveller may choose between fasting and not fasting. If a traveller breaks his fast on a journey, no expiation is due from him, so none would be due from this man either.

Analogy, however, requires that expiation be due from him. The reason for this is that he abandoned the journey part-way and came back to his home town, so he came under the ruling of a resident; and when a resident deliberately breaks his fast, expiation is due from him. On this question the ruling is given on the basis of analogy — that is, expiation is due.

قال في الدر المختار: إلا إذا دخل مصره لشيء نسيه فأفطر فإنه يكفر، وقال في الشامية: أي قياساً لأنه مقيم عند الأكل حيث رفض سفره بالعود إلى منزله وبالقياس نأخذ، خانية، فتزاد هذه على المسائل التي قدم فيها القياس على الاستحسان.
