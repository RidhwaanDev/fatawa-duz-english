### TITLE
The ruling on fixing a percentage for an employee in addition to his salary

### QUESTION
A man has several sons. Two of the sons are engaged with their father in his business; some of the sons are young, and some are engaged in religious work. The father wishes to fix a salary for those sons who work, in return for their work; but, as is the custom and practice, they are not content with a salary alone — they want a percentage along with the salary. The question now is: is it permissible to fix a percentage for an employee in addition to his salary or not? For a percentage is fixed for a working partner in a muḍārabah or for a partner, whereas this man is an employee. بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, fixing a percentage for an employee in addition to his salary is not permissible according to the manifest transmitted reports of the school; the later scholars, however, have issued a ruling permitting it because of the general practice of people and in order to give employees an incentive. Consider what is recorded on the law of hire in Jadīd Maʿāshī Niẓām meṅ Qānūn-i Ijārah:

The second form is that there should be a formal agreement between the employer and the employee, and it should be settled that the employer will pay the employee, in addition to his salary, say three per cent of the profit. In that case the amount from the profit, over and above the salary, will also be regarded as part of the wage. If the hirer does not pay it, the employee will have the right to demand that amount, and in the event of non-payment the employee will have the right to seek redress in court. This form too is permissible in the Sharīʿah, and there is no doubt whatever as to its permissibility.

But a question arises here: when one portion of the wage has been fixed as a percentage, and the amount that the percentage will come to is not yet definitely known, then there is uncertainty in the wage; and uncertainty in the wage renders the contract defective. Hence here too a defect ought to arise in the contract of hire, on account of which the contract ought to be defective.

The answer to this question is that not every kind of uncertainty causes a defect; rather, it is uncertainty which is مفضی الی النزاع (that is, which leads to quarrelling and dispute) that causes a defect. In this case, therefore, although there is uncertainty in the wage — since one portion of the wage is fixed as a percentage whose definite amount is not known — this uncertainty is not such as leads to mutual dispute, because established practice runs upon this uncertainty.

Accordingly the honourable jurists have declared permissible a great many forms in which the wage is in reality uncertain but is not considered uncertain in common usage and does not give rise to disputes. For example, a broker's commission is not fixed at any particular figure but is settled as a proportion, a percentage; yet, despite this uncertainty, the honourable jurists declared this dealing permissible precisely because it is not a cause of mutual dispute and is very widely current among people.

As ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) has said in Radd al-Muḥtār:

قال في التاتارخانية: وفي الدلال والسمسار: يجب أجر المثل، وما تواضعوا عليه أن في كل عشرة دنانير كذا فذاك حرام عليهم وفي الحاوي: سئل محمد بن سلمة عن أجرة السمسار فقال: أرجو أنه لا بأس به وإن كان في الأصل فاسداً لكثرة التعامل وكثير من هذا غير جائز وجوزوه لحاجة الناس إليه. (رد المحتار: ٦/٦۳، سعيد).

Then, after a detailed discussion, it is recorded at the end: Therefore the arrangement whereby the employee shares in the profits of the hirer will also be permissible. (Jadīd Maʿāshī Niẓām meṅ Islāmī Qānūn-i Ijārah, pp. 194–205, ed. Idārat al-Maʿārif, Karachi).

Mālī Muʿāmalāt par Gharar ke Asarāt records:

One form of fixing a percentage as wage is that of a share in the profits of the employer, "the one who takes on the employee". The substance of this form is that the employer gives the employee a certain percentage of his profits as well. For example, a man engages someone in his business on the condition that, in addition to a stated salary, five per cent of the profit obtained will also be given to him, and that this amount too will be regarded as part of the wage. Businessmen generally adopt this form in order to develop their business further, because by doing so the employee makes the greatest possible effort to develop the business.

There are two forms of making the employee a sharer in the profit in this way.

The first form is that the employer gives the employee some portion of the profit by way of a bonus, this bonus not having been settled between them beforehand. This form is permissible, because it is a bonus given by the owner; and so, if at some time the employer does not give this bonus, the employee will have no right to demand it, nor will the employee be able to seek redress in court.

The second form is that a formal agreement is settled between the employer and the employee that at the end of the month, in addition to the salary, a certain percentage of the profit — five per cent, for example — will be paid to the employee. In this form the amount over and above the salary will also be regarded as part of the wage, and in the event of non-payment the employee will also have the right to seek redress in court.

Although uncertainty is present in this form, yet, since fixing a wage as a percentage does not become a cause of mutual dispute, this form too is permissible. (Mālī Muʿāmalāt par Gharar ke Asarāt, p. 91, by Dr Mawlānā Iʿjāz Aḥmad Ṣamadānī, ed. Idārat al-Maʿārif).

Not every uncertainty vitiates a contract; rather, that which becomes a cause of dispute vitiates it. Consider what Jamharat al-Qawāʿid states:

الجهالة لست مانعة لذاتها، بل لكونها مفضية إلى النزاع، وهذا أصل مهم ينبغي التعويل عليه في الأحكام، فإن به حل كثير من المشكلات، وليعلم أن أحكام المعاملات الشرعية على أصلين عادلين:
الأول: منع كل ما فيه ظلم وأكل لأموال الناس بالباطل.
الثاني: منع ما يؤدي إلى الاختلاف والنزاع بسبب الجهالة، فإذا انتفي ما يؤدي إلى الظلم والنزاع بسبب الجهالة، صح التعامل، والعرف أصل عظيم يرجع إليه في ذلك بعد الشرع. (جمهرة القواعد الفقهية في المعاملات المالية: ۱/۳۱۹، تحت القاعدة: الجهالة إنما توجب الفساد إذا كانت مفضية إلى النزاع المشكل).
