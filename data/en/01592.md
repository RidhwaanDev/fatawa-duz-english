### TITLE
Alternative ways of obtaining an annulment in non-Muslim courts

### QUESTION
Does the decision of non-Muslim courts take effect in the sharia in matters of divorce, annulment and separation, or not? For Muslims are now permanently settled in non-Muslim countries and cases of this kind do arise; indeed, in some countries it is obligatory to go to court for matters of this sort. So if such a decision does not take effect, is there some alternative way of giving it effect or not? Or is there some other course that may be adopted?

### ANSWER
A non-Muslim judge's decision may take several forms; they are set out below together with the ruling of the sharia.

Case one: The husband files a suit in court for divorce from his wife, and after the legal proceedings the court's non-Muslim judge decrees a divorce between the two.

From the point of view of the sharia a divorce will take effect by this decision. For although the sharia has given the full right of divorce to the man, by filing the suit the man made the court's non-Muslim judge his agent, so that as his agent he might bring the marriage between the two to an end.

Therefore: "The said marriage will be dissolved". In this case the marriage will be dissolved and, in the sharia, one irrevocable divorce will take effect, because it is valid to have work of this kind done by appointing a non-Muslim as one's agent; and the ʿiddah (waiting period) will be reckoned from the date of the "decree" (that is, of the issuing of the order).

Case two: The wife files a suit in court in accordance with the law of the land and asks for a separation, and the non-Muslim judge decrees a separation (divorce) between the two. The details of the court's decision and of the separation in this case are as follows:

(1) The official proceedings began, notice reached the husband from the judge, and the husband duly gave the judge permission to proceed with the case. In this case a divorce will take effect.

(2) The proceedings began, and when notice reached the husband from the judge he went to a lawyer. The lawyer advised him that defending the suit would bring no benefit other than delay and expense, and that the court would decree the separation in any case. So, with the husband's consent, the lawyer wrote that a separation should be effected between the two; or the husband himself willingly signed the divorce papers. In this case too a divorce takes effect in the sharia.

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

"إذا قال الرجل طلق امرأتي كان توكيلاً ولم يقتصر علي المجلس
۔
(الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/٤۰۲).

That is, when a man says to someone, "Divorce my wife," that person becomes his agent to pronounce the divorce.

In another place, on page 407, it is written:

"من قال لامرأته انطلقي إلي فلان حتي يطلقك، فذهبت فطلقها فلان ويصيرفلان وكيلاً بالتطليق وإن لم يعلم بوكالته"
۔

That is, someone said to his wife, "Come with me to so-and-so, so that he may divorce you." She went, and that person (the judge) pronounced the divorce upon her: the divorce will be valid, and that person (that is, the judge) will be deemed an agent for pronouncing the divorce, even though he did not know that he had been made an agent.

(3) The woman filed the suit and the judge sent notice to the husband; the man defended the suit and expressed no consent to the divorce but rather opposed it — yet in spite of this the judge, by force of the law, decreed a divorce.

(4) The husband admitted his faults and gave a full assurance that he would fulfil the rights of marriage in future — yet in spite of this the judge decreed a divorce.

(5) The man began no proceedings, nor did he do anything by which his consent to the divorce might be established — yet in spite of this the judge ordered a divorce.

(6) The man refused to defend the suit but explicitly refused to pronounce a divorce — yet in spite of this the judge decreed a divorce.

In these four cases (3, 4, 5 and 6) the two will be counted as out of the marriage so far as the law of the state is concerned; but according to Islamic jurisprudence their marriage remains intact as before, and in these circumstances the woman may not marry another man. She does, however, have the right to refer the matter to a sharia panchayat, and if after a proper investigation the woman's claim is established as correct, the sharia panchayat will be authorised to effect a separation.

(مستفاد از فتویٰ مفتی اسماعیل کچھولوی صاحب، اسلامی قانون طلاق نکاح و طلاق) ۔

"Yes — if he appoints the non-Muslim judge as his agent and the judge then decrees the divorce, it will take effect."

Under the new British law of 2004 a further question has been added to those which the court sends to the husband; it is as follows:

(5) "Do you consent to the decree being granted ?"

Translation: (5) Do you give the court permission to issue the decree nisi (divorce)?

If in reply the husband writes "yes", then the husband has, as it were, appointed the court's non-Muslim judge as his agent to bring about the divorce on his behalf, and the non-Muslim judge, acting as the husband's agent, brings the divorce into effect upon the woman. By this an Islamic and lawful divorce also takes place, since it is not necessary for an agent to be a Muslim; a divorce takes effect through a non-Muslim agent as well. (Summarised from Islāmī Qānūn-e Nikāḥ o Ṭalāq by Mawlānā Yaʿqūb Qāsmī Ṣāḥib, under the heading "The legal procedure for divorce in Britain", pp. 140–143.)

For the evidence in brief: Al-Mabsūṭ states:

إذا وكلت الذمية مسلماً بخلعها من الذمي على خمر أو خنزير جاز، وكذلك النكاح

ولوكان أحد الزوجين مسلماً والوكيل كافراً جاز الخلع ويبطل الجعل

لأن الوكيل سفير ومعبر لا يتعلق به شيء من حقوق العقد هنا. (المبسوط للإمام السرخسي رحمه الله: ۱۹/۱۳۲، باب توكيل الزوج بالطلاق والخلع، ط: إدارة القرآن. وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ٦/٦۱۳).

Al-Hidāyah states:

إن الوكيل في النكاح معبر وسفير والتمانع في الحقوق دون التعبير ولا ترجع الحقوق إليه بخلاف البيع لأنه مباشر حتى رجعت الحقوق إليه. (الهداية: ۲/۳۲۲، فصل في الوكالة بالنكاح. ومثله في البحر الرائق: ۳/۱۳٦، فصل في الوكالة، كوئته).

Radd al-Muḥtār states:

الوكيل في النكاح وما بعده سفير محض فلا بد من إضافة هذه العقود المذكورة إلى المؤكل. (فتاوى الشامي: ۳/۸۱۷، مطلب في العقود التي لا بد من إضافتها إلى المؤكل، سعيد).
