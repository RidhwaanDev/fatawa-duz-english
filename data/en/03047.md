### TITLE
The ruling on a buyer becoming an agent for other buyers and taking commission from them

### QUESTION
The question is a detailed one, but it is given here in brief. There is a company called Tiens which is registered in about 120 countries and sells a variety of goods. The company's rule is that a person first registers with the company, after which he obtains a code, and goods are bought from the company by means of it. When the value of the goods bought reaches 35,000 rupees, the buyer becomes a "3 star" — that is, he must have three other people registered. When those three men each become "3 star", the first buyer becomes a "4 star", and the profit on the goods bought by all three buyers is given to the first man. Then when those three buyers become "3 star" — that is, when they have three more people each registered with the company — the first buyer becomes a "5 star", and so on; in other words, as the number of people increases, the first buyer's level rises accordingly, and the profit of all of them goes to that first man. Those under the first buyer, too, will keep receiving the profit of those below them as they rise from level to level.

It should be borne in mind that the profit of each is fixed as a percentage. What we wish to know, then, is this: is it permissible to take part in this business of the Tiens company and to earn a profit from it, or not?

بينوا بالتفصيل توجروا بالأجر الجزيل.

### ANSWER
In the view of the muftis there are two features of this arrangement that are especially open to objection. The first is that the first commission agent has no part in the trade and profit of the second commission agent, so why should he take a profit from it? For this reason the muftis generally say that if the first commission agent is sitting at his ease while the commission agents after him are producing buyers by their own effort, then it is not permissible to fix a share for the first commission agent out of the earnings of those who come after him, since the first agent has done no work along with them. A question in the chapter on muḍārabah (silent partnership), however, suggests that there is room for this arrangement.

The question in the chapter on muḍārabah is this. Suppose an owner of capital gave capital to an agent-manager and said that half the profit would be his, and gave him permission to hand the capital on to another as a muḍārabah; and the first agent-manager then gave the capital to a second agent-manager on the terms that he would receive one third of the whole profit. Then, for example, if the profit is six thousand, the owner of the capital will take three thousand of it, the second agent-manager two thousand, and the first agent-manager one thousand as his share of the profit — even though here too the first agent-manager did no work: all the work was done by the second agent-manager, and the capital belongs to the owner. In spite of this the noble jurists have declared this arrangement permissible.

Thus ʿAllāmah al-Ḥaṣkafī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in al-Durr al-Mukhtār:

فإن أذن المالك بالدفع ودفع بالثلث وقد قيل للأول ما رزق الله فبيننا نصفان فللمالك النصف، عملاً بشرطه وللأول السدس الباقي وللثاني الثلث المشروط ولو قيل ما رزقك الله بكاف الخطاب والمسألة بحالها فللثاني ثلثه والباقي بين الأول والمالك نصفان باعتبار الخطاب فيكون لكل ثلث. (الدر المختار: ۵/٦۵۳، سعيد).

قال في رد المحتار: قال في البحر: وطاب الربح للجميع لأن عمل الثاني عمل عن المضارب كالأجير المشترك إذا استأجر آخر بأقل مما استؤجر. (فتاوى الشامي: ۵/٦۵۳، باب المضارب يضارب، سعيد).

(وكذا في البحر الرائق: ۷/۲٦٦، باب المضارب يضارب، ط: دار المعرفة، بيروت).

وقال في الهداية: قال: وإذا دفع إليه رب المال مضاربة بالنصف وأذن له أن يدفعه إلى غيره فدفعه بالثلث وقد تصرف الثاني وربح فإن كان رب المال قال له على أن ما رزق الله فهو بيننا نصفان فلرب المال النصف وللمضارب الثاني الثلث وللمضارب الأول السدس لأن الدفع إلى الثاني مضاربة قد صح لوجود الأمر به من جهة المالك. (الهداية: ۳/۲٦۳، ۲٦٤، باب المضارب يضارب).

قال في العناية: وإنما قال: يطيب لهما ذلك: أي للمضارب الأول والثاني الثلث والسدس لأن الأول وإن لم يعمل بنفسه شيئاً فقد باشر العقدين. (العناية شرح الهداية: ۸/٤٦۳، ط: دار الفكر).

ʿAllāmah Akmal al-Dīn al-Bābartī (may Allah have mercy on him) says that the profit is lawful and wholesome for the first agent-manager even though he did no work at all, because he was the means and the channel of the contract. In just the same way, in our question, the first man made the second an agent, and on that account the profit will be lawful for both. ʿAllāmah al-Zaylaʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) added a further point: that no doubt should be entertained about this — the profit is lawful and wholesome for both agent-managers, even though the first agent-manager is sitting comfortably at home.

قال: فهذه لا شبهة فيها وهي تجارة حسنة حيث يستحق الأول سدس الربح، وهو قاعد. (تبيين الحقائق: ۵/٦۵، إمدادية، ملتان).

See further: (تبيين الحقائق: ۵/٦٤، ط: إمدادية، ملتان، والفقه الحنفي وأدلته: ۲/۱۲۲).

In sum, here the first agent-manager has no part in the work or in the earning of the profit, and yet he receives one sixth — because the second agent-manager is trading with the permission of the first and standing in his place. In the same way here too the new commission agent, with the permission of the first, does work as though standing in his place. So if this arrangement is free of interest and gambling, there ought to be room for it.

An objection to the above ruling, and its answer:

Objection: the following passage of al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah shows that this arrangement is impermissible. Consider:

قال للدلال أعرض ضيعتي وبعها على أنك إذا بعتها فلك من الأجر كذا فلم يقدر الدلال إتمام الأمر ثم باعها دلال آخر، قال أبو القاسم: ولو عرضها الأول وصرف فيه روزجارا يعتد به فأجر مثله له واجب بقدر عنائه وعمله

ولا يجب له استحساناً إذا تركه وبه نأخذ. (الفتاوى الهندية: ٤/٤۵۱).

The answer: what the passage of ʿĀlamgīrī means is that the second broker worked by the permission of his own principal and without the permission of the first broker, so that the second broker did not stand in the place of the first; and therefore the second broker will receive nothing out of the first broker's profit.

A second parallel supporting the permissibility of the above ruling:

Some hadiths and particular rulings of the jurists show that if the first man has some part in a business, he may take a payment even though he does no work. For example, if a tailor seats a man of standing in his shop and settles with him that he shall have half the profit and half shall be the tailor's, this arrangement is permissible; for although the man of standing has no share in the tailor's work, his standing does have a part in it, and on that basis it is permissible for him to take a payment. Here too the work was done by number two or number three, but the first broker has a part in it: had he not enrolled number two as a member, the business would not have grown.

Sunan Ibn Mājah reports:

عن المنذر بن جرير عن أبيه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: "من سن سنة حسنة فعمل بها كان له أجرها ومثل أجرمن عمل بها لا ينقص من أجورهم شيئاً، ومن سن سنة سيئة فعمل بها كان عليه وزرها ووزر من عمل بها لا ينقص من أوزارهم شيئاً. (سنن ابن ماجه: ۱/۱۸).

The Noble Prophet ﷺ said: whoever institutes a good practice and acts upon it will receive its reward, and a reward like the reward of those who act upon it, without their rewards being diminished in any way; and whoever sets an evil practice going and adopts it will bear its sin, and the sin of those who do that evil deed will also be upon his head, without their sins being diminished in any way.

Ṭabarānī reports:

عن سهل بن سعد رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: "الدال على الخير كفاعله". (رواه الطبراني في المعجم الكبير: ۵/٤۷۷).

The Noble Prophet ﷺ said: the one who directs another to good is like the one who does it. That is, the two are equal partners in the reward.

These hadiths show that a man becomes deserving of reward or of punishment by being the cause and the means of another's act. In the same way, here too the first member is the cause and the channel of bringing the second member into the trade and of carrying the business forward.

The books of fiqh likewise mention this question: if a tailor seats a famous tailor, or some well-known personality, in his shop, and because of his fame customers come in large numbers to have clothes made, while the famous man does nothing and merely sits there, and they settle between themselves that half the wage shall go to one and half to the other, then this arrangement is permissible — even though the famous man does nothing and it is only his standing that is at work. In the same way here too the first commission agent does no work or dealing in the second man's business, but he laid the foundation of the trade that is running and set the practice going.

قال في الدر المختار: أقعد خياط أو صباغ في حانوته من يطرح عليه العمل بالنصف، سواء اتحد العمل أو اختلف كخياط مع قصار صح استحساناً، لأنه شركة الصنائع، فهذا بوجاهته يقبل وهذا بحذاقته يعمل.

قوله صح لأن شركة الصنائع يتقبل كل منهما العمل على أن ما يتقبله يكون أصلاً فيه بنفسه ووكيلاً عن شريكه فيكون الربح بينهما وهنا كذلك، فإن ما يلقيه عليه صاحب الحانوت من العمل يعمله الصانع أصالة عن نفسه ووكالة عن صاحب الحانوت فيكون الأجر بينهما كذلك، رحمتي. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۸۹، سعيد).

وفي فتح القدير: صورة المسألة إذا كان للخياط أو الصباغ دكان معروف وهو رجل مشهور عند الناس وله وجاهة ولكنه غير حاذق فيقعد في دكانة رجلاً حاذقاً ليتقبل العمل من الناس ويعمل ذلك الرجل على أن ما أصابا من شيء فهو بينهما نصفان. (فتح القدير: ۸/۸۹، مسائل منثورة من كتاب الإجارة، ط: كوئته). (وكذا في الكفاية على هامش فتح القدير: ۸/۸۹).

Maḥmūd al-Fatāwā, answering a detailed question about becoming a customer of a company called "A to Z Marketing", states:

To give Zayd a commission at the rate of two per cent on whatever is purchased by those who become direct customers through him is in order, since it amounts to brokerage — provided that the chain does not carry on beyond that. But if this commission is also given for customers who become customers indirectly through him, then it is not in order, since it no longer answers to the description of brokerage. (Maḥmūd al-Fatāwā: 3/20–21).

The second objection is that it involves a sale with a condition attached, which is not permissible. If, however, the condition becomes customary, then it does not vitiate the contract. See what Sharḥ al-Majallah states:

وحاصل ما ذكره الفقهاء في البيع مع الشرط أن الشرط الذي يقترن به البيع إما أن يقتضيه العقد، وإما أن لا يقتضيه العقد لكن يلائمه، وإما أن لا يقتضيه العقد ولا يلائمه لكن قد جري العرف باشتراطه، وإما أن لا يقتضيه العقد ولا يلائمه ولا جري العرف باشتراطه، لكن لا منفعة فيه لا حد، فالبيع في هذه الوجوه الأربعة صحيح والشرط معتبر في الوجوه الثلاثة الأولى منها، ويلغو في الوجه الرابع. (شرح المجلة لمحمد خالد الأتاسي، ۲/۵۹، الفصل الرابع في حق البيع بشرط).

Muftī Taqī ʿUthmānī Ṣāḥib says in the Takmilah:

وخلاصة مذهب الحنفية في ذلك أنه إن كان شرطاً يقتضيه العقد، أو يلائم العقد أو شرطاً جري به التعامل بين الناس، فهو جائز ولا يفسد به البيع

ومثال الشرط الذي جري به التعامل ما إذا اشتري نعلاً على أن يحذوه البائع أو جراباً على أن يخرزه له خفاً، قال السرخسي رحمه الله في المبسوط: وإن كان شرطاً لا يقتضيه العقد، وفيه عرف ظاهر فذلك جائز أيضاً، كما لو اشتري نعلاً وشراكاً بشرط أن يحذوه البائع، لأن الثابت بالعرف ثابت بدليل شرعي، ولأن في النزع عن العادة الظاهرة حرجاً بيناً، وقال الكاساني: في "البدائع: ۵/۱۷۲" والقياس أن لا يجوز وهو قول زفر رحمه الله، وجه القياس أن هذا شرط لا يقتضيه العقد وفيه منفعة لا حد العاقدين، وإنه مفسد

ولنا أن الناس تعاملوا هذا الشرط في البيع، كما تعاملوا الاستصناع فسقط القياس بتعامل الناس، كما سقط في الاستصناع. (تكملة فتح الملهم: ۱/٦۲۹، مسألة الشرط في البيع).

Al-Hidāyah states:

ومن اشتري نعلاً على أن يحذوه البائع أو يشركه فالبيع فاسد

وفي الاستحسان يجوز للتعامل فيه. (الهداية: ۳/٦۱).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

In order to promote their products and to attract customers, the practice current today is that the buyer is given an assurance that the goods will be put right and repaired for a certain period. This question is important because the sharia has not declared any such additional condition permissible in buying and selling; and on that very basis the jurists have declared a transaction of sale vitiated on account of such conditions. This requires that a transaction of this kind should be held impermissible on account of such a guarantee. But in the view of the jurists the purpose of this prohibition of the sharia is to close the door to possible dispute, and conditions that have become customary and current do not become a cause of dispute. Such conditions have therefore been declared permissible and dependable.

The author of al-Hidāyah, making an exception of conditions of this sort, says:

إلا أن يكون متعارفاً". (الهداية: ۳/۵۹، باب البيع الفاسد).

(Taken from Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/387, and likewise in Taqrīr al-Tirmidhī: 1/109).

In sum, not every condition vitiates a contract; the condition that vitiates a contract is the one that leads to dispute. Otherwise, where a condition accords with what the contract itself requires and is a compatible condition, there is room for it. Nowadays most such conditions are current in custom and also accord with what the contract requires, and so a ruling of a vitiated contract will not be given.

For further detail on a sale with a condition see: (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā, vol. 5, p. 55, published by Zamzam).

It may be said, however, in this particular case, that since the condition is not a general one but is peculiar to this company, it ought to vitiate the contract.

We had written this answer when afterwards a fatwa was published by the Dār al-Iftāʾ of Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah, Karachi, in the monthly al-Fārūq, in which an arrangement of this kind was declared impermissible and certain difficulties were raised about it. In the light of those difficulties one should avoid an arrangement of this kind.

A few of the difficulties are as follows:

(1) The object of the (DXN) company is not the selling of products.

(2) The buyer's object in buying is to become a member, not to use the goods himself.

(3) Even after spending his money the buyer is not fully satisfied about his profit, since it is not known whether he will be able to find further members or not. This is uncertainty. And to enter into it merely in order to obtain commission and bonus, when buying is not the object, resembles interest — that is, it becomes a transaction of money for money.

(4) According to the company's own announcement, a member of the (DXN) company does not become a hired employee but a buyer; and it is not permissible to impose conditions upon a buyer.

Condition (a): the obligation to sell at the price fixed by the company. This condition vitiates the contract, because it takes away the buyer's freedom of action.

Condition (b): he may not sell openly in the market. (c) Nor is it permissible to give the company's products to anyone as a gift by way of publicity without the company's knowledge.

Then at the end this is recorded: this, then, is a business which involves lying and deception, in which there is a tinge of interest and of uncertainty, and in whose contract there are conditions of the kind that vitiate a contract. It is therefore not permissible to enter into a distributorship with a multi-level marketing company. And even supposing that there were no deception, interest, gambling or uncertainty in their method, it would still not be permissible to enter into a distributorship with the DXN company, because of the things contrary to the sharia that are present in their rules and regulations. (From the monthly al-Fārūq, pp. 29–32, under the title: "The method of the DXN company, and the ruling on becoming an agent of the company").

A fatwa declaring this arrangement impermissible has also been published by Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, which is as follows:

According to the procedure of the said China company which you have described, registering oneself in it and earning money by working in it is impermissible and unlawful. In the first place, the recruiting of members in the way the company's people intend is not within your power. If you recruit members in the hope that if three members are made you will receive advancement upon advancement, and that if they cannot be made you will receive no advancement — and then that you should take a payment for the recruiting of members done by others — this too is against the principles of the sharia: the one who recruits members has the right to take payment, and others have no right to take it. For these reasons it is not permissible for Muslims to take part in this company. Muslims should adopt some other lawful and permissible way of earning.

(By (Muftī) Ḥabīb al-Raḥmān, may Allah pardon him. The answer is correct: Maḥmūd Ḥasan Bulandshahrī, may Allah forgive him; Muḥammad Nuʿmān Sītāpūrī, may Allah forgive him. Muftis of the Dār al-Iftāʾ, Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband).
