### TITLE
The ruling on working in a shop that sells unlawful and impermissible items

### QUESTION
Is the income of a Muslim lawful if he works in hotels belonging to non-Muslims where liquor, pork and other unlawful things are sold?

### ANSWER
It is permissible and correct for a Muslim to work in a non-Muslim's establishment, provided that the Muslim does not himself serve liquor or pork, or place other unlawful things before non-Muslims, or engage directly in buying and selling them — because to do these things directly is impermissible for a Muslim.

See what the noble hadith states:

عن أنس بن مالك رضي الله عنه لعن رسول الله ﷺ في الخمر عشرة عاصرها ومعتصرها وشاربها وحاملها والمحمولة إليه وساقيها وبايعها وآكل ثمنها والمشتري لها والمشتراة له. (رواه الترمذي: ۱/۳۸۰، باب ما جاء في بيع الخمر).

Ḥaḍrat Anas ibn Mālik (may Allah be pleased with him) reports that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ cursed ten people in connection with liquor: the one who presses it, the one for whom it is pressed, the one who drinks it, the one who carries it, the one to whom it is carried, the one who serves it, the one who sells it, the one who consumes its price, the one who buys it, and the one for whom it is bought.

Al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

وإذا استأجر الذمي مسلماً ليحمل له ميتة أو دماً يجوز عندهم جميعاً

ولو استأجر مسلماً ليرعي له الخنازير يجب أن يكون على الخلاف كما في الخمر ولو استأجره ليبيع له ميتة لم يجز هكذا في الذخيرة، مسلم آجر نفسه من مجوسي ليوقد له النار لا بأس به كذا في الخلاصة

وسئل إبراهيم بن يوسف رحمه الله عمن آجر نفسه من النصارى ليضرب لهم الناقوس كل يوم بخمسة ويعطي كل يوم خمسة دراهم في ذلك العمل وفي عمل آخر درهمان قال: لا يؤاجر نفسه منهم ويطلب الرزق من طريق آخر ويكره له أن يؤاجر نفسه منهم لعصر العنب ليتخذوا منه خمراً كذا في الحاوي للفتاوى. (الفتاوى الهندية: ٤/٤۵۰).

(وكذا في فتاوى قاضيخان على هامش الهندية: ۲/۳۲٤).

Qāmūs al-Fiqh states:

Just as it is not correct to do an impermissible act contrary to the sharia oneself, so too it is not correct to be employed in such work or to assist in it. Employment in banks, insurance companies, taverns and brothels will therefore not be permissible where a man becomes the means of the business of interest, gambling, liquor or prostitution, invites people to it, or writes up the accounts of interest. Employment as a peon and the like, however, may be taken, since such a person has no direct connection with the business. (Qāmūs al-Fiqh: 1/500, "Employment in impermissible work").

Fiqhī Maqālāt states:

It is permissible for a Muslim to take employment in a non-Muslim's hotel, provided that the Muslim does not serve liquor or place pork or other unlawful things before non-Muslims, because serving liquor or placing it before others is unlawful. And from the verdict of Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) this too has become clear: that even in a region where the making of liquor and trade in it is common practice, it is still not lawful for a Muslim to take up the liquor trade as a means of livelihood. (Fiqhī Maqālāt: 1/250–252).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Work that is impermissible is also impermissible to be employed in; one should look for another means of livelihood and give up such a job. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 17/121, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

Taʾlīfāt-e Rashīdiyyah states:

Employment with disbelievers is correct so long as it involves nothing contrary to the sharia. (Taʾlīfāt-e Rashīdiyyah, p. 420).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

Employment in buying and selling liquor and in serving it is not permissible. For some other kind of work there is room to be employed in a disbeliever's liquor business, but there are several religious dangers in that too, so keeping away is better. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 7/332).

See further: (Imdād al-Aḥkām: 3/533; and Fiqhī Maqālāt: 1/252–255).
