### TITLE
Objections to the murābaḥah contract of an Islamic bank, and their answers

### QUESTION
Where we live there are some non-interest banks that work on the murābaḥah pattern, the form of which is roughly this: Zayd needs goods but has no money for the price, so he applies to the bank for a murābaḥah transaction to be made with him. After making its enquiries the bank gives him an agreement document. The method is that Zayd goes to some shop or factory and an agent of the bank, or Zayd himself, buys the required goods for the bank for one hundred thousand rand. Then Zayd buys these same goods from the bank for one hundred and 30 thousand on a deferred price. Is this transaction permissible or not? Some of the learned scholars raise objections to it, two of which deserve attention.

(1) The first is that if an agent of the bank bought the goods for the bank, then on the face of it no great objection arises. But if Zayd himself becomes the bank's agent to purchase and then becomes the buyer himself, then his first taking of possession, in his capacity as agent and trustee, is possession as a trust, while his taking possession for himself is possession under liability, or possession by way of exchange; and possession as a trust, which is the earlier possession, cannot stand in the place of possession by way of exchange, which is the later possession?

(2) The second objection is that if Zayd is himself the agent to purchase, and then, as agent to sell, is also the buyer, then one man becomes both seller and buyer, which is forbidden in the light of the statements of the jurists. Or, to put it another way, Zayd is both the principal, that is the buyer, and the agent, that is the agent to sell; in other words Zayd is both the one who demands the price and the one from whom the price is demanded. How can these two capacities be combined?

See what ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

الوكيل بالبيع لا يملك شراءه لنفسه لأن الواحد لا يكون مشترياً وبائعاً فيبيعه من غيره ثم يشتريه منه. (فتاوى الشامي: ۵/۵۲۱، سعيد).

### ANSWER
(1) In the case described, possession as a trust cannot stand in the place of possession under liability, because possession as a trust is weak and slight while possession under liability is firm and strong, and a weak, slight thing cannot stand in the place of a firm and strong one. However, once a person deals with what he holds in trust, that possession automatically becomes possession under liability, and if the goods are destroyed the compensation will fall on the buyer himself.

The upshot is that where the goods have been dealt with, possession as a trust has automatically become possession under liability. Had possession as a trust not become possession under liability, then in the case of destruction the compensation would have fallen on the bank, which is the seller; but where the goods have been dealt with or moved, the compensation will fall on the buyer. That is, the question of possession as a trust not standing in the place of possession under liability has to do with liability — on whom the compensation falls if the goods are destroyed. It is not a question of lawful and unlawful. The buyer may also make an agreement with the bank, or state verbally, that if the goods are destroyed before he deals with them the bank will be responsible.

See what Sharḥ al-Qawāʿid al-Fiqhiyyah states:

بخلاف ما لو باع الأمانة ممن هي عنده فإنه لا ينوب قبض الأمانة عن قبض البيع لأن قبض البيع قبض الضمان وقبض الأمانة دونه فلا ينوب عنه بل لا بد من تجديد القبض بأن يخلي بين نفسه وبين الأمانة المبيعة بعد العقد. (شرح القواعد الفقهية للشيخ احمد بن الشيخ محمد الزرقا، ص ۳۰۱).

Al-Hidāyah states:

وإن كان له مال في يد أجنبي فأنفق عليهما بغير إذن القاضي ضمن لأنه تصرف في مال الغير بغير ولاية لأنه نائب في الحفظ لا غير، بخلاف ما إذا أمره القاضي لأن أمره ملزم لعموم ولايته، وإذا ضمن لا يرجع على القابض لأنه ملكه بالضمان فظهر أنه كان متبرعاً به. (الهداية، باب النفقة، ۲/٤٤۸).

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states:

The first possession, which is by way of agency, is in the capacity of a trust, while the second possession is on one's own account and is possession under liability; and such a second possession is recognised only where the first was of the same kind, as when someone has usurped property, in which case the possession is possession under liability. But it is expressly stated that, in the case of possession as a trust, once the sale transaction has been made, if the man gains access to that property or exercises some ownerly dealing in it, then his possession of those goods on his own account is established. And the matter that gives rise to the objection comes down to this: if the first possession is one of trust, then the sale by itself will not turn that possession into his own possession under liability; and if the first possession is itself one of liability, then by the sale alone it becomes his own possession. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 3/427, 428, quoting al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq 6/86 and Radd al-Muḥtār 4/112).

Dealing with what is held in trust turns possession as a trust into possession under liability. See what al-Mabsūṭ states:

ثم الخلط أنواع ثلاثة خلط يتعذر التمييز بعده كخلط الشيء بجنسه فهذا موجب للضمان لأنه يتعذر به على المالك الوصول إلى عين ملكه

وخلط يتعسر معه التمييز كخلط الحنطة بالشعير فهو موجب للضمان لأنه يتعذر على المالك الوصول إلى عين ملكه إلا بحرج

وإذا كان عند الرجل وديعة دراهم أو دنانير أو شيء من المكيل أو الموزون فأنفق طائفة منهما في حاجته كان ضامناً لما أنفق

فإن جاء بمثل ما أنفق فخلطه بالباقي صار ضامناً لجميعها لأن ما أنفق صار دينا في ذمته وهو لا ينفرد بقضاء الدين بغير محضر من صاحبه فيكون فعله هذا خلطاً لما بقي بملك نفسه وذلك موجب للضمان عليه. (المبسوط للإمام السرخسي رحمه الله، ۱۱/۱۱۰، ۱۱۱، كتاب الوديعة، إدارة القرآن).

Taʾlīfāt-e Rashīdiyyah states:

It is not correct for a trustee to deal with what he holds; if he does so he becomes liable for it. (Taʾlīfāt-e Rashīdiyyah, p. 431).

Moreover, if the depositee rides an animal held in deposit and it perishes while being ridden, the compensation will fall on the depositee. ʿAllāmah ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

لأن الضمان وجب عليه بنفس الركوب حتى لو هلك في حالة الاستعمال يضمن بلا خلاف. (حاشية هداية: ۳/۲۷۲)

This shows that riding an animal held in deposit, or wearing clothes held in deposit, turned possession as a trust into possession under liability; and it is for this reason that the jurists call the lending of dirhams for spending a loan. (الهداية: ۳/۲۷۹)

This is because borrowed dirhams, once they are spent, automatically become a loan for which one is liable.

Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

When something held in trust is spent with permission, it becomes a loan, even if that was not intended. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 3/145).

He also says in another place: As for what is deposited in banks, even though it is styled a deposit, it is certain and well known under these rules that these deposits are not kept there as they are; rather business is done with them. So, by the rule "المعروف کالمشروط" — what is customary is as though it were stipulated — this dealing will be treated as permitted by the depositor, and to give permission to deal is to make a loan. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 2/571, Kitāb al-Waqf).

وللاستزادة انظر: فتاوى الشامي: ۵/٦٦۸. ٦۷۰، كتاب الإيداع، سعيد. وحاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ۳/۳۷۹، كوئته).

An objection and its answer:

Objection: An objection arises on this point, namely that a person becomes sinful by dealing with what is held in trust, so why has permission been given to deal with it?

Answer: The answer is that the owner is content with the dealing, so in this case there will be no sin.

(2) The answer to the second objection is that ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) has also recorded a position that if, by the instruction of the principal, the agent buys the goods for himself — that is, becomes seller and buyer at one and the same time — that too is correct.

See what Fatāwā Shāmī states:

وإن أمره المؤكل أن يبيعه من نفسه وأولاده الصغار أو ممن لا يقبل شهادته فباع منهم جاز بزازية كذا في البحر، ولا يخفي ما بينهما من المخالفة، وذكر مثل ما في السراج في النهاية عن المبسوط، ومثل ما في البزازية في الذخيرة عن الطحاوي، وكأن في المسألة قولين خلافاً لمن ادعي أنه لا مخالفة بينهما. (فتاوى الشامي: ۵/۵۲۲، سعيد. وكذا في البحر الرائق: ۷/۱٦۷، كوئته).

In order to save people from the curse of interest, fatwa may be given on this position of ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him). And if the bank appoints someone else as agent to sell, that is light upon light.

The upshot is that if the goods are destroyed before the buyer deals with them, the bank is responsible for them; and if they are destroyed after the buyer has dealt with them, the compensation will fall on the buyer.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
