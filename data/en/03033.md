### TITLE
The rights of non-Muslims, and an explanation of the narration about the narrow road

### QUESTION
In Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim (at 2/214) the following hadith is recorded:

لا تبدءوا اليهود ولا النصارى بالسلام وإذا لقيتم أحدهم في طريق فاضطروه إلى أضيقه". وهكذا رواه أبو داود والترمذي وغيرهما

That is: when you meet Jews and Christians, do not be the first to greet them with salām, and when you come across one of them on a road, force him towards the narrowest part of it. In non-Muslim countries — America, Europe and so on — Muslims are taunted with the charge that in their religion non-Muslims have no rights at all, and that they are fit only to be pushed into a narrow road. What does this hadith mean? Kindly explain. بینوا بالبیان توجروا بالجنان۔

### ANSWER
Before the explanation and clarification of the hadith in question, a few submissions are offered by way of introduction.

Islam is a religion that loves justice, that crushes oppression and tyranny, that guards the rights of humanity, that encourages good character and discourages deeds which corrupt character; it is a universal code of conduct and a law for living. Just as it laid down laws in the Islamic state to protect the rights of Muslims, so it imposed and made binding, in exactly the same way, laws to protect the rights of the non-Muslim dhimmīs (protected subjects). In transactions, dealings and rights it allowed no distinction between Muslim and non-Muslim. On the contrary, by displaying justice and fairness in this matter it won the hearts of all humanity and made them its devoted admirers. It was because of these very qualities that within a few decades people entered the fold of Islam in droves, something the world is unable to match. Yes, it is quite another matter that if some painter presents a lion to the public in the shape of a coward, that is no fault of the lion, but a betrayal by the man holding the pen.

A few examples of the justice, fairness, kindness and honour shown to non-Muslims are offered here:

(1) Allah, blessed and exalted, has commanded in the Noble Qur'an that all human beings be honoured.

قال الله تعالى: ﴿ولقد كرمنا بني آدم﴾ [الاسراء: ۷۰].

(2) When delegations of non-Muslims arrive, we have been commanded to honour them.

أجيزوا الوفد بنحو ما كنت أجيزهم". (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī: 1/449).

(3) During the famine at Makkah al-Mukarramah the Messenger of Allah ﷺ sent 500 dinars as relief. (al-Mabsūṭ: 10/92, Dār al-Fikr; Sharḥ al-Siyar al-Kabīr: 1/70; and Fatāwā Shāmī, bāb al-maṣrif: 3/302).

(4) When Ḥaḍrat ʿIkrimah ibn Abī Jahl (may Allah be pleased with him) came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ rose quickly in delight and came forward to meet him without his cloak. It was after this that Ḥaḍrat ʿIkrimah became a Muslim. (Ṣuwar min Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, p. 120).

(5) Ḥaḍrat ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim (may Allah be pleased with him) presented himself before the Messenger of Allah ﷺ before he was honoured with Islam. In the house of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ there was a cushion, or mattress, stuffed with palm bark. He placed that very thing before him and said, "Sit on this." Ḥaḍrat ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim (may Allah be pleased with him) said, "You be seated." He ﷺ said, "No, you sit." Then Ḥaḍrat ʿAdī (may Allah be pleased with him) sat on it, and after that he accepted Islam. (Ṣuwar min Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, p. 139; Sīrat Ibn Hishām: 2/580; al-Rawḍ al-Unuf: 4/360; and Tārīkh al-Rusul wa al-Umam: 2/188, Beirut).

(6) When the sister of Ḥaḍrat ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim (may Allah be pleased with him) was brought as a captive before the Noble Prophet ﷺ, he ﷺ sent her home without any ransom, with maintenance, goods and a mount, and with honour and respect. (al-Rawḍ al-Unuf: 4/360; Sīrat Ibn Hishām: 3/579; ʿUyūn al-Athar: 2/286; Tārīkh al-Rusul wa al-Mulūk: 2/187; al-Sīrah al-Nabawiyyah of Ibn Kathīr: 4/125; and Tārīkh Madīnat Dimashq: 69/199, published by Dār al-Fikr).

(7) When Shaymāʾ bint al-Ḥārith, the foster sister of the Noble Prophet ﷺ, was brought before him ﷺ as a captive, he ﷺ spread out his own cloak and seated her upon it, and sent her home with honour and dignity and with gifts and bounty. (al-Bidāyah wa al-Nihāyah: 4/467, 763, Dār al-Maʿrifah, Beirut; al-Rawḍ al-Unuf: 4/227; Sīrat Ibn Hishām: 2/457; and ʿUyūn al-Athar: 2/221).

(8) When Ḥaḍrat ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr (may Allah be pleased with him) slaughtered a goat, he would send a portion of the cooked meat to his Jewish neighbour. (Shuʿab al-Īmān of al-Bayhaqī: 7/84; Sunan al-Tirmidhī: 2/16; and al-Adab al-Mufrad: 1/5).

(9) Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (may Allah be pleased with him) said: as for those who have been given a guarantee of safety by us, the sanctity of their blood is like the sanctity of the blood of Muslims, and their blood money is like the blood money of Muslims. (Sunan al-Dāraquṭnī: 3/147, Cairo; Naṣb al-Rāyah: 3/381, Beirut; and Musnad al-Imām al-Shāfiʿī: 1/344, Beirut).

(10) On the occasion of the revelation of

﴿ليس عليك هداهم ولكن الله يهدي من يشاء، وما تنفقوا من خير فلأنفسكم﴾ [البقرة: ۲۷۲]

the commentators have written that some of the Anṣār used to hold back from spending on their idolatrous relatives, so that they might become Muslims. They were cautioned about this: guidance is not in your hands; whatever you spend, you will be repaid and rewarded for it. (Rūḥ al-Maʿānī: 3/73). This shows that good treatment is to be shown to non-Muslims too.

(11) Allah says in the Noble Qur'an:

﴿لاينها كم الله عن الذين لم يقاتلوكم في الدين ولم يخرجوكم من دياركم أن تبروهم وتقسطوا إليهم﴾ [الممتحنة: ۸]

There is no harm in dealing kindly with those non-Muslims who do not oppose you, who do not fight you over religion, and who have not driven you out of your homes.

(12)

من قتل معاهداً لم يرح رائحة الجنة وإن ريحها توجد من مسيرة أربعين عاماً". (رواه البخاري، رقم: ۳۱٦٦).

Whoever kills a non-Muslim citizen of a Muslim state will not smell the fragrance of Paradise, and that fragrance is perceived from a distance of forty years' travel.

(13) The Noble Prophet ﷺ wrote a letter to Heraclius, who was an unbeliever, and in it he used the words "the great one of the Romans", which convey honour and respect. (Bukhārī: 1/5/7, with ʿUmdat al-Qārī: 1/159, Dār al-Ḥadīth, Multan).

(14) The delegation of Najrān and the delegation of Thaqīf were lodged in the mosque, although they were Christians and non-Muslims. (al-Bidāyah wa al-Nihāyah: 5/60, wafd ahl Najrān, published at Riyadh; Sharḥ Mawāhib: 4/41; al-Bidāyah wa al-Nihāyah: 5/33, qudūm wafd Thaqīf ʿalā Rasūlillāh ﷺ fī Ramaḍān min sanat tisʿ, published at Riyadh; and Tārīkh al-Islām of Imām al-Dhahabī: 2/669, Beirut).

(15) Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote a letter to his governors in which he wrote, among other things: look after the non-Muslim dhimmīs; whichever of them reaches old age, whose strength has failed and whose means of earning have come to an end, fix a stipend for him from the public treasury.

ذكر أبو عبيد في "الأموال" كتاب عمر بن عبد العزيز رحمه الله لعامله على البصرة وفيه: وانظر من قبلك من أهل الذمة قد كبرت سنه، وضعفت قوته، وولت عنه المكاسب، فأجر عليه من بيت مال المسلمين ما يصلحه. (الاموال، ص ٤٦).

Commenting on this passage, Dr. Yūsuf al-Qaraḍāwī says: see, this is the justice and fairness of Islam. It did not leave the non-Muslims to come to the governor themselves and ask for help; rather, before that, it directed the governors to go and see for themselves, to look into their circumstances, and to meet their needs and claims out of the public treasury.

قال الشيخ: ومعني أجر عليه: اجعل له شيئاً جارياً، وراتباً دورياً، والجميل حقاً أنه لم يدع أهل الذمة حتى يطلبوا هم المعونة، بل طلب الخليفة من الوالي أن يبادر هو فينظر في حالاتهم ومطالبهم، فيسدها من بيت المال، هذا هو عدل الإسلام. (فقه الزكاة: ۲/۱۵۸).

(16) Al-Ashbāh wa al-Naẓāʾir states that if the word "unbeliever" weighs heavily on a non-Muslim, he should not be addressed as "O unbeliever".

ولو قال لذمي: يا كافر يأثم إن شق عليه. (الأشباه والنظائر: ۲/۲۵۷).

(17) A marriage with a non-Muslim Jewish or Christian woman is validly contracted — and a man shares a bed with his wife; he does not push her up against the wall.

(18) Al-Ashbāh states:

الذمي حكمه حكم المسلمين، إلا أنه لا يؤمر بالعبادات، وقال العلامة الحموي: يعني في غير ما يوجب تعظيمه

الخ". (الأشباه: ۳/۸۳، ط: إدارة القرآن)

That is, the ruling on a non-Muslim dhimmī is like the ruling on the Muslims; except that they are not fit for acts of worship, and so they will not be commanded to perform them. On this, ʿAllāmah al-Ḥamawī (may Allah have mercy on him) remarks that their eminent figures will not be shown the honour and veneration that is shown to the eminent figures of the Muslims — so that no doubt may arise about the truth of an abrogated religion.

To say, then, that non-Muslims have no rights in Islam is nothing but a fabrication.

After this brief introduction we come to the answer proper. The hadith about which you have enquired has been discussed by the commentators in the commentaries on hadith — for example, Badhl al-Majhūd Sharḥ Abī Dāwūd: 2/142; Takmilah Fatḥ al-Mulhim: 4/255; and Mirqāt al-Mafātīḥ. The discussion is also found in the books of fiqh: Shāmī: 4/208, and al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq: 5/113.

But one clear and straightforward meaning of this blessed hadith is this. Out of respect for their own revered figures, Muslims used to push themselves towards the narrow side in a crowd or a press of people and leave the open way for them, and they would not walk ahead of them; indeed, even on an open road students would stand back against the wall and let their teacher pass.

It was for this reason that when a bedouin's she-camel got ahead of the she-camel of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, the noble Companions found it hard to bear, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: whatever goes ahead in this world sometimes falls behind as well. (Muṣannaf Ibn Abī Shaybah: 18/187, published by al-Majlis al-ʿIlmī; Musnad Aḥmad: 3/103, Dār al-Fikr; and al-Bukhārī: 1/402, bāb nāqat al-Nabī ﷺ).

And one narration mentions that Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) and Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) used to walk in front of the funeral bier in order to make things easy for the people; for if they had walked behind, the people in the crowd would have left the way open for them and put up with the difficulty and hardship themselves. (Sharḥ Maʿānī al-Āthār: 1/312, published by Fayṣal; Musnad al-Bazzār: 2/137, published by Maktabat al-ʿUlūm wa al-Ḥikam; and al-Sunan al-Kubrā of al-Bayhaqī: 2/25, Dār al-Maʿrifah). And this is still the custom today.

The hadith, then, points to this: as for the non-Muslims who are called dhimmīs — and dhimmī means that the protection of their lives and property is the responsibility of the Muslims — their protection is one thing, but do not give them in general, and their revered figures in particular, the honour that you give to your own revered figures. If they are standing or walking in a narrow place, let them walk on. Were we to give them the same honour that we give to our teachers and our parents, it would raise in the minds of ordinary Muslims the doubt that their religion is true, whereas their religion has been corrupted and abrogated, while our religion abides and is true until the Day of Judgement.

From these words of the hadith in question,

لا تبدءوا اليهود ولا النصارى

it is clear that what is meant are their revered figures in particular and non-Muslims generally; for the rule of the salām is that the one of lower standing greets the one of higher standing. So this hadith tells us that however high a non-Muslim's standing may be, Muslims should neither greet him first with salām, nor stand aside to widen the way for him. In the books of hadith the words

يسلم الصغير على الكبير" (رواه البخاري، رقم: ٦۲۳۲)

occur, and this covers both the one who is younger in years and the one who is lower in rank.

Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (may Allah have mercy on him) said in Fatḥ al-Bārī:

حاصل ما في هذا الحديث أن المفضول بنوع ما يبدأ الفاضل. (فتح الباري: ۱۱/۱۷).

In another place, quoting from al-Māzarī, he writes:

يبدأ الأدنى منهما الأعلى قدراً في الدين إجلالاً لفضله. (فتح الباري: ۱۱/۱٦).

It is therefore established that Muslims should not initiate the sharia greeting of salām — which is an act of worship — with the eminent figures of the non-Muslims in particular, or with the rest of them generally, nor should they widen the way for them. Yes, if instead of the salām they gesture and say "good morning" or something of that sort, there is room for it, so that a friendly relationship may grow. But the words of the salām are words of the sharia laid down for Muslims alone, appointed by the sharia itself.

والله أعلم وعلمه أحكم
