### TITLE
Building a house with zakat money and making a poor man its owner

### QUESTION
If someone buys a house with zakat money and makes a poor man the owner of that house, has the zakat been discharged or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, if the house has been put in the poor man's name and he has been given its papers and made its owner, the zakat is discharged.

It is necessary, however, that once the house has been built its value be fixed according to the market. It should not happen that two hundred thousand was spent on the house, its market value is two hundred and fifty thousand, and the person paying zakat prices it at three hundred thousand; rather he should price it at the ordinary market rate. There is admittedly a drawback here, in that a single poor person has been given more than the amount of the nisab. But in view of the poor man's need, the provision of housing for his family, and the meeting of an important need, it is to be hoped that Allah will accept this act and grant its reward.

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

هي تمليك خرج الإباحة، فلو أطعم يتيماً ناوياً الزكاة لا يجزيه إلا إذا دفع إليه المطعوم لأنه بالدفع إليه بنية الزكاة يملكه. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ۲/۲۵۷، كتاب الزكاة، سعيد).

See further:

حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح: ۷۱٤، قديمي. والبحر الرائق: ۲/۳۵۳، كوئته).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

If the money is not handed to the poor man, but a house is built with that money and given to him, there is nothing disliked in it, because he has not become an owner of the nisab through it. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/290).

Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil states:

Suppose a man did not set the zakat money apart from his other wealth, but built a house himself out of the combined funds, then worked out how much had been spent on it, and with the intention of zakat made some poor, homeless person its owner, had it registered in that man's name, put him in possession of it, and kept no further connection with it. Building a house in this way is permissible and correct and involves nothing disliked, because the poor man has not thereby been made a wealthy owner of the nisab; he has simply been provided with the house he needed. (Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil: p. 115, Nuʿaymiyyah).
