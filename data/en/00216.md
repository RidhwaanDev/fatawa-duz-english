### TITLE
An objection to women travelling for tablīgh and its answer

### QUESTION
An objection: some people say that it is not correct for women to go out with the Tablīghī Jamāʿat, and they present the noble verse ﴿وقرن في بيوتكن﴾ as evidence for this. What is the answer to it? And what is the correct meaning of the noble verse?

### ANSWER
The meaning of the noble verse ﴿وقرن في بيوتكن﴾ is "وقرن في بيوتكن واخرجن لحاجة ولكن لا تخرجن متبرجات بزينة" — that is, there is permission to go out when there is a need, but going out with one's adornment on display is forbidden. This meaning is evident from the part of the noble verse that immediately follows. And in this age, what greater need can there be than conveying the religion?

Consider what Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

In ﴿وقرن في بيوتكن﴾ women have been obliged to remain in their homes, the apparent sense of which would be that leaving the house is absolutely forbidden and unlawful for women. But, first, the same verse itself indicates by the words "ولا تبرجن" that going out for a need is not absolutely forbidden; what is forbidden is going out in a way that displays adornment. Secondly, the verse of Sūrat al-Aḥzāb that comes later, with its command ﴿يدنين عليهن من جلابيبهن﴾, itself shows that women are permitted to leave the house to some degree, provided that they go out veiled with a burqaʿ or the like.

Besides this, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ himself made clear in a hadith that occasions of need are excepted from this command. Addressing the pure wives, he said: "قد أذن لكنّ أن تخرجن لحاجتكن" (رواه مسلم، رقم: ۲۱۷۰) — that is, you are permitted to leave the house for your need. Then the practice of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ after the revelation of the verse of veiling bears witness to this, that women are permitted to leave their homes on occasions of need: their going with the Prophet ﷺ for Hajj and Umrah is established by sound hadiths, and their going with him on many expeditions is likewise established. Many narrations also establish that the pure wives (may Allah be pleased with them) would leave their homes to visit their parents and others, and would take part in visiting sick relatives, offering condolences and the like. Not only that: even after the passing of the Prophet ﷺ, the pure wives (may Allah be pleased with them) are known to have gone for Hajj and Umrah.

In short, by the indications of the Qur'an, by the practice of the Prophet ﷺ and by the consensus of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), occasions of need are excepted from the sense of the verse ﴿وقرن في بيوتكن﴾. These include the acts of worship of Hajj and Umrah; natural necessities; visiting one's parents and maḥrams and calling on the sick; and likewise, if a woman has no other provision for her maintenance and the necessities of life, going out to work and earn while observing the veil. The condition for going out on occasions of need, however, is that they should not go out with their adornment on display, but in a burqaʿ or with a jilbāb (a large sheet). (معارف القرآن: ۷/۱۳۳ ۔ ۱۳۵، ادارۃ المعارف، کراچی) ۔

Rūḥ al-Maʿānī states:

وهو لا ينافي لخروجهن للحج أو لما فيه مصلحة دينية مع التستر وعدم الابتذال. (روح المعاني: ۲۲/۹، القاهرة).

Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr states:

فلا تخرجن بغير حاجة. (تفسير ابن كثير: ۳/۵۳۱، بيروت)

Al-Tafsīr al-Badīʿ states:

فلا تخرجن بغير حاجة شرعية. (التفسير البديع، تحت الآية: وقرن في بيوتكن، رحيم يارخان).
