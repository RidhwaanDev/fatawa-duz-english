### TITLE
Exchanging the currencies of different countries

### QUESTION
If someone was given 500 rand and it was said that after three months he will have to give me so many dollars in exchange for it, is this form permissible or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question the currency is not a real medium of price but a conventional one; moreover, the currencies of different countries are termed different in genus. Therefore mutual taking of possession in the session of the contract is not necessary, and it is permissible on a deferred basis as well.

Consider what Fiqhī Maqālāt states:

It is customary among traders and among people generally to give the currency of one country to another person on the condition that in return for it he will hand over the currency of such-and-such a country at such-and-such a place after a stated period. In the view of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) this transaction is permissible, because in his view it is not a condition, in the sale of media of price, that the price be in the ownership of the contracting party at the time of the sale; therefore, when the two genera differ, dealing on credit is permissible. Thus Shams al-Aʾimmah al-Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

وإذا اشتري الرجل فلوساً بدراهم ونقد الثمن ولم تكن الفلوس عند البائع فالبيع جائز لأن الفلوس الرائجة كالنقود، وقد بينا أن حكم العقد في الثمن وجوبها ووجودها معا ولا يشترط قيامها في ملك بائعها لصحة العقد كما لا يشترط ذلك في الدراهم والدنانير. (المبسوط للإمام السرخسي رحمه الله: ۱٤/۲٤، باب البيع بالفلوس، إدارة القرآن).

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

Since the currencies of two countries fall under different genera — which is also why their names, units and the like differ — when the genus of the two is different it is permissible to sell the currency of one country for the currency of another with a difference in amount, and it is permissible to trade in this as well. It is, however, necessary that in the session of the contract one of the two parties take possession of the counter-currency. If neither party took possession of the counter-currency in the session of the contract, but rather both parties concluded the transaction and separated upon a promise of payment later, then this is not permissible, because it entails separating upon a debt for a debt, which is forbidden in the light of the hadith:

قال العلامة برهان الدين المرغيناني: وإذا عدم الوصفان الجنس والمعني المضموم إليه حل التفاضل والنساء لعدم العلة المحرمة والأصل فيه الإباحة وإذا وجد حرم التفاضل والنساء لوجود العلة وإذا وجد أحدهما وعدم الآخر حل التفاضل وحرم النساء. (الهداية: ۳/۷۹، باب الربا، دار الفكر). (Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām: 1/139).
