### TITLE
The retirement policy

### QUESTION
During his lifetime the deceased, of his own choice, nominated his wife for his life insurance and retirement policy. Now, after his death, the insurance company will pay his wife a large sum, and a monthly pension will also continue to be received. Is this transaction permissible or not?

### ANSWER
A life insurance and retirement policy in which money is received in exchange for money, along with an additional sum, is an interest-based transaction and is impermissible; it is binding and necessary to keep away from it. But if someone had entered into such a contract by mistake, or through ignorance, or through sinfulness, then whatever exceeds the sum paid in should be given away in charity without the intention of reward. The sum that was paid in will then, after the death of the deceased, be distributed among all his heirs in the manner laid down by the sharia. As for the company's monthly pension, it comes under the ruling of a gratuity, and there is room for it.

Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil states:

Prohibition is found in life insurance in three respects.

(1) An additional sum is received by way of interest upon the money deposited in it, so in reality it falls under ribā and interest.

(2) Life insurance, in respect of its very form and shape, is qimār and gambling, because it is not known when the person insured will die and how much additional money will be received; and if, through some compulsion, the paying in of the money is discontinued, the whole of the sum previously deposited is forfeited. All these matters belong to the category of suspending ownership upon risk, which in the sharia is called qimār and gambling.

(3) A man's life and limbs are not among things of assessable value, and a thing of no assessable value never has a consideration; and if a consideration were supposedly fixed for it, it would not be a consideration but would in form be a bribe, and a bribe too is forbidden by the sacred texts and a cause of punishment. Therefore, on account of these grounds of prohibition, life insurance is absolutely impermissible and forbidden, and there is no ground for its permissibility in the Islamic sharia. (Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil, p. 93, citing Imdād al-Fatāwā: 3/161; Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 2/200; Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 6/308; Kifāyat al-Muftī: 8/76; Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥall: 6/255; Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/260; Niẓām al-Fatāwā: 1/192).

Majmūʿah Qawānīn-i Islāmī states:

In transactions such as the provident fund, life insurance and a cooperative society, if a person has, in accordance with their regulations, nominated another for his money, then the nominee — whether he is an heir or not an heir — will have the status neither of a legatee nor of a donee, but only of a trustee. Accordingly, the money deposited and deducted belonging to the person who made the nomination will, after his death, be distributed among his heirs according to the shares laid down by the sharia, and it will be necessary to give the interest money to the poor without the intention of reward. Likewise, if the life or the property of the person who made the nomination is lost, the money received as compensation will be distributed among his heirs according to the shares laid down by the sharia. (Majmūʿah Qawānīn-i Islāmī, compiled by Mawlānā Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām Qāsimī (may Allah have mercy on him), p. 255, section 10).
