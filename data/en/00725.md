### TITLE
Where a sharia journey begins when two settlements have merged through growth

### QUESTION
These days, in some places, two settlements become joined together because of population growth. In such a case, from where is the journey reckoned — from one's own settlement, or from the adjoining settlement?

### ANSWER
In the case described, even though the second settlement adjoins the first, if the two have separate names and separate boundaries have been fixed for each, then both count as independent settlements, and the sharia journey is reckoned from the point at which a person passes beyond his own settlement. But if they have been joined in such a way that the government has made them one and the second settlement serves as a district of the first, then it is part of the city, and so the rulings of a sharia journey will only apply once a person passes beyond it. Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

من خرج من عمارة موضع إقامته من جانب خروجه وإن لم يجاوز من الجانب الآخر. وفي الشامي: ويشترط مفارقة ما كان من توابع موضع الإقامة كربض المصر وهو ما حول المدينة من بيوت ومساكن فإنه في حكم المصر وكذا القري المتصلة بالربض في الصحيح بخلاف البساتين ولو متصلة بالبناء لا نها ليست من البلدة ولو سكنها أهل البلدة في جميع السنة. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ۲/۱۲۱، باب صلاة المسافر، سعيد).

وكذا في الهداية: ۱/۱٦٦. والبحر الرائق: ۲/۱۲۸. وحاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح: ص ٤۲۳، باب صلاة المسافر، قديمي. والفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۳۹. وشرح منية المصلي: ص٥۳٦. والفقه الإسلامي وأدلته: ۳۲٤، دار الفكر)

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

A person is called a traveller in the sharia sense once he is outside the built-up area of his home town or of his place of temporary residence. The other settlement may adjoin it, but it is still a different settlement: the two have separate names, and the government and the corporation — that is, the municipality (nagar pālikā) — have fixed separate boundaries for the two settlements. So the two count as two independent settlements, that is, two cities, and a person is called a traveller in the sharia sense once he passes beyond the boundary of his own settlement, that is, his city. But if, because the two adjoin one another, the corporation has made them one, then that settlement is now a district of the city, and that district is part of the city; so the rulings of travel will now apply only once a person passes beyond it. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 6/364; Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/74; and Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband: 4/472, Mudallal wa Mukammal, Dār al-Ishāʿat).
