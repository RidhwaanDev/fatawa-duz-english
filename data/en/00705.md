### TITLE
Prostration of recitation and reward for listening to the Qur'an on a tape recorder

### QUESTION
While listening to the Noble Qur'an on a tape recorder or a CD player, a verse of prostration is recited. What is the ruling for the listener — does he have to prostrate? And if the prostration is not necessary, does he still earn reward for listening to that recitation?

### ANSWER
For the prostration of recitation to become necessary upon a listener, he must hear a valid recitation, and a valid recitation only comes about through reason and discernment. A tape recorder or a CD player is nothing more than a device that transmits sound. The prostration of recitation is therefore not necessary. A parallel to this is found in the words of the jurists, who write that prostration does not become necessary on hearing a verse from birds or from an echo. As for reward, that is achieved simply by the words of Allah reaching one's ears, and it is earned by staying silent and listening with due respect. Consider what Imdād al-Fattāḥ states:

ولا تجب بسماعها من الطيور والصدى

لأن السبب سماع تلاوة صحيحة وصحة التلاوة بالتمييز ولم يوجد. وفي الخلاصة: إذا سمعها من طير لا تجب هو المختار. (إمداد الفتاح، ص: ٥۳۱، ط: بيروت. وكذا في التجنيس والمزيد: ۲/۱۳۷، باب في سجود التلاوة).

Consider now the evidence that reward is earned for listening to recitation from a tape recorder:

قال الله تعالى: ﴿وإذا قرئ القرآن فاستمعوا له وأنصتوا لعلكم ترحمون﴾.

This noble verse shows that whenever the Qur'an is recited it should be listened to, whoever the reciter may be. Allah did not say "وإذا قرأتم". This shows that reward is earned by listening from a tape recorder as well.

ʿAllāmah Shaykh Muḥammad Bakhīt al-Muṭīʿī al-Ḥanafī, Muftī of the Egyptian lands, writes in his treatise "أحكام قراءة الفونوغراف" (ص: ۹):

ومتي علمت أن ما يسمع من الصندوق من ألفاظ القرآن قرآن حقيقة وهو كلام الله بلا شك وأن صدوره منه وسماعه كصدوره من الإنسان وسماعه فإذا صدرت الكلمات القرآنية من ذلك الصندوق مستوفية للشروط بدون أن يكون بها خلل وقصد من رسم مخارج تلك الكلمات في الأسطوانات سماعها للعظة والتدبير فلا شك في الجواز وفي أن السماع عبادة.

Regarding the prostration of recitation he says:

ولا يجب ولا يسن على سماع آية السجدة من الصندوق سجود التلاوة لعدم القصد والشعور لا لأن المسموع ليس آية من القرآن ألا تري أنه لو سمع الآية من شجرة لا يجب سجود التلاوة وإن كان المسموع قرآناً وكذلك لو سمعها من المأموم عند الحنفية لا يجب السجود لأن المأموم محجور عليه في القراءة فعدم الوجوب للحجر على القاري لا لكون المسموع ليس قرآناً وبالجملة: فوجوب سجود التلاوة عند سماع آية السجود أو شبهه ذلك يتوقف على شروط بعد كون المسموع قرآناً، هذا ما رأيناه في ذلك والله الموفق. (أحكام قراءة الفونوغراف، ص: ۱۰).

Fatāwā Bayyināt states:

Since he is hearing the speech of Allah from the cassette, and the greatness of Allah's speech is growing in his heart, and he is keeping his ears safe from other sinful things, he will certainly be rewarded for listening to the recorded recitation. (Fatāwā Bayyināt, vol. 4, p. 422).

Furthermore, if there is sin in listening to a song from a tape recorder, there ought likewise to be reward in listening to recitation from it. Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Yūsuf Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote that there is no reward in listening to recitation from a tape recorder; this is his own view, and it is beyond our understanding. See (Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal: 3/228–231).

Mawlānā Khālid Sayfullāh says:

When recitation comes from a tape recorder, two questions arise here. First, does it carry the same etiquette as listening directly to a reciter? Second, if a verse of prostration is recited in this way, does prostration become necessary because of it?

The etiquette of listening to the Qur'an applies to every situation in which the words of Allah reach a Muslim's ears, whether from the tongue of the reciter himself or through some other medium. So the etiquette of listening — keeping silent and giving the Noble Qur'an one's attention — is required when listening to the Qur'an from a tape recorder too; and since it is on this very basis that the listener is rewarded, he will, if Allah wills, receive that reward as well.

As for the prostration of recitation, it requires that one hear the recitation from the reciter himself, with his tongue moving at that moment. The recitation of a tape recorder is not the reciter's own recitation; rather, the sound waves produced by his tongue are stored, and then the sound is generated within the device by other means. So the prostration of recitation does not become necessary from it. The parallel to this is the explicit ruling of the jurists that prostration of recitation does not become necessary from trained birds or from the echo produced by a reverberation. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/170).

If, however, a reciter's recitation is being broadcast live on radio or television, then the prostration of recitation is necessary.

For more, see (Fatāwā Bayyināt: 4/421–424).
