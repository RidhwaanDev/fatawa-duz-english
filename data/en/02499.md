### TITLE
Eating at a table and on a chair

### QUESTION
What is the ruling on eating at a table and on a chair? Is it disliked in the lesser sense, or in the graver sense, or is it permitted? Please explain with evidence.

### ANSWER
The temper of the pure Sharia in matters of this kind is one of latitude and ease; excessive formality and a severe attitude are not the way of the Sharia. On that basis, eating at a table and on a chair is permissible and correct. Care should be taken, however, not to lean back against the chair; rather one should bend towards the food and eat humbly. Nevertheless, eating while seated on the ground was the practice of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), so in following him one should make eating on the ground one's regular habit.

Note what the noble hadith says:

عن قتادة عن أنس رضي الله عنه قال: ما علمت النبي ﷺ أكل على سكرجة قط ولا خبز له مرقق قط ولا أكل على خوان قط قيل: لقتادة فعلام كانوا يأكلون قال: على السفر[ السفرة ما يوضع عليها الطعام جلداً كان أو غيره]. (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۵۳۸٦، باب الخبز المرقق والأكل على الخوان والسفرة).
وعن سعيد بن جبير رحمه الله قال: سمعت ابن عباس رضي الله عنه يقول: أهدت خالتي أم حفيد إلى رسول الله ﷺ سمناً وأقطاً وأضباً فأكل من السمن والأقط وترك الضب تقذراً وأكل على مائدة رسول الله ﷺ

الخ. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۱۵۱، ط: فيصل).
وعن يزيد بن الأصم قال: دعانا عروس بالمدينة

أن رسول الله ﷺ بينما هو عند ميمونة رضي الله عنه وعنده الفضل بن عباس رضي الله عنه وخالد بن الوليد رضي الله عنه وامرأة أخري إذا قرب إليهم خوان عليه لحم

الخ. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۱۵۱، ط: فيصل).
قال الإمام النووي رحمه الله: وليس المراد بهذا الخوان ما نفاه في الحديث المشهور في قوله: ما أكل رسول الله ﷺ على خوان قط بل شيء من نحو السفرة. (الشرح الكامل: ۲/۱۵۱).
وعن حميد أنه سمع أنساً يقول: قام النبي ﷺ يبني بصفية فدعوت المسلمين إلى وليمة أمر بالأنطاع فبسطت فألقي عليها التمر والأقط والسمن. (رواه البخاري، رقم: ۵۳۸۷، باب الخبز المرقق والأكل على الخوان والسفرة).

The upshot of the hadiths quoted above is that spreading a cloth on the ground and eating was the practice of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), for the narrator of most of these reports is Anas (may Allah be pleased with him), who was his personal attendant and had the honour of serving him for about ten years; he was therefore thoroughly acquainted with the Prophet's domestic life.

That said, no narration has come to notice in which the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) forbade eating at a table, so eating in this manner will also be permissible; but one should avoid eating while reclining. Zād al-Maʿād states:

صح عنه أنه قال: "لا آكل متكئاً"، وقال: "إنما أجلس كما يجلس العبد، وآكل كما يأكل العبد". (زاد المعاد: ٤/۲۲۰، ط: مؤسسة الرسالة).

Takmilah Fatḥ al-Mulhim states:

إن المرأ ينبغي له أن يجلس على الطعام جلوساً متواضعاً ويجتنب هيئة المتكبرين. (تكملة فتح الملهم: ٤/٤۸، باب استحباب تواضع الآكل، وصفة قعوده).

Objection: some people call eating at a table disliked and present the following narration as evidence:

عن قتادة عن أنس رضي الله عنه قال: ما علمت النبي ﷺ أكل على سكرجة قط ولا خبز له مرقق قط ولا أكل على خوان قط قيل: لقتادة فعلام كانوا يأكلون قال: على السفر. (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۵۳۸٦، باب الخبز المرقق والأكل على الخوان والسفرة).

In this narration they take *khiwān* to mean a table.

Answer: the word *khiwān* carries several meanings.

(1) Al-ʿAynī says in ʿUmdat al-Qārī:

قال العيني في العمدة: وهو طبق كبير من نحاس تحته كرسي من نحاس ملزوق به، طوله قدر ذراع يرص فيه الزبادي ويوضع بين يدي كبير من المترفين ولا يحمله إلا اثنان فما فوقهما. (عمدة القاري: ۱٤/۳۹۵، ط: دار الحديث، ملتان).

That is, a large brass platter with a brass stand attached beneath it, about a cubit high, on which bowls are arranged; it is placed before some great man among the affluent and cannot be carried by fewer than two people.

For further detail see: (Shamāʾil-e Kubrā: 1/39–41, under the heading "Eating at a table is contrary to the sunnah", published by Zamzam).

(2) Shāh Ṣāḥib, in Fayḍ al-Bārī, did not explain *khiwān* to mean a table. Note:

والخوان هو الصيني من خشب، وليس بطاولة ولا بمنضدة. (فيض الباري: ٤/۳۳۳).

That is, the *khiwān* is a wooden tray — a *khwāncha*, which some people also call a *kashtī* — and it is neither a table (*ṭāwilah*) nor a stand (*minḍadah*).

Al-Nihāyah states:

خوان هو ما يوضع عليه الطعام عند الأكل. (النهاية: ۲/۱۸۳، ).

The dictionaries too translate *khiwān* simply as an eating spread. See: (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/487).

Most authorities say that once food is present on the spread it is a *māʾidah*, while so long as no food has been placed on it, it is called a *khiwān*.

Fiqh al-Lughah states:

ولا يقال مائدة إلا إذا كان عليها طعام وإلا فهي خوان. (فقه اللغة: ٦۲، وكذا في لسان العرب).

It is thus clear that the meaning of *khiwān* as "table" is not established. Indeed, even if that were its established meaning, the narration in Ibn Mājah records that a *khiwān* was present in the house of Anas (may Allah be pleased with him). This shows that in his view eating at a *khiwān* or table was permissible, and that in the Prophet's era the *khiwān* was simply absent, not forbidden. Elaborate tables, however, are a thing of the wealthy and should be avoided. Note:

عن قتادة قال: كنا نأتي أنس بن مالك رضي الله عنه (قال إسحاق وخبازه قائم وقال الدارمي وخوانه موضوع) فقال: يوماً كلوا، فما اعلم رسول الله ﷺ رأي رغيفاً مرققاً بعينه حتى لحق بالله

الخ. (رواه ابن ماجه، وإسناده صحيح، رقم ۳۳۳۹).

Even if *khiwān* in the narration quoted above did mean a table, all that the narration shows is that its use was absent — that the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) was not given to elaborate arrangements, and this is such an arrangement, so he did not use it. Despite that, it is not established that he forbade its use.

Moreover, the narration of Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) quoted above mentions other things as well which nobody holds to be disliked.

Objection (2): some senior scholars (may Allah have mercy on them) forbade eating at a table and on a chair on the grounds of resembling the disbelievers. What is the answer to this? See: (Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal: 8/382, new edition; Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 18/79, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah; Imdād al-Fatāwā: 4/266; Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 8/121; and others).

Answer: Ḥakīm al-Ummah Mawlānā Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) says that once something becomes widespread, no suspicion of imitation remains in it, provided there is no intention of self-admiration or pride. Furthermore, since such a thing was present in the house of Anas (may Allah be pleased with him), it is no longer a case of resembling the disbelievers.

Note what he writes in Imdād al-Fatāwā under the question of riding a bicycle:

Although outwardly a suspicion of imitation may attach to this conveyance, on reflection its having become widespread removes that suspicion. The only remaining possible objection is that it gives rise to self-admiration; the implication of that is that there is no harm in it for one who is free of self-admiration. However, certain permissible things are unbecoming for the pious and the trustworthy, so abstaining is preferable where the matter is merely one of convenience and not of necessity; and if there is a genuine need, then in that case it is not even contrary to what is preferable. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 4/267).

Faqīh al-Ummah Muftī Maḥmūd Ḥasan Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

(Eating at a table) This practice is contrary to the sunnah. Where it is a distinguishing mark of the disbelievers and the openly sinful, it is entirely forbidden; where it is not such a mark but is widespread, so that the righteous also follow this practice, the ruling there is not so severe — indeed it is somewhat lighter — but it is still contrary to the sunnah. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 18/79, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

Today this practice is widespread among the righteous as well.

Mawlānā Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) says the following about the definition of imitation and how to recognise that imitation has ceased:

The criterion is this: where something is the distinctive fashion of a particular group and it is known that this thing belongs to the disbelievers, and the mind turns to something characteristic of the disbelievers, then it is imitation; otherwise it is not. (Ḥusn al-ʿAzīz, p. 213).

The way to recognise it is that on seeing these things it does not strike ordinary people that "this fashion was started by such-and-such people." But so long as that distinctive association remains, the practice will be forbidden; and once the association is gone, the prohibition too will not remain. (Ḥayāt al-Muslimīn, p. 224).

Fiqh-e Ḥanafī ke Uṣūl-o-Ḍawābiṭ states:

Resembling the disbelievers in matters of religion is unlawful, and in national insignia it is disliked in the graver sense. In the remaining matters of inventions and administrative arrangements it is permissible; that is not really imitation at all. (Fiqh-e Ḥanafī ke Uṣūl-o-Ḍawābiṭ, p. 152).

It is therefore clear that eating on a chair and at a table is not forbidden and disliked without qualification. It is forbidden and disliked where it involves resembling the disbelievers and the openly sinful, or where there is an intention of self-admiration and pride; but if it is free of both these factors, there is no doubt about its being permissible and allowed. In our times it is in general use, without being peculiar to any one sect or people, and on that basis there is room for using it. Eating while seated on the ground, however, is a perpetual sunnah, and adopting it is a proof of love.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
