### TITLE
Answers to certain scholars' objections to a fatwa of Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā concerning digital photographs

### QUESTION
Answers to certain scholars' objections to a fatwa of Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā concerning digital photographs.

### ANSWER
All the scholars and muftis of the present age are agreed that there is room for taking and having made a photograph for an identity card, an ID, a passport, and employment and educational documents. Then, whatever kind of journey is undertaken with a passport — whether it be the journey of the obligatory Hajj, or the journey of a supererogatory Hajj or ʿumrah, or a journey to visit someone, or a journey for sightseeing — every kind of journey is included in this. Now, if a passport and the like is obtained for a merely permissible or a sightseeing or a recommended journey, an objection arises: how has something unlawful or impermissible been tolerated for the sake of a recommended or merely permissible act? Accordingly, in the view of those who hold this to be a real picture, one would have to pass through an unlawful or impermissible act for the sake of a recommended act, which is not correct in the Sacred Law. Some muftis, however, have found this solution: that a real picture is the likening of what is manufactured to what is created (tashbīh al-maṣnūʿ bi'l-makhlūq), which is supported by those hadiths in which the wording of likening occurs explicitly in connection with pictures; whereas the photograph in current use is the capturing of the shadow of a creature (ḥabs ẓill al-makhlūq), and so this photograph is not a real picture but a resemblance of a picture. Therefore, if there is no harm in it from the point of view of the Sacred Law, and there is a religious benefit in it, and there is need and necessity, and it is not for self-indulgence or for the pleasure of looking, and there is no aspect of illicit glances in it, then in the view of some muftis there is room for it in the present age.

It should be borne in mind that saying that it is not a real picture does not mean giving free rein to such photographs. One must therefore look at whether there is harm in it from the point of view of the Sacred Law; and if there is such harm, then this resemblance of a picture ought to be entirely forbidden — as when people generally take pointless photographs, or take photographs of mixed gatherings of men and women, or when photographs are taken of some ordinary assembly or function. And if there is a religious benefit in it, then in the view of some scholars this act will be permissible; for example, there will be room for taking a photograph for the journey of a supererogatory Hajj or ʿumrah, or for a journey abroad to visit someone. My purpose in quoting this statement of some scholars is not to deny that it is harmful; my purpose is rather to seek a way of escaping the threats that have come in the hadiths. For example:

أشد الناس عذاباً يوم القيمة المصورون، يقال لهم أحيوا ما خلقتم" (مسند أحمد، رقم: ٤۷۹۲، قال الشيخ شعيب: صحيح وهذا اسناد ضعيف لضعف عاصم بن عبيد الله بن عاصم بن عمر)

That is: those who will suffer the severest punishment are the picture-makers; they will be told: bring to life what you have made.

أشد الناس عذاباً يوم القيامة الذين يضاهون بخلق الله، (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۵۹۵٤، باب ما وطئ من التصاوير، وصحيح مسلم، رقم: ۲۱۰۷)، من صور صورة في الدنيا كلف يوم القيامة أن ينفخ فيها الروح، (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۵۹٦۳، باب من صور صورة، وصحيح مسلم، رقم: ۲۱۱۰)

This was a device for saving people from these threats. Otherwise, if the angels should ask why you took upon yourself so great a threat for the sake of a merely permissible and recommended journey, then this answer is easy: that this is not a picture in the sense of the Sacred Law — that is, I did not make something manufactured to resemble a creature of God, but rather preserved the shadow of Allah Most High's creature by pressing a button; and there is a religious benefit in it and no harm, or it is a way of exercising the right to travel. But if a photograph of some gathering is taken to no purpose, then even if it is given the name of a keepsake it is still not correct.

The religious programmes in which the honourable scholars permit photography with the modern camera include, besides the supererogatory Hajj and ʿumrah, government documents and certificates and the installing of cameras for identifying thieves. Moreover, in some countries mountains of oppression are being brought down upon the Muslims, and by publishing photographs of them a voice is raised against the oppressors and the way is made smooth for helping the oppressed; through such photographs the Muslim and non-Muslim nations of the world turn to their assistance, and the oppressed are given refuge. In volume seven of Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā we wrote, in the light of the statements of certain honourable muftis, that there is room for it in religious programmes. By that is meant a religious programme of this kind, and not ordinary religious assemblies and processions, in the photographing of which there is no benefit but rather harm. Our earlier writing should be read in the light of the present one.

I have also heard that if a man from certain countries such as Pakistan or India is resident in America or Canada and his marriage takes place in his own country of Pakistan or India, those countries will not grant the girl a visa until there is a video recording of the marriage gathering. Likewise, people give donations to certain relief organisations, and those organisations then distribute foodstuffs and other things among the afflicted, and photographs are taken of this so that the donors may be satisfied that their donations are being spent in the right place. Likewise, the authorities of some African countries complain that wealthy Muslims spend their charity in their own countries in Asia and do not spend it in Africa, where they reside; so photographs of the distribution of charity among the Africans satisfy them that work is being done there too. Those Deobandi scholars, then, who hold digital photographs to be permitted, or hold that there is room for them, hold this in cases such as these. They do not hold it for photographs taken for pleasure, or keepsake photographs, or photographs of every religious assembly and procession, or photographs of mixed gatherings, or photographs of some gathering taken merely for the sake of applause.

To come to the point: a religious scholar of the province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa in Pakistan published a treatise on the prohibition of pictures, adorned with the signatures of various scholars and muftis, and in it he has also made certain passages of our Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā the target of criticism. This treatise was sent to this humble servant by its compiler during the ʿīd al-aḍḥā holidays, at a time when I had my foot in the stirrup for a journey to South Africa. This critical essay reached me through our friend and relative Mawlānā Maḥbūb Sulṭān (may his blessings endure). I kept the critical essay by me and thought that if there were anything acceptable in it I would accept it, and that if the essay called for a reply I would write a brief reply. This essay lay by for a good many days, and I kept wondering whether or not to write an answer to some of its points. Sometimes it came to my mind that it would be better not to write, because Ḥālī composed this verse long ago:

Ḥālī, do not you meddle in the quarrels of the people of religion;

this affair will not be settled by your good self.

Then it came to my mind that this essay of the Mawlānā's was giving rise to certain misunderstandings about me — that I regard every kind of photograph as correct, whereas it is not so, as I have explained — and moreover that some useful matters would come before the readers in a written reply. I therefore resolved to write a brief answer. Before the answer, let me submit this: since in the Mawlānā's view committing something unlawful is permissible for a recommended or merely permissible matter — as, for instance, that it is permissible to give a bribe in order to obtain one's own right, the obtaining of which is not obligatory — then if the members of some committee should invite a certain Mawlānā to give a lecture and should also tell him that photography would take place there, then, according to the compiler, that Mawlānā ought not to refuse to go, because an assembly is a recommended matter, that is,

ما يكون فعله أولي من تركه. (حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح، ص ۷۵، فصل من آداب الوضوء، قديمي، وفتاوى الشامي: ۱/۱۰۲، سعيد، Dhikr-e Ijtimāʿī Jahrī Sharīʿat ke Āʾīnah mein, p. 263, published by Zamzam),

so that, according to the Mawlānā, committing what is impermissible ought to be permissible for its sake — whereas the Mawlānā does not hold this. And the passages of the jurists are not merely arguments ad hominem but arguments of fact as well; so how can the committing of something unlawful ever be permissible for the sake of a sunnah or a recommended act?

We now come to the main point. The rule or principle that we stated — that committing something unlawful is not permissible for the sake of a recommended or a sunnah act — has been mentioned by a number of jurists. The manuscript of Khizānat al-Riwāyāt records:

في الذخيرة: أن المسلم يختتن ما لم يبلغ فإذا بلغ لم يختن لأن ستر عورة البالغ فرض والختان سنة فلا يترك الفرض للسنة والكافر إذا أسلم يختن بالاتفاق لمخالفته دين الإسلام وهو بالغ. (خزانة الروايات، ص ۳٦۱، مخوطة)،

A photocopy of the manuscript of Khizānat al-Riwāyāt is present in the library of our Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā in South Africa, and this passage has also been quoted by Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy Lakhnawī (may Allah Most High have mercy on him) in Majmūʿat al-Fatāwā. In the Urdu translation of Majmūʿat al-Fatāwā the passage runs thus: Question: Is circumcision necessary for an adult who has not been circumcised, or for an unbeliever who has become a Muslim, or not? Answer: Circumcision is not obligatory; rather, according to the more correct view, it is an emphasised sunnah. There is therefore no need for the circumcision of a grown man. However, if an adult has himself circumcised, or buys a slave-woman who performs circumcisions, or marries a woman who performs circumcisions and has himself circumcised by her, then it is permissible; and an unbeliever who has become a Muslim should be circumcised. (Khizānat al-Riwāyāt, p. 361.) From (Muʿallim al-Fiqh, the Urdu translation of Majmūʿat al-Fatāwā: 2/85, published at Aram Bagh, Karachi.) In this passage of Khizānat al-Riwāyāt the rule is stated explicitly that in order to fulfil a sunnah an obligation will not be abandoned — that is, something unlawful will not be committed. This principle is mentioned in the books of the other schools as well, which we shall mention later, if Allah Most High wills.

2. Reconciliation is better and more commendable than dispute and an outright judgement. Allah Most High says:

﴿والصلح خير﴾

Imām al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) set the chapter heading

باب فضل الإصلاح بين الناس. (۱/۳۷۳)

and he set another chapter heading

باب إذا أشار الإمام بالصلح فأبي حكم عليه بالحكم البين. (۱/۳۷۳)۔

And ʿAllāmah al-ʿAynī has written at the beginning of Bukhārī's Kitāb al-Ṣulḥ:

ذكر هذه الآية في بيان فضل الإصلاح بين الناس وأن الصلح أمر مندوب إليه. (۹/۵۷۲)،

In the Noble Qurʾān reconciliation is called good, and Tafsīr al-Samarqandī states:

والصلح خير من الخصومة، [النساء: ۱۲۸]،

that is, reconciliation is better than litigation. But the jurists have forbidden the telling of a lie for the sake of reconciliation, and have said instead that allusive and indirect expressions should be used, so that something unlawful may not be committed for a recommended act. Sakb al-Anhur (4/221) and و الدر المختار مع رد المحتار (6/427, published by Saʿīd) record:

والكذب حرام إلا في الحرب للخدعة وفي الصلح بين اثنين في إرضاء الأهل وفي دفع الظالم عن الظلم والمراد التعريض لأن عين الكذب حرام،

In this passage an outright lie and the committing of what is unlawful are forbidden for the sake of a good work such as reconciliation; and where some authorities do permit an outright lie, that is either a weaker view or applies to saving an oppressed person from being killed.

3. Imām al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) has cited those narrations in which there is a prohibition against praying with one's clothes and hair gathered up; yet along with this, if a man's garment is short, there is an instruction to gather it up and pray, because uncovering the private parts is unlawful — so an unlawful act will not be committed in order to complete a sunnah or a recommended act. That is why Imām al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) set the chapter heading باب عقد الثياب. Ḥaḍrat Shaykh al-Ḥadīth Mawlānā Muḥammad Zakariyyā (may Allah have mercy on him) has written in his commentary on the chapter headings:

والغرض من عقد هذا الباب ههنا أن ما سيأتي من النهي عن كف الثياب محمول على الأمن من الكشف أما إذا خاف كشف العورة فلا بد من عقدها، لأن الفرض أهم من المستحبات. (الأبواب والتراجم لصحيح البخاري، ص 95، ط: سعيد).

And ʿAllāmah al-ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written:

أن في ضم الثوب أمناً من كشف العورة. (عمدة القاري: ٤/۵٦۱، ط: دار الحديث، ملتان)،

In this passage ʿAllāmah al-ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) and Shaykh al-Ḥadīth Mawlānā Zakariyyā (may Allah have mercy on him) have plainly stated this rule: that covering the private parts is obligatory and therefore uncovering them is unlawful, and hence an unlawful act will not be done in order to complete a recommended one.

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

الصلح جائز بين المسلمين إلا صلحاً حرم حلالاً أو أحل حراماً

إسناده صحيح. (سنن الترمذي، رقم ۱۳۵۲، باب ما ذكر عن رسول الله ﷺ في الصلح بين المسلمين)،

The jurists have cited this hadith in various places in their books. The meaning of this hadith is that every reconciliation is permissible except a reconciliation by which the unlawful becomes lawful or the reverse — that is, that the unlawful should not be made lawful for the sake of reconciliation; or, in other words, that something unlawful should not be committed.

Objections and answers. To come to the point: the Mawlānā who compiled the fatwas about pictures has raised this objection against the writing of Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: that uncovering the private parts for the circumcision of an adult is the committing of something unlawful for the sake of a sunnah — in other words, that this principle is not correct, that an unlawful act should not be committed for the sake of a recommended or sunnah act.

The answer to this objection is that adults are of two kinds. 1. A new Muslim adult: his circumcision is necessary in any case, unless he cannot bear it, in which case it will be omitted. Since he has abandoned unbelief and entered Islam, it is necessary for him to abandon the marks of unbelief and to emerge entirely from the shell of unbelief; no mark of unbelief should remain upon him. Circumcision is therefore emphasised in his case, and the jurists are agreed upon his being circumcised, so that this new Muslim may display complete disavowal of the marks of unbelief. Circumcision is therefore necessary for him. That is to say, here the sunnah is joined by the symbols of Islam and the removal of the marks of unbelief, and for that reason circumcision became necessary and the uncovering of the private parts became permissible — just as, if the people of a settlement abandon the call to prayer, they will be fought, since the call to prayer, besides being an emphasised sunnah, is included among the symbols of Islam, so that abandoning it entails fighting. The new Muslim is commanded by:

﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا ادخلوا في السلم كافة﴾ [البقرة: ۲۰۸]،

He should not act as certain new Muslims did, who said: We shall act on the law of Moses and give up the flesh of the camel, and we shall also honour the Sabbath day, and we shall also act on the commands of Islam. Concerning them this verse:

﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا ادخلوا في السلم كافة﴾ [البقرة: ۲۰۸]،

was revealed. This is the summary of a fatwa in Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah. However, if a non-Muslim has not the strength to bear circumcision, then circumcision will be omitted:

الشيخ الضعيف إذا أسلم ولا يطيق الختان إن قال أهل البصر لا يطيق يترك لأن ترك الواجب بالعذر جائز فترك السنة أولي كذا في الخلاصة. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳۵۷).

In sum, if a new Muslim can endure it, his circumcision is necessary. And if a man born a Muslim was not circumcised during his minority, will he be circumcised now that he has reached maturity or not? On this there are two views. 1. One view is that he will not be circumcised; but if he can circumcise himself, or his wife can do it, then he will be circumcised, otherwise not. This view has been adopted by the author of al-Dhakhīrah, the author of Khizānat al-Riwāyāt, Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him) and others; and the proof they have given for it is that the uncovering of the private parts and the committing of something unlawful will not be undertaken for the sake of a sunnah. The Mālikīs too have adopted this same view.

Ḥāshiyat al-ʿAdawī ʿalā Sharḥ Kifāyat al-Ṭālib al-Rabbānī records:

والنظر لعورة الكبير المراهق أو البالغ حرام لقول اللخمي المناهز ككبير ولا يرتكب محرماً لفعل سنة ويظهر أنه يؤمر بختن نفسه لأن المكلف مأمور بفعل ما تم له إسلامه. (۱/۵۹٦، دار الفكر)،

And Ashal al-Madārik Sharḥ Irshād al-Sālik fī Madhhab Imām al-Aʾimmah Mālik (3/364, Dār al-Fikr) records:

أما الكبير فإنه يومر باختتان نفسه إن أمكن كمن أسلم بعد البلوغ بحرمة نظر عورة البالغ،

اور بلغة السالك لا قر ب المسالك states:

ولا يجوز للبالغ أن يكشف عورته لغيره لا جل الختان بل إن لم يمكنه الفعل بنفسه سقطت السنة. (حاشية الصاوي: ۲/۱۵۲، دار المعارف).

However, since in the view of the Shāfiʿīs and the Ḥanbalīs circumcision is obligatory, an adult's uncovering of his private parts for it is correct. Al-Tahdhīb fī Fiqh al-Imām al-Shāfiʿī states:

و يجوز كشف عورة المختون لا جل الختان ولولا وجوبه لما جاز ترك الواجب لأجله. (۱/۲۱۸)،

And Imām al-Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in المجموع شرح المهذب:

فلوكان الختان سنة لما كشفت العورة المحرم كشفها له. (۱/۳۰۰، باب السواك، دار الفكر).

The second view is that an adult will be circumcised. According to the most correct view among the Ḥanafīs circumcision is an emphasised sunnah; but since it is among the symbols of Islam, and the symbols of Islam are annexed to the obligatory duties, circumcision will be performed. Al-Durr al-Mukhtār maʿa al-Shāmī states:

الأصل أن الختان سنة كما جاء في الخبر وهو من شعائر الإسلام، وخصائصه فلو اجتمع أهل بلدة على تركه حاربهم الإمام فلا يترك إلا لعذر وعذر شيخ لا يطيقه ظاهر. (الدر المختار مع فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۷۵۱، مسائل شتي، ط: سعيد).

Imdād al-Aḥkām states: Concerning circumcision the jurists have stated explicitly that it is permissible to circumcise an adult and to look at and touch his body to the extent of necessity; but uncovering beyond what is necessary is not permissible. Therefore no one but the circumciser should look, and the rest of the body should be kept covered even from the circumciser. And circumcision is not merely a sunnah but is also among the symbols of Islam. (Imdād al-Aḥkām, by Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him): 4/428, published by Maktabah Dār al-ʿUlūm, Karachi.)

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states: Although it is not permissible to look at the private parts even of a four-year-old child without need, yet it is permissible to uncover the private parts out of the need of medical treatment; and circumcision is among the symbols of Islam, so uncovering the private parts for it is permissible with all the greater reason. The same ruling applies to an adult and to a new Muslim. However, if he can circumcise himself, or can have it done by his wife or his slave-woman, then uncovering the private parts before another is not permissible. And if for some reason he cannot endure the hardship of circumcision, this ruling will lapse. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 9/61, published by Saʿīd.)

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

In answer to a question about an uncircumcised adult, he said: If such a person cannot circumcise himself, and there are no slave-women nowadays who could perform circumcisions, then he may have himself circumcised. (12/351, published by Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah.)

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states: Circumcision is an emphasised sunnah and is among the symbols of Islam; it does not lapse even in the case of an adult without a severe excuse. (19/466, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah.)

Al-Mabsūṭ, concerning fighting those who abandon the call to prayer, states that the abandoning of anything that is among the symbols of Islam is a dishonouring of the religion, and that fighting will be waged over its abandonment. ʿAllāmah al-Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

وقال محمد: ما كان من أعلام الدين فالإصرار على تركه استخفاف بالدين فيقاتلون على ذلك. (المبسوط للسرخسي: ۱/۱۳۳، ط: إدارة القرآن والعلوم الإسلامية، كراتشي).

Al-Shāmī maʿa al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

والقتال عليه لما أنه من أعلام الدين. (فتاوى الشامي مع الدر: ۱/۳۸٤، سعيد).

In در مختار (1/384) the words وهي كالواجب are written. Moreover, al-Durr al-Mukhtār states in another place:

ينظر الطبيب إلى موضع مرضها بقدر الضرورة إذ الضرورات تتقدر بقدرها، وكذا نظر قابلة وختان، (الدر المختار: ٦/۳۷۰، سعيد)

On this ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

لأن الختان سنة للرجال من جملة الفطرة لا يمكن تركها. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۳۷۱، سعيد).

In sum, committing something unlawful for the sake of a recommended act is not correct; and therefore in the view of some jurists the circumcision of an adult, or of one approaching puberty, will be omitted, while in the view of other jurists he will be circumcised, because circumcision, besides being an emphasised sunnah, is among the symbols of Islam and is annexed to the obligatory duties, and for that reason the uncovering of the private parts was tolerated for it. Al-Shāmī states concerning the abandonment of the call to prayer:

لو اجتمع أهل بلدة على تركه قاتلهم عليه ولو تركه واحد ضربته وحبسته وعامة المشايخ على الأول (أي كونه سنة) والقتال عليه لما أنه من أعلام الدين. (فتاوى الشامي: ۱/۳۸٤، سعيد).

The second matter, from which the Mawlānā who criticises our writing has argued for the committing of something unlawful for the sake of a recommended act, is the giving of a bribe in order to obtain one's own right. The Mawlānā says: A bribe is unlawful and it is obligatory to keep away from it; but for one's own right, the obtaining of which is neither obligatory nor necessary, one may give a bribe:

إذا دفع الرشوة خوفاً على نفسه أو ماله فهو حرام على الآخذ غير حرام على الدافع.

This threat does not apply to one who gives a bribe in order to recover his own right or to save himself from oppression. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 18/463, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah.)

What the Mawlānā means is that the obtaining of one's right is not at all obligatory, and yet giving a bribe for it — that is, committing something unlawful — is permissible; so, according to him, committing something unlawful for a recommended or sunnah act will be correct.

The answer to this is that giving something in order to recover one's own right is outwardly a bribe but is not in reality a bribe, because one definition of a bribe, which is generally well known, is:

الرشوة ما يعطي لإبطال حق أو لا حقاق باطل. (التعريفات للجرجاني، ص ۱۱۱، ط: دار الكتب العلمية).

Wealth given in order to obtain one's own right does not fall under bribery, nor does it come under the heading of the unlawful. ʿAllāmah Ibn ʿĀbidīn al-Shāmī's (may Allah have mercy on him) son, ʿAllāmah ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him), who was a great researching scholar, who was among the compilers and authors of al-Majallah al-ʿAdliyyah and who compiled the Takmilah of al-Shāmī, compiled a useful book for young men whose name is الهدية العلائية. In it the departed says:

دفع المال للحاكم الجائر لدفع الظلم عن نفسه وماله أو استخراج حق له لا يتوصل إليه إلا به ليس برشوة في حق الدافع. (Al-Hadiyyah al-ʿAlāʾiyyah, 219, published by Dār Ibn Ḥazm.)

To give wealth to an unjust ruler in order to repel oppression, or to obtain a right which a man cannot reach without giving wealth, is not a bribe on the part of the giver. Al-Rasāʾil al-Zayniyyah fī al-Madhhab al-Ḥanafiyyah records:

أما الرشوة في اصطلاح الفقهاء فقد عرفها الجرجاني بأنها ما يعطي لا بطال حق أو لا حقاق باطل، وقال النووي رحمه الله: الرشوة محرمة على القاضي وغيره من الولاة مطلقاً لأنها تدفع إليه ليحكم بحق أو ليمتنع من ظلم وكلاهما واجب عليه فلا يجوز أخذ العوض عليه. وأما دافع الرشوة فإن توصل بها إلى باطل فحرام عليه وهو المراد بالراشي وإن توصل بها إلى تحصيل حق ودفع ظلم فليس بحرام. (الرسائل الزينية في مذهب الحنفية، ص ٦۰۵، ط: دار السلام، وفقه المعاملات: ٤/۷۷، والفتاوى الاقتصادية: ۱/۸۸)

The gist of Imām al-Nawawī's (may Allah have mercy on him) words is that it is not permissible for a qadi to take a bribe, because it is binding upon a qadi to give a correct judgement and to save people from oppression, and it is impermissible to take a payment for that. And if the purpose in giving a bribe is to obtain something impermissible, then giving it is unlawful; but if it is to obtain one's own right or to repel oppression, then giving it is not unlawful. There is further detail on this in Fatḥ al-Qadīr, Kitāb al-Ṣulḥ, and in Qurrat ʿAyn al-Akhyār; to avoid prolixity we have omitted the passages of these and other books.

From the above detail and the passages quoted it appears that the wealth given in order to obtain one's own right does not come under bribery at all; and even if it outwardly comes under it, it is nevertheless not unlawful but is excepted from the unlawful. Hence no committing of something unlawful for a non-obligatory purpose is to be found here.

The third objection which the Mawlānā has raised is that medical treatment is a sunnah and covering the private parts is obligatory, yet the jurists have permitted the uncovering of the private parts for the sake of medical treatment, and this is the committing of something unlawful for the sake of a sunnah. He has then quoted references from Faqīh Abū al-Layth and from Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah, Fatāwā Nawāzil and others, to the effect that if someone dies as a result of not eating he will be disobedient and sinful, whereas if someone dies as a result of not taking medical treatment he will not be sinful; and at the end he has drawn the conclusion that an unlawful act was done in order to fulfil a sunnah.

The answer to this is that to call medical treatment a sunnah in every circumstance, and not to call it necessary and obligatory, is open to question. The honourable jurists do indeed write:

قال الفقيه أبو الليث: ولو مرض ولم يعالج حتى مات لم يأثم بخلاف الجائع إذا لم يأكل حتى مات بالجوع يأثم به. (فتاوى النوازل، ص ۲۰۰، كتاب الكراهية، ط: آرام باغ كراتشي).

But the words of the jurists are to be understood as applying to a case in which the success of the treatment is not certain. If, however, the treatment is certain to hit the mark and escape from death is certain or as good as certain, then treatment too is obligatory. Imām al-Ghazālī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn:
