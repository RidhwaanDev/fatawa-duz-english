### TITLE
Development loans do not prevent zakat

### QUESTION
Is a long-term debt deducted from a person's capital, or is zakat due on the whole amount? In other words, does a long-term debt prevent zakat or not?

### ANSWER
In the case of long-term development loans, only the instalment payable in a given year is set aside for that year's zakat, and zakat is due on the remaining wealth. In other words, the whole loan is not deducted, and it does not prevent zakat.

Consider what Ḥāshiyat al-Ṭaḥṭāwī states:

(قوله المؤجل) وقيل المهر المؤجل لا يمنع لأنه غير مطالب به عادة بخلاف المعجل وقيل إن كان الزوج عزم على الأداء منع وإلا فلا، لأنه لا يعد ديناً بحر عن غاية البيان، وفي القهستاني والصحيح أن المؤجل غير مانع كما في الجواهر. (حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر: ۱/۳۹۱).

Shāmī states:

(قوله أو مؤجلا) عزاه في المعراج إلى شرح الطحاوي وقال: وعن أبي حنيفة رحمه الله لا يمنع وقال الصدر الشهيد: لا رواية فيه ولكل من المنع وعدمه وجه، زاد القهستاني عن الجواهر والصحيح أنه غير مانع. (شامي: ۲/۲٦۱، سعيد).

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

وعلى هذا يخرج مهر المرأة فإنه يمنع وجوب الزكاة عندنا معجلاً كان أو مؤجلاً لأنها إذا طالبته يؤاخذ به، وقال بعض مشايخنا: إن المؤجل لا يمنع لأنه غير مطالب به عادة. (بدائع الصنائع: ۲/٦).

Al-Fatāwā al-Tātārkhāniyyah states:

السراجية: الدين المؤجل قال بعضهم: يمنع الزكاة، وذكر مجد الأئمة السرخسي رحمه الله عن مشايخه أنه لا يمنع. (الفتاوى التاتارخانية: ۲/۲۹۲).

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states:

When considering a question of zakat, it is essential to look beyond the individual rulings of the jurists and to keep in view the aim and intent of the sharia and the spirit of the laws of zakat. Plainly, the spirit of the sharia in the matter of zakat is that a person should recognise, for Allah's sake, the right that His poor servants have in his wealth, and should spend on the needy. This is why the jurists laid down the principle that wherever there are grounds both for an obligation and for its absence, preference is given to the side that benefits the poor. Now, the position today is that development loans for trade and business are in common use. They are repayable over a long term in easy instalments. The debtor gains great economic benefit from this money; it does not lie idle in his hands but stays in circulation, and in the language of the jurists it actually has the status of wealth that grows. If such a debt were held to prevent zakat, the poor would be permanently deprived of their right. So, just as the later scholars, in view of changed circumstances, have not treated a woman's deferred dower as preventing zakat, it is entirely appropriate that in long-term investment debts the instalment payable in a given year be exempted from that year's zakat, and that zakat be held obligatory on the remaining wealth. Light is thrown on this by another ruling of the jurists as well, in which a debt owed for a wife's maintenance is not treated as preventing zakat; the reason given is that maintenance does not become due all at once but becomes due bit by bit.
