### TITLE
The ruling on holding a condolence gathering

### QUESTION
What do the honourable scholars say about this: an eminent person passes away, and without fixing any particular day his associates are called together for a gathering whose purpose is to speak about his achievements and to mention his deeds and character, so that those connected with him may walk in his footsteps, keep his way alive and benefit from one another's views. Yes, for the sake of convenience a day may be fixed, but that fixing is only for ease and is not done in the belief that it is a ruling of the sharia. What is the ruling on such a gathering? The aim of the gathering is also to prepare for the situation created by the death of an elder. Moreover, this gathering is not regarded as binding, necessary or a sunnah, so no objection is raised against those who do not hold it.

### ANSWER
When the gathering concerns a person with whom countless religious benefits are bound up, every aspect of whose life deserves to be explained and followed, whose deeds and character need to be spread, whose associates benefit from one another, and whose mission needs to be carried forward, then a day may be fixed for the convenience of those associates. After the passing of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) addressed the gathering that had assembled after the Prophet's ﷺ death, in order to put down countless trials. Support for this is also found in a narration of Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī. Consider it:

عن زياد بن علاقة قال: سمعت جرير بن عبد الله رضي الله عنه يوم مات المغيرة بن شعبة رضي الله عنه قام فحمدالله وأثني عليه وقال عليكم باتقاء الله وحده لا شريك له والوقار والسكينة حتى يأتيكم أمير فإنما يأتيكم الآن ثم قال: استغفروا لأميركم فإنه كان يحب العفو، الحديث. (رواه البخاري: ۱/۱٤/٥۸، باب قول النبي ﷺ الدين النصيحة، فيصل).
