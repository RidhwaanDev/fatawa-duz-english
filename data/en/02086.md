### TITLE
Prizes given by lucky draw to promote trade

### QUESTION
In most countries the practice is common in large shops that, in order to sell as many goods as possible and to bring their trade to its height, they hold draws for prizes, and whoever's name comes out in the draw receives a prize either in cash or in kind. In the purchase the buyer pays the full price, but the prize is something separate which only a few out of hundreds of buyers receive. The question is whether it is permissible to obtain such a prize or not, given that some muftis say that such a prize is impermissible because it involves interest and gambling.

### ANSWER
In the case in question, fixing such a prize and obtaining it when one's name comes out in the draw is permissible and correct. It falls neither under interest nor under gambling. It is not interest, because in the view of the jurists interest is the increase that is not in exchange for any countervalue and that is stipulated in the contract.

See what the author of al-Hidāyah states:

الربا هو الفضل المستحق لأحد المتعاقدين في المعاوضة الخالي عن عوض شرط فيه. (الهداية: ۳/ ۷۸).

Nor can it be called gambling, because the jurists have defined gambling as money being stipulated from both sides:

تعليق الملك بالخطر من الجانبين.

The following conditions must, however, be observed:

(1) The seller must be selling the goods at the equivalent price, that is, at the same price at which the goods are sold in ordinary shops. If the seller has raised the price because of the prize, then this will not be permissible or correct, and the transaction will fall under gambling.

See what Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

If the goods bought are being sold at more than the market price because of the prize coupon, while the same article is available at a lower price without the prize coupon, then in that case it is impermissible and unlawful to strive to obtain the hoped-for prizes. It is necessary to keep away from this, because in such a case it will fall under gambling, which is unlawful in the sacred law. (جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام ج: ۱ / ۱۲٦، واسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ٤ / ۷٦) ۔

(2) Some scholars have also laid down the condition that the buyer should not go with the aim of obtaining the prize. If his real object is the purchase and he then receives the prize, this will be permissible; but if he went for the sake of the prize itself, then it is gambling. Ḥaḍrat Muftī Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Jawāhir al-Fiqh:

But now everything turns on the intention. Whoever buys this ticket for the sake of a hoped-for prize is committing one degree of gambling; while whoever has in view only going to the exhibition, with no craving for the prize before him, and then happens to receive a prize as well, is by the rules taken out of the ruling of gambling. (جواہر الفقہ: ٤ / ۵٦٦) ۔

Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh Ṣāḥib states:

If the buyer's object was to buy the goods, and along with them he received a prize coupon, and his name happened to come out in the coupon draw, there is no fault in it. But if the real object was to obtain the prize coupon, and the goods were bought for that very purpose, this form will not be permissible; rather it will take the ruling of gambling. (کتاب الفتاوی: ۵ / ۲٤۹) ۔

Muftī Iḥsān Allāh Shāʾiq Ṣāḥib, however, has declared this form permissible as well. See: (جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام: ۱ / ۱۲٦) ۔

In sum, the view of those who hold it impermissible rests upon caution, while the view of those who hold it permissible is to be taken as a concession.

Obtaining a prize coupon is neither interest nor does it fall under gambling. See the following few evidences and parallels.

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Niẓām al-Dīn Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) states:

Since the reality is that whether his numbers come out or not he will receive the whole of the money he deposited, and can take it back whenever he wishes, and there is no fear of that money being lost, there is no gambling in this transaction. There remains the ruling of the sacred law on the extra sum that is received when the numbers come out: will that sum be counted as interest or not? Since at the time of depositing the money the agreement is that no extra sum will be given on it at the time of repayment and that the transaction will be free of interest, this transaction will not be one of interest. Then, since there is no certainty of receiving this increase, nor can it be demanded, nor does everyone receive that increase; rather the department, purely at its own discretion and purely according to its own rules, announces that an extra sum will be given for certain numbers and gives it only to those whose names come out, and no one has any right to demand that extra sum — for this reason, if someone's numbers come out and he receives an extra sum, even though it works out at an increase of a hundred pounds upon one pound, this increase will not be called interest in the sacred law.

After all these discussions this much has become clear and settled: that this increase is a voluntary gift on the part of the department, and a voluntary gift is permissible, not forbidden. Taking this increase will therefore be permissible. If someone adopts the way of God-fearingness, that is another matter and is better. (منتخباتِ نظام الفتاوی: ۱ / ۱۹٤) ۔

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) states:

This transfer of ownership may be left absolute, and it may also be made dependent on a condition. For instance, "after my death such and such a house of mine is bequeathed to you" is an example of the absolute; and to say, "if you perform the Hajj next year, I bequeath such and such a house to you after my death" is an example of the conditional. So if he performs the Hajj, then after the death of the one making the bequest he will become the owner of the thing bequeathed. (مجموعہ قوانین اسلامی ص ۲٤۸) ۔ (In this case of a bequest too there is a suspension of ownership upon a risk from one side, and yet it has been declared permissible, because property is not stipulated from both sides. In sum, it is gambling only when the stipulation is found on both sides.)

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Taqī Ṣāḥib states:

The only condition is that the article being sold should be sold at the equivalent price. If the seller then gives someone a prize by way of a draw, this is a voluntary gift, which is permissible. (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ٤ /۷۷) ۔

Muftī Iʿjāz Aḥmad Ṣamadānī Ṣāḥib states:

From this fourth condition the ruling on many of those prize schemes becomes known which trading companies launch to promote their products. If, under these schemes, the trading companies ask for the same market price for their products as would be demanded if there were no prize scheme, then that form does not fall under gambling, because in that case the buyer has already received the full countervalue of the money he paid. Now, even if no prize comes out for him he suffers no loss; and if some prize does come out, it will be a mere prize from the company, which it will be permissible to take. (مالی معاملات پر غرر کے اثرات، ص: ۳۸۷) ۔

The draw too is permissible and correct, because a draw is permissible on every occasion where it does not entail the nullifying of any binding right; rather where either the distinguishing and allotting of rights is intended by it, or where it is a matter of an initial voluntary gift and kindness and the draw is held in order to set hearts at rest. And in the case in question the prize is an initial voluntary gift; no one has any binding right in it.

See what Takmilat Fatḥ al-Qadīr states:

قوله: (والقرعة لتطييب القلوب إزاحة الميل) قال الشراح: هذا جواب الاستحسان، والقياس يأباها

ولكنا تركنا القياس هاهنا بالسنة والتعامل الظاهر من لدن رسول الله ﷺ إلى يومنا هذا من غير نكير منكر

ألا يري أن يونس عليه السلام في مثل هذا استعمل القرعة مع أصحاب السفينة

وكذلك زكريا عليه السلام استعمل القرعة مع الأحبار في ضم مريم إلى نفسه

وكان رسول الله ﷺ يقرع بين نسائه إذا أراد السفر تطييباً لقلوبهن. (تكملة فتح القدير: ۹/٤٤۰، دار الفكر، وكذا في العناية على هامش فتح القدير: ۹/٤٤۰).

Fatāwā ʿUthmānī states:

A draw is permissible for the distribution of prizes. (فتاوی عثمانی: ۳ / ۳٤۲) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم

Objections and their answers:

Objection (1): Some scholars say that in this the coming out of the name is uncertain, so it is impermissible because it is a suspension of ownership upon a risk.

See what Sharḥ al-Majallah states:

وأما الذي لا يصح تعليقه بالشرط شرعاً فضابطه كل ما كان من التمليكات سواء كان مبادلة المال بمال من الطرفين أو لا كالبيع والإجارة والاستئجار والقسمة والهبة. (شرح مجلة: ۱/۲۳٤).

The answer: First of all it should be borne in mind that the form of

"تعليق الملك بالخطر"

is forbidden when property is stipulated from both sides, because that falls under gambling by the text of the Qur'an.

قال الله تعالى: ﴿إنما الخمر والميسر رجس من عمل الشيطان، فاجتنبوه﴾. (المائدة: ۹۰). والميسر القمار. وقال في التعريفات الفقهية: القمار مصدر قامر هو كل لعب يشترط فيه غالباً أن يأخذ الغالب شيئاً من المغلوب وأصله أن يأخذ الواحد من صاحبه شيئاً فشيئاً في اللعب ثم عرفوه بأنه تعليق الملك على الخطر والمال في الجانبين. (التعريفات الفقهية، ص: ۱۷۷، بيروت).

وفي معجم لغة الفقهاء: القمار تعليق الملك على الخطر والمال من الجانبين (معجم الفقهاء، ص: ۳٦۹).

وفي تيسير الكريم الرحمن في تفسير كلام المنان: وأما الميسر فهو كل المغالبات التي يكون فيها عوض من الطرفين. (تيسير الكريم الرحمن، ص: ۸۱).

Aḥkām al-Qurʾān states:

لا خلاف بين أهل العلم في تحريم القمار وأن المخاطرة من القمار قال ابن عباس رضي الله عنه: إن المخاطرة قمار وإن أهل الجاهلية كانوا يخاطرون على المال والزوجة وقد كان ذلك مباحاً إلى أن ورد تحريمه. (أحكام القرآن للجصاص: ۱/۳۷۹، سهيل).

Fatāwā al-Shāmī states:

لأن القمار من القمر الذي يزداد تارة وينقص أخري وسمي القمار قماراً لأن كل واحد من المقامرين ممن يجوز أن يذهب ماله إلى صاحبه ويجوز أن يستفيد مال صاحبه وهو حرام بالنص ولا كذلك إذا شرط من جانب واحد. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦ /٤۰۳، وكذا في البحر الرائق: ۸ /٤۸٦).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

تعليق الملك على الخطر والمال من الجانبين. (إمداد الفتاوى: ۳/۱٦۱، ومثله في جواهر الفقه: ۲/۳٤۲).

See further: (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ٤ / ۷۲) ۔

Since the case in question does not fall under

"تعليق الملك بالخطر من الجانبين"

— because the buyer has paid his money and taken its countervalue in the form of goods, and now the condition of the name coming out for obtaining the prize is from the seller's side alone — for this reason it is not gambling.

Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil states:

This much should be understood: that gambling occurs when payment is certain from one side and merely possible from the other. But where payment is certain from both sides, and then one party says that he will hold a draw, this is not gambling. There are two traders who say that they will give a slip to everyone who buys goods from them and will hold a draw at some later time — now this is not gambling. (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ٤ / ۷٦) ۔

Muftī Iʿjāz Aḥmad Ṣamadānī states:

If a man has obtained the real countervalue of his money and then also receives a prize, this is not gambling. (غرر کے اثرات، ص: ۳۸٦، و مثلہ فی جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام: ۱ / ۱۲٦) ۔

Muftī Taqī ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) states:

If no fee has been taken for the prize or for taking part in the draw, then this is not gambling; a draw is permissible for the distribution of prizes. (فتاوی عثمانی: ۳ / ۳٤۲) ۔

A difficulty, however, remains: the commentator on the Majallah has declared

"تعليق الملك بالخطر من جانب واحد"

impermissible and forbidden as well when it occurs in transfers of ownership. What is the solution to this?

The answer is that if the other passages of the commentator on the Majallah, and the passages of other books, are examined closely, it becomes clear that this is not a universal rule. There are exceptions to this rule which show that laying down a condition from one side is sound, even if there is an aspect of transfer of ownership in it.

See what Sharḥ al-Majallah states:

وما كان من الإطلاقات كالإذن بالتجارة وكالوكالة أو الولايات كالقضاء والإمارة

أو ما كان من التحريضات صح تعليقه بالملائم وذلك كقول المولي لعبده إن قبضت لي من فلان الألف التي لي عليه فقد أذنت لك بالتجارة

وإن وصل إليك كتابي فقد عزلتك وكقول الإنسان للدائن إذا جاء مديونك من سفره فأناْ كفيل لما لك عليه

وأما غير الملائم كأن هبت الريح أو دخلت الحمام فلا يصح. (شرح المجلة: ۱/۲۳۳).

From the above rule it is learnt that in matters of inducement and encouragement it is permissible and correct to lay down a suitable condition from one side; and in the case in question too there can be an aspect of inducement, in the sense that the aim is to spur buyers on so as to make one's trade well known and to promote it, while the condition of a prize upon a draw is suitable to the contract. It should therefore be sound.

Furthermore, this is like a conditional voluntary gift, because the prize which the seller gives is not in exchange for anything; rather it is an initial voluntary gift, and it is permissible to lay down a condition in a voluntary gift, namely that whoever's name comes out shall receive the prize — just as the Messenger of Allah ﷺ laid down the condition of ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her) being entertained as well, and just as the father of Barāʾ (may Allah be pleased with him) laid down a condition with Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him), and so on.

See what Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim states:

عن أنس رضي الله عنه أن جاراً لرسول الله ﷺ فارسياً كان طيب المرق فصنع لرسول الله ﷺ ثم جاء يدعوه، فقال: وهذه لعائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها فقال: لا، فقال رسول الله ﷺ: لا، فعاد يدعوه، فقال رسول الله ﷺ: وهذه، قال: لا، قال رسول الله ﷺ: لا، ثم عاد يدعوه، فقال رسول الله ﷺ: وهذه، قال: نعم في الثالثة، فقاما يتدافعان حتى أتيا منزله. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۱۷٦).

The substance of the above hadith is that a man presented an invitation to a meal to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. He ﷺ laid down the condition that the Mother of the Believers ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her) should come too. The man refused, but on the third occasion he gave permission, and so the Messenger of Allah ﷺ went along with ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her).

Similarly, Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) bought a camel saddle from ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him) and requested him to tell his son Barāʾ (may Allah be pleased with him) to carry the saddle along with him. ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him) said: No, except on the condition that you relate to us the story of the emigration. So Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) related the story of the emigration, and Barāʾ (may Allah be pleased with him) carried the saddle.

See what Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī states:

عن البراء رضي الله عنه قال: اشتري أبو بكر رضي الله عنه من عازب رضي الله عنه رحلاً بثلاثة عشر درهماً، فقال أبو بكر رضي الله عنه: لعازب رضي الله عنه مر البراء فليحمل إلى رحلي، فقال عازب: لا، حتى تحدثنا كيف صنعت أنت ورسول الله ﷺ حين خرجتما من مكة والمشركون يطلبونكم، قال: ارتحلنا

الخ. (رواه البخاري ۱/۵۱۵، في مناقب المهاجرين).

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

This prize is a voluntary gift from the shopkeeper; it is not the countervalue of anything. (جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام: ۱ /۱۲٦) ۔

An objection: it appears from the rule of Sharḥ al-Majallah that laying down a condition from one side in transfers of ownership is not sound, and gift has also been counted among transfers of ownership, whereas gift is a voluntary donation. The result would then be that laying down a condition in voluntary donations is also impermissible.

The answer: from the fuller passage of Sharḥ al-Majallah it emerges that the transfers of ownership which are made dependent upon a condition are of two kinds:

(1) A transaction in which there is an exchange of property for property.

(2) One in which there is no exchange of property for property.

The ruling on each of the two is different: transactions of the first kind become invalid themselves because of a corrupt condition, whereas in those of the second kind a corrupt condition that is unsuitable is simply void and the transaction itself remains sound — that is, in a gift the ownership of the one gifted to will be established over it. And from the passage of Shāmī it appears that by "condition" is meant an unsuitable condition; that is, keeping a suitable condition is sound, while an unsuitable condition is void in a gift.

See what Sharḥ al-Majallah states:

لكن ما كان فيه مبادلة مال بمال يبطل أيضاً بالشرط الفاسد كالبيع وما ليس كذلك كالهبة والصدقة والإبراء

لا يبطل بالشرط الفاسد بل يصح ويلغو الشرط. (شرح المجلة: ۱/ ۲۳٤).

Voluntary donations belong to the second kind, and for this reason an unsuitable condition will itself be void while the transaction remains sound.

A note: it is worth attention that in Sharḥ al-Majallah every kind of condition in voluntary donations has apparently been declared corrupt, whereas from the writing of ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) it becomes clear that in voluntary donations a suitable condition is sound and correct while an unsuitable condition is corrupt. If, therefore, the passage of Sharḥ al-Majallah is taken to refer to an unsuitable condition, the difficulty will be removed.

See what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ويصح تعليق هبة وحوالة وكفالة وإبراء عنها بملائم. (الدر المختار: ۵/۲۵۵).

In another place it is stated:

وما يصح ولا يبطل بالشرط الفاسد

قوله والهبة والصدقة كوهبتك هذه المائة أو تصدقت عليك على أن تخدمني سنة فتصح ويبطل الشرط لأنه فاسد وفي جامع الفصولين ويصح تعليق الهبة بشرط ملائم

ولو مخالفاً تصح الهبة لا الشرط. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۵ /۲٤۹، سعيد).

The hadiths mentioned above also show that laying down a suitable condition in voluntary donations is sound and correct.

Objection (2): Obtaining prizes for horse races, archery and similar contests, and for educational contests, belongs to the class of inducements and encouragements, in which the aim is to develop some particular quality — in the first case military training and in the second academic progress — whereas in the case you have been asked about no development of any quality is intended.

See what Fatāwā al-Shāmī states:

فيباح في كل الملاعب أي التي تعلم الفروسية وتعين على الجهاد لأن جواز الجعل فيما مر إنما ثبت بالحديث على خلاف القياس فيجوز ما عداها بدون الجعل. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/٤۰۲، سعيد).

The answer: (1) The answer to this is that the occasions on which a prize is fixed are of three kinds. (a) It is of the class of contests and the contest is also beneficial: in this case fixing a prize from one side is permissible and correct by agreement — indeed it is better.

(b) It is of the class of contests, but the contest neither assists in jihād nor is of much benefit. In this case it is not better to fix a prize from one side, even though the contest itself is permissible and correct, so that such an activity may be discouraged.

(c) It is of the class of voluntary donations, inducements and encouragements, and a one-sided prize is fixed in it. Of this ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says that it is permissible and correct.

قال ابن عابدين رحمه الله: ألقى شيئاً وقال: من أخذه فهو له فلمن سمعه أو بلغه ذلك القول أن يأخذه

لأنه أخذه على وجه الهبة وقد تمت بالقبض. (فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۲۸۵، سعيد).

Other jurists too hold it permissible.

Fatāwā Sirājiyyah states:

رجل قال لآخر من أكل من مالي فهو في حل قيل لا يحل لأحد أن يأكل والفتوى على أنه يحل. (الفتاوى السراجية، ص: ٤۰۳).

ʿĀlamgīrī states:

إذا وضع الرجل مقداراً من السكر أو عدداً من الدراهم بين قوم وقال من شاء أخذ منه شيئاً أو قال من أخذ منه شيئاً فهو له فكل من أخذ منه شيئاً يصير ملكاً له. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵ /۳٤۵).

The matter under discussion belongs to the third kind, and on this basis fixing a one-sided prize in it is permissible and correct, even though no development of any quality is intended.

Objection (3): Impermissibility appears from a passage of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muḥammad Yūsuf Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him). The passage is as follows:

The substance of this business is buying and selling on condition of a prize, and in the sacred law such buying and selling is impermissible in which some extraneous condition is laid down bringing a benefit to one of the two parties to the transaction. (آپ کے مسائل اور انکا حل: ٦ / ۲۷۷) ۔

The answer: On the face of it the passage of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā (may Allah have mercy on him) is not easy to follow, because the prize is not stipulated along with the trade. If a man does not wish to take a prize coupon there is no compulsion, and he obtains the goods without a coupon as well. Then his saying that it is buying and selling on condition of a prize is also hard to follow, for here the prize is not stipulated but merely hoped for; being stipulated would mean that every buyer is entitled to the prize, whereas that is not the case.

Permissibility also appears from the writing of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Niẓām al-Dīn (may Allah have mercy on him), and it also appears from it that this increase is not stipulated but is a pure voluntary gift. See:

Then, since there is no certainty of receiving this increase, nor can it be demanded, nor does everyone receive that increase; rather the department, purely at its own discretion and purely according to its own rules, announces that an extra sum will be given for certain numbers and gives it only to those whose names come out — this increase is a voluntary gift on the part of the department, and a voluntary gift is permissible, not forbidden. Taking this increase will therefore be permissible. Then the noble hadith kull qarḍ jarra nafʿan applies to that drawing of benefit in which the benefit is stipulated in the contract of the transaction, and here that is not so. (منتخباتِ نظام الفتاوی: ۱ / ۱۹٤، ۱۹۵) ۔

Objection (4): Sometimes the prize is received in the form of cash, and this is interest, because it is many times more than the sum he paid, and this increase has been obtained without any countervalue; it is therefore interest.

See what Tabyīn al-Ḥaqāʾiq states:

الربا فضل مال بلا عوض في معاوضة مال بمال. (تبيين الحقائق: ٤/۸۵، ط: ملتان).

The answer: There is no profit in bringing the case in question under interest, because interest is that increase which is stipulated in an exchange of property. In the matter under discussion, however, the increase is not stipulated but merely hoped for: not everyone receives it, nor does everyone have the right to demand it. Moreover the buyer's transaction is in exchange for the goods; he has obtained the goods and the article sold in exchange for his cash, and apparently he has no further connection with the prize. It is only this much: that because he is on the list of buyers, a prize will be received from the seller as a pure voluntary gift if his name comes out in the draw, and that is merely hoped for.

See the definition of interest. Al-Hidāyah states:

الربا هو الفضل المستحق لأحد المتعاقدين في المعاوضة الخالي عن عوض شرط فيه. (الهداية: ۳/۷۸).

Al-ʿInāyah Sharḥ al-Hidāyah states:

الربا في اللغة هو الزيادة

وفي الاصطلاح هو الفضل الخالي عن العوض المشروط في البيع. (العناية: ۳ / ۷، ومثله في الدر المختار: ۵/ ۱٦۸، سعيد).

Muʿjam Lughat al-Fuqahāʾ states:

الربا كل زيادة مشروطة في العقد خالية عن عوض مشروع. (معجم لغة الفقهاء، ص ۲۱۸، وكذا في التعريفات الفقهية، ص ۱۰۲).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Niẓām al-Dīn (may Allah have mercy on him) states: The upshot of all the passages the jurists have quoted for the meaning of interest in the sacred law is that there should be a contract of exchange in goods subject to the law of interest, and that in that very contract there should be an increase without countervalue for one of the two sides — that is, one in which the benefit is stipulated in the contract of the transaction. (منتخباتِ نظام الفتاوی: ۱ / ۱۹۵) ۔

Moreover, this transaction would only be open to objection if the prize were in the form of cash. If the prize is in the form of some other kind of goods, then there is no ground for objection at all, because in the Ḥanafī school it is necessary, for interest to be established, that both sides be of the same kind.

See what the author of al-Hidāyah states:

فالعلة عندنا الكيل مع الجنس أو الوزن مع الجنس. (الهداية: ۳/۷۸).

وفي معجم لغة الفقهاء: ربا الفضل بيع شيء من الأموال الربوية بجنسه متفاضلاً. (معجم لغة الفقهاء، ص: ۲۱۸).
