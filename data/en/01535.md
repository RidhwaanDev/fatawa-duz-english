### TITLE
Ruling on a signature obtained on a divorce document while intoxicated

### QUESTION
A man's enemies made him drink alcohol, or rendered him senseless in some other way, and then brought along a scribe (one who draws up divorce documents and petitions) and, while the man was intoxicated, took his thumbprint or his signature on a divorce document. They then wrote out a ṭalāq to his wife on his behalf, though neither the man nor his wife knew anything at all about the document. In short, the whole business was carried out by deception. Does ṭalāq take effect on his wife in this case or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question no ṭalāq has taken effect, since the signature was obtained by deception on a blank sheet of paper without the man being informed.

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states: No ṭalāq takes effect where a divorce document is drawn up on a man's behalf without any basis and his thumbprint is obtained on it without his being informed that a ṭalāq is written in that paper. In the same way, if by some ruse the husband's thumbprint is obtained on a blank white sheet and a ṭalāq is written on that paper afterwards, no ṭalāq will take effect from the husband.

كما في حديث ابن ماجة: "الطلاق لمن أخذ بالساق". الخ.

And Radd al-Muḥtār states:

وكذا كل كتاب لم يكتبه بخطه ولم يمله بنفسه لا يقع الطلاق مالم يقر أنه كتابه.

So in this case no ṭalāq has taken effect from the husband. (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: ۹/۱۰۲، دار الاشاعت) ۔

و کذا فی فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۲/٦٤٤) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
