### TITLE
Are the disbelievers bound by the subsidiary rulings or not?

### QUESTION
Are the disbelievers bound by the subsidiary rulings or not? Or is there a difference of opinion in this? If the question is one of difference, what is the preponderant position? Kindly explain and be rewarded.

### ANSWER
In the case in question, in matters of belief there is no difference of anyone's opinion: they are addressed in both worlds, this world and the hereafter. (2) In the penalties too they are addressed by agreement, except for the prescribed penalty for drinking. But if there is no commitment to the rulings of Islam — that is, no covenant to abide by them — then the prescribed penalty will not be carried out upon them.

(3) In transactions they are addressed in the hereafter by agreement; concerning their being addressed in this world there is a difference of opinion. In the view of Imām Zufar (raḥimahullāh) they are addressed, whether one is a protected non-Muslim subject or a hostile disbeliever; therefore whatever transactions they carry out contrary to the Sharīʿah will be ruled invalid.

In the view of the two companions (raḥimahumullāh), the hostile disbeliever is not addressed; the protected subjects, however, are addressed by it, because in transactions they have committed themselves to the rulings of Islam.

In the view of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (raḥimahullāh), even the transactions of the protected subjects that are contrary to the Sharīʿah will not be ruled invalid, provided that those transactions are in accordance with their own religion, because the protected subjects did not commit themselves to the rulings of Islam in transactions in a manner contrary to their own beliefs.

(4) In acts of worship they are addressed in the hereafter by agreement; concerning their being addressed in this world, however, there is a difference of opinion. In the view of the majority they are addressed in respect of belief but not in respect of performance, unless they come to faith. But in the view of most of the Shāfiʿīs they are addressed both in respect of belief and in respect of performance. Some people have interpreted their statement to mean that what is meant is that they are addressed in respect of performance after coming to faith; if this is correct, then the Shāfiʿīs too are with the majority.

Consider the brief evidences. Consider a hadith of al-Bukhārī and Muslim:

وعن ابن عباس رضي الله عنه أن النبي ﷺ بعث معاذاً رضي الله عنه إلى اليمن فقال: ادعهم إلى شهادة: أن لا إله إلا الله، وأني رسول الله فإن هم أطاعوا لذلك فاعلمهم أن الله قد افترض عليهم خمس صلوات في كل يوم وليلة فإن هم أطاعوا لذلك فاعلمهم أن الله قد افترض عليهم صدقة في أموالهم تؤخذ من أغنيائهم وترد على فقرائهم. (رواه البخاري، رقم: ۱۳۹۵، باب وجوب الزكاة).

ʿAllāmah al-ʿAynī (raḥimahullāh) writes under this hadith:

يستدل لمن يذهب إلى أن الكفار غير مخاطبين بشريعة الدين وإنما خوطبوا بالشهادة فإذا أقاموها توجهت عليهم بعد ذلك الشرائع والعبادات لأنه صلي الله عليه وسلم قد أوجبها مرتبة وقدم فيها الشهادة ثم تلا بالصلاة والزكاة. (عمدة القاري: ٦/۳۲۵، دار الحديث، ملتان).

In Fatḥ al-Mulhim it is recorded:

وقال علامة عصرنا الفاضل الكشميري: إن قولهم الكفار مخاطبون بالمعاملات إن كان المراد به ثواباً وعقاباً في الآخرة فمسلم لا شك فيه وإن كان المراد صحة وفساداً في أحكام الدنيا فليس هذا عندي على الإطلاق

فكما أنهم استثنوا من العقوبات حد الشرب كذلك ينبغي تقييد المعاملات أيضاً بشيء يخرج أمثال هذه الفروع المنصوصة عليها في كتب الفقه. (فتح الملهم: ۱/۵٤۱، مكتبة دار العلوم كراتشي).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (raḥimahullāh), after quoting the passages of fiqh in Imdād al-Aḥkām, says:

From the passages mentioned the following matters are derived:

(1) In the view of Imām Zufar (raḥimahullāh) all the general addresses that occur in the Sharīʿah concerning transactions include the disbelievers as well, whether they are hostile disbelievers or protected subjects; and because of the generality of the address the ruling too will be established generally. Therefore whatever transactions the disbelievers — the people of war and the protected subjects — carry out contrary to the Sharīʿah will be ruled invalid, though the people of war will not be interfered with because of the absence of authority over them, and the protected subjects will not be interfered with because of the covenant.

(2) In the view of the two companions, the transactions of the people of war which are contrary to the Sharīʿah will not each be ruled invalid, because they have not committed themselves to the rulings of Islam; as it were, in their view the generality of the address is not sufficient for a ruling to be established, but commitment is also a condition, and that is absent in the people of war. But those transactions of the protected subjects which are contrary to the agreed-upon rulings of Islam will be ruled invalid, because they have committed themselves in transactions to the rulings of Islam.

(3) In the view of the Greatest Imām (raḥimahullāh), even the transactions of the protected subjects that are contrary to the Sharīʿah will not be ruled invalid, provided that those transactions are in accordance with their own religion, because the protected subjects did not commit themselves to the rulings of Islam in transactions in a manner contrary to their own beliefs. Yes, if concerning some transaction a condition is made with them in the covenant itself, then commitment to it will be on their part. As for the remaining transactions of the Sharīʿah which are not in accordance with their beliefs, and concerning which no condition has been made with them, commitment on the part of the protected subjects is not found in them; therefore, when they carry out such transactions in accordance with their own religion, they will be regarded as valid and will not be ruled invalid.

The upshot of the answer is that although the disbelievers are addressed by the subsidiary rulings in the penalties and in transactions, yet for a ruling of unlawfulness and invalidity the general address is not sufficient; rather commitment is also a condition. The people of war have not committed themselves to the rulings of Islam at all, neither in what accords with their belief nor in what opposes it. Therefore, in whatever way they earn money — whether by interest, or by usurpation, or by void and invalid sales, whether by a way that accords with their religion or by one that opposes it — in every case that money and the like will enter into their ownership, and it is permissible for a Muslim to take it as a salary. And the protected subjects have committed themselves to the rulings of Islam in the matters that accord with their beliefs, and have not done so in the matters that oppose them, apart from those matters concerning which a condition is made with them. So whatever contract the protected subjects carry out in accordance with their religion, and which is also not contrary to the terms of the covenant, will be ruled valid, even though it is contrary to the Sharīʿah. But whatever contract is contrary to their religion too, or contrary to the terms of the covenant, will be ruled invalid.

(امداد الاحکام: ٤/۳۸۹۔۳۹۰)۔
