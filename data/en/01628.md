### TITLE
Marrying during the waiting period

### QUESTION
A man gave his wife one irrevocable divorce. After one menstrual period had passed, he renewed the marriage. He then gave her a second irrevocable divorce. After that, the first menstrual period of her waiting period came from 1 March to 11 March. The woman then says that a second period came from 17 March to 26 March. After that she married another man. Then, when a third period came from 12 April to 21 April, the marriage was renewed; and on the same day, after the marriage, a few drops of blood appeared. Is this marriage valid or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, the second marriage took place during the waiting period. The bleeding seen from 17 March to 26 March cannot be menstrual blood, because the fifteen-day interval that must follow the end of the previous period — the minimum period of purity — had not passed. A marriage with a woman who is in another man's waiting period is void. The husband is therefore obliged to leave her; and if he is not willing to do so, the wife should have the marriage annulled through the Jamiat. After that she should observe a waiting period as a precaution, and then she will be free and may marry elsewhere if she wishes.

Consider the following from al-Durr al-Mukhtār:

أقل الطهر بين الحيضتين أو النفاس والحيض خمسة عشر يوماً ولياليها إجماعاً. (الدر المختار: ۱/۲۸۵، سعيد).

In Imdād al-Fatāwā, Mawlānā Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) quotes a number of Arabic passages and then says:

From these narrations it is established that such a marriage — that is, a marriage contracted during the waiting period — is void. It is also clear that whether a waiting period is obligatory in the case asked about is a matter on which there is a difference of opinion, and the more cautious view is that it is obligatory. (امداد الفتاوی: ۲/۵۰۵) ۔

Majmūʿah Qawānīn Islāmī states:

Where a marriage is defective, the couple are obliged to separate; and if they do not separate of their own accord, the qāḍī will separate them. (مجموعہ قوانین اسلامی، ص ۲۰٦) ۔

Since there is no qāḍī here, the Jamiat stands in the place of the qāḍī, and it will have the authority to annul such a marriage.
