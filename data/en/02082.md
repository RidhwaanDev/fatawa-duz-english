### TITLE
Taking part in the Vodacom competition

### QUESTION
Nowadays many companies run competitions in which valuable prizes are given to the winner. One instance of this is the competition offered by the Vodacom company.

The way this company works is that a person sends a message (SMS) to Vodacom, on which about ten rand is spent. At the end of the day a draw is held among those who sent messages, and the one whose name comes out is given a BMW car worth two hundred and fifty thousand rand.

Nowadays our Muslim brothers too take part in this competition with great enthusiasm. In the sight of the sacred law, is it permissible to take part in such a competition or not? And if someone is given a car as a prize, will it be permissible for him to take it or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, since the Vodacom competition consists of gambling, taking part in it and obtaining the prize is not permissible or correct. If someone does receive the car, it is obligatory and necessary for him to sell it and give the money in charity; he may, however, keep only as much as he spent on entering.

قال الله تعالى: ﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا إنما الخمر والميسر والأنصاب والأزلام رجس من عمل الشيطان فاجتنبوه لعلكم تفلحون﴾ (سورة المائدة، الآية: ۹۰).

وعن عبد الله بن عمرو رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: "إن الله حرم على أمتى الخمر والميسر. (مسند احمد: ۲/۳۵۱/۵٦۱۱، بيروت، وسنن أبي داود، ص ۳۲۷، باب ما جاء في السكر).

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

وسمي القمار قماراً لأن كل واحد من المقامرين ممن يجوز أن يذهب ماله إلى صاحبه، ويجوز أن يستفيد مال صاحبه، وهو حرام بالنص. (رد المحتار: ٦/٤۰۳، كتاب الحظر والإباحة، سعيد).

Aḥkām al-Qurʾān states:

إن أهل الجاهلية كانوا يخطرون على المال والزوج وقد كان مباحاً إلى أن ورد تحريمه. (أحكام القرآن للجصاص رحمه الله، ۱/۳۲۹).

Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil states:

One form of gharar, which the noble jurists (may Allah have mercy on them) have expressed as "تعلیق التملیک علی الخطر", is that in contracts of exchange the transfer of ownership is made to depend upon a risk. The meaning of khaṭar is some future event which may or may not occur; the transfer of ownership is suspended upon that event, so that if the event occurs, I have made you owner of such and such a thing of mine. This is also called qimār.

One branch of it is gambling: qimār, that is, gambling or maysir, in which payment from one side is certain while payment from the other side is a mere hope. For example, someone says: let everyone deposit two hundred rupees each with me, then I shall hold a draw, and whoever's name comes out in the draw I shall give one hundred thousand rupees. This is called qimār, and it is also called maysir. So all the lotteries that are well known in our time — for instance a car is parked at an airport and people are told to buy tickets for two hundred rupees, after which a draw will be held and whoever's number comes out will get the car — this is gambling. It is

"تعليق التمليك على الخطر"

and it is a branch of gharar, which is unlawful. (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ٤/۷٦) ۔

See further: (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱٦/٤۳۵، جامعہ فاروقیہ، و احسن الفتاوی: ۷/۲٦، و منتخباتِ نظام الفتاوی: ۲/۳۰۲)

An objection: some people think that the 10 rand for the message is an entry fee, and that the prize is offered by the company simply to attract people, so what is the reason for saying it is not permissible?

The answer to this is that entry as such is not the object; the object is to obtain the prize, and it is for that alone that a man is willing to bear the fee.

See what Ḥaḍrat Muftī Maḥmūd Ḥasan Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) states:

Everyone knows that mere entry is not the object, such that a fee would be borne for it; the object is to obtain the sum of money which has been given the name "prize". And everyone also knows that the object of the one giving the prize is not merely to fill in a form, nor has he any particular interest of that kind; his object is to collect money by holding out the lure of a large prize. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱٦/٤٤۲، جامعہ فاروقیہ) ۔
