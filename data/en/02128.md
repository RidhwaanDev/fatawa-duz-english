### TITLE
Conditional gifts at a wedding feast

### QUESTION
At wedding feasts and other such gatherings it is the custom for people to collect gifts, and at the end of the function the hosts go through all the gifts and write them down. Their purpose in doing so is that when there is a function at the giver's house, they will have to give the same amount. And those who give gifts have it in mind that when their own turn comes they too should receive a gift of the same value. Is it permissible to give and receive gifts in this way or not?

### ANSWER
This is a conditional gratuity, and when what is given by the other side falls short in quantity or is of poor quality, it sometimes gives rise to ill feeling and backbiting. For poor people this custom is a millstone round their necks, and it is therefore necessary to keep away from it. In the Noble Qur'an Allah Most High says:

﴿وماآتيتم من ربا ليربو في أموال الناس فلا يربو عند الله﴾ (سورة الروم: ۳۹).

Tafsīr ʿUthmānī states:

Some commentators have explained the meaning of the verse thus: when a man gives something to another with the object that the other will repay the favour with something greater, then such giving brings no blessing or reward in the sight of Allah Most High, although it is permitted. (Tafsīr ʿUthmānī, p. 543.)

In Maʿārif al-Qurʾān this interpretation is reported from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) and Imām al-Shāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him). (Maʿārif al-Qurʾān: 6/158, by Mawlānā Idrīs Kāndhlawī.)

In Iṣlāḥ al-Rusūm Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

This is like a conditional loan, because when these people give money at his function, he will give money at their functions too. Even though nothing is said in so many words, it comes under the maxim "المعروف کالمشروط" — what is customary is as good as stipulated — and this is not correct. (Iṣlāḥ al-Rusūm: 41.)

From the words of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) in Imdād al-Aḥkām, however, it appears that an arrangement of this kind is a conditional gratuity. (Imdād al-Aḥkām: 3/386, 606.)

A conditional gratuity is indeed permissible, but because of the many harms involved one ought to avoid this practice.
