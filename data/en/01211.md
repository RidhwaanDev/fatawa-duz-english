### TITLE
Whether a dam is due for changing the order of stoning, sacrifice and shaving

### QUESTION
According to the three imams (may Allah have mercy on them) and the two companions of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on them), keeping the order between the stoning, the sacrifice and the shaving is a sunnah and not necessary. Please give the views of present-day jurists.

### ANSWER
Niẓām al-Fatāwā states:

Question: It has been seen at the slaughter area itself that, because of the crowds of people and the great number of animals, one is thrown into distress and there is a fear of injury as well; and on top of that one has to walk three miles in the heat. At such a time a vehicle is hard to find, and poor people do not even have the fare. In the light of these reasons, and treating the matter as one of need, does a Ḥanafī have the right in this particular case to act on the position of the Shāfiʿīs, since their books plainly show that the order is not necessary?

Answer: A few passages are given here to make the matter easier to understand:

(۱) وأما ترك الواجبات بعذر فلا شيء عليه، ثم مرادهم بالعذر ما يكون من الله تعالى، فلو كان من العباد فليس بعذر، (إلى قوله) بخلاف ماذا منعه خوف الزحام فإنه من الله تعالى، فلا شيء عليه. (غنية الناسك في بغية المناسك: ص ۱۳۸).

(۲) وفي الشامية: إن ترك الواجب بعذر مسقط للدم.

(۳) ولو طاف المفرد وغيره قبل الرمي والحلق لا شيء عليه ويكره، لترك السنة. (زبدة المناسك: ص ۱٦٦).

These passages show that although "الترتيب بين الرمي والذبح والحلق" is in itself necessary, if it is missed or broken because of a valid legal excuse and cannot be observed, then no dam (compensatory sacrifice) for a violation, and no sin or expiation of any kind, will be incurred; rather the Hajj will be completed without even any dislike attaching to it. (Niẓām al-Fatāwā: 1/158).

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states:

Question: Among the Ḥanafīs it is necessary to keep the order of stoning, sacrifice and shaving. In today's extremely difficult conditions, maintaining that order has become hard because of administrative constraints. May one, in order to resolve this, adopt the view of those who hold the order not to be necessary, and, within the Ḥanafī school, the view of the two companions?

The writers of papers on this question hold two views in all:

(1) Most of them, in view of the times and circumstances, gave the ruling according to the position of the two companions and of the three imams. Some of their honoured names are as follows:

(1) Mawlānā Khālid Sayfullāh Raḥmānī. (2) Mawlānā Khurshīd Anwar Aʿẓamī. (3) Mawlānā Irshād al-Ḥaqq Qāsmī. (4) Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Laṭīf Maẓāhirī. (5) Mawlānā Muṣliḥ al-Dīn Brodwī. (6) Mufti Shabbīr Aḥmad Qāsmī. (7) Mawlānā Rāshid Ḥusayn Nadwī. (8) Mawlānā Anwār al-Ḥaqq Raḥmānī. (9) Mufti Anwar ʿAlī Aʿẓamī. (10) Mawlānā Shams Pīrzādah.

Evidence:

عن عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص رضي الله تعالى عنه أن رسول الله ﷺ وقف في حجة الوداع بمنى للناس يسئلونه، فجاء رجل فقال: يا رسول الله لم أشعر، فحلقت قبل أن أذبح، فقال: "اذبح ولا حرج" فجاء ه آخر، فقال: لم أشعر، فنحرت قبل أن أرمي فقال: "ارم ولا حرج" فما سئل النبي ﷺ عن شيء قدم أو أخر إلا قال: "افعل ولا حرج". متفق عليه.

(Imām al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) has recorded this narration with different wordings in several places.)

قال محمد رحمه الله: وبالحديث الذي روي عن النبي ﷺ نأخذ، أنه قال: "لا حرج في شيء من ذلك" وقال أبو حنيفة رحمه الله: لا حرج في شيء من ذلك ولا كفارة، إلا في خصلة واحدة، المتمتع والقارن إذا حلق قبل أن يذبح، قال: عليه دم، وأما نحن، فلا نري عليه شيئاً. (موطأ امام محمد رحمه الله: ص ۲۳۵).

The second view: (1) Mufti ʿAbd al-Raḥīm Qāsmī. (2) Mawlānā Ibrāhīm Falāḥī. (3) Mawlānā Manẓūr Aḥmad Qāsmī. (4) Mufti Ḥabībullāh Qāsmī. These scholars have held the order to be necessary.

Evidence:

ــ (۱) ويبدء إذا وافي مني برمي جمرة العقبة ثم بالذبح إن كان قارناً أو متمتعاً ثم بالحلق، لحديث عائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها، أن النبي ﷺ قال: "إن أول نُسُكنَا في هذا اليوم أن نرمي ثم نذبح ثم نحلق" ولأن الذبح والحلق من أسباب التّحلّل، ألا تري أن تحلّل المحصر بالذبح، فيتقدم الرمي عليها. (المبسوط للسرخسي: ٤/٦٤، باب رمي الجمار).

(۲) اعلم أن في يوم النحر أربعة نسك رمي ونحر وطواف على ترتيب ما ذكر والترتيب في الثلاثة واجب. (العرف الشذي: ۱ /۸۲).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil records:

Among the Ḥanafī jurists too, the two companions hold the order to be a sunnah and not necessary; so if these acts are performed somewhat out of order, nothing becomes due.

"أما عندهما فعدم التأخير سنة حتى لو ذبح قبل التحلّل بالحلق لا شيء عليه. (الشامي: ۲ /۲۵۰. وبدائع الصنائع: ۲ /۱٤۱).

Moreover, the position of the two companions (may Allah have mercy on them) is in reality one of the positions of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) himself. Indeed, where the two companions (may Allah have mercy on them) hold one view and Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) another, some scholars allow a ruling to be given on either of the two views. (شرح عقود رسم المفتي: ۱۹، مكتبه دار الإشاعة).

For this reason it seems appropriate in our time to give the ruling on the view of the two companions (may Allah have mercy on them) and to act upon it. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 2/200).

Summary: Among the various resolutions of the scholars of India recorded in Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith, one resolution is this:

According to the Ḥanafī position it is necessary to perform the rites of the 10th of Dhū al-Ḥijjah — the stoning, the sacrifice and the shaving — in their proper order, while according to the two companions and many other jurists it is a sunnah, the breach of which does not make a dam due. Pilgrims should keep to the order as far as they can; but because of the crowds, the severity of the weather, the distance of the slaughter area and the like, there is room to act on the view of the two companions and of the other imams. So even if these rites are performed out of order, no dam will be due. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 13/599).

In Anmol Ḥajj, Mufti Sayyid Muṣliḥ al-Dīn Aḥmad Brodwī has quoted the following on the authority of the Sixth Fiqh Conference held at Shaykh al-Hind Hall, Deoband, under the heading that failure to observe the order in the acts of the Day of Sacrifice does not make a dam due:

Resolution (3) — the order between the stoning, the sacrifice and the shaving:

For one performing tamattuʿ (ʿumrah and then Hajj in the same journey) or qirān (ʿumrah and Hajj together in one iḥrām), the order between the stoning, the sacrifice and the shaving is binding according to the position of Imām al-Aʿẓam (may Allah have mercy on him), which is the position given in fatwa, and abandoning it makes a dam due. According to the two companions (may Allah have mercy on them), this order is a sunnah, and abandoning it does not make a dam due.

If pilgrims today cannot keep to the order because of the crowds or other distressing difficulties, there is room to act on the view of the two companions. (Sixth Fiqh Conference at Shaykh al-Hind Hall, Deoband, held on 26, 27 and 28 March 1997. Anmol Ḥajj: p. 115).

A summary of the paper of Mufti Shabbīr Aḥmad Murādābādī:

The acts of Hajj on the Day of Sacrifice are: (1) the stoning; (2) the sacrifice; (3) the shaving; and (4) the ṭawāf al-ziyārah (the circuits of visitation).

Everyone agrees that keeping ṭawāf al-ziyārah in its place in this order is a sunnah; nobody holds it to be necessary.

The question, then, is whether the order among the other three acts is necessary or not.

The answer is that changing the order takes two forms: (1) the order is changed deliberately; (2) it is changed out of ignorance or forgetfulness. Each of the two has its own details.

If the order is changed deliberately, then according to Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), and likewise according to Imām Mālik (may Allah have mercy on him), Imām al-Shāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) (Nawawī, vol. 1, p. 421) and one narration from Imām Aḥmad (may Allah have mercy on him) (al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq: 3/24), a dam becomes due. But according to the well-known position of Imām al-Shāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him), Imām Aḥmad (may Allah have mercy on him) and Imām Mālik (may Allah have mercy on him), and according to the two companions (may Allah have mercy on them), no dam is due, since in the view of all of them the order is a sunnah, and abandoning a sunnah does not make a dam due. Furthermore, the narration of Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with them both) on which Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) based his argument is weak: ʿAllāmah Badr al-Dīn al-ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him), in his commentary on al-Ṭaḥāwī, Nukhab al-Afkār (in manuscript), graded this report of Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with them both) — the report from which the obligation of a dam is derived — as weak, saying "ولا يصح ذلك عنه". (نخب الأفكار قلمي ج: ۵ ص: ۸۱).

If the order is changed because the ruling was not known, or through forgetfulness, then too a dam becomes due according to the well-known position of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), as this position of his is found in the general books of fiqh. Imām Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him), however, has quoted Imām Abū Ḥanīfah's position explicitly in "كتاب الحجة على أهل المدينة". The passage runs:

"أخبرنا محمد رحمه الله عن أبي حنيفة رحمه الله في الرجل يجهل وهو حاج فيحلق رأسه قبل أن يرمي الجمرة أنه لا شيء عليه. (كتاب الحجة: ۲/۳۷۱).

In the footnote beneath it, Mufti Sayyid Mahdī Ḥasan says:

فإن الأحاديث الواردة في الباب إنما تدلّ على من جهل عن شيء ولم يشعر به ثم فعل خلافه فلا شيء عليه ولا دم، ومن علم الترتيب بين الواجبات ثم خالفه عمداً وقدم الشيء أو أخره عن موضعه فهو غير داخل في الأحاديث المذكورة. (تعليق: ۲ /۳۷۱).

The two companions (may Allah have mercy on them), the three imams (may Allah have mercy on them), Ḥasan al-Baṣrī (may Allah have mercy on him), Qatādah (may Allah have mercy on him) and the majority of the scholars hold that no dam is due when the order is changed through forgetfulness or ignorance. The scholars of the ummah have recorded this in words such as these:

فإن أخل بترتيبها ناسياً أو جاهلاً بالسنة فلا شيء عليه في قول كثير من أهل العلم منهم الحسن وطاؤس

وإليه ذهب الشافعي رحمه الله وأحمد رحمه الله وإسحاق رحمه الله

وقال ابن عباس رضي الله تعالى عنهما: عليه دم، وهو قول النخعي رحمه الله

وإليه ذهب أبو حنيفة رحمه الله والنخعي رحمه الله وابن الماجشون رحمه الله. (معارف السنن: ٦ /۲۱۰، سعيد. وأوجز المسالك: ۳ /۷۱۵).

Imām Abū Ḥanīfah's (may Allah have mercy on him) evidence is the report of Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with them both).

عن ابن مسعود رضي الله تعالى عنه قال: من قدم نسكاً على نسك فعليه دم. قلت: هكذا هو في غالب النسخ، ويوجد في بعضها ابن عباس رضي الله تعالى عنهما وهو أصح. وقال: إبراهيم ابن مهاجر ضعيف. (نصب الراية: ۳/۱۲۹).

The evidence of the majority is that narration of Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with them both) in which several people asked the Prophet ﷺ about not keeping to the order, and he ﷺ answered each of them: "افعل ولا حرج". (بخاري شريف: ۱/ ۲۳۲).

The outcome of the discussion:

Reflecting now on the discussion as a whole, it will be seen that the evidence of the majority is stronger and sounder, and that the best way of reconciling the reports may be this: that the statement in the marfūʿ narrations of the two Ṣaḥīḥs that no expiation is due applies where the order was changed out of ignorance or forgetfulness, while the report of Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) that expiation is due should be taken of one who changed the order deliberately. In that case it becomes possible for all to act upon every one of the narrations. So if a person changes the order out of ignorance or forgetfulness, no expiation should be due from him, while whoever changes it deliberately does incur expiation. On this view a great many difficulties can be removed. Accordingly, if one performing tamattuʿ or qirān deliberately changes the order between the stoning, the sacrifice and the shaving without an excuse, a dam will be due; but if he is unable to keep the order because of distressing difficulties or through ignorance, there will be room to act on the view of the two companions (may Allah have mercy on them) and on the lesser-known position of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), and no ruling that a dam is due should be given merely because the order was changed. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 13/173).

An objection to the necessity of the order drawn from a Qur'anic verse, and its answer:

Some scholars have argued for the necessity of the order from Allah's words ﴿ولا تحلقوا رؤوسكم حتى يبلغ الهدي محله﴾ by way of dalālat al-naṣṣ (the implied import of a text). The explanation of this is that dalālat al-naṣṣ means that the unmentioned case deserves the ruling even more than the case actually mentioned — as in ﴿لا تقل لهما أف﴾, where "أف" is what is mentioned and speaking harshly to one's parents is left unmentioned, yet speaking harshly to them deserves the prohibition even more.

In the same way, they argue, in the verse ﴿ولا تحلقوا رؤوسكم حتى يبلغ الهدي محله﴾ the muḥṣar is commanded, without qualification, to put the sacrifice first; and by consensus it is not permissible to shave before the sacrifice, and doing so makes a dam due. Now, when this ruling is stated for the muḥṣar — a person who entered iḥrām and was then prevented from performing the Hajj — namely that he is to shave only after the sacrificial animal has been slaughtered, then one performing qirān and the like should be subject to that ruling all the more: that shaving before the sacrifice is not valid, and that a dam is due for breaking the order, for he was not even prevented, so the order ought to be binding on him with even greater reason.

Answer: The reply to this argument is that the muḥṣar has only entered iḥrām for Hajj and has then been prevented from the acts of Hajj. He has not performed any pillar of the Hajj; he has only entered iḥrām, and iḥrām has the standing of a condition for Hajj.

To come out of iḥrām, at least one of the acts of Hajj must be performed, so that release from iḥrām may follow upon that act; and so for the muḥṣar it is necessary that the sacrificial animal be slaughtered in the Ḥaram.

This is unlike the one performing qirān or tamattuʿ, who has already performed several acts of Hajj — the standing at ʿArafah, for instance — and for whom only the sacrifice and the shaving now remain.

The upshot is that the muḥṣar must perform at least one act of Hajj before shaving, since in most cases he will have been prevented immediately after entering iḥrām, and that act is the sacrifice; whereas a person who has not been prevented has already performed a great many other acts even if he does not sacrifice before shaving.

Iḥrām has the standing of a condition.

قال في غنية الناسك: الإحرام قبل الوقوف بعرفة

وهو شرط ابتداء حتى صح تقديمه على الوقت، وله حكم الركن انتهاء. (غنية الناسك: ۲۱، إدارة القرآن، عمدة الفقه: ٤/٦۹).

So the ruling for the ordinary pilgrim performing tamattuʿ or qirān will differ from that of the muḥṣar. On this basis, the ruling on the order for one performing qirān or tamattuʿ is the one already written in the detailed fatwa above, which may be consulted.
