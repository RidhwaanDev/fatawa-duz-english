### TITLE
Leading the prayer when the beard has been shaved on account of an excuse

### QUESTION
Can a person become an imam who has had to have his beard shaved for medical reasons (for instance acne)? Further, what is the ruling on such a person leading the prayer in the presence of other scholars and memorisers of the Qurʾān?

### ANSWER
A person who has his beard shaved on account of some excuse is not, in the view of the Sacred Law, a transgressor or a sinner, provided there is nothing else sinful in him. On this basis such a person may lead the prayer. Yes, if this is a matter of repugnance to the followers, he should not lead the prayer. Moreover, when other scholars and memorisers of the Qurʾān are present in whom the conditions of leading the prayer are found in the most complete manner, they have the better right; nevertheless, if this excused person leads the prayer in their presence, the prayer will still be valid. The same ruling applies when the hair falls out because of cancer.

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah records:

Question: there is a man in whose beard a disease has appeared. He has been treated a great deal, but with no benefit, and the doctors say: have the beard shaved off, after which your treatment will succeed. In such a case, is having the beard shaved permissible in the Sacred Law?

Answer: for the treatment of illnesses, when no permissible medicine is of benefit, the use of an impure or unlawful medicine is also permitted out of necessity, provided an experienced and religious physician prescribes it and says that a cure can come only from the unlawful thing. In the same way, if health cannot be restored without having the beard shaved, then there is room for it out of necessity, in order to obtain health. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah 27/498, Maktabah Maḥmūdiyyah)

وبهامشه: قال: يجوز للعليل شرب الدم والبول وأكل الميتة للتداوي إذا أخبره طبيب مسلم إن شفاء فيه ولم يجد من المباح ما يقوم مقامه، الخ. (ʿĀlamgīrī 5/355)

والضرورات تبيح المحظورات، الخ. (الأشباه والنظائر، ص ۱٤۰، ط: ديوبند).

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

مطلب في إمامة الأمرد، قوله وكذا تكره خلف أمرد، الظاهر تنزيهيه أيضاً، والظاهر أيضاً كما قال الرحمتي: إن المراد به الصبيح الوجه لأنه محل الفتنة وهل يقال: هنا أيضاً إذا كان أعلم القوم تنفي الكراهة فإن كانت علة الكراهة خشية الشهوة وهو الأظهر فلا، وإن كانت غلبة الجهل أو نفرة الناس من الصلاة خلفه فنعم فتأمل. (فتاوى الشامي: ۱/۵٦۲، سعيد).

Kitāb al-Fatāwā records:

The leading of the prayer by a person who is physically disabled but is able to bow and prostrate is valid. But since people generally feel a distaste for praying behind such a person, and the imam ought to be someone whom people follow willingly, so that the state of humility and submissiveness may remain in the prayer, the jurists have therefore declared the leading of the prayer by such people to be disliked. ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written the same concerning the paralysed, the lame and the maimed:

(قوله ومفلوج وأبرص شاع برصه) وكذلك أعرج يقوم ببعض قدمه، فالاقتداء بغيره أولي تتارخانية، وكذا أجزم برجندي، ومجبوب وحاقن، ومن له يد واحدة فتاوى الصوفية عن التحفة، والظاهر أن العلة النفرة. (رد المحتار: ۱/۵٦۲)

From this it is learnt that if a person has a physical defect, but on account of his knowledge and piety people like to pray behind him, then there is no harm in such a person leading the prayer. (Kitāb al-Fatāwā 2/297)
