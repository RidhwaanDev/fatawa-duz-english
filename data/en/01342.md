### TITLE
The ruling on ʿazl

### QUESTION
Is it correct to practise ʿazl with one's wife or not? Is any instance of it found in the age of the Prophet ﷺ or not? And what is the ruling if someone practises ʿazl in order to space out children?

### ANSWER
The form of contraception that was current in the age of the Prophet ﷺ is what the jurists call ʿazl in their terminology. The statements the Messenger of Allah ﷺ made about it in reply to various questions are such that neither is a clear prohibition to be understood from them, nor is plain permissibility derived from them. This much, however, does become clear: that he did not approve of this practice. It is for this reason that the imams of the early generations differed over this question. Some declared it impermissible outright, while others said that the practice is in itself disliked, though permission for it may be granted in the face of particular needs; and if it is done for some corrupt purpose, it is impermissible. For example, if the thought behind it is that the birth of a daughter will bring disgrace, this practice cannot be called permissible, since its basis is that very notion which the Noble Qur'ān has censured in many places. Likewise, if a person does this out of an imagined fear of poverty, that too will not be permissible, because its purpose is entirely contrary to the fundamental principles of Islam.

A mention of the excuses that are regarded as valid in the sharīʿah: the woman is so weak that she cannot bear the burden of pregnancy; or she is on a long journey; or she is in a place where there is no possibility of settled residence and danger is at hand; or the mutual relations of the couple are not smooth and separation is intended. Likewise, if there is a strong likelihood of the children being corrupted because of a corrupt environment and the corruption of the times, ʿazl will be permissible in that case too.

Radd al-Muḥtār records:

وفي الفتاوى إن خاف من الولد السؤء في الحرة يسعه العزل بغير رضاها لفساد الزمان، ويحتمل أنه أراد الحاق مثل هذا العذر به كأن يكون في سفر بعيد، أو في دار الحرب فخاف على الولد، أو كانت الزوجة سيئة الخلق ويريد فراقها فخاف أن تحبل. (رد المحتار: ۳/۱۷٦، سعيد).

The sum of all these excuses is that if some excuse arises for a person on an individual, personal basis, then there is room for such an act to the extent of that excuse. Once the excuse is removed, it is not correct even for him; and promoting it collectively among people at large is in any case undesirable and disliked. (Ḍabṭ-e Wilādat kī ʿAqlī o Sharʿī Ḥaythiyyat, by Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him), pp. 13–20.)

Kitāb al-Fatāwā states:

The form you have mentioned is called ʿazl in the Arabic language. To practise ʿazl merely out of the fear of who will provide the food and livelihood of any children who are born is not permissible; but practising ʿazl on account of a medical need is permissible. (کتاب الفتاوی: ٤/٤٤٤، متفرقاتِ نکاح)۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
