### TITLE
Giving conditional permission for an ATM to be installed

### QUESTION
A Muslim runs a petrol station. The land belongs to the petrol company itself and he has taken it on rent. A bank wishes to install an ATM machine at this petrol station — a machine into which people pay money and from which they draw money. The bank has laid down the condition that for the first three thousand people who use it each month this Muslim will receive nothing, but that after three thousand he will receive 75 cents for every use, whether money is paid in or drawn out. Is it permissible to enter into such an arrangement or not?

### ANSWER
A contract of this kind appears to be sound and valid on two grounds:

(1) It is a conditional gratuity — that is, attaching a condition to an act of kindness. Granting free use up to three thousand is a gratuity, and making it conditional upon a lease, so that the bank pays for the excess, turns it into a conditional gratuity. On this ground the contract is permissible and valid.

(2) It is a contract of hire in which a condition has been stipulated; but because of custom, and because it does not lead to dispute, this condition does not invalidate the contract.

Consider what al-Hidāyah states:

إلا أن يكون متعارفاً لأن العرف قاضٍ على القياس. (الهداية: ۳/۵۹، باب البيع الفاسد).

Fuller detail has already been given in the Book of Hire.

Consider the evidence for the conditional gratuity.

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim records:

عن أنس رضي الله عنه أن جاراً لرسول الله ﷺ فارسياً كان طيب المرق فصنع لرسول الله ﷺ ثم جاء يدعوه، فقال: وهذه لعائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها فقال: لا، فقال رسول الله ﷺ: لا، فعاد يدعوه، فقال رسول الله ﷺ: وهذه، قال: لا، قال رسول الله ﷺ: لا، ثم عاد يدعوه، فقال رسول الله ﷺ: وهذه، قال: نعم في الثالثة، فقاما يتدافعان حتى أتيا منزله. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۱۷٦).

The gist of the above hadith is that a man invited the Messenger of Allah ﷺ to a meal. He ﷺ made it conditional upon the Mother of the Believers ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her) being included as well; the man refused, but on the third occasion he gave his consent, and so he ﷺ went along together with ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her).

Similarly, Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) bought a camel saddle from ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him) and asked ʿĀzib to tell his son al-Barāʾ (may Allah be pleased with him) to carry the saddle along with him. ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him) said: no, only on the condition that you relate to us the account of the Emigration. So Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) related the account of the Emigration, and al-Barāʾ (may Allah be pleased with him) carried the saddle.

Consider what Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī records:

عن البراء رضي الله عنه قال: اشتري أبو بكر رضي الله عنه من عازب رضي الله عنه رحلاً بثلاثة عشر درهماً، فقال أبو بكر رضي الله عنه: لعازب رضي الله عنه مر البراء فليحمل إلى رحلي، فقال عازب: لا، حتى تحدثنا كيف صنعت أنت ورسول الله ﷺ حين خرجتما من مكة والمشركون يطلبونكم، قال: ارتحلنا

الخ. (رواه البخاري ۱/۵۱۵، في مناقب المهاجرين).

These two hadiths show that a conditional gratuity is permissible. A conditional gratuity means attaching a condition to an act of kindness, or stipulating some benefit for oneself in a transaction. The case in question takes exactly this form: the bank asked for permission to install the ATM machine, and the man running the petrol station said, very well, I give permission free of charge, but after the first three thousand you will pay me 75 cents for every use. The bank accepted this, and so the arrangement is valid.
