### TITLE

The ruling on receiving a prize from a company for success in cricket

### QUESTION

Some Muslims play cricket, on account of which they receive a monthly salary. Then, when they are successful in a match against other countries, some companies give them a prize. So is it permissible or not to take this prize, given that generally the companies are involved in interest-based business? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER

If a Muslim cricketer receives a prize from non-Muslim companies which are involved in interest and the like, then to take it and to use it is correct, because non-Muslims become the owners of wealth acquired through interest and the like. Moreover, this prize is from the company, that is, from a third party, and for this reason it will also fall outside of gambling, and to take it will be permissible and correct.
Consider: Al-Hidāyah states:

ولأبي حنيفة رحمه الله أن أهل الذمة لا يلتزمون أحكامنا في الديانات وفيما يعتقدون خلافه في المعاملات الخ. (الهداية: ۲/۳۳۸).
وفيه أيضاً: وإذا باع المسلم خمراً وأخذ ثمنها، وعليه دين، فإنه يكره لصاحب الدين أن يأخذه، وإن كان البائع نصرانياً فلا بأس به. (الهداية: ٤/٤۷۰).

ʿAllāmah al-Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Al-Mabsūṭ:

وأما الخمر والخنزير فقد قيل: الحرمة بخطاب خاص في حق المسلمين وهو قوله تعالى: ﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا إنما الخمر والميسر﴾ [المائدة: ۹۰] إلى قوله تعالى: ﴿فهل أنتم منتهون﴾ [المائدة: ۹۱]، وقيل: ليس من ضرورة الحرمة سقوط المالية والتقوم فالمال قد يكون حراماً وقد يكون حلالاً وإنما تنبني المالية على التمول وهم يتمولون ذلك. (المبسوط: ۵/۳۹، إدارة القرآن).

Muntakhabāt Niẓām al-Fatāwā records:
Non-Muslims carry on interest-based business and interest-based dealings among themselves; they also conclude many void and corrupt sales; they make and sell liquor and take its price; they also sell swine and take its price; and then they give this money to Muslims as wages, as prizes and so forth. Despite it being known that all this money is unlawful, taking this money is reckoned in the Sharīʿah to be permissible and not disliked, so long as it is not certain that the very thing he is selling is the very same thing which he brought by stealing or by robbery, or that the price he is giving is the very same money which he brought, for instance, by stealing or by robbery (that is to say, if it is the very same property of a theft, a robbery and the like, then it is not permissible). In short, to take all of this and to use it all remains correct. Now the question arises as to why the ruling of permissibility applies. There are several reasons for it, and several principles of the Sharīʿah are its basis: for example, (a) by a change of ownership there occurs, in ruling, a change of the very substance; or (b) the principle of admixture and consumption, which is accepted. In the case of a department whose men of authority are purely non-Muslim, or are mixed, and supreme power is in the hands of a non-Muslim, and he on his own account carries on interest-based business, earns a profit and obtains some amount, and they give it as a voluntary gift to some Muslim employee, then taking it will remain permissible and, coming under the heading of a prize, will be reckoned as wholesome and lawful.
