### TITLE
The meaning of annulment and separation, and the woman's power to end the marriage tie

### QUESTION
The pure sharia has given the full right of divorce to the man; a woman is not authorised to bring a divorce into effect upon herself of her own accord. But is there any form in which a woman too has some power to bring the marriage tie to an end? If there is, kindly set it out in detail and earn a great reward.

### ANSWER
If at some stage after the marriage bond has been tied the life of husband and wife passes beyond the bounds of happiness and becomes unbearable, and living together and sharing a life becomes impossible, then, just as the pure sharia has given the man the right to end the marriage tie by divorce while remaining within the limits of the religion, so too the woman has the right to end the marital relationship by khulʿ or by annulment and separation.

At the same time, however, it has given a warning that she must not misuse this rule by procuring a separation needlessly over some trifling grievance. Thus the Noble Prophet ﷺ said:

"أيما امرأة اختلعت من زوجها من غير بأس لم ترح رائحة الجنة". (رواه الترمذي: ۱/۲۲٦، باب ما جاء في المختلعات).

He also said:

"أيما امرأة سألت زوجها طلاقاً في غير ما بأس به فحرام عليها رائحة الجنة". (رواه الترمذي: ۱/۲۲٦، باب ما جاء في المختلعات).

Translation: "The woman who seeks a divorce from her husband without cause and without occasion — the fragrance of Paradise will be forbidden to her."

Yes: under compulsion, and in a critical situation in which no other lawful remedy remains, the woman will have the right to demand annulment and separation.

Tafrīq (separation) is the term for bringing the marriage tie to an end.

Separation is of two kinds: (1) that in which the judgement of a qāḍī is a condition; (2) that in which the judgement of a qāḍī is not a condition.

(1) The forms of separation in which the judgement of a qāḍī is a condition are as follows:

(1) That the marriage was contracted with someone who is not an equal match.

(2) That the marriage was contracted with an extraordinary shortfall in the dower — that is, with gross undervaluation.

(3) That a girl who was a minor exercises the option of puberty.

(4) That the husband does not fulfil the rights of marriage.

(5) That the husband is incapable of intercourse.

(6) That the husband is afflicted with leprosy, vitiligo, AIDS or some similar harmful disease.

(7) That the husband is insane. (8) That the husband is missing without trace. (9) That the husband is absent though not missing.

(10) That the husband does not pay maintenance despite being able to. (11) That the husband is incapable of paying maintenance.

(12) That the husband causes his wife suffering and beats her severely. (13) That discord (shiqāq) exists between the spouses.

(14) That the man contracted the marriage by deceiving the woman about his condition.

(15) Separation on account of the prohibition arising from affinity. (If the spouses themselves adopt a mutual renunciation, the judgement of a qāḍī is not a condition; otherwise the judgement of a qāḍī is necessary.)

(16) Separation on account of the invalidity of the marriage. (If the spouses themselves adopt a mutual renunciation, the judgement of a qāḍī is not a condition; otherwise the judgement of a qāḍī is necessary.)

(2) The forms of separation in which the judgement of a qāḍī is not a condition are as follows:

(1) Once affinity is established, the husband's adopting a mutual renunciation is enough to end the marriage.

(2) In an invalid marriage, either spouse's adopting a mutual renunciation is enough to end the marriage.

(3) Separation on account of īlāʾ (a vow of abstinence). (4) Separation on account of the husband's apostasy.

For details see: (Majmūʿah Qawānīn-e Islāmī, pp. 187–208, the law of annulment and separation; Kitāb al-Faskh wa-al-Tafrīq by Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ṣamad Raḥmānī, deputy Amīr-e Sharīʿat of Bihar; Al-Ḥīlah al-Nājizah, part two, Dār al-Ishāʿat, Deoband; Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil, volume three, Kutub Khānah Naʿīmiyyah; and Ṭibbī Akhlāqiyyāt by Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām, Idārat al-Qurʾān.)

The conditions of judicial authority, and the rulings concerning a body of Muslims or the Jamʿiyyat al-ʿUlamāʾ.

In most forms of annulment and separation the judgement of a qāḍī is a condition; but where no sharia judge is present, the decision of the Jamʿiyyat al-ʿUlamāʾ or of a body of Muslims standing in his place is also sufficient — though certain conditions and regulations must be observed. Moreover, not just anyone at all is qualified to exercise judicial authority; for that too there are conditions, which are as follows.

An explanation concerning the office of judge:

Qaḍāʾ is to give judgement justly in people's disputes in accordance with the law sent down by Allah Most High.

It is necessary that a qāḍī's decision not be contrary to the consensus.

A qāḍī's decision is not a mere report; rather, it has the status of the creation of a ruling.

The conditions necessary for fitness to hold judicial office:

(1) That he be of sound mind: no insane person or person of disordered faculties is fit for the office of judge.

(2) That he be of full age: the appointment of a minor is not valid either.

(3) That he be a Muslim: a non-Muslim cannot be made a qāḍī, and if a judge is a non-Muslim and gives a decision, it will not take effect.

(4) That he be free.

(5) That he be sighted: a blind man will not be made a qāḍī.

(6) That he be able to speak and not dumb, and able to hear and not deaf.

(7) That he not have been punished under the prescribed penalty for slanderous accusation.

Some of the qualities of a qāḍī are set out below:

He should be a man of learning and excellence, with a sound grasp of the lawful and the unlawful and of the other necessary rulings.

He should be conversant with the Book and the Sunnah and with the method of ijtihād, so that he may judge well in the cases and incidents that arise.

He should have the necessary knowledge of the Arabic language, of its various expressions and idioms, and of its literature.

He should be familiar with the language, the social life and custom, the idiom and the vocabulary of the country and region in which he serves.

He should not think it beneath him to seek the advice of scholars.

He should be characterised by uprightness. In the terminology of the jurists an upright person is one who avoids major sins and does not persist in minor ones.

His good deeds should outweigh his minor sins.

He should keep clear of whatever lays him open to suspicion.

He should not be hasty by temperament.

He should not be ill-mannered; he should be of firm character, wise, discerning and righteous.

(Summarised from Islāmī ʿAdālat by Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām Qāsmī, may Allah have mercy on him; and Īḍāḥ al-Nawādir by Muftī Shabbīr Aḥmad Qāsmī.)

The conditions for a body of Muslims:

There are several conditions for such a body to take the place of a qāḍī; a body in which these conditions are not met will not be valid in the sharia.

(1) It must be a body of at least three men; if one or two men give a decision, it is not valid.

(2) It is a condition that all the members of this body be upright; and an upright person is one who avoids all major sins, does not persist in minor ones, and, if some sin is committed, repents at once. Hence one who devours interest, one who takes bribes, one who shaves his beard, one who tells lies, and one who neglects the prayer cannot be a member of the body. (If unfortunately the influential people in some place are not religious, then this arrangement may be made: those influential persons should delegate authority to a few religious people, so that in the sharia the decision is attributed to the religious body, while those influential persons receive the reward of their effort.)

(3) The participation of scholars in the decision is necessary and is a condition; the decision of a body of laymen alone cannot take the place of a qāḍī's ruling. Ideally, therefore, all the members of the body should be men of learning; and if that is not possible, at least one scholar with a grasp of practical affairs must be made a member of the body, and the other members should form their opinion after thoroughly understanding every aspect of the case from that scholar. And if in some place even this is not possible, then it is essential that the members of the body, after completing the proceedings of the case, ask verifying scholars for the ruling on each particular point and that the decision be given in accordance with their fatwa. If this is not done, and laymen give a decision merely on their own opinion, then that ruling will not take effect and the decision will be altogether useless and invalid, even if the decision happens to accord with the sharia.

(4) The fourth condition is that all the members of the body of Muslims give a unanimous decision. If their opinions differ and they wish to decide on the basis of a majority, that decision will not be valid; so if the members remain divided, the case should be dismissed.

A useful point: if, because of a difference of opinion, no order of separation could be made on some application, that application is not rejected for ever. Rather, the petitioner has the right, should the state of the matter change or the severity of the need increase, to submit her application again; and if on the second application the members' opinion is unanimous, the separation should be effected. (Al-Ḥīlah al-Nājizah, p. 135, "The form of a qāḍī's jurisdiction in India", Dār al-Ishāʿat, Deoband; and likewise in Islāmī Fiqh, 2/261.)

The conditions for the arbitrators:

In the view of the Mālikīs the arbitrators have the authority to effect a separation; in the view of the majority of scholars, however, they do not have this right.

Since in the chapter of annulment and separation the Hanafis have given their fatwa on the position of the Mālikīs, in the Hanafi view too the arbitrators may effect a separation. Some detail on this, together with the conditions for the arbitrators, has already been given under the question of discord (shiqāq), and may be consulted there.

One further passage may be consulted. Al-Fiqh al-Islāmī wa Adillatuh states:

والحكمان: حران مسلمان ذكران عدلان مكلفان فقيهان عالمان بالجمع والتفريق لأن التحكيم يفتقر إلى الرأي والنظر، ويجوز أن يكونا من غير أهلهما؛ والأولي أن يكونا من أهلهما؛ لأن القرابة ليست شرطاً في الحكم ولا الوكالة. وينبغي لهما أن ينويا الإصلاح لقوله تعالى: ﴿إن يريدا إصلاحاً يوفق الله بينهما﴾ وأن يلطفا القول، وأن ينصفا، ويرغبا ويخوفا، ولا يخصا بذلك أحد الزوجين دون الآخر، ليكون أقرب للتوفيق بينهما.

وينفذ عند المالكية تصرف الحكمين في أمر الزوجين بما رأياه من تطليق أو خلع، من غير إذن الزوج ولا موافقة الحاكم، بعد أن يعجز عن الإصلاح بينهما، وإذا حكما بالفراق فهي طلقة بائنة. (الفقه الإسلامي وأدلته: ۷/۳٤۰. ۳٤۱، دار الفكر).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
