### TITLE
Dividing the Qur'an into parts and sections

### QUESTION
Who fixed the thirty parts (juzʾ) of the Qur'an, and why? Some critics have written in certain pamphlets of theirs that this is what the verse ﴿كما أنزلنا على المقتسمين الذين جعلوا القرآن عضين﴾ refers to — that is, "We sent down punishment upon those who tore their scriptures into pieces" — and that this too is a breaking up of the Qur'an. They point out that a part has even been made to begin at ﴿ربما يود الذين كفروا﴾, while the natural stopping place, the beginning of the surah one line earlier, was passed over.

### ANSWER
An indication for dividing the Qur'an into thirty parts — an indication that is as good as an explicit statement — is found in the Qur'an itself. There is a long hadith in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, part of which is that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ gave this advice to ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ (may Allah be pleased with him):

"اقرأ القرآن في شهر قال: إني أطيق أكثر فما زال حتى قال: في ثلاث". (صحيح البخاري: ۲/۲٦۵، باب صوم يوم وإفطار يوم).

That is, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ wished ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ (may Allah be pleased with him) to complete the Qur'an in one month. If the Qur'an is spread over thirty days, thirty parts result, because when a month is mentioned, what is ordinarily and usually meant by it is thirty days. If a woman's husband dies, her ʿiddah (waiting period) is one hundred and thirty days — unless she is pregnant, in which case it lasts until she gives birth; or unless the death occurred on the first of a lunar month, in which case it will be four months and ten days by the lunar calendar. If a woman who has despaired of menstruation is divorced, her waiting period is 90 days, unless the divorce was given on the first of a lunar month, in which case the lunar reckoning applies, whether the month turns out to be 29 days or 30. This is because when the Prophet ﷺ decided to keep apart from his wives for a month and came down from the upper room after 29 days, his blessed wives were surprised, since in their minds a month meant 30 days. But the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told them that this month was one of 29 days; and since the īlāʾ (the oath to keep apart) had evidently begun on the first of the month, that month was 29 days.

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī states:

عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنه قال عمر: فاعتزل النبي ﷺ من أجل ذلك، الحديث وكان قد قال: ما أناْ بداخل عليهن شهراً، من شدة موجدته عليهن حين عاتبه الله فلما مضت تسع وعشرون دخل على عائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها فبدأ بها، فقالت له عائشة: إنك أقسمت ألا تدخل علينا شهراً، وإنا أصبحنا بتسع وعشرين ليلة أعدها عداً، فقال النبي ﷺ: الشهر تسع وعشرون، وكان ذلك الشهر تسعاً وعشرين. (رواه البخاري: ۲/۳۳۵).

وعن أنس رضي الله عنه قال: إلى رسول الله ﷺ من نسائه وكانت انفكت رجله في مشربه تسعاً وعشرين ليلة ثم نزل فقالوا يا رسول الله آليت شهراً فقال: إن الشهر يكون تسعة وعشرين. (صحيح البخاري: ۱/۲۵٦).

As for who first fixed this division, that is difficult to say with certainty.

Mawlānā Mufti Taqi says:

It is difficult to say with certainty who made this division into thirty parts. Some hold that ʿUthmān (may Allah be pleased with him) had the Qur'an written in 30 separate booklets when he prepared the official copies, but this humble one has not been able to find any evidence for that in the works of the earlier scholars. ʿAllāmah Badr al-Dīn al-Zarkashī, however, has written that the 30 parts of the Qur'an have long been well known and are in regular use in the copies of the Qur'an kept in the madrasas. Al-Burhān: 1/250. (ʿUlūm al-Qur'ān by Mawlānā Mufti Taqi, p. 196).

Manāhil al-ʿIrfān states:

منهم من قسم القرآن ثلاثين قسماً وأطلقوا على كل قسم منها اسم الجزء بحيث لا يخطر بالبال عند الإطلاق غيره. (مناهل العرفان: ۱/٤۰۲).

The verse ﴿كما أنزلنا على المقتسمين الذين جعلوا القرآن عضين﴾ does not carry the meaning that some people have understood from it. Its meaning is rather this: We have sent down the Book upon you just as We sent down a Book upon those who divided up their scriptures, accepting whatever part agreed with their desires and rejecting the rest. Or the meaning is: We warn you against a punishment like the one We sent down upon those who carved the Qur'an up — some calling it magic, some poetry, some soothsaying, and so on. They would post their agents on the various roads leading into Makkah, and one of them would keep telling the new arrivals that the Qur'an was magic and the Prophet ﷺ a magician, while another kept saying that the Qur'an was poetry and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ a poet or a soothsayer. The verse does not mean that dividing the Qur'an into parts for convenience, in the light of the hadith, is a reprehensible innovation. The Companions themselves made sections (aḥzāb) of the Qur'an for their own convenience and as a fixed daily portion of recitation. Did the Companions do something wrong in this too? Certainly not. Sunan Ibn Mājah has a long narration, part of which is this:

قال أوس: فسألت أصحاب رسول الله ﷺ كيف تحزبون القرآن قالوا: ثلاث، وخمس وسبع وتسع وإحدى عشرة وثلاث عشرة، وحزب المفصل. (سنن ابن ماجه: ٩٥).

وفي إسناده عبد الله بن عبد الرحمن بن يعلى الطائفي وفيه ضعف.

The gist of this is that the first section contains al-Baqarah, Āl ʿImrān and al-Nisāʾ; the second al-Māʾidah, al-Anʿām, al-Aʿrāf, al-Anfāl and Barāʾah; the third Yūnus, Hūd, Yūsuf, al-Raʿd, Ibrāhīm, al-Ḥijr and al-Naḥl; the fourth Banī Isrāʾīl, al-Kahf, Maryam, Ṭā Hā, al-Anbiyāʾ, al-Ḥajj, al-Muʾminūn, al-Nūr and al-Furqān; the fifth al-Shuʿarāʾ, al-Naml, al-Qaṣaṣ, al-ʿAnkabūt, al-Rūm, Luqmān, al-Sajdah, al-Aḥzāb, Sabaʾ, Fāṭir and Yā Sīn; the sixth al-Ṣāffāt, Ṣād, al-Zumar, the seven Ḥawāmīm, Muḥammad, al-Fatḥ and al-Ḥujurāt; and the seventh from Qāf to the end.

Someone has indicated this arrangement with the mnemonic "famī bi-shawq":

F for al-Fātiḥah, M for al-Māʾidah, Y for Yūnus, B for Banī Isrāʾīl, Sh for al-Shuʿarāʾ, W for wa'l-Ṣāffāt, and Q for Sūrat Qāf to the end.

Just as these sections are perfectly sound, so dividing the Qur'an into parts is no innovation either. In any case, it would only be an innovation if people were to regard the division itself as a source of reward, holding that reciting one particular part carries such and such a fixed reward and another part a different one — and nobody claims that.

As for beginning a part at ﴿ربما يود الذين﴾ when a new surah begins one line earlier: in the copies of the Qur'an printed in the non-Arab lands someone counted the letters or words and took this to be the start of the part, whereas in the Qur'ans printed in Arabic lands the part is made to begin with the start of the surah, a difference of a few words either way not being considered worth bothering about. In my view this second approach is the correct one.
