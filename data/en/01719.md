### TITLE
A vow in which both the well-off and the poor are included

### QUESTION
A student vowed that if he passed his examination he would feed all the students of the madrasah for the sake of Allah. Has a vow come about by this or not? And is the word "Allah" necessary in a vow or not?

### ANSWER
On this question Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānawī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) has written that the vow does not take effect to the extent of the well-off but does take effect to the extent of the poor, whereas Ḥaḍrat Muftī Kifāyat Allāh Ṣāḥib has written that the vow is neither valid nor binding. On the face of it there is a conflict between the rulings of Ḥaḍrat Thānawī and Ḥaḍrat Muftī Kifāyat Allāh. They may be reconciled in this way: if only the poor were in the mind of the one making the vow, then the vow has taken effect; but if he had both the wealthy and the poor in mind, then the vow has not taken effect.

Consider what Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

A questioner asked: "If a certain purpose of mine is achieved, I will slaughter a cow in the name of Allah Most High and feed the people of the neighbourhood" — but in the neighbourhood both wealthy people and poor people live. Will the vow have to be fulfilled or not?

Answer:

في الدر المختار: نذر التصدق على الأغنياء لم يصح ما لم ينو أبناء السبيل. وفيه: ولو قال: إن برئت من مرضي هذا ذبحت شاة أو على شاة أذبحها

الخ.

From this transmitted text it appears that the vow has not taken effect to the extent of the well-off but has taken effect to the extent of the poor, so feeding the poor will be necessary. And if the well-off have eaten, one must see whether he had food prepared only to the measure of the poor's share or more than that: in the first case it is not correct for the well-off to eat, and in the second case it is correct. (امداد الفتاوی: ۲/۵٦۰ ۔ و امداد الاحکام: ۳/۳۳) ۔

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

A person named Zayd, having been struck by illness, made a vow that if the One who alone grants healing granted him complete health he would feed the worshippers of the mosque opposite, or of such-and-such a mosque. Now Zayd, whether after recovering or before recovering, has resolved to distribute the thing vowed among all the worshippers. The question is whether the rich and well-off who join in the prayer may eat these sweets of the vow or not.

(Answer): If feeding food or sweets to all the worshippers of that mosque was intended as a gift or as merely allowing them to partake, then this is not an act of devotion intended for its own sake. And if it was meant as charity, then, since the worshippers include both the well-off and the needy, and charity given to a well-off person is not valid, this vow is not valid — that is, it is not binding. If he now feeds them the food or the sweets it will be a voluntary donation, and if there is no intention of charity in this donation, it is permissible for both the well-off and the poor to eat.

نذر التصدق على الأغنياء لم يصح ما لم ينو أبناء السبيل. (الدر المختار).
قلت: ولعل وجه عدم الصحة في الأول عدم كونها قربة، الخ. (رد المحتار).
ولو قال: إن فعلت كذا فلله على أن أضيف جماعة قرابتي فحنث لا يلزمه شيء.
الفتاوی الہندیۃ: ۲/٦٦ ۔ (کفایت المفتی: ۲/۲٤٦) ۔

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

To the extent of the share of the well-off the vow has not taken effect, so fulfilling it is not obligatory; to the extent of the share of the poor the vow is valid, and fulfilling it is obligatory, and it is not permissible for the well-off to eat from it. (احسن الفتاوی: ۵/٤۹۰) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
