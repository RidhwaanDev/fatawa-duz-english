### TITLE
The ruling on a contract of hire being concluded over permissible ownerless things

### QUESTION
Some people go to catch fish and take some boys along with them for the purpose of help, whose responsibility it is to prepare the fish-hook, attach the bait, cast the feed and so forth. Sometimes these boys engaged for a wage catch fish using the hirer's equipment and give them to the hirer. So who will be the owner of these fish — the hirer, or the boys who caught the fish? For catching fish is a permissible ownerless activity, and hire is not permissible in respect of permissible ownerless things.

### ANSWER
In the case in question, if the boys were engaged as labourers and a wage was fixed for them on a daily or monthly basis, and one of their tasks is that they will catch fish and give them to the owner, then this is a hire of time; therefore the fish will belong to the hirer. Consider what is recorded in Sharḥ al-Majallah:

يسوغ الاحتطاب من أشجار الجبال المباحة كائناً من كان ويملك الحطب بمجرد الاحتطاب أي بجمعه، والربط ليس بشرط، المحتطب يملك الحطب بنفس الاحتطاب ولا يحتاج إلى أن يشده ويجمعه حتى يثبت له الملك. (شرح المجلة: ٤/۱۸۸، المادة: ۱۲۵۳).

In the view of the jurists, partnership is not valid in gathering firewood, catching fish, drawing water and other permissible ownerless activities; but if the work is such that skill is necessary in it, then it will fall under the category of a partnership of crafts and will be permissible.

قال في الدر المختار: فصل في الشركة الفاسدة لا تصح شركة في احتطاب واحتشاش واصطياد واستقاء وسائر مباحات، كاجتناء ثمار من جبال وطلب معدن من كنز وطبخ آجر من طين مباح لتضمنها الوكالة والتوكيل في أخذ المباح لا يصح، وما حصله أحدهما فله وما حصلاه معاً فلهما، نصفين إن لم يعلم ما لكل، قوله من طين مباح، فإن كان الطين أو النورة أو سهلة الزجاج مملوكاً فاشتركا على أن يشتريا ذلك ويطبخاه ويبيعاه جاز وهو كشركة الوجوه. (الدر المختار: ٤/۳۲۵، سعيد). (وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ۲/۳۳۲).

It is recorded in ʿIṭr-i Hidāyah: Every permissible ownerless thing may enter into ownership through taking possession of it; therefore jewels, wood, fruit, flowers, grass, water, animals, fish, pebbles, stones and all minerals become owned after being taken into possession, and before they come into anyone's possession every person has the right to take possession of them. (ʿIṭr-i Hidāyah, p. 313).

Yes — if the hirer engages someone for a wage to gather permissible ownerless things and specifies the time, then this hire is permissible, and the firewood will belong to the hirer while the employee will receive his wage. Consider what is mentioned in Sharḥ al-Majallah:

إذا استأجر شخص أجيراً لا جل جمع الأحطاب المتكسرة أو إمساك الصيد فما جمعه من الحطب أو أمسكه من الصيد فهو للمستأجر، لأن الأجير عامل لمستأجره. (شرح المجلة: ٤/۱۸۸، لمحمد خالد الأتاسي).

It is mentioned in Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah:

If you make an arrangement with a labourer in this way, that, for example, "I am to take this work from you for eight hours in a day, and I shall give, say, four rupees as the wage for the eight hours," and the labourer accepts it, then according to the Sharīʿah this arrangement is correct, and that firewood will be your property. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 25/119, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

It is recorded in Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil:

If he engages them as employees on a monthly or yearly basis and pays the wage for the time, whether fish are obtained or not, then this is permissible; the wage will be lawful and all the fish obtained will belong to the owner. And if he does not fix a time but rather hands the net over to the labourers and sends them out to catch fish, fixing a certain quantity of the fish as the wage — as is customary in our time — then this form is impermissible; this hire will be void, the fish will be the property of the one who caught them, and the owner of the net will receive the standard rental value of the net. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/226).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
