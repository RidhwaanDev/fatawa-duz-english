### TITLE
The farewell sermon on the last Friday of the blessed month of Ramadan

### QUESTION
In some localities the custom prevails that on the last Friday of the blessed month of Ramadan the preachers utter words of farewell, as though they were bidding Ramadan goodbye. Is doing this correct or not? Is there any evidence for it or not?

### ANSWER
Delivering a farewell sermon on the last Friday of the blessed month of Ramadan is not established from the generations whose goodness has been testified to. Yes, if some admonition is given in the sermon, and encouragement to make the most of the closing days, and a few words of sorrow are spoken at the departure of the blessed month of Ramadan, there is no great harm in it; but if the common people take it to be a sunnah or something binding, then abandoning it becomes binding. Consider what Majmūʿat al-Fatāwā states:

To utter "al-widāʿ" or "al-firāq" in the sermon of the last Friday of Ramadan, and to speak words of sorrow and leave-taking, is in itself a permissible matter; indeed, if these words become a cause of remorse and repentance in the listeners, reward may be hoped for. But this practice is not established in the first three generations. A sermon welcoming Ramadan at the end of Shaʿbān is, however, reported in the hadiths, and perhaps whoever invented this practice drew an analogy between the sermon at the end of Ramadan and the sermon of welcome. Yet to make a point of the farewell sermon as is current in this age, and to carry it to the degree of something binding, is not free of innovation. It is necessary for reliable scholars to abandon this insistence, so that the common people may be saved from the belief that this particular practice is recommended and a sunnah — indeed, from the belief that it is necessary. (Majmūʿat al-Fatāwā on the margin of Khulāṣat al-Fatāwā 4/329, Kitāb al-Karāhiyyah, al-Maktabah al-Rashīdiyyah)

Ashraf al-Aḥkām (the supplement to Imdād al-Fatāwā) records:

He said: on one of the last Fridays of Shaʿbān the Prophet ﷺ delivered a sermon (on the occasion of the coming of Ramadan) which he did not deliver on other Fridays. It is astonishing that the Muslims paid no attention to the sermon for which there is an explicit text and appointed no particular sermon for the last Friday of Shaʿbān, by which they would have been acting upon the Sunnah; instead of that they appointed a special farewell sermon for the last Friday of Ramadan, of which there is no trace anywhere in the hadith. Then such an attachment to it arose that without reciting this special sermon it is as though the Friday prayer has not taken place at all. Many people regard the farewell sermon as a necessary part of the last Friday of Ramadan; and what is most astonishing is that some people of learning too have been deceived and have fallen into a grave error, for they say that although appointing a special sermon for the last Friday is an innovation, yet since people generally gather because of it, it should be retained on the ground that it helps them assemble and is a means of performing the prayer — whereas this is a grave error and is, in a way, an objection against Allah and His Messenger ﷺ. (Ashraf al-Aḥkām, p. 126, Idārah Islāmiyyāt, Lahore)

وفي ردع الاخوان عن محدثات جمعة آخر رمضان: ومن الأمور المحدثة: ما شاع في أكثر بلاد الهند والدكن وغيرهما من تسمية خطبة الجمعة الأخيرة بخطبة الوداع، وتضمينها جملاً دالة على التحسر بذهاب ذلك الشهر، فيدرجون جملاً دالة على فضائل ذلك الشهر، ويقولون بعد جملة أو جملتين: الوداع والوداع، أو الفراق والفراق لشهر رمضان أو الوداع والوداع يا شهر رمضان، ونحو ذلك من الألفاظ الدالة على ذلك، ومنهم من يقرأ خطبة الوداع يوم عيد الفطر. وهذا المحدث لا يدرى من أي زمان حدث، وأين حدث؟ وكتب الفقه والحديث من المتقدمين والمتأخرين لا يوجد فيها أثر من ذلك (Radʿ al-Ikhwān ʿan Muḥdathāt Jumuʿah Ākhir Ramaḍān, Majmūʿah Rasāʾil al-Laknawī 2/369, Idārat al-Qurʾān Karachi)

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah records:

Delivering this farewell sermon is not established from the generations whose goodness has been testified to; the jurists have made no mention of reciting it. Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy Ṣāḥib Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him) has set out in detail, with proofs, that it is a forbidden innovation. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah 8/295, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah)

Bāqiyāt Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah records: to recite the farewell in the sermon is an innovation. (Bāqiyāt Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah, p. 186)

ʿAzīz al-Fatāwā states: the farewell sermon is not established from the predecessors. (ʿAzīz al-Fatāwā, vol. 1, p. 306, Dār al-Ishāʿat)
