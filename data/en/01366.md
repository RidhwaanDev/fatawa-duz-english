### TITLE
A second view on zakat on diamonds and precious stones

### QUESTION
A second view on zakat on diamonds and precious stones.

### ANSWER
Naye Masāʾil awr ʿUlamāʾ-e Hind ke Fayṣle records the following: "This question came up for discussion at a seminar of the Majmaʿ al-Fiqh al-Islāmī. In such a case hundreds of thousands in cash are kept safe in the form of diamonds and precious stones, and can be turned back into cash at any moment. In the course of the discussion two aspects emerged. The first is that diamonds and precious stones are not gold and silver, which are recognised as wealth that grows by its very nature; the person concerned does not deal in diamonds and precious stones as a trade either, nor was a formal intention of trade made at the time of purchase, so that they might be classed as growing wealth on the ground of being merchandise. This aspect requires that no zakat be due on them.

The second aspect is that diamonds and precious stones are not among the necessities of life. Men of capital, for purposes of their own, keep their money — in sums that are unusually large — safe in the form of diamonds and precious stones, and gain various advantages by doing so. It also gives them the assurance that, in the form of these diamonds and stones, 'ready cash' is with them at all times. The result is serious loss to the poor, because zakat is due on cash, whereas on the general principle it is not ordinarily due on diamonds and precious stones.

A good number of the scholars and muftis taking part in the seminar, keeping the first aspect in view, gave the opinion that in this particular case no zakat would be due on the value of the diamonds and precious stones kept in this way.

Another large group of scholars and muftis, keeping the second aspect in view, treated the diamonds and precious stones hoarded in this particular way as merchandise in the eye of the law and ruled that zakat is due on them. The names of those who held zakat to be due are as follows:

(1) Mawlānā Mujāhid al-Islām Qāsmī.

(2) Mawlānā Ṭayyib al-Raḥmān, Amīr-e Sharīʿat of Assam.

(3) Mawlānā Muftī ʿAzīz al-Raḥmān of Bombay.

(4) Mawlānā Iʿjāz Aḥmad Aʿẓamī.

(5) Mawlānā Mujīb Allāh Nadwī.

(6) Mawlānā Shams Pīrzādah.

(7) Mawlānā Anīs al-Raḥmān Qāsmī.

(8) Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Raḥīm of Bhopal.

(9) Mawlānā Muftī ʿAbd al-Raḥmān.

(10) Mawlānā Zubayr Aḥmad Qāsmī.

(11) Mawlānā Rafīq al-Manān. (For his detailed arguments see his paper in Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 7/498.)

(12) Mawlānā Muftī Nadhīd Aḥmad.

(13) Mawlānā Muḥammad Shuʿayb. (For his detailed arguments see his paper in Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 7/652.)

(14) Mawlānā ʿAtīq Aḥmad Qāsmī, and others."

In Fiqh al-Zakāh, Dr. Yūsuf al-Qaraḍāwī has also shown an inclination towards this view. See:

وينبغي أن يكون هذا هو حكم اللؤلؤ والياقوت والالماس وكل الأحجار الكريمة والجواهر النفيسة، فما اتخذ منها للحلية والزينة ولم يبلغ درجة الإسراف فلا زكاة فيه، وما جاوز المعتاد مجاوزة بينة فهو من الإسراف المحرم، ولا يصح أن يتمتع بالإعفاء من الزكاة وكذلك ما اتخذ بقصد الاكتناز جدير أن تجب فيه الزكاة، إذ اقتناؤه حينئذٍ حيلة للتهرب مما في المال من حق معلوم للسائل والمحروم، والنية هي الفيصل في هذا، ويدل عليها مجاوزة المعتاد. (فقه الزكاة: ۱/۳۱۰).

Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh has pointed in the same direction in Rāh-e ʿAmal. Consider the following:

One important question that has arisen in present circumstances is that some people convert their cash into diamonds and precious stones in order to protect their wealth and to avoid tax. The purpose of the conversion is not trade, but it is money in a changed form; so is zakat due on such diamonds and precious stones? One opinion is that zakat is not due on them, and this is the opinion of most scholars.

The second opinion is that, since the aim behind these diamonds and precious stones is to keep money safe and not to acquire the stones themselves, they are as good as cash held in a changed form, and so zakat should be due on them as well. In this writer's view the first opinion is the stronger, while the second rests on greater caution. It is therefore better to pay zakat on diamonds and precious stones that have been bought not to be worn but to safeguard capital. (Rāh-e ʿAmal, p. 797).
