### TITLE
Enforcing the will of a non-Muslim

### QUESTION
A non-Muslim passed away. He has a wife, and a sister and a sister's son; his sister and his sister's son, that is his nephew, are both Muslims. During his lifetime he drew up a will that his entire wealth would go to his sister, except for a particular sum which would go to the nephew. He left nothing for his disbelieving wife, and according to his religion (Tamil) there is nothing for the wife. He also appointed his Muslim sister as his executor. Now whatever decision the Muslim sister makes, the court will enforce it. The question now is: will such a will be enforced? And the disbelieving wife has married, although only two weeks have passed since the husband's death. بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, since in this country there are no Islamic rulings and no law of bequest, we cannot bind non-Muslims to Islamic rulings. On that basis, the will will be enforced exactly as the non-Muslim wrote it, that is, the wealth will be divided between the sister and the nephew according to his writing, and this bequest will not be restricted to one third.

Yes, if some non-Muslim is settled in a Muslim country in which the law of bequest according to Islam is in force, then, since he has bound himself to Islamic rulings and has accepted the bequest and the law of bequest, his bequest will be enforced only in one third. Whereas the case in question is entirely different from that. Note what Fatāwā Shāmī states:

قوله (لأن المستأمن كالذمي) فإذا أوصي لمسلم أو ذمي بجميع ماله جاز كما مر. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/٦۵۵، سعيد).

See further: (Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ: 7/335, Saʿīd; and Imdād al-Aḥkām: 4/386–390).
