### TITLE
Leaving one's wife for more than four months

### QUESTION
From an incident of Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) mentioned in Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah it appears that a man should not go out in the path of Allah for more than four or six months, leaving his wife behind. Now the question is: married scholars who go out with a group for a year — what is the ruling concerning them? Likewise, what is the ruling for unmarried scholars going out with a group for a year? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, the married scholars who go out with a group for a year go with the permission of their families, and this going is like people going to other countries for employment by mutual agreement and permission. In the sacred law there is no harm in it.

Consider the narration of Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah Most High be pleased with him). In Muṣannaf ʿAbd al-Razzāq it is stated:

عبد الرزاق عن ابن جريج قال: أخبرني من أصدق أن عمر رضي الله عنه وهو يطوف سمع امرأة وهي تقول:

تطاول هذا الليل وأخضل جانبه خ وأرقني إذ لا خليل ألاعبه

فلولا حذار الله لا شيء مثله خ لزعزع من هذا السرير جوانبه

فقال عمر رضي الله عنه: فما لك قالت: أغربت زوجي منذ أربعة أشهر وقد اشتقت إليه، فقال: أردت سوء ا ً، قالت: معاذ الله، قال: فأملكي نفسك، فإنما هو البريد إليه فبعث إليه ثم دخل حفصة فقال: إني سائلك عن أمر قد أهمني فأفرجيه عنه، كم تشتاق المرأة إلى زوجها فخفضت رأسها فاستحيت فقال: إن الله لا يستحيي من الحق فأشارت ثلاثة أشهر وإلا فأربعة فكتب عمر رضي الله عنه ألا تحبس الجيوش فوق أربعة أشهر. (مصنف عبد الرزاق، رقم: ۱۲۵۹۳، باب حق المرأة على زوجها وفي كم تشتاق).

In the chain of this narration one narrator is unknown; but another narration is sound. See:

عن عبد الله بن دينار عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه قال: خرج عمر بن الخطاب رضي الله عنه من الليل فسمع امرأة تقول:

فقال عمر بن الخطاب رضي الله عنه لحفصة بنت عمر: كم أكثر ما تصبر المرأة عن زوجها؟ فقالت: ستة أو أربعة أشهر، فقال عمر رضي الله عنه: لا أحبس الجيش أكثر من هذا. (السنن الكبرى للبيهقي ۹/۲۹، باب الامام لا يجمر بالغزي).

The scholars have offered two explanations of this incident: (1) Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) forbade compelling any soldier to remain, and fixed this dividing line — that is, after four months a husband should not be compelled to stay away; yes, if he stays away by mutual agreement there is no harm.

(2) This incident is based on precaution, and the matter will be dealt with according to the circumstances of each person — as Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Yūsuf Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) said, that everyone's circumstances differ, and each person should write down his circumstances and seek counsel. (Āp kē Masāʾil awr Un kā Ḥal 8/204.)

In another place he writes: if the holder of the right himself forgives it, or if Allah Most High has it forgiven by granting compensation for that right, what argument will be left to these unpaid advocates? And also this: why does this violation of rights occur to them only in the work of religion? People leave their wives and spend years upon years in unbelieving countries gathering the rubbish of this world; why does the philosophy of the violation of rights not occur to anyone then? (Āp kē Masāʾil awr Un kā Ḥal 8/191.)

In Maḥmūd al-Fatāwā it is recorded: learning the religion, teaching it and acting upon it is an obligation. One way of learning the religion is that a man enrols in the Arabic madrasas, studies the whole syllabus in due form and acquires knowledge by remaining in the service of the teachers. And for one who does not have that much time, or that much financial capacity, or whose age has passed, or who has other excuses, one way of learning the religion is also to go out with the tablīghī group. Just as in the first case it is permissible and correct for a man to leave his home for a year or nine years or for more or less time — and this is established by the continuous practice of the pious predecessors and the elders — so too in the second case it is correct and permissible. (Maḥmūd al-Fatāwā 5/71.)

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
