### TITLE
Verification of the chain of transmission of the Naqshbandī order

### QUESTION
Among the orders of taṣawwuf there is also the Naqshbandī order. Some people raise the objection that the chain of transmission of this order is broken. What we wish to ascertain from you is: is the chain of this order broken or not? If it is broken, what answer do those attached to this order give? Kindly explain and be rewarded.

### ANSWER
In the case in question the chain of the Naqshbandī order is broken on account of the several reasons given below. But it should also be borne in mind that the remembrance of Allah Most High is established by explicit texts, and in a remembrance established by explicit text there is no need for an unbroken chain from the elders of an order; its methods, numbers and so forth are all matters of independent judgement, so if these matters of independent judgement are not established by an unbroken chain there is no harm in it — just as the acquisition of knowledge is established by explicit text while its methods are matters of independent judgement, in which every madrasah has its own judgement and procedure. For this reason this method is not open to objection. For example, if in this age some people perform the remembrance by joining voice to voice while the elders of old did not do this, then this too is a matter of independent judgement and no one can be blamed for it, because the purpose of joining the voices is to engage the heart.

The details of the break in the chain have been set out by Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Sayyid Zawwār Ḥusayn Shāh Ṣāḥib (raḥimahullāh) in his book ʿUmdat al-Sulūk. It seems appropriate to quote a portion of that very book. Consider:

In the chain which is generally presented there is a break. The details are that in the Naqshbandī order two chains are well known and famous.

(1) The masters of the exalted Naqshbandī Uwaysī order: that is, in this path some elders obtained spiritual benefit from the sacred souls of others and received authorisation from them.

Since in the Uwaysī attribution the intermediaries are fewer and there is no doubt or uncertainty about the attribution being strong and sound, the noble shaykhs (raḥimahumullāh) generally state the chain of this very Uwaysī attribution, and this has become the general practice. But it does not follow from this that the chain of physical companionship of these masters is broken. Thus all the elders have written that Ḥaḍrat Shaykh Abū ʿAlī Fārmadī (raḥimahullāh), despite having remained in the company of many shaykhs (such as Abū al-Qāsim al-Qushayrī (raḥimahullāh) and Abū al-Qāsim Gūrgānī (raḥimahullāh)), received spiritual benefit from Ḥaḍrat Abū al-Ḥasan Kharaqānī (raḥimahullāh), and the latter from the spirituality of Ḥaḍrat Bāyazīd Basṭāmī (raḥimahullāh) — the birth of Shaykh Abū al-Ḥasan took place a long time after the death of Shaykh Bāyazīd (raḥimahullāh). In the same way Shaykh Bāyazīd (raḥimahullāh) obtained the training of the spiritual path from the spirituality of Ḥaḍrat Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq (raḥimahullāh), whereas it is established by sound transmission that the birth of Ḥaḍrat Bāyazīd (raḥimahullāh) occurred after the death of Ḥaḍrat Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq (raḥimahullāh). (عمدۃ السلوک، حصہ دوم، ص 358-359، ط: زوار اکیڈمی).

The upshot is that this break occurs in three places: (1) Khwājah Abū ʿAlī Fārmadī [who is mentioned at number 8 in the chart] did not meet Khwājah Abū al-Ḥasan al-Kharaqānī [who is mentioned at number 7]. (2) Then Khwājah Abū al-Ḥasan al-Kharaqānī did not meet Ḥaḍrat Shaykh Bāyazīd [who is at number 6]. (3) Ḥaḍrat Bāyazīd Basṭāmī (raḥimahullāh) did not meet Ḥaḍrat Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq [who is at number 5].

This chain reaches up to the Commander of the Believers, Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhu).
