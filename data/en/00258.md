### TITLE
Whether falling asleep in a seat breaks the ablution

### QUESTION
If a person falls asleep while sitting in the seat of a car or an aeroplane, does his ablution break or not?

### ANSWER
When a person falls asleep leaning against a wall or against something else, there are two cases.

If his backside is settled firmly on the ground, there are two positions among the Hanafis.

(1) The first position is that his ablution breaks. The author of al-Qudūrī, the author of al-Hidāyah, and Imam al-Ṭaḥāwī (may Allah have mercy on him) adopted this position.

(2) The second position is that his ablution does not break. This is the ẓāhir al-riwāyah (the standard transmitted position of the school), and most scholars have taken it; it is what has been adopted in Shāmī, al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah, al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq, Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ and other books.

Al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

ولو نام مستنداً إلى ما لو أزيل عنه لسقط إن كانت مقعدته زائلة عن الأرض نقض بالإجماع وإن كانت غير زائلة فالصحيح أنه لا ينقض هكذا في التبيين. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۲).

This question is also set out in detail in:

شامي: ۱/۱٤۱، وبدائع الصنائع: ۱/۳۱، والبحر الرائق: ۱/۳۸

In short, if the backside of the person who falls asleep is settled firmly on the seat, then according to the ẓāhir al-riwāyah his ablution does not break, and this is the sounder position. But if his backside has lifted off the ground, the ablution breaks.
