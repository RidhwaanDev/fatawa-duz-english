### TITLE
A gift without transfer of possession

### QUESTION
A man died in 2006. He had written in a will that after his death his estate should be divided among his heirs in accordance with the sharia. Nine months after this man's death one of his sons, Yaḥyā, laid claim to a piece of land in the estate, saying that his father had gifted this land to him during his lifetime on 2 March 2004. In proof of his claim he produced a document in which the gift is expressly set out, and on this document are the signatures of Yaḥyā's son Muḥammad and of a non-Muslim African woman; apart from these there is no other witness. Furthermore, Yaḥyā had not taken possession of this land, nor had he had it registered in his name; and beyond that, the other heirs know nothing at all about it. In the case in question, will this land be regarded as Yaḥyā's, or will all the heirs share in it?

### ANSWER
In the case described the gift was not completed. The reason is that for a gift to be complete the taking of possession is necessary, and possession did not take place. A second reason is that the testimony of the witnesses whose signatures appear on the written agreement is also not acceptable, since a son's testimony in favour of his father is not acceptable, and likewise a non-Muslim's testimony against a Muslim is not accepted. On this basis, the land left behind did not enter Yaḥyā's ownership, and it will therefore be divided among all the heirs according to the shares laid down by the sharia. Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وشرائط صحتها في الموهوب أن يكون مقبوضاً غير مشاع مميزاً غير مشغول

وتتم الهبة بالقبض الكامل. (الدر المختار: ۵/٦۸۸، ٦۹۰، كتاب الهبة، سعيد).

Fatāwā Hindiyyah states:

منها (شرائط الهبة) أن يكون الموهوب مقبوضاً حتى لا يثبت الملك للموهوب له قبل القبض. (الفتاوى الهندية: ٤/۳۷٤).

Fatāwā Hindiyyah also states:

ولا شهادة الولد لوالديه وأجداده وجداته من قبلهما وإن علو. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۳/٤٦۹ كتاب الشهادات، الفصل الثالث فيمن لا تقبل شهادته للتهمة).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

فيشترط الإسلام لو المدعي عليه مسلماً. (الدر المختار: ۵/٤٦۲، كتاب الشهادات، سعيد).

Fatāwā ʿUthmānī states:

In the sharia it is not necessary to draw up a written deed of gift; that is, a gift may also be made verbally. What is necessary, however, is that the person to whom the gift is made should take possession of the thing gifted. (Fatāwā ʿUthmānī: 3/443, Kitāb al-Hibah).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
