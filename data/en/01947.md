### TITLE
Counting the weight of the container in the weight of the goods sold

### QUESTION
A man bought ten kilos of rice or fertiliser in a box, but one kilo of that is the weight of the box. Is this permissible or not, since the container has been counted in along with the goods?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, since the weight written on the container is not made a condition, and in most cases the sale takes place by mutual handover with the consent of both parties (bayʿ al-taʿāṭī), it is permissible and valid.

See Al-Hidāyah, which states:

ينعقد بالتعاطي في النفيس والخسيس وهو الصحيح لتحقق المراضاة. (الهداية: ۳/۱۹).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states: What both the seller and the buyer have in view is that particular box or packet; the weight written on it is not made a condition of the sale, and so it is permissible to make use of the contents without weighing them. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 6/499).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states: Question: A shopkeeper weighs out sugar in a bag, although the bag has a price of its own and also has some weight, so that the customer receives that much less sugar. Is this giving and taking valid? Answer: If the one who takes and the one who gives are both content with it, it is valid. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 24/40, Maktabah Maḥmūdiyyah).

Maḥmūd al-Fatāwā states: Because of custom it will be understood that the weight of the goods sold together with the container is that much, and so it is valid. (Maḥmūd al-Fatāwā: 2/405).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
