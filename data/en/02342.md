### TITLE
The ruling on hunting animals whose meat is not eaten

### QUESTION
Some people go out hunting and also hunt lions, leopards, wolves and the like, even though these are among the unlawful animals; their purpose, however, is the hide or some other part of the body. Is there any scope in the sharīʿah for hunting these animals or not?

### ANSWER
Hunting is permissible and correct, whether the animal is one whose meat is eaten or one whose meat is not eaten, provided that some lawful and legitimate purpose of the hunter is bound up with the hunt. For example, if the object is to eat the meat of an animal whose meat is eaten, or if the feathers, hair, hide, horn, bone or the like of an animal whose meat is not eaten are the object and aim, or if the sole object is to repel harm, then there is scope for hunting. If, however, the object is nothing but amusement and idle sport and the wasting of time, then it is impermissible.

Consider the following. Al-Hidāyah states:

قال ويجوز اصطياد ما يؤكل لحمه من الحيوان وما لا يؤكل لا طلاق ما تلونا (أي: وإذا حللتم فاصطادوا)، والصيد لا يختص بمأكول اللحم، قال قائلهم:
صيد الملوك أرانب وثعالب

ولأن صيده سبب للانتفاع بجلده أو شعره أو ريشه أو لاستدفاع شره وكل ذلك مشروع. (الهداية: ٤/۵۱۵، كتاب الصيد).
وفي رد المحتار: قوله لنفع ما أي ولو قليلاً، والهرة لو مؤذية لا تضرب ولا تفرك أذنها بل تذبح. (رد المحتار: ٦/٤۷٤، سعيد).
