### TITLE
Trading in blood

### QUESTION
Is the sale of blood permissible or not?

### ANSWER
Blood is not property of legal value, so selling it is not permissible. There is, however, room for buying it alone in a situation of dire necessity, when it cannot be obtained free of charge. Muslims should establish blood banks and make arrangements for blood to be supplied free, so that buying and selling blood can be avoided as far as possible.

See Al-Hidāyah, which states:

وإذا كان أحد العوضين أو كلاهما محرماً فالبيع فاسد كالبيع بالميتة والدم. (الهداية: ۳/٤۹، باب البيع الفاسد).

Tabyīn al-Ḥaqāʾiq states:

لم يجز بيع الميتة والدم والخنزير

لعدم ركن البيع وهو مبادلة المال بالمال وبيع هذه الأشياء باطل. (تبيين الحقائق: ٤/۳٦۲).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

بطل بيع ما ليس بمال والمال ما يميل إليه الطبع ويجري فيه البخل والمنع فخرج تراب ونحوه كالدم المسفوح. (الدر المختار: ۵/۵۰، سعيد).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states: Buying and selling flowing blood is unlawful. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 6/522).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states: The scholars have permitted blood to be transfused where there is a necessity, but they have not permitted it to be sold. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/335).

Imdād al-Aḥkām states: What these statements imply is that if at some time blood too comes to have a price by custom, then buying and selling it is valid. (Imdād al-Aḥkām: 3/355).

Jawāhir al-Fiqh states: The sale of blood is not permissible; but in those circumstances, and on those conditions, in which it has been ruled permissible to give blood to a patient, if a person cannot obtain blood without payment then it is permissible for him to obtain it by paying a price — though it is not proper for the one giving the blood to take a price for it. (Jawāhir al-Fiqh: 7/47).

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states: The blood of lawful animals that flows out at the time of slaughter may not be bought or sold, and if someone does sell it, the money received for it may not be used. In the same way, selling human blood and using the price of it is also unlawful. Donating blood, however, is permissible: just as it is permissible when the need arises to suckle someone else's child, so too it is permissible when the need arises to save a life by donating blood — indeed, in some circumstances it even becomes necessary. (Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām: 1/48).

For further detail see: Jawāhir al-Fiqh: 7/44–50.
