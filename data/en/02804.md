### TITLE
The good meanings of the names Sudays, Shurraym, Nawfal, Zaydān and Bahlūl

### QUESTION
A man is considering one of the following names for his newborn child and asks what they mean: Sudays, Shurraym, Nawfal, Zaydān, Bahlūl. What meanings can they carry, and what does the diminutive form in names signify?

### ANSWER
(1) Sudays: it is the diminutive of sudus, and sudus is the name for a camel of a particular age. The camel has many fine qualities: it is obedient, it has great endurance, and it is strong, sturdy and full of spirit. In this light Sudays can mean obedient, patient, strong and high-spirited — all of them good meanings.

Sudus also denotes the number six, which among the Arabs, and in the light of hadith, is considered a perfect number, since six is both even and odd; it is the even of the even, that is four, and the even of the odd, that is six itself; and it comprises the deficient number and the perfect number, and the rational and the surd. So Sudays, which is the diminutive of sudus, carries an indication of perfection, like the perfect number.

راجع: شرح البخاري للعيني والشروح الأخرى، تحت حديث: "الإيمان بضع وستون شعبة".

Consider what al-Nihāyah fī Gharīb al-Athar states:

السديس: من الإبل ما دخل في السنة الثامنة وذلك إذا ألقي السن التي بعد الرباعية. (النهاية: ۲: ۸۹٦).

Imam al-Akhfash's al-Ikhtiyārayn states:

والسديس: السن التي وراء الرباعية. يقال: قد أسدست وهي سديس، وسدس يقول: حين بزلت. (ص ۲٦). (وكذا في معجم الوسيط، ص ٤۲۳).

Tafsīr ʿAzīzī records:

For all its bulk it is so submissive that if a child — indeed, if a mouse — takes hold of its halter, it will go wherever it is led. If you consider its back, it is as though a lofty throne were set upon four pillars; and for all that height, which a man's hand cannot even reach, a man may make it kneel and mount it whenever he wishes. It is so patient that it stays thirsty for ten days and never falls short in labour and hardship. Among all the animals the camel is famed for its spirit; no other animal has this. If you load it with goods it will carry away everything in a house, and if it is to be sent somewhere it runs the whole way and goes so far that no other animal can reach it. (Khulāṣah Tafsīr ʿAzīzī, Pārah ʿAmm, 4/245, Saʿīd).

(2) Shurraym: it is the diminutive of sharm, which means a bay — that is, as useful as a bay where boats can come and lie at anchor. So Shurraym means a refuge and a place of shelter.

وشرم من البحر: خليج منه. (الصحاح: ٤/۱۵۹۰، وتاج العروس: ۸/۱٤۲، ولسان العرب: ۷/۱۰۱).
والخليج: شرم من البحر وقال ابن سيده: هوما انقطع من معظم الماء، لأنه يجبذ منه. (تاج العروس: ۵/۵۳۱).

The diminutive is also used in names to convey affection and kindness. Consider what a noble hadith states:

عن أنس رضي الله عنه ان كان النبي ﷺ ليخالطنا حتى يقول لا خ لي صغير: يا أبا عمير ما فعل النغير وكان له نغير يلعب به فمات. متفق عليه.
قال: وفيه إباحة تصغير الأسماء، قلت: لأنه مبني على اللطف والشفقة. (مرقاة المفاتيح: ۹/۱۷۲، ط: إمداديه).

The necessary discussion of the diminutive has already been given earlier.

(3) Nawfal: the lexicographers give two good meanings for Nawfal: (1) one who gives lavishly; (2) a handsome young man.

النفل بالتحريك: الغنيمة، والهبة. النوفل: العطية، والنوفل: السيد المعطاء يشبهان بالبحر. (لسان العرب: ۱٤/۲٤۵، دار الباز).
وقال في تاج العروس (۸/۱٤۲): والنوفل: الشاب الجميل. وكذا في (الصحاح: ٤/۱٤۹۱، دار إحياء التراث العربي).

(4) Zaydān: this is the verbal noun of zāda, yazīdu, and its meaning is to increase; so the sense would be increase in goodness and blessing, and in right guidance and direction. See:

(تاج العروس: ۲/۳۲۲، والصحاح: ۲/٤۱٦، دار إحياء التراث).

(5) Bahlūl: (1) Bahlūl means a noble and modest man; (2) an honoured man who combines every good quality. Ghiyāth al-Lughāt states: the name of a gnostic, and a cheerful chief.

البهلول من الرجال الضحاك، والبهلول العزيز الجامع لكل خير عن

. والبهلول الحيي الكريم. (تاج العروس: ۷/۲۳۹، والصحاح: ٤/۱۳٤۸، ولسان العرب: ۱۱/۷۳، دار الفكر).
قال في اللطائف في اللغة: أما البهلول: فالسيد الجامع لكل خير. (ص: ۱٤٦).
قال في فقه اللغة: البهلول السيد الحسن البشر. (ص: ۵٦۲).
