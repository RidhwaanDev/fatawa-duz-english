### TITLE
Prizes for solving a newspaper puzzle

### QUESTION
Newspapers often carry announcements that whoever explains the meaning of a certain thing, or solves a certain puzzle, will receive a prize of such and such an amount. Since no fee or the like is paid along with the newspaper, is it correct to take this prize or not? One does, of course, have to buy the newspaper; what is the ruling on that? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
A prize scheme is published in the newspaper, and taking the prize for solving it is permissible and correct. There is no harm in it. It is a one-sided arrangement, involving neither gambling nor interest. And the money spent on buying the newspaper was paid for the newspaper, which was received in exchange.

See what Tanwīr al-Abṣār states:

حل الجعل إن شرط المال من جانب واحد

وفي الشامية: جواز الجعل فيما ذكر استحسان. (تنوير الأبصار مع فتاوى الشامي: ٦/٤۰۲، كتاب الحظر والإباحة، سعيد).

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

وحرم شرط الجعل من الجانبين، لا من أحد الجانبين،

وإذا شرط من جانب واحد بأن يقول إن سبقتني فلك على كذا، وإن سبقتك فلا شيء لي عليك جاز استحساناً. (الفتاوى الهندية: ٦/٤٤۵، مسائل شتى).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

An announcement was published in a newspaper that whoever explains the meaning of a certain thing, or solves a certain problem, will be given a prize of such and such an amount. Someone then solved it and received the prize. This prize is his property and there is no difficulty in it, because it is a one-sided arrangement. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱٦/٤٤۳، جامعہ فاروقیہ) ۔

Fatāwā ʿUthmānī states:

If no fee has been taken from the children for the prize or for taking part in the draw, then this is not gambling; and a draw is permissible as a means of distributing prizes. (فتاوی عثمانی: ۳/۳٤۲) ۔

Ḥalāl awr Ḥarām states:

If no fee is taken from those who send in the puzzle, this form will be correct, and the money will count as a pure prize. (حلال و حرام، ص ۳۸۱) ۔
