### TITLE
Ruling on the loss of blessing in investing the price of a house in trade

### QUESTION
A certain Mawlānā Ṣāḥib's salary is very small and he makes ends meet only with difficulty. He has two houses; he wishes to sell one and invest its price in some trade or business. But Mawlānā Ṣāḥib came across the hadith which says that the price of a house should be invested in a house or land, and that if one invests it in some other undertaking there will be no blessing in it. Now what should be done? Kindly guide us as to what is better in the light of the ruling of the Sharīʿah. Furthermore, what is the grade of the above hadith, and what is the meaning of the hadith? Kindly explain and be rewarded.

### ANSWER
Consider the hadith:

عن سعيد بن حريث قال: سمعت رسول الله ﷺ يقول: "من باع داراً أو عقاراً فلم يجعل ثمنه في مثله كان قمناً أن لا يبارك فيه. (رواه ابن ماجه، رقم: ۲٤۹۰).

This narration is weak; in its chain the narrator Ismāʿīl ibn Ibrāhīm is weak. However, Shaykh Shuʿayb al-Arnāʾūṭ (raḥimahullāh) declared it ḥasan on account of its corroborations and supporting witnesses.

قال في مصباح الزجاجة: قلت: ليس لسعيد بن حريث عند ابن ماجه سوى هذا الحديث وليس له رواية في شيء من الخمسة الأصول وإسناد حديثه ضعيف من الطريقين معاً لضعف إسماعيل بن إبراهيم بن مهاجر. (۳/۸۷).

وأخرجه أحمد في مسنده (رقم: ۱۸۷۳۹)، والدارمي في سننه (۲٦۲۵)، قال حسين سليم أسد: إسناده ضعيف لضعف إسماعيل بن إبراهيم بن مهاجر. قال الشيخ شعيب: حديث حسن بمتابعاته وشواهده، وهذا إسناد ضعيف لضعف إسماعيل بن إبراهيم، واضطرابه فيه، وقد سلف في مسند المكيين برقم (۱۵۸٤۲).

The meaning of the noble hadith:

قال سفيان: إن الله تعالى يقول: ﴿وبارك فيها وقدر فيها أقواتها﴾ يقول: فلما خرج من البركة ثم لم يعدها في مثلها لم يبارك له. (السنن الكبرى للبيهقي: ٦/۳٤/۱۱۵۰۸).

Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (raḥimahullāh) says: It is as though Allah Taʿālā has made the earth so magnificent a storehouse for all the needs — food, dwelling, clothing and the rest — of the human beings and animals that dwell upon it, that He has placed in it all the needs of the billions and trillions of human beings and countless animals who will come and dwell upon it until the Day of Judgement. They keep on growing in the belly of the earth and will keep coming forth as needed until the Day of Judgement. Man's task will only be to extract his needs from the earth and use them according to his need. (معارف القرآن: ۷/٦۳۸، ادارۃ المعارف کراچی)۔

It is recorded in the marginal notes on Sunan Ibn Mājah:

بيع الأرض والدور ومصرف ثمنها في المنقولات غير مستحب لأنها كثيرة المنافع قليلة الآفة لا يسرقها سارق ولا يلحقها غارة، ذكره الطيبي. (إنجاح الحاجة حاشية سنن ابن ماجه ص: ۱۷۹).

In the light of the transmitted passages cited above it has become clear that this is not an obligatory and binding matter; rather it is a recommended matter — an advice from the Lawgiver (ʿalayhis salām) that one should sell one's property and spend the price on property of the same kind, since there is more profit in that and the risks of calamities and the like are also far fewer.

Yes, to sell land and to invest its price in land or in some profitable business — such as a factory, machinery and so forth, that is, in something whose substance and benefit endure — is permissible and correct; it is not restricted to the purchase of land alone. But if one spends the price of land on eating, drinking and self-indulgence, then this is not correct; this is ruin and destruction.

Examples of the selling of land are found in the era of the noble Companions as well:

1. Imām al-Bukhārī (raḥimahullāh) has transmitted, under

"باب بركة الغازي في ماله حياً وميتاً"

the detailed incident of the estate and debts of Ḥaḍrat ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu). The hadith is lengthy; consider the translation in abridged form:

Ḥaḍrat al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) was martyred and had not left behind cash — dīnārs or dirhams; rather he left lands. One piece of land was at al-Ghābah, eleven houses were in Madīnah Munawwarah, one house was in Baṣrah, one house in Kūfah and one house in Egypt. Ḥaḍrat ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) says: I reckoned up his debt and it came to twenty-two hundred thousand. Then Ḥakīm ibn Ḥizām (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) met ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) and asked: How much debt has been found against my brother al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu)? ʿAbdullāh (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) concealed it and said: One hundred thousand (for some prudent consideration he did not think it appropriate to disclose it). Ḥakīm (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) said: In my opinion the payment of such a sum is difficult. ʿAbdullāh (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) said: And what if the debt were twenty-two hundred thousand! Ḥakīm (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) said: I do not think you will be able to pay off this debt; if you cannot manage it, then take help from me. ʿAbdullāh (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) says: Ḥaḍrat al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) had bought the land of al-Ghābah for one hundred and seventy thousand. ʿAbdullāh sold it for sixteen hundred thousand, and he stood up and announced: Whoever has a debt owed to him by Ḥaḍrat al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu), let him come to al-Ghābah and meet us. ʿAbdullāh ibn Jaʿfar came there; he had four hundred thousand owed to him by Ḥaḍrat al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu). He said to ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu): If you wish, I will remit this debt. ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) said: We do not wish to have it remitted. ʿAbdullāh ibn Jaʿfar (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) said: Very well, if you wish I will grant you a respite. ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) said: I do not wish for this either. Then ʿAbdullāh ibn Jaʿfar (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) said: Give me some of the land of al-Ghābah. ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) sold all the land of al-Ghābah and the houses, and paid off the debt, and four and a half shares of the land of al-Ghābah were left over. So ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Zubayr went to Ḥaḍrat Muʿāwiyah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu). Ḥaḍrat Muʿāwiyah asked: What price was fetched for al-Ghābah? ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) said: One hundred thousand for each share. He asked: How many shares remain now? ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Zubayr answered: Four and a half shares. Al-Mundhir ibn al-Zubayr (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) said: I will take one share for one hundred thousand. ʿAmr ibn ʿUthmān said: I will take one share. Ḥaḍrat Muʿāwiyah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) said: What is left now? ʿAbdullāh (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) said: One and a half shares remain. Ḥaḍrat Muʿāwiyah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) said: I will buy them for one hundred and fifty thousand. The share which ʿAbdullāh ibn Jaʿfar (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) had taken against his debt he sold to Ḥaḍrat Muʿāwiyah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) for six hundred thousand — and so on. (بخاری شریف: ۱/٤٤۱، ط: فیصل)۔

In this incident there is mention of the estate of Ḥaḍrat al-Zubayr ibn al-ʿAwwām (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) and of the sale of the land of Ḥaḍrat ʿAbdullāh ibn Jaʿfar (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) at a profit. It is thus known that if a profit is to be had in the sale of land, there is no harm in selling it.

2. In a narration of Bukhārī Sharīf it is stated that the orchard which Ḥaḍrat Abū Ṭalḥah al-Anṣārī (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) had given in charity, he — upon the advice of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ — gave in charity to Ḥaḍrat Ḥassān ibn Thābit (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) and Ubayy ibn Kaʿb (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu). Of this, Ḥaḍrat Ḥassān (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) sold his share; and when objection was raised against him as to why he had sold it, he said: Shall I not sell one ṣāʿ of dates for one ṣāʿ of dirhams?

عن أنس بن مالك رضي الله عنه قال: لما نزلت ﴿لن تنالوا البرحتي تنفقوا مما تحبون﴾ جاء أبو طلحة إلى رسول الله ﷺ فقال: يا رسول الله يقول الله تبارك وتعالى في كتابه

فقال رسول الله ﷺ: بخ يا أبا طلحة ذلك مال رابح قبلناه منك ورددناه عليك فاجعله في الأقربين فتصدق به أبو طلحة على ذوي رحمه قال: وكان منهم أبي وحسان قال: وباع حسان حصته منه من معاوية فقيل له تبيع صدقة أبي طلحة فقال: ألا أبيع صاعاً من تمر بصاع من دراهم، قال: وكانت تلك الحديقة في موضع قصر بني حديلة الذي بناه معاوية. (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۲۷۵۸).

The upshot is that the share which Ḥaḍrat Ḥassān (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) received out of that orchard he sold to Ḥaḍrat Muʿāwiyah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) for one hundred thousand dirhams; there Ḥaḍrat Muʿāwiyah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) built a palace whose name was Qaṣr Banī Ḥudaylah. It is thus known that to sell one's property is permissible and correct.

3. In a narration of Ṭaḥāwī and Bayhaqī it is stated that Ḥaḍrat ʿUthmān (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu) sold a piece of land to Ḥaḍrat Ṭalḥah (raḍiyallāhu ʿanhu). (نصب الرایہ: ٤/۹، ط: بیروت)۔

4. Kulayb ibn Wāʾil says: I said to ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar

رضي الله عنه

: I have bought a piece of land. ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar

رضي الله عنه

said: Buying is a very good undertaking.

(السنن الكبرى للبيهقي: ۹/۱٤۰/۱۸۸٦٦).

Imām al-Bayhaqī (raḥimahullāh) has transmitted several further reports in al-Sunan al-Kubrā; for details see:
