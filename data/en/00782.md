### TITLE
Holding a staff during the sermon

### QUESTION
What does the sharia say about the staff for the sermon on Friday — is it necessary, or obligatory, or sunnah? Will a person who does not hold a staff become an unbeliever?

### ANSWER
The jurists have written that if a city was conquered by the sword, the imam should hold a sword in his left hand and deliver the sermon leaning on it. ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) has explained the wisdom behind this. Consider the following:

أي بالسيف ليريهم أنها فتحت بالسيف فإذا رجعتم عن الإسلام فذلك باقي في أيدي المسلمين يقاتلونكم حتى ترجعوا إلى الإسلام، درر. (شامي: ۲/۱٦۲، سعيد)

That is, he shows the people that this city was conquered by the sword; and if you turn away from Islam, the sword is still in the hands of the Muslims, and they will fight you and bring you back to Islam.

If a city or country was not conquered by the sword, however, but came to Islam through invitation and preaching or by a peace agreement, then the sermon should be delivered leaning on a staff. This act is neither obligatory nor necessary nor an emphasised sunnah; it is a recommended act, on condition that people do not attach to it the belief that it is obligatory and binding. If people begin to regard it as necessary, then it should be given up.

ʿAllāmah al-Ṭībī has written in his commentary on Mishkāt that insisting on a recommended act turns it into an innovation. Further, anyone who calls a person who does not hold the staff an unbeliever is gravely sinful and disobedient and must repent; to call a Muslim an unbeliever, or to regard him as one, is a serious sin.

Imdād al-Aḥkām states: Taking the staff is recommended, but if it is regarded as necessary and the one who omits it is blamed, then it will be forbidden, since that amounts to binding oneself to what is not binding (التزام مالا یلزم).

في الدر: ويكره أن يتكئ على قوس أو عصا. وفي الشامي: نقل القهستاني: عن المحيط أن أخذ العصا سنة كالقيام. (Imdād al-Aḥkām: 1/759, Karachi)

See also: Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 8/270, chaptered and arranged edition, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah; and Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/143.

In short, the imam should for the most part not hold a staff. Yes, once the beliefs of the ordinary people have been put right, he may take a staff in his hand from time to time, acting upon the recommended practice.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
