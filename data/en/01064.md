### TITLE
Establishing the crescent moon by a telephone report

### QUESTION
Can the sighting of the crescent moon be established by a report received over the telephone or not?

### ANSWER
If the person speaking on the telephone is a known and recognised man about whom there is no doubt, and there is a strong probability that he is telling the truth, and he is reporting that a decision on the sighting has been reached in his area in accordance with the sharia, then it is permissible — though not obligatory — to treat his report as reliable and to act upon it as an individual. And if reports come in one after another by telephone from every direction, and a large number of people give the same report so that it reaches the level of a widely circulated report, then it attains the rank of testimony in the sharia and it becomes binding to act upon it.

Kitāb al-Fatāwā states:

If the sky is clear — whether the question concerns the moon of Eid or that of Ramadan — then, for a sighting to be established, a widely circulated report is required, that is, the report of so many people that it is inconceivable in the ordinary course of things that they should agree upon a lie. If the sky is overcast and the question concerns the moon of Ramadan, then the report of one reliable man is enough to establish the moon. In both these cases, then, the establishment of the moon rests upon a report; so if a report is given over the telephone and one is satisfied that the account the informant gives of himself is correct and that he is a reliable person, such telephone reports may be accepted. (کتاب الفتاوی: ۳/۳۷٦) ۔
