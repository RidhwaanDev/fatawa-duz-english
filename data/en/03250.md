### TITLE
The ruling on some of the heirs making a settlement over particular items of property

### QUESTION
Zayd passed away leaving two sons. One son took his house, his car and his goods. The other son took his small house, and he is also to collect the three hundred thousand which a certain company owed the deceased — that is, he will collect on the other brother's behalf the share of that debt which belongs to him too. Is a division of this kind permissible or not? If it is impermissible, why? And what is a simple way of making it permissible, given that ordinary people constantly enter into transactions of this sort? (b) Then, if the debtor died insolvent, can he now recover his share from the other brother or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
When, with the agreement of all the heirs, one heir withdraws from his right in the estate in exchange for a specified sum out of it, this is termed takhāruj in the terminology of the jurists.

The jurists have stated expressly that if the estate also contains debts and sums receivable, and the party making the settlement wishes to settle out of those debts and receivables in such a way that he will have no share in them, then this settlement is void.

The ground of its invalidity is that this transaction involves تملیک الدین من غیر المدیون (transferring ownership of a debt to someone other than the debtor), which is impermissible. For example: Zayd had two sons, Khālid and Bakr. After Zayd's death Khālid took the house from his father's estate and Bakr took the car, and along with it the five hundred thousand rand which someone owed Zayd also fell to Bakr's share. In that case Khālid has made Bakr the owner of two hundred and fifty thousand rand, whereas Bakr is not the debtor but the creditor. And since people very frequently enter into transactions of this kind, the jurists have devised various legal expedients and arrangements to make such a transaction valid.

In short, Khālid has transferred ownership of two hundred and fifty thousand rand to Bakr, which entails تملیک الدین من غیر المدیون. The author of Fatḥ al-Qadīr has therefore written that transferring ownership of a debt to someone other than the debtor is permissible with the creditor's leave.

Consider what Fatḥ al-Qadīr states:

أجيب بأن هبة الدين من غير من عليه الدين إنما لا يجوز إذا لم يأذن للغير في قبضه، فأما إذا وهب الدين من آخر وأذن له في قبضه جاز استحساناً. (فتح القدير: ۷/۱۹۰، كتاب الكفالة، ط: دار الفكر). (وكذا في العناية بهامش فتح القدير: ۷/۱۹۰، والفتاوى الهندية: ٤/۳۸٤، and Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 5/600, ed. Bombay).

(b) If the debtor died insolvent, and one brother, having bought his share as a specific item, referred the other brother to the debtor — that is, the debtor referred his creditor to his own debtor — and that debtor's debtor has died insolvent, then the creditor may now recover his share of the debt from his own debtor, namely his brother.

Consider what is recorded in al-Hidāyah:

فلو سلم له ما قبض ثم توي ما على الغريم له أن يشارك القابض لأنه إنما رضي بالتسليم ليسلم له ما في ذمة الغريم ولم يسلم. (الهداية: ۳/۲۵٤، كتاب الصلح).

That is, if one party to the settlement took possession of his item, and the other party could not obtain his share, which was owed by the debtor, then this second party may share with the first in what the first received; for this second party consented to the first taking that item only so that he himself might obtain his share from the debtor, and he did not obtain it.

وللاستزادة راجع: (الهداية: ۳/۱۲۹، والعناية ٦/۳۵۰، وبدائع الصنائع: ٦/۱۸، وتبيين الحقائق: ٤/۱۷۲، and Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 7/194, and Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām: 2/69, والدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۵/ ۳٤۵، سعيد).
