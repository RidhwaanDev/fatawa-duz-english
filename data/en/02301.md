### TITLE
The ruling on compensation for usurping a house

### QUESTION
A man took forcible and usurping possession of another's house and lived in it for two months; then the house was recovered from that man. Is rent due from the usurper or not?

### ANSWER
The question of the usurpation of immovable property has already been mentioned. The upshot is that if no loss was caused to the house, compensation is not obligatory; if, however, the house had been prepared for letting, then two months' rent will be due. Furthermore, even if it was not kept for letting, compensation ought still to be due. کما مر ۔

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

ومنافع الغصب غير مضمونة

إلا في ثلاث فيجب أجر المثل عند المتأخرين

أن يكون وقفاً

أو مال اليتيم

أو معداً للاستغلال. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۲۰٦، سعيد).

Al-Bināyah Sharḥ al-Hidāyah states:

قال مشايخنا: هذا إذا لم يكن معداً للاستغلال فإن كان معداً له يضمن المنافع بالغصب. (البناية: ۱۰/۲۹٤).

For further evidence see the questions "The ruling on the usurpation of immovable property" and "The ruling on the benefits derived from a usurped item".

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
