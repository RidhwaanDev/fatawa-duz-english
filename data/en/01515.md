### TITLE
Saying "I have retired you"

### QUESTION
A man said to his wife, "I have retired you." Afterwards the wife went off to her parents' home. The husband says that he said it in anger but had no intention of divorce. What does the noble Sharīʿah rule now?

### ANSWER
In the case described, this word is among the allusive expressions, and it carries the sense of "فارقتک", that is, giving the woman up. The ruling for "فارقتک" is that in a state of anger, or during a discussion of divorce, a divorce takes effect without any intention. So in the case described one irrevocable divorce has taken effect upon the woman. During the ʿiddah the two of them may contract a fresh marriage and live together again; and once the ʿiddah has ended the woman is free and may marry whomever she wishes.

Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

يحتمل رداً، ونحو خلية، برية، حرام، بائن
