### TITLE
Whether household work is the wife's responsibility

### QUESTION
Is the housework binding upon a woman or not?

### ANSWER
If the woman belongs to a family in which the women do not do the housework themselves, or if because of some excuse — illness, for instance — the housework is difficult for her, then it is not binding or necessary for her to do it. But if the women of her family do the housework, or the husband's income does not stretch to employing a servant, then the housework is binding upon her as a matter of conscience. In our society (South Africa) women generally do the housework — cooking and the like — besides the cleaning, so here it is binding in the sharia upon women to look after their husband and children and to do the cooking, unless by mutual agreement she does not cook.

Consider what Islāmī Fiqh states:

If the woman comes from a home where people do not do the work with their own hands but servants come and do it, or if the woman is so weak or ill that she cannot manage the housework, then the husband cannot compel such a woman to work. He will have to let her sit without working and provide her with food and clothing. If such a woman asks for a servant for her own needs or for the housework, then if the husband is well off he will have to engage a servant and bear the cost of one servant. But if the husband's income does not stretch to employing a servant, or if the woman used to do the work with her own hands in her parents' home, then she will have to do the work with her own hands in her husband's house too, and it will be the man's responsibility to do the outside work himself — for example, to bring the shopping, the provisions, the firewood, the water and so on into the house. If the man does not provide these things, the woman bears no responsibility for them.

Some of the jurists have written that washing the clothes is not obligatory upon the man; he need only provide the water and the soap, and the woman should wash her clothes with her own hands. If the man does provide for the washing, that is a kindness on his part — that is to say, it is not the woman's legal right. But this is so where the woman was used to doing it in her own home, or where the man provides it even though he does not have the means. Otherwise, when the man has the means, or the woman is not used to this work, the washing of the clothes ought to be given to her as a right: when a servant can be provided, why should the other things that pertain to health and cleanliness not be provided as an entitlement?

If, however, the woman does not place this burden on the man, that is a kindness on her part. What this relationship calls for is that the two should not deal with each other on a merely legal footing, but on a moral one as well — that is, each should have regard for the other's hardship and comfort. (اسلامی فقہ: ۲/۱۱۷) ۔

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

وإن قالت: لا أطبخ ولا أخبز قال في الكتاب لا تجبر على الطبخ والخبز وعلى الزوج أن يأتيها بطعام مهيأ أو يأتيها بمن يكفيها عمل الطبخ والخبز، قال الفقيه أبو الليث رحمه الله تعالى: إن امتنعت المرأة عن الطبخ والخبز إنما يجب على الزوج أن يأتيها بطعام مهيأ إذا كانت من بنات الأشراف لا تخدم بنفسها في أهلها أو لم تكن من بنات الأشراف لكن بها علة تمنعها من الطبخ والخبز، أما إذا لم تكن كذلك فلا يجب على الزوج أن يأتيها بطعام مهيأ كذا في الظهيرية. قالوا إن هذه الأعمال واجبة عليها ديانة وإن كانت لا يجبرها القاضي كذا في البحر الرائق. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۵٤۸، باب النفقة).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

أنه عليه الصلاة والسلام قسم الأعمال بين علي رضي الله تعالى عنه وفاطمة رضي الله تعالى عنها، فجعل أعمال الخارج على علي رضي الله تعالى عنه والداخل على فاطمة رضي الله تعالى عنها مع أنها سيدة نساء العالمين بحر. (الدر المختار: ۳/۵۷۹، باب النفقة، ط: سعيد).

وفي الطحطاوي: قوله قسم الأعمال أي أعمال المعيشة، قوله فجعل أعمال الخارج أي خارج البيت كإتيان الحطب والماء وتحصيل النفقة، قوله الداخل على فاطمة رضي الله تعالى عنها أي داخل البيت كالطحن والخبز والعجن. (حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ۲/۲۵۵، كوئته، نفقه. وكذا في البحر الرائق: ٤/۱۸۳، باب النفقة، كوئته).
