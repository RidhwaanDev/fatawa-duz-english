### TITLE
Dismissing an imam without cause out of personal ill-feeling

### QUESTION
Our imam had been serving the mosque and the madrasah for two and a half years, and praise be to Allah, there has been no complaint about him to this day. A few months ago, however, a minor incident took place between the imam and the father of one of the students. As it happens, this man is the son of the mosque chairman. After the incident the chairman and some of the mosque trustees began to work against the imam and kept trying to have him removed, and in the end they did remove him. Not only that: they also closed the madrasah down and told the parents of the students to enrol their children in some other madrasah. When the other trustees saw the treatment that these few had meted out to the imam, they resigned. The imam belongs to this same area and still leads the prayer from time to time, since the worshippers have no quarrel with him; but when the trustees are present they show their displeasure and tell the muezzin to lead the prayer. With this background, here are a few questions.

(1) Does the sharia allow scholars to be treated in the way the mosque trustees have treated this scholar-imam?

(2) Will the ordinary people of the neighbourhood and the worshippers be held answerable for the deeds and the conduct of these few trustees, or not?

(3) Can a scholar be barred from leading the prayer when he is the most learned man in the neighbourhood?

### ANSWER
قال في التنوير وشرحه:

"ولو أم قوماً وهم له كارهون، إن الكراهة لفساد فيه أو لانّهم احق بالإمامة منه كره له ذلك تحريماً لحديث أبي داود لا يقبل الله صلوة من تقدم قوما وهم له كارهون وإن هو احق لا والكراهة عليهم وهكذا في الهندية. ۱/۸۷."

وفي الدر أيضا:

"الأحق بالإمامة تقديماً، بل نصبا الأعلم بأحكام الصلاة (إلى إن قال) والخيار إلى القوم فإن اختلفوا اعتبر أكثرهم ولو قدموا غير الأولى اساوأ بلا إثم، وفي رد المحتار: قال في التتارخانية ولو أن رجلين في الفقه والصلاح سواء إلا إن أحدهما أقرأ فقدم القوم الأخر فقد اساؤا وتركوا السنة، ولكن لا يأثمون لأنهم قدموا رجلاً صالحاً". (التنوير مع الدر المختار: ۱/۵۵۷).

وفي الدر أيضاً:

"الباني للمسجد أولى من القوم بنصب الإمام والمؤذن في المختار إلا إذا عين القوم اصلح ممن عين الباني لأن منفعة ذلك ترجع إليهم.". الدر المختار: ٤/٤۳۰).

وفي شرح النقاية:

"وفي رواية: إن سركم أن تقبل صلوتكم فليؤكم علماءكم فإنهم وفدكم فيما بينكم وبين ربكم، رواه الطبراني وفي رواية الحاكم فليؤمكم خياركم، وفي رواية، اكرموا حملة القرآن فمن اكرمهم فقد اكرمني وفي رواية حامل القرآن راية الإسلام ومن اكرمه فقد اكرم الله ومن أهانه فعليه لعنة الله" (الجامع الصغير للإمام الحافظ السيوطي. ۱/۱٤۲). (شرح نقايه: ۱/۸٦).

The following points emerge from these passages.

If the imam is not at fault, if he bears no blame for the displeasure of these few trustees, and if there is no shortcoming in him, then their displeasure carries no weight. Prayer behind him will be valid and free of any dislike, and the sin in this affair will fall upon those few trustees. Further, when the trustees are at odds with an imam, it is the view of the majority of the worshippers that counts; and since the majority of the worshippers here are content with the imam, their view is the one to be followed. Moreover, when the imam in question is the most deserving man in the whole neighbourhood and the most fit to lead the prayer, appointing anyone else as imam is contrary to the sunnah. So in the case put to us, since the imam is not at fault and the worshippers are happy with him, it is not permissible to dismiss him on account of these few trustees. Those who dismiss an imam because of personal enmity and quarrels, and who humiliate him, are wrongdoers and offenders and are gravely sinful. They should ask the imam's forgiveness, and repentance is obligatory upon them; otherwise they are openly sinful and deserve to be called to account.

This question has come from one side only. We have not heard the position of those trustees who are opposing the imam. If they dislike the imam for reasons recognised by the sharia, then their opposition to him would be correct.
