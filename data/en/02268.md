### TITLE

The ruling on profit made by selling shares that are not Sharīʿah-compliant

### QUESTION

In 2004 a man bought 20,000 rand worth of shares in Awais Investments at a time when it was operating under Sharīʿah rules. Now, according to this man, it no longer operates on Sharīʿah rules, so the shareholder sold his shares and received a total of 47,000 — that is, he made a profit over and above his capital. May he use the profit he has received or not?

### ANSWER

For as long as the company traded in accordance with Sharīʿah rules, the profit of that period is lawful and permissible; but from the time it began trading on non-Sharīʿah lines, the profit should be given away in charity without the intention of earning reward.
Fiqhī Maqālāt states:
There are four conditions for the permissibility of buying and selling shares:
(1) The underlying business must be lawful.
(2) If the company carries out some interest-based dealings, one must raise one's voice against this at its annual meeting.
(3) When the profits are distributed, whatever portion of the profit was derived from interest-bearing deposits must be given away in charity. (فقہی مقالات: ۱/۱۵۱) ۔
