### TITLE
Having ḥajj performed on another's behalf from the home town of the one commissioning it

### QUESTION
If ḥajj was obligatory upon someone and he passed away — he used to live in India — and now a person performs ḥajj on his behalf from South Africa instead of from India, will this be correct or not?

### ANSWER
It is necessary that the ḥajj be performed from the home town of the one who commissions it, provided that a third of the estate left behind allows for it. If it does not, then, by way of istiḥsān, it should be arranged from whatever place before the mīqāt is possible. Hence in the case asked about too, the ḥajj should be performed from the home town of the one commissioning it, that is, from India.

See what is stated in Shāmī:

شروط الحج عن الغير عشرون

الحادي عشر أن يحج عنه من وطنه إن اتسع، وإلا فمن حيث يبلغ. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/٦۰۰، مطلب شروط الحج عن الغير، سعيد).

Ḥāshiyat al-Ṭaḥṭāwī states:

قوله من بلده، وإن كان للموصي أوطان حج عنه من أقرب أوطانه إلى مكة، لأنه متيقن به وقوله من بلده محله ما إذا كان له بلد، أما إذا لم يكن له وطن فمن حيث مات بحر. (حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ۱/۵۵۰باب الحج عن الغير، كوئته).

Sharḥ Lubāb al-Manāsik states:

الثامن أن يحج عنه من وطنه إن اتسع الثلث أي ثلث مال الميت، وإن لم يتسع أي الثلث يحج عنه من حيث يبلغ، أي استحساناً

لعل المكان مقيد بما قبل المواقيت، وإلا فبأي شيء يمكن أن يحج عنه من مكة، وكذا الحكم إذا أوصى أن يحج عنه بماله وسمي مبلغه، فإنه إن كان يبلغ أن يحج عنه من بلده حج عنه منه وإلا فمن حيث يبلغ. (شرح لباب المناسك: ٤۸۳، فصل في شرائط جواز الإحجاج، بيروت).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

If ḥajj is being performed on behalf of another at the instruction of a living person who is unable to perform it himself, or by the bequest of a deceased person, then it is necessary to perform the ḥajj from the home town of the testator or of the one commissioning it. If a third of the estate is insufficient and the heirs do not permit more than that, then it may be performed from wherever the ḥajj can be managed with a third of the estate. If the testator or the one commissioning it has himself specified a particular place or a particular sum of money, then it should be performed from there, even if that is from Makkah itself — though for one who has the means it is disliked to do this. If he did not give any instruction or make any bequest about the ḥajj, but someone wishes to perform ḥajj on his behalf as a voluntary favour, then it is permissible even from Makkah; for one who has the means, however, it is better to have it performed from the mīqāt. (احسن الفتاوی: ٤/۵۱۹ ۔ و عمدۃ الفقہ: ٤/۳٤۹، مجددیہ).
