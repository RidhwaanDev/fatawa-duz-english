### TITLE
A judge's decision takes precedence over a muftī's ruling in three divorces

### QUESTION
A man's wife was absent, and he told her that he had said "امرأتي طالق" three times but that his intention was emphasis. A muftī gave a ruling of one divorce, and he takes her back. The wife, however, went to a judge, who ruled that irrevocable prohibition had come about. There is now a conflict between the fatwa and the judicial decision. Which is to be followed?

### ANSWER
In the case described the judge's decision takes precedence over the muftī's ruling. The woman therefore remains unlawful for this man and will not become lawful for the first husband without ḥalālah (an intervening valid marriage to another man which is consummated and then comes to an end). Moreover, it is not permissible for the woman to give him access to herself; and if the husband is unwilling, release should be obtained by khulʿ (release from the marriage at the wife's request in return for a payment).

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

المقلد إذا أفتاه إنسان في حادثة ثم رفعت إلى القاضي فقضي بخلاف رأي المفتي فإنه يأخذ بقضاء القاضي ويترك رأي المفتي لأن رأي المفتي يصير متروكاً لقضاء القاضي. (بدائع الصنائع في ترتيب الشرائع: ۷/٦، كتاب آداب القاضي، فصل في شرائط القضاء، سعيد).

Al-Mabsūṭ states:

وإذا كان الزوج غائباً أو كان يعتقد أن الطلاق غير واقع فعليه أن يتبع رأي القاضي

وكذا لو حكمنا فقيهاً فحكمه كفتواه لأن سببه تراضيهما لا ولاية ثابتة له حكماً فكان تراضيهما على تحكيمه كسؤالهما إياه والفتوي لا تعارض قضاء القاضي فإذا قضي القاضي عليه بخلاف ذلك كان عليه أن يتبع رأي القاضي. (المبسوط للإمام السرخسي رحمه الله: ۱۰/۱۸۵، كتاب الاستحسان، باب الرجل يري الرجل يقتل أباه أو غيره، دار الفكر).

Īḍāḥ al-Nawādir states:

If, in one and the same case, the party concerned first obtains a fatwa from a muftī and then obtains a decision from a judge, and the muftī gave his ruling on the basis of conscience while the judge ruled on the basis of judicial process and outward appearance — for example, the husband said to his wife, "You are divorced, divorced, divorced," and states that he intended only one divorce, so that the muftī, taking conscience into account, ruled that one divorce had occurred in accordance with the questioner's intention and purpose and allowed him to take her back, while the judge, taking the outward wording into account, ruled that three divorces had occurred and forbade him to take her back — then in such a case the party concerned is obliged to set aside the muftī's fatwa and act upon the judge's decision. (ایضاح النوادر: جلد دوم: ۹۲، مکتبہ علمیہ) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
