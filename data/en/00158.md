### TITLE
Shia objections against ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) over the hadith of the paper

### QUESTION
In the hadith of the paper the Shia raise four objections against ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him):

(1) He rejected a bequest, because the Prophet's ﷺ words "bring pen and paper" were a bequest.

(2) He attributed hajr (raving) to the Prophet ﷺ.

(3) He raised his voice above the voice of the Prophet ﷺ and so acted against ﴿لا ترفعوا أصواتكم فوق صوت النبي﴾.

(4) He rejected a hadith by saying "حسبكم كتاب الله".

What are the answers to these?

### ANSWER
اشتد برسول الله ﷺ وجعه فقال ائتوني أكتب لكم كتابا لن تضلوا بعده أبداً فتنازعوا ولا ينبغي عند النبي تنازع فقالوا ما شأنه أ هجر استفهموا يردون عنه الخ.

لما حضر رسول الله ﷺ وفي البيت رجال فقال النبي ﷺ هلمّوا أكتب لكم كتابا لا تضلّوا بعده فقال بعضهم إن رسول الله ﷺ قد غلبه الوجع وعندكم القرآن حسبنا كتاب الله فاختلف أهل البيت فاختصموا فمنهم من يقول قربوا يكتب لكم كتابا لا تضلوا بعده ومنهم من يقول غير ذلك فلما أكثروا اللغو والاختلاف قال رسول ا ﷲ ﷺ قوموا الخ. (رواه البخاري: ۲/٦۳۸).

لما اشتد بالنبي ﷺ وجعه قال ائتوني بكتاب أكتب لكم كتابا لا تضلوا بعده قال عمر رضي الله عنه أن النبي ﷺ غلبه الوجع وعند نا كتاب الله حسبنا فاختلفوا وكثر اللغط قال قوموا عني ولا ينبغي عندي التنازع الخ. (رواه البخاري كتاب العلم: ۱/۲۳).

Answer to the first objection:

There are two answers to this objection: one that turns the argument back on the objector, and one that examines the matter itself.

(a) The answer that turns the argument back: if a Shia draws from this incident the conclusion that revelation was rejected, then incidents of the same kind are established from ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him) as well.

فما هو جوابكم فهو جوابنا.

(1) When the Prophet ﷺ was in his final illness he said to ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him):

أن آتيه بطبق يكتب فيه ما لا تضل امته من بعده قال وخشيت أن تفوتني نفسه قال قلت أني أحفظ واعي قال أوصي بالصلاة والزكاة وما ملكت أيمانكم. (رواه أحمد في مسنده ۱/۹۰).

(2) When the Prophet ﷺ woke ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him) for the night prayer, he said:

ما نصلي إلا ما كتب الله. (رواه النسائي: ۱/۲٤۰ باب الترغيب في قيام الليل).

(3) At Ḥudaybiyah, when the Prophet ﷺ told ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him) to erase the words "Messenger of Allah", he refused.

صحيح البخاري باب عمرة القضاء (۲/٦۱۰) قال لعلي أمح رسول الله قال على لا والله لا أمحو.

(٤) وروى محمد بن بابويه في الأمالي والديلمي في إرشاد القلوب أن رسول الله ﷺ أعطي فاطمة رضي الله عنه سبعة دراهم وقال أعطيها عليا ومريه أن يشتري لأهل بيته طعاما فقد غلبهم الجوع فأعطتها عليا وقالت إن رسول الله ﷺ أمرك أن تبتاع لنا طعاما فأخذها على رضي الله عنه وخرج من بيته ليبتاع طعاما لأهل بيته فسمع رجلا يقول من يقرض الملي الوفي فأعطاه الدراهم. (تحفة اثنا عشرية ص ۵۹۵)

(b) The considered answer: the truth of the matter is that ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) did not reject revelation. He offered advice, and this was a matter open to independent judgement; in such matters the Prophet ﷺ used to accept his advice, as in the case of the veiling of the noble wives and the like.

So this too he offered as advice. Had the Prophet ﷺ not wished to accept it, he would have turned it down; but he accepted his opinion. And had the Prophet ﷺ wished to have the document written, he would certainly have had it written, for he lived on for five days after this incident and never even mentioned it again.

ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) was, as it were, a minister and adviser to the Prophet ﷺ, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ sometimes accepted his advice and sometimes did not. For example, he advised that ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl be put to death, and the Prophet ﷺ did not accept it; he advised that Ḥāṭib ibn Abī Baltaʿah (may Allah be pleased with him) be put to death, and the Prophet ﷺ did not accept it; he proposed that poetry be forbidden in the mosque, and that was not accepted either. But most of his advice was accepted — for instance, his advice that the Station of Ibrāhīm be taken as a place of prayer, his advice that the funeral prayer not be offered over the hypocrites, and his advice that the noble wives be kept in seclusion. All of these suggestions were accepted. In this matter too the Prophet ﷺ accepted his advice and gave up the intention of writing; and although he lived for five more days, he never again spoke of the writing.

قال عمر رضي الله عنه وافقت ربي في ثلاث في مقام إبراهيم وفي الحجاب وفي أسارى بدر. (صحيح البخاري: ۱/۵۸، باب ما جاء في القبلة، وصحيح مسلم: ۲/۲۷۹، باب فضائل عمر رضي الله عنه).

قال رسول الله ﷺ ما من نبي إلا وله وزيران من أهل السماء ووزيران من أهل الأرض فأما وزيراي من أهل السماء فجبرئيل وميكائيل وأما وزيراي من أهل الأرض فأبو بكر وعمر رضي الله تعالٰي عنهما. (أخرجه الترمذي، قبيل مناقب أبي حفص عمر بن الخطاب: ۲/۲۰۸).

In short, ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) did not reject a statement of the Prophet ﷺ. What he had in view was the Prophet's ﷺ ease and comfort, and sparing him strain and distress in the severity of his illness. To turn this round and treat it as rejecting the command of the Prophet ﷺ is nothing but prejudice and malice. Everyone protects a sick relative from exertion and distress. If at some moment a sick man in the grip of severe pain and illness wants to take some trouble upon himself for the good and benefit of those present, they put him off and make light of the need, saying that there is no call for it at all. This is very much the custom with elders. (تحفہ اثنا عشریہ اردو، ص ۵۹۲).

فلوكان ما يكتبه في الكتاب مما يجب بيانه وكتابته لكان النبي ﷺ يبينه ويكتبه ولا يلتفت إلى قول أحد فانه أطوع الخلق له فعلم أنه لمّا ترك الكتاب لم يكن الكتاب واجبا ولا كان فيه من الدين ما تجب كتابته حينئذٍ إذ لو وجب لفعله ولو أن عمر رضي الله عنه اشتبه عليه أمر ثم تبين له أو شك في بعض الأمور فليس هو أعظم ممن يفتي ويقضي بأمور ويكون النبي ﷺ قد حكم بخلافها مجتهداً في ذلك. (منهاج السنة ۳/۱۳٦).

Answer to the second objection:

Nowhere in the sound hadiths is it said that ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) attributed raving to the Prophet ﷺ. The narrations say that some people said this (that is, those who were agreed on the writing). Besides, this is a question asked to deny, not to enquire: has he ﷺ fallen into raving and delirium? Certainly not. In Bukhārī this report occurs in several places: in six of them the interrogative hamzah is present and in one it is not, and even where it is absent the interrogative sense is intended.

That ʿUmar attributed incoherent talk to the Prophet ﷺ is also without foundation, because, first of all, how has it been established for certain that it was ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) who spoke these words, "أهجر استفهموه"? In most narrations the wording is "قالوا". It may well be that those who were urging that paper and inkpot be brought strengthened their case with this remark; or it may be a question asked in denial. (تحفه اثنا عشريه ص ۵۹۷).

ومنها أن يكون "الهجر" في هذا الكلام بمعنى الفراق لا بمعنى الهذيان وقد صرح علماء اللغة بأن قولهم هجر يهجر يستعمل بمعنى الترك والمفارقة أيضا راجع تاج العروس، فالمراد "استفهموا رسول الله هل هو يفارقنا حيث أمرنا بكتابة وصيته". (تكملة فتح الملهم: ۲/۱٤۵).

Or the sense of "اهجر استفهموه" may be: has he given up the intention of writing? Ask him about it. Or it may mean: is he about to depart from this world?

Answer to the third objection:

﴿لا ترفعوا أصواتكم﴾ means that you must not raise your voices while the Prophet ﷺ is speaking; and at that moment the Prophet ﷺ was silent while the people were talking loudly. So no objection arises.

What is forbidden is raising the voice — that is, shouting — above the voice of the Prophet (peace be upon him), and nothing of the sort came from anyone in this episode, neither from ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) nor from anyone else. Raised voices among the Companions themselves, in discussions and disputes in the Prophet's ﷺ own presence, went on all the time, and he never forbade it. Indeed, the Qur'an indicates that such discussions are permissible, and in two ways. First, He said ﴿لا ترفعوا أصواتكم فوق صوت النبي﴾ and did not say لا ترفعوا أصواتكم فيما بينكم عند النبي، Second, the noble verse says ﴿كجهر بعضكم لبعض﴾, from which it is plain that speaking loudly to one another is permissible.

Apart from this, where is the proof that ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) was the first to raise his voice and the cause of the dispute? There was a large gathering in that very room, and when many people are talking, raised voices are unavoidable. (تحفه اثنا عشريه ص ٦۰۰).

Answer to the fourth objection:

What ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) meant by "حسبنا كتاب الله" was that the Book of Allah is sufficient for belief, character, principles, and for creating unity. It is an allusion to ﴿واعتصموا بحبل الله جميعاً ولا تفرقوا﴾.

Another answer, which appears to be stronger, is that ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) meant that writing down the Book of Allah is enough and that writing down hadith is not necessary. For in the Prophet's ﷺ time the Qur'an was being written down, while there was no great effort to write down the hadiths; the scribes of revelation were those who wrote the Qur'an. Writing the Qur'an, as it were, was what was necessary. So the meaning of حسبنا كتاب الله is that the Qur'an is the Book meant to be written, and it is its writing that is necessary. This is why the hadith uses the expression "the Book of Allah" and not the word "Qur'an", so that it points to the writing down of the Qur'an.

"حسبنا كتاب الله" — what need was there, then, for the Prophet ﷺ to take such trouble upon himself at that time for something that was not particularly necessary? It was better that he should remain in ease and comfort. And the words "إن رسول الله قد غلبه الوجع وعندنا كتاب الله حسبنا" are clear evidence of precisely this intention. (اثنا عشريه، ص ۵۹۳).
