### TITLE
Investigating the famous incident concerning Shaykh Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī

### QUESTION
Ḥaḍrat Shaykh Mawlānā Muḥammad Zakariyyā (may Allah Most High have mercy on him) has written the strange and remarkable incident of Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī on the authority of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah Most High have mercy on him), and in it there is a reference to Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān. Is this incident in Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān or not? And is it sound in its chain or not? I am quoting this incident from Ḥaḍrat Shaykh Mawlānā Zakariyyā's (may Allah have mercy on him) book Āp Bītī; kindly investigate it.

In Āp Bītī Ḥaḍrat Shaykh Mawlānā Muḥammad Zakariyyā (may Allah have mercy on him) writes: "The instructive incident below is quoted from ʿAllāmah Damīrī's Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān, printed in Egypt. The second century of the hijrah is drawing to a close; not very long has passed since the sun of prophethood set; among the people the element of trustworthiness, honesty, religiousness and God-fearingness is dominant; the promising sons of Islam at whose hands it is to flourish, some are already at work and some are still being trained; it is the age of the leading imams of the religion. Every city appears peopled with religious scholars and God-fearing righteous men, and especially Madīnat al-Salām (Baghdad), which is at that time the capital of the Muslims, is adorned with its outward and inward embellishments and has become a garden. If on the one side its charming buildings and the canals running through them steal the heart, on the other side the gatherings of the scholars and the righteous, the circles of teaching and learning, and the captivating sounds of remembrance and recitation are ample provision for the contentment of the righteous servants of Allah Most High. There is a wonderful assembly of jurists, hadith scholars, devoted worshippers and ascetics. In this blessed assembly there is a saintly man well known by the name of Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī, who is the spiritual guide of most of the people of Iraq and a teacher of hadith. The number of his disciples has reached twelve thousand. It is an instructive incident concerning them that we wish now to present to our readers.

Besides being a devoted worshipper, an ascetic and a knower of Allah, this saintly man is also an imam of high standing in hadith and tafsīr. It is said that he had thirty thousand hadiths committed to memory and that he used to recite the noble Qur'an with all the narrations of the recitation. Once he resolved to undertake a journey, and many men from among his students and disciples set out with him, among whom were Ḥaḍrat Junayd Baghdādī and Ḥaḍrat Shiblī (may Allah be pleased with them both). Ḥaḍrat Shiblī (may his secret be sanctified) states: our caravan, by the grace and favour of Allah Most High, was advancing stage by stage towards its destination in complete safety and ease, when we passed by a village of Christians. The time of prayer had come, but since no water was available we had not been able to perform it. On reaching the village we searched for water, and we went round the village. In the course of this we came upon temples and churches in which there was a gathering of the monks and priests of sun-worshippers, Jews and cross-worshipping Christians; one worshipped the sun, another bowed down to fire, and another had made the cross the object of his devotion. Seeing this we were astonished, and marvelling at the foolishness and misguidance of these people we moved on. At last, wandering about, we came to a well at the edge of the village at which a few young girls were drawing water. It so happened that the glance of the Shaykh and guide Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī fell upon one of the girls, who, besides being distinguished among all her companions in her God-given beauty and grace, was adorned with jewellery and fine clothes. No sooner had the Shaykh's eyes met hers than his condition began to change and his face began to alter. In this state of agitation the Shaykh addressed her companions and said: whose daughter is she?

The girls: she is the daughter of the chief of this village.

The Shaykh: then why has her father kept her in such a lowly state that she draws water from the well herself? Can he not keep some maid or servant for her who would serve her?

The girls: why not? But her father is an extremely wise and understanding man. His object is that the girl should not, by taking pride in her father's wealth and property, servants and retinue, spoil her natural character, and that after her marriage, when she goes to her husband's house, she should fall short in nothing in serving him.

Ḥaḍrat Shiblī (may Allah have mercy on him) says: after this the Shaykh sat down with his head bowed, and three full days passed over him in which he neither ate nor drank nor spoke to anyone; when the time of prayer came, however, he would perform the prayer. The large body of his disciples and students was with him, but they were in severe distress and could see no remedy.

Ḥaḍrat Shiblī (may Allah have mercy on him) says: on the third day, seeing this condition, I stepped forward and submitted: O Shaykh, your disciples are astonished and distressed at this continued silence of yours; do say something — what is the matter?

The Shaykh (turning towards the company): my dear ones, how long shall I hide my condition from you? The love of the girl whom I saw the day before yesterday has so overpowered me that she has taken possession of all my limbs and faculties; it is now in no way possible for me to leave this land.

Ḥaḍrat Shiblī: O our master, you are the spiritual guide of the people of Iraq, world-renowned in knowledge and excellence and in abstinence and worship; the number of your disciples has passed twelve thousand. For the sake of the noble Qur'an, do not disgrace us and all of them.

The Shaykh: my dear ones, my destiny and yours have been decreed by the decree of Allah. The robe of sainthood has been stripped from me and the signs of guidance have been taken away. Saying this, he began to weep: "O my people, the divine decree has taken effect; the matter is no longer in my power." Ḥaḍrat Shiblī (may Allah have mercy on him) says: we were struck with utter amazement at this strange event, and we began to weep in grief. The Shaykh too was weeping with us, until the ground became wet with the flood of tears that poured forth. After this we were compelled to return to our homeland Baghdad, and when the people heard the news of our arrival they came out of the city to visit the Shaykh; and not seeing the Shaykh with us they asked the reason. ### ANSWER
A few matters concerning this incident deserve consideration:

(1) The incident of Shaykh Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī mentioned above is found in al-Mustaṭraf fī Kull Fann Mustaẓraf, part one, pp. 153–155, printed by Maktabat wa Maṭbaʿat al-Mashhad al-Ḥusaynī, Cairo, where it is related without any chain, with the word

"حكي"

ʿAbd al-Ḥalīm Sharar too quoted this incident on the authority of al-Mustaṭraf in his biography of Abū Bakr Shiblī (Lucknow edition), pp. 80–82.

In the margin of al-Mustaṭraf the book of Ibn Ḥijjah al-Ḥamawī, Thamarāt al-Awrāq, is also printed. In this book too the incident is related, in part 2, p. 267, without any chain, with the word

"حكي"

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) mentioned this incident in Anfās-e ʿĪsā (part two, pp. 652–653) without any reference.

(2) Ḥaḍrat Shaykh Mawlānā Muḥammad Zakariyyā (may Allah have mercy on him) quoted this incident in the appendix to Āp Bītī, number 5, pp. 71–83, on the authority of Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān printed in Egypt, and he also wrote that this incident had been translated from Damīrī's Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān and published by Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ, formerly chief muftī of Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband and at that time director of Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi, in Muḥarram 40 AH.

Muftī Iḥsān Allāh Shāʾiq quoted this incident on the authority of Ḥaḍrat Shaykh's Āp Bītī in his book Sabaq Āmōz Wāqiʿāt (pp. 28–33).

(3) We first searched for this incident in numerous copies of Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān and in some manuscripts, but did not find it. Finally we obtained that copy of Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān which has been printed in four volumes with the critical edition of Ibrāhīm Ṣāliḥ by Dār al-Bashāʾir, Damascus. In editing this book the editor kept three copies before him:

1. A manuscript consisting of 572 pages, preserved under number 1149 in the library of the Arabic Language Academy, Damascus, under the title Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān.

2. A manuscript consisting of 349 pages, preserved under number 1072 in the library of the Arabic Language Academy, Damascus, under the title Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān al-Wusṭā.

3. The printed copy issued from Būlāq at the press of Bābī Ḥalabī, Cairo, together with which the ʿAjāʾib al-Makhlūqāt of Qazwīnī is also printed.

In the last volume, on p. 334, the editor has also given an index of names, that is, an index in which the names of all those persons who are mentioned once or several times in the book are given with the volume and page number. But in this index there is no mention of Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī. The name of Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Qurṭubī does occur in one place only, in volume 2, p. 729; but this story is not mentioned there — rather, what is mentioned there is a frog glorifying Allah in the time of Ḥaḍrat Dāwūd (peace be upon him).

Moreover, wherever in this book Shiblī or Junayd Baghdādī is mentioned, whether as the subject or incidentally, there too there is no mention of Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī among their shaykhs.

(3) The famous personalities mentioned in this story are: 1. Junayd Baghdādī; 2. Abū Bakr Shiblī.

Junayd Baghdādī passed away at the end of the third century of the hijrah (297 AH), and Abū Bakr Shiblī died at the beginning of the fourth century of the hijrah (334 AH).

Among the famous personalities of Andalusia in the third century of the hijrah we find mention of Muḥammad ibn Waḍḍāḥ ibn Bazīgh, Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī, who was born in 199 or 200 AH and passed away in 287 AH. Besides being a reciter of the Qur'an and a scholar of hadith, he was also a devoted worshipper and an ascetic, and he undertook journeys more in search of the men of Allah than in the search for hadith.

From what Ḥaḍrat Shaykh Zakariyyā (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote about Shaykh Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī at the beginning of the incident, it also appears that the Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī mentioned in the incident is this same Muḥammad ibn Waḍḍāḥ ibn Bazīgh, because in the books of history and biography we have not found any personality of the third century of the hijrah in Andalusia or its surroundings who, along with being a reciter of the Qur'an and a scholar of hadith, also occupied a high station in abstinence and God-fearingness.

But what is astonishing is this:

Ibn al-Faraḍī (d. 403 AH) gave a detailed account of Muḥammad ibn Waḍḍāḥ, Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī, in Tārīkh ʿUlamāʾ al-Andalus (2/17–19, biography number 1136), but he neither mentioned Junayd and Shiblī among his students nor wrote any story of this kind.

Ibn al-Faraḍī writes:

محمد بن وضاح بن بزيغ مولي الإمام عبد الرحمن بن معاوية رضي الله عنه، من أهل قرطبة يكني: أبا عبد الله. روي بالأندلس

ولم يكن مذهبه في رحلته هذه طلب الحديث، وإنما كان شأنه الزهد، وطلب العُبّاد، ولو سمع في رحلته هذه لكان أرفع أهل زمانه درجة وأعلاهم إسناداً.

ورحل رحلة ثانية فسمع فيها: من إسماعيل بن أبي أويس، ويعقوب بن حميد بن كاسب

وعدة الرجال الذين سمع منهم في الأمصار خمس وسبعين ومائة رجلا.

وبمحمد بن وضاح وبقي بن مخلد صارت الأندلس دار حديث. وكان محمد بن وضاح عالما بالحديث، بصيرا بطرقه، متكلما على علله، كثير الحكاية عن العباد، ورعاً، زاهداً، فقيراً، متعففاً، صابراً على الأسماع، محتسباً في نشر علمه، سمع منه الناس كثيراً، ونفع الله به أهل الأندلس.

(Tārīkh ʿUlamāʾ al-Andalus 1/17–19, by ʿAbd Allāh ibn Muḥammad ibn Yūsuf, known as Ibn al-Faraḍī, published by Maktabat al-Khānjī, Cairo).

Ibn al-Jazarī writes in Ghāyat al-Nihāyah fī Ṭabaqāt al-Qurrāʾ:

محمد بن وضاح بن بزيغ أبو عبد الله الأندلسي القرطبي، إمام زاهد ثقة، روي القراءة عن عبد الصمد بن عبد الرحمن عن ورش،

وروي عنه عدد القرآن على عدد المدني الأول، قال الداني: ومن وقته اعتمد أهل الأندلس على رواية ورش وصارت عندهم مدونة، وكانوا قبل ذلك معتمدين على رواية الغاز بن قيس عن نافع.

(Ghāyat al-Nihāyah fī Ṭabaqāt al-Qurrāʾ, by Shams al-Dīn Abu'l-Khayr Ibn al-Jazarī 2/275, published by Maktabat Ibn Taymiyyah).

In the same way, in

جذوة المقتبـس في ذكـر ولاة الأندلس

(pp. 93–94, published by al-Dār al-Miṣriyyah, Cairo), Muḥammad ibn Futūḥ al-Azdī (d. 488 AH) wrote the circumstances of Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī; but in this too there is no mention of this story.

In the same way Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā al-Ḍabbī (d. 599 AH), in

بغية الملتمس في تاريخ رجال أهل الأندلس

(pp. 133–134, published by Dār al-Kitāb al-ʿArabī, Cairo), made no mention of this story in his account of Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī.

Likewise ʿAllāmah Dhahabī in Tārīkh al-Islām (6/828, edited by Bashshār ʿAwwād Maʿrūf), Tadhkirat al-Ḥuffāẓ (2/162–163, Dār al-Kutub al-ʿIlmiyyah, Beirut) and Siyar Aʿlām al-Nubalāʾ (13/445–446, al-Risālah), and Ibrāhīm ibn ʿAlī al-Yaʿmurī (d. 799) in al-Dībāj al-Mudhahhab fī Maʿrifat Aʿyān ʿUlamāʾ al-Madhhab (2/179–181, Dār al-Turāth, Cairo), wrote detailed accounts of Muḥammad ibn Waḍḍāḥ Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī al-Qurṭubī and enumerated those who narrated from him; but here too there is no mention of Shiblī and Junayd Baghdādī, nor did they mention this incident.

Besides these, others too have written brief and detailed accounts of Muḥammad ibn Waḍḍāḥ Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī. For fear of prolixity we have confined ourselves to the references mentioned.

We also examined the accounts of Abū Bakr Shiblī and Junayd Baghdādī in the books of biography; but despite much searching we found neither any mention of Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī among the shaykhs of these two men, nor this or any similar incident.

The summing up:

In the light of the passages quoted, a few things have become clear:

1. This incident is not mentioned in Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān — or at least it is not in the copies that are before us.

2. In al-Mustaṭraf and Thamarāt al-Awrāq the incident is mentioned, but with the word

"حكي"

and no chain is mentioned.

3. Of the later writers, some have given the reference of al-Mustaṭraf, as ʿAbd al-Ḥalīm Sharar did in his biography of Abū Bakr Shiblī; some have mentioned it without any reference, as Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) did; some have given the reference of Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān, as Ḥaḍrat Shaykh did in Āp Bītī — but this is the result of a slip of the pen of the author or the copyist; and some have given the reference of Ḥaḍrat Shaykh's Āp Bītī, as Muftī Iḥsān Allāh Shāʾiq did in Sabaq Āmōz Wāqiʿāt.

4. The qualities of Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī described in the incident — that he was a reciter of the Qur'an, a scholar of hadith and an imam in abstinence and God-fearingness — were all present in Muḥammad ibn Waḍḍāḥ Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī al-Qurṭubī, from which it appears that by the Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Andalusī mentioned in the incident this same Muḥammad ibn Waḍḍāḥ is meant.

5. The circumstances of Muḥammad ibn Waḍḍāḥ are found in the books of history and biography, both in detail and in brief; but in no book at all did we find, in the account of his life, either any mention of Abū Bakr Shiblī and Junayd Baghdādī or this incident, on account of which the authenticity of this incident is doubtful. واللہ سبحانہ ٗ و تعالی اعلم بالصواب۔
