### TITLE
Answering the argument from a hadith for a woman travelling without a maḥram

### QUESTION
Can a woman travel for Hajj without a maḥram or not? A hadith says that a woman will travel from Iraq to Makkah and will fear none but Allah.

### ANSWER
A woman cannot undertake a journey of the distance fixed by the sharia without a maḥram; there is no room for it in the sharia. She is therefore not permitted to travel for Hajj without a maḥram either, and this prohibition is established by hadith. (Some senior scholars, may Allah have mercy on them, do say that if an elderly woman's going without a maḥram involves no temptation, and no mixing or being alone with unrelated men, then there is room for her to travel in a group of women.)

See the hadith:

عن أبي سعيد الخدري رضي الله تعالى عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: لا يحل لامرأة تؤمن بالله واليوم الآخر أن تسافر سفراً يكون ثلاثة أيام فصاعداً إلا ومعها أبو ها أو ابنها أو زوجها أو أخوها أو ذو محرم منها. (مسلم شريف: ۱/٤۳٤، باب سفر المرأة مع محرم إلى حج وغيره)

Now consider the hadith cited in the question:

لترين الظعينة ترتحل من الحيرة حتى تطوف بالكعبة لا تخاف أحداً إلا الله. (رواه البخاري: ۱/۵۰۷).

The answer regarding this hadith is that its purpose is to describe the peace and safety that will prevail, not to declare it permissible for a woman to travel without a maḥram. At the beginning of the hadith the Prophet ﷺ was telling ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim (may Allah be pleased with him) of things to come: that a time would come when there would be such peace and safety that a woman would travel from al-Ḥīrah to Makkah with no fear of anyone but Allah — that is, a security we can scarcely imagine. Besides, the body of hadith literature contains many examples where the point of a statement is one thing while the actual ruling is given elsewhere. Here is one example:

A hadith says:

وعن أبي هريرة رضي الله تعالى عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: والذي نفسي بيده لو لم تذنبوا لذهب الله تعالى بكم ولجاء بقوم يذنبون ويستغفرون الله تعالى فيغفر لهم. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۳۵۵، باب سقوط الذنوب بالاستغفار والتوبة).

This hadith is not meant to praise sin or to encourage it. Its purpose is to show how beloved and sought-after repentance is. As for sin, its ruling is learnt from other hadiths — how blameworthy and dangerous it is, and that it is rebellion against Allah.

(In the light of what Mawlānā Anwar Shāh (may Allah have mercy on him) has said, one possible way of explaining this hadith is that where there is no fear of temptation there is room for a woman to travel alone, كما سيأتي.)

The upshot is that a woman is not permitted to undertake a journey of the sharia-defined distance without a maḥram.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
