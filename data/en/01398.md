### TITLE
Making the intention of ʿĀshūrāʾ along with a make-up fast

### QUESTION
A man had some fasts of Ramadan still outstanding. On the tenth of Muḥarram, the day of ʿĀshūrāʾ, he kept one of these make-up fasts and also made the intention of ʿĀshūrāʾ along with it. Is this valid or not? That is, will he receive the reward of ʿĀshūrāʾ or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described this is permissible: the make-up fast of Ramadan will be discharged, and the reward and merit of ʿĀshūrāʾ will be attained as well. ʿAllāmah Sayyid al-Ḥamawī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in his notes on al-Ashbāh:

قيل: يستفاد منه أجزاء النية عن الاثنين وعرفة لحصول المقصود فيه أيضاً كما لا يخفي، انتهى، وفيه تأمل، أقول: في فتح القدير: صام في يوم عرفة مثل قضاء أو نذر أو كفارة ونوي معه الصوم عن يوم عرفة أفتي بعضهم بالصحة والحصول عنهما انتهى. ومنه يستفاد الحكم الذي لم يذكره المصنف رحمه الله بالطريق الأولي. (غمز عيون البصائر: ۱/۱٤۷، السادس في بيان الجمع بين عبادتين).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā, however, draws a distinction: if the reward and merit in question is specific to a voluntary fast, it will not be attained by making that intention within a make-up fast — as with the six fasts of Shawwāl; but if it attaches to fasting in general, it will be attained — as with the fast of the Day of ʿArafah and the fifteenth of Shaʿbān. Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

The hadith of Muslim,

من صام رمضان ثم أتبعه ستاً من شوال كان كصيام الدهر

shows that these six fasts are meant to be fasts other than those of Ramadan. Moreover, the reason given for the reward of fasting a lifetime is that the reward of every good deed is at least tenfold; on this reckoning the month of Ramadan stands in place of ten months, two months of the full year then remain, and to complete them there are the six fasts of Shawwāl, which stand in place of sixty days, that is two months. This too establishes that voluntary fasts are meant, and that this merit will not be attained by keeping make-up fasts on those days.

The fast of ʿĀshūrāʾ was prescribed as an act of thanksgiving, which shows that its merit too is specific to a voluntary fast, so keeping a make-up fast on that day will not earn this reward.

As for the fast of the fifteenth of Shaʿbān and the Day of ʿArafah, no restriction to voluntary fasting is established from any source. Although such merits are generally mentioned in connection with voluntary acts, the wording of the narrations is unrestricted; in view of the plain wording it is not far-fetched that the merit is attained by keeping a make-up fast on these two days, especially since there is also the glad tiding, "I am as My servant thinks of Me."

Some scholars have argued that the merit is not attained, on the grounds that an intention of a voluntary act is not valid within an obligatory one. This argument is not sound, because the issue here is not one of making an intention for a voluntary fast; the discussion is rather whether the promised merit is for voluntary fasting alone or for fasting in general, whether voluntary or obligatory.

ونظيره ما حرر ابن عابدين رحمه الله: أنه ينال فضل التهجد بقضاء الفوائت بعد العشاء،

فقط واللہ تعالی اعلم (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/430).

In any case, the commentary on al-Ashbāh shows that in a single fast the intention of any kind of voluntary fast may be made along with an obligatory or necessary one, and this accords with the mercy and bounty of Allah Most High. Allah Most High makes sleep taken with the intention of tahajjud, and waking for tahajjud by chance, stand in place of tahajjud; and He makes the omission of voluntary prayers by the sick and by travellers stand in place of those prayers. The view mentioned in the commentary on al-Ashbāh is therefore the one closer to His mercy.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
