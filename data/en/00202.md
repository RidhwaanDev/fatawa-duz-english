### TITLE
The ruling on Duʿāʾ-i Jamīlah

### QUESTION
A booklet of supplications is in circulation among ordinary people which goes by the name Duʿāʾ-i Jamīlah. Who — or what — is "Jamīlah"? To whom is this supplication attributed, and is that attribution sound or not? And are its words objectionable or praiseworthy? بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
As for the booklet published under the name Duʿāʾ-i Jamīlah, we have no verified information about who or what "Jamīlah" is; it may possibly be used in the sense of "beautiful supplications". The merits set out at the beginning of the booklet are as follows.

Whoever recites it after the Fajr prayer will receive the reward of three hundred Hajj, equal to that of Ādam (peace be upon him); whoever recites it after the Ẓuhr prayer will receive the reward of five hundred Hajj, equal to that of Ibrāhīm (peace be upon him); whoever recites it after the ʿAṣr prayer will receive the reward of a hundred Hajj, equal to that of Yūnus (peace be upon him); whoever recites it after the ʿIshāʾ prayer will receive the reward of a thousand Hajj, equal to that of Mūsā (peace be upon him); and whoever recites it after the tahajjud prayer will receive the reward of a hundred thousand Hajj, equal to that of Muḥammad, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. And if anyone doubts this, he will gain no benefit.

There is a narration that one day the Prophet ﷺ was sitting in the Prophet's Mosque when Jibrīl (peace be upon him) came and said: "O Messenger of Allah, Allah sends His greetings of peace, and He has sent this Duʿāʾ-i Jamīlah for your community." The Prophet ﷺ asked how much reward there was in it. Jibrīl (peace be upon him) said that whoever recites it or keeps it with him will be forgiven by Allah, even if his sins were like the foam of the sea, or like the sand of the wilderness, or as many as the leaves of the trees; that at the moment of death Allah will grant him a good end with His own hand of power; that an angel will guard him in the grave until the Day of Judgement; and that whoever recites it on the fifteenth of Ramadan at the time of breaking the fast — or, if he cannot read, keeps it in his hand — and recites the blessing eleven times:

"اللهم صل على سيدنا محمد وعلي آل سيدنا ومولنا محمد وبارك وسلم برحمتك يا أرحم الراحمين"

with ablution, will receive reward beyond limit and beyond counting, and Allah will fulfil whatever need he has. If he recites it even once in his whole life, or keeps it with him, he will pass easily over the bridge of Ṣirāṭ on the Day of Judgement and enter Paradise. Then Jibrīl (peace be upon him) said: "O Messenger of Allah, warn your community of the fire of Hell; if you announce the merit of this supplication, the whole of creation will give up prayer and fasting." Whoever recites this supplication, or keeps it with him, will not be affected by magic, and his enemies will become friends. Whoever recites this supplication, or keeps it with him, will have a face shining like the moon on the Day of Judgement; it will be an expiation for missed prayers, and there will be no slackness in prayer while travelling. On the Day of Judgement, when he rises from his grave, people will ask, "Which prophet is this?" and Allah will say, "He is not a prophet. This man recited Duʿāʾ-i Jamīlah in the world with a sincere heart, and it is for that reason that this favour and mercy are his." And on the Day of Judgement Allah will admit him into Paradise. (Risālah Duʿāʾ-i Jamīlah)

Attributing these merits to the Prophet ﷺ is not sound; the report is fabricated. It is therefore not correct to recite this supplication with these merits in view — even though the supplication itself contains no wording that is objectionable or that suggests associating partners with Allah. One should rather take care to use the sunnah supplications of the Qur'an and the hadiths.

Consider what Shaykh Ibrāhīm al-Ḥalabī (d. 956 AH) says about supplications of this kind:

موضوع باطل لا أصل له ولا يجوز العمل به ولا نقله إلا لبيان بطلانه كما هو شأن الأحاديث الموضوعة ويدلك على وضعه ركاكته والمبالغة الغير الموافقة للشرع والعقل فإن الأجر على قدر المشقة شرعاً وعقلاً وأفضل الأعمال أحمزها وإنما قصد بعض الملحدين بمثل هذا الحديث إفساد الدين وإضلال الخلق وإغراء هم بالفسق وتثبيطهم عن الجد في العبادة فيغتر به بعض من ليس له خبرة بعلوم الحديث وطرقه ولا ملكة يميزها بين صحيحه وسقيمه، قال الربيع بن خيثم: إن للحديث ضوء مثل ضوء النهار يعرفه وظلمة كظلمة الليل تنكره. وقال ابن الجوزي: إن الحديث المنكر يقشعر منه جلد الطالب للعلم وينفر منه قلبه في الغالب انتهى. (شرح منية المصلي، ص ٦۱۷، فصل في مسائل شتى، ط: سهيل).

About the supplication written at the end of this booklet under the name "Duʿāʾ Dafʿ-i Wabāʾ" (the supplication for repelling epidemic), Muftī Kifāyatullāh Ṣāḥib (1292–1372 AH) says:

These words were most probably composed by the Shīʿah. Sunnis should keep away from them, because they suggest associating partners with Allah. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 9/58)

In another place he says:

This is certainly the work of the Shīʿah sect, and its content does not agree with the beliefs of the Ahl al-Sunnah. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 9/63, Dār al-Ishāʿat)
