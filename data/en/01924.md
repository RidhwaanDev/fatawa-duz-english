### TITLE
Trading in shoes made from pigskin

### QUESTION
We are shoe retailers. We travel to Europe and elsewhere to buy stock. The shoemakers there use parts of the pig in making shoes — for example, they line the shoes with pigskin. We tell them to replace the pigskin lining with some other leather, and they usually do so. In one case we bought goods from a trader and told him to change the lining before sending them on to us, and we paid the price in advance through the bank. When the consignment reached us and we inspected it, all the shoes still had their pigskin linings. It is now impossible to return the goods, the price has already been paid, and on top of that we have paid the duties and taxes due on the consignment. What should we do with the shoes now? Please tell us the ruling of the sharia and how we may recover our money. بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case described, the sale of footwear made with parts of the pig never took place at all; the sale is void, because the pig, in all its parts, is impure in its very substance. Returning the shoes is therefore necessary and obligatory. But since return is impossible and the price has already been paid, one way of recovering what is due to you is found in the words of the jurists: appoint a non-Muslim as your agent to sell them, recover what is due to you, and then rid yourself of unlawful wealth by giving the remaining amount away in charity without any intention of reward.

Fatāwā Bayyināt states:

Question: Shoes and leather goods are coming into our country from China, Spain and other countries, and pigskin is being used in them. Some shoes are made of rexine but are lined inside with pigskin, and some are made entirely of pigskin. Is it unlawful to wear these shoes? Is it unlawful to sell them? And what should those shopkeepers do who have unwittingly bought stock worth millions of rupees?

Answer, in the name of Allah Most High: There is no doubt that the pig is impure in its very substance. The jurists have declared the use of all its parts, and the buying and selling of them, unlawful, and the same ruling applies to goods that contain parts of the pig, its skin and so on. In goods made from the pig and its parts no sale is concluded at all, and the price is unlawful for the seller — indeed, it does not even enter his ownership. Those who have bought such products unwittingly should return them to the shopkeepers, and the shopkeepers should return the goods to the companies from which they bought them, so that those companies can return the goods to the non-Muslims who supplied them and recover their money. (فتاوی بینات، کتاب الحظر و الاباحۃ، جلد چہارم، ص 470-471، مکتبہ بینات) ۔

Here is a precedent for recovering what is due to one:

قال الإمام السرخسي رحمه الله: وإذا كان في تركة الذمي خمر وخنزير وغرماؤه مسلمون وليس له وصي فإن القاضي يوكل ببيع ذلك رجلاً من أهل الذمة فيبيعه ويقضي به دين الميت لأن من يأمره القاضي يكون نائباً عن الميت

والميت كافر فيجوز بيع الذمي خمره على سبيل النيابة عنه والغرماء إنما يقبضون الثمن بدينهم لا أن يكون بيع قيم القاضي واقعاً لهم. (المبسوط: ۱۵/۱۳۱، باب قسمة الدار للميت وعليه دين أو وصية، بيروت).

وفي العناية شرح الهداية: وإذا أمر المسلم نصرانياً ببيع خمر أو شرائها ففعل جاز عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله، وقالا: لا يجوز على المسلم

وقولهما الموكل لا يليه فلا يوليه غيره منقوض

بالقاضي إذا أمر ذمياً ببيع خمر أو خنزير خلفه ذمي آخر وهو لا يلي التصرف بنفسه وبالذمي إذا أوصي لمسلم وقد تركهما فإن الوصي يوكل ذمياً بالبيع والقسمة وهو لا يلي ذلك بنفسه. (العناية شرح الهداية على هامش فتح القدير: ٦/٤۳۹، باب البيع الفاسد، دار الفكر).
