### TITLE
Delegating the power of divorce to a dār al-qaḍāʾ or to the Jamʿiyyat al-ʿUlamāʾ

### QUESTION
A husband delegates (assigns) the power of divorce to the body of Muslims, that is to a dār al-qaḍāʾ, in these words: "If I am unable to appear before the dār al-qaḍāʾ at the appointed time without a valid reason and without notice, the dār al-qaḍāʾ shall have the authority, whenever it wishes, to effect one irrevocable divorce upon my wife." By delegating in this way, will the dār al-qaḍāʾ have the authority of divorce in the event of his non-appearance or not? And will this man have the right to withdraw from this statement of his or not? Please explain in detail with evidence.

### ANSWER
In the case in question, if the husband gave this authority to the body of Muslims or to the dār al-qaḍāʾ and signed their agreement, to the effect that if he does not appear before the court session without a valid reason the dār al-qaḍāʾ will have the authority to effect one irrevocable divorce upon his wife, then according to the Sharia this form will be called delegation, and the dār al-qaḍāʾ will have the authority to give divorce. Thereafter the husband cannot withdraw from his statement; it will be for all time, and it may also be made temporary — for twenty-five years, for example, and so on.

But because of signing this deed of delegation and agreement the husband's own right to give divorce does not come to an end; if the husband himself gives divorce, it will still take effect. Note what Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

(1) If a man gives his authority to his wife so that she may effect divorce upon herself on his behalf, or gives some other person the authority that he may, if he wishes, divorce his wife, this is delegation. And if he instructs another sane adult to give divorce and does not leave it to that person's discretion, this is appointing an agent.

(2) Delegation is an assignment of ownership, whereas appointing an agent is not. For this reason withdrawal is not valid in the delegation of divorce, whereas withdrawal is valid in the appointment of an agent for divorce. Moreover, an owner acts upon his own wish, whereas an agent has nothing to do with wish and opinion; only the act is required of him.

(3) Delegation is limited to the sitting, though if the wording is general it will be kept to its generality — for example, if he says "whenever you wish" and so forth. Appointing an agent is the opposite, in that it is not limited to the sitting. (Majmūʿah Qawānīn-e Islāmī: from 150 to 154).

قال في الدر المختار: لا تطلق بعده أي المجلس إلا إذا زاد على قوله طلقي نفسك، وأخواته متي شئت أو متي ما شئت أو إذا شئت أو إذا ما شئت، فلا يتقيد بالمجلس ولم يصح رجوعه. وفي الشامية: قوله فلا يتقيد بالمجلس، أما في متي ومتي ما فلأنهما لعموم الأوقات فكأنه قال: في أي وقت شئت فلا يقتصر على المجلس. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۳/۳۱۷، سعيد).

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ records:

أما بيان صفته فهو أنه لا زم من جانب الزوج حتى لا يملك الرجوع عنه ولا نهي المرأة عما فعل إليها ولا فسخ ذلك لأنه ملكها الطلاق ومن ملك غيره شيئاً فقد زالت ولايته من الملك فلا يملك إبطاله بالرجوع والنهي والفسخ

وإن كان مؤقتاً بوقت وعلمت في شيء من الوقت صار الأمر بيدها، فأما إذا علمت بعد مضي الوقت كله لا يصير الأمر بيدها بهذا التفويض أبداً لأن ذلك علم لا ينفع لأن التفويض المؤقت بوقت ينتهي عند انتهاء الوقت. (بدائع الصنائع: ۳/۱۱۳، سعيد).
وفيه أيضاً: فإن وقته بوقت خاص بأن قال: أمرك بيدك يوماً أو شهراً أو سنة أو قال: اليوم أو الشهر أو السنة أو قال: هذا اليوم أو هذا الشهر أو هذه السنة لا يتقيد بالمجلس ولها الأمر في الوقت كله تختار نفسها فيما شاءت منه. (۳/۱۱۵، سعيد).
وفيه أيضاً: وأجمعوا على أن قوله لا جنبي طلق امرأتي توكيل ولا يتقيد بالمجلس وهو فصل التوكيل فإن قيده بالمشيئة بأن قال له: طلق امرأتي إن شئت فهذا تمليك عند أصحابنا الثلاثة

(بدائع الصنائع: ۳/۱۲۲، سعيد).

Majmūʿah Qawānīn-e Islāmī states:

After delegating the power of divorce the husband cannot withdraw from it. If the husband made the delegation for a fixed period and that period passed without the wife effecting divorce within it, the delegation has come to an end. The husband's own right of divorce does not come to an end on account of a delegation or an appointment of an agent. (Majmūʿah Qawānīn-e Islāmī, p. 154).
