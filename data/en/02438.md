### TITLE
The ruling on sacrifice when animals are sold at a grossly inflated price

### QUESTION
If in some place a sacrificial animal is sold at a grossly excessive price — for example, an animal worth 500 rand is sold for 1,000 rand — what is the ruling on sacrifice in that case?

### ANSWER
Sacrifice is necessary upon people who have the means, so it is necessary to buy an animal within one's means and offer the sacrifice. If a person does not possess the threshold of wealth, that is, he does not have the means to buy a sacrificial animal, then sacrifice is not necessary upon him. And lawful measures should be adopted to bring prices back to a moderate level.

Consider what Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

وأما شرائط الوجوب

منها الغني لما روي عن رسول الله ﷺ أنه قال: من وجد سعة فليضح شرط عليه الصلاة والسلام السعة وهي الغني. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/٦٤، ط: سعيد).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muftī Yūsuf Ludhyānwī, the martyr (may Allah have mercy on him), says:

Sacrifice is necessary upon people who have the means, and the authority to lift the obligations laid down by the Sharīʿah, or to suspend or abolish them, belongs to Allah; the noble scholars do not possess this authority. As for the question of keeping prices moderate, other measures can be adopted for that, and certainly should be. Those who cannot afford to buy expensive animals are not obliged to sacrifice, and they need not do so; but the remedy for this is not to announce that the sacrifice itself is cancelled for the year. (Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥall: 5/433, new edition).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
