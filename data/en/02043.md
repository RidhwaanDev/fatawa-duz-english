### TITLE
Building a lavatory with interest money

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to build the lavatory of a madrasah or a school with interest money, or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, in the view of some muftis it is permissible and correct to build the lavatory of a madrasah, a school and so on with interest money.

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

The question is a disputed one. Giving the money to a poor, needy person is preferable. There is room to use it for public welfare works such as roads. It cannot be used for a mosque, but it may be used to repair a mosque's lavatory. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 9/279).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

Once the money has been received, it may be spent on good works connected with public welfare, or on works that serve to meet the needs of the poor and destitute — for example, on stipends for orphans, the destitute and the students of Islamic madrasahs, on providing them with books and the like, or on building a travellers' lodge, a well or a road, or on lighting the roads. All these forms are permissible. It should not, however, be spent on a mosque, since that is contrary to the sanctity of the mosque. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 7/105, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

Interest money may be used to meet the individual need of some needy person, or to carry out a work of collective benefit such as digging a well. This money should not be put into mosques and madrasahs, but the scholars have permitted its use for building lavatories. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil, vol. 1, p. 253).

Islāmī Fiqh states:

If a person is compelled to deposit money in a bank or the like and receives interest on it, he should not use it for himself or for his children, but should give it to the poor without any intention of earning reward, or have it put through some reliable person into a welfare project — for instance into the roads of the neighbourhood or village, public lavatories and the like. (Islāmī Fiqh: 2/369).

Caution, however, lies in building the lavatory with interest money only after the device of transferring ownership has been employed.

Niẓām al-Fatāwā states:

Once the device of transferring ownership to an entitled recipient has been used, it will be correct to use the money even for cleaning a mosque's lavatory and the like. The device of transferring ownership is that the whole of the interest money is given to poor and needy people who are entitled to charity, so that they become its owners, and the recipient then gives it to the mosque as a donation of his own.
