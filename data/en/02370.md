### TITLE
The ruling on eating whale

### QUESTION
The whale is a very large sea creature, or fish. What is the ruling of the sharia on eating it?

### ANSWER
Concerning the whale it is plainly established from a noble hadith that eating it is lawful and that it is like ordinary fish. The noble Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) ate it, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ himself also ate of it. There is therefore no doubt or uncertainty about its being lawful.

See what Bukhārī Sharīf states:

عن عمرو أنه سمع جابراً رضي الله عنه يقول: غزونا جيش الخبط وأمر أبو عبيدة فجعنا جوعاً شديداً فألقي البحر حوتاً ميتاً لم نر مثله يقول له العنبر فأكلنا منه نصف شهر

فلما قدمنا المدينة ذكرنا ذلك للنبي ﷺ فقال: كلوا رزقاً أخرجه الله، أطعمونا إن كان معكم فأتاه بعضهم أكله. (رواه البخاري، برقم: ٤۳٦۲، باب غزوة سيف البحر).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Kifāyatullāh wrote a detailed fatwa in answer to a question put to him. Some extracts from it are given below:

The animal which in present-day English is called the whale was called *whal* in old English, and in the German language its name is *wal*.

البال الحوت العظيم من حيتان البحر وليس بعربي كما في الصحاح يدعي جمل البحر وهو معرب وال كما في العُباب قال شيخنا وهي سمكة طولها خمسون ذراعاً. (تاج العروس ۷/۲۳۷).

That is, the bāl is one of the great fish among the fish of the sea; it is also called jamal al-baḥr. Our shaykh said that the bāl is a fish which is fifty cubits (75 feet) long.

In Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān, in Fatḥ al-Bārī the commentary on Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, and in al-Farāʾid al-Durriyyah, a second name for the bāl is given as ʿanbar; and in Lisān al-ʿArab and Tāj al-ʿArūs a third name for it, jamal al-baḥr, is also mentioned.

From all this research it is established that the bāl, the ʿanbar and the jamal al-baḥr are one great fish, which in English is called the whale. And once it is established to be a fish, there is no difficulty in regarding it as lawful in the Ḥanafī school either, because in the view of the Ḥanafīs fish is lawful (notwithstanding that it takes thousands of forms and shapes). Besides this, there is also a specific and explicit proof of the lawfulness of the bāl and the ʿanbar — that is, the whale — namely an explicit and sound hadith which is reported in the authoritative books of hadith and especially in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī. And so on. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 9/134–137).

In sum, whale, bāl, ʿanbar and wal are all different names for one and the same fish. This fish is like ordinary fish in shape, so there is little need to define it or to investigate it. A definition of "fish" is needed only when there is uncertainty about something's being a fish — just as, with a hermaphrodite of indeterminate sex, uncertainty arises only when no beard or moustache appears; if there is a beard and a moustache and the appearance of a man, the uncertainty comes to an end. A definition of a city is needed where there is uncertainty; but for cities like Calcutta, Bombay, Durban and Cape Town there is no need to go looking for a definition of a city.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
