### TITLE
Divorce pronounced in the present tense

### QUESTION
In the course of a quarrel a man said to his wife, "I divorce you." He said it three times; he did not say, "I have divorced you." Has divorce taken place by this or not? And if he intends by it a threat about the future, is that acceptable or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question divorce does take effect by the present-tense form, "یعنی طلاق دیتا ہوں" — that is, "I divorce you." Muftī Kifāyatullāh (may Allah have mercy on him) has written, however, that if he said "I divorce you" with the intention "I have divorced you", then an irrevocable divorce has fallen upon his wife (that is, when the word "three" accompanies it); but if he says that by "I divorce you" he meant that he intended to divorce her, then divorce will not take place. (کفایت المفتی: ٦/۷۹، ۸۰) ۔

Fatāwā al-Shāmī states:

وكذا المضارع إذا غلب في الحال مثل أطلقك كما في البحر. (فتاوى الشامي: ۳/۲٤۸، سعيد، والبحر الرائق: ۳/۲۵۲، كوئته).

Fatḥ al-Qadīr states:

ولا يقع بأطلقك إلا إذا غلب في الحال. (فتح القدير: ٤/۷، دار الفكر).

Fatāwā ʿĀlamgīrī states:

قالت: لزوجها: من با تو نمي باشم، فقال الزوج: مباش، فقالت: طلاق بدست تو است، مرا طلاق كن، فقال الزوج: طلاق مي كنم، طلاق مي كنم، وكرر ثلاثاً، طلقت ثلاثاً، بخلاف كنم، لأنه استقبال. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۳۸٤).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

The expression "I divorce you" is coined for the present, so divorce has taken effect by it, even though this sentence is now and then used for the near future as well, etc. (احسن الفتاوی: ۵/۱۵۳) ۔
