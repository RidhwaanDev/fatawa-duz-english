### TITLE
The ruling on reciting Durūd-i Tāj

### QUESTION
What is the position on reciting Durūd-i Tāj, and what is known about its author?

### ANSWER
Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

"The chains of transmission of Durūd-i Tāj and of Duʿāʾ-i Ganj al-ʿArsh are baseless." (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 2/99, Dār al-Ishāʿat)

It should be understood that Durūd-i Tāj is not a formula of blessing narrated from the Prophet ﷺ, and some of its sentences are objectionable in their meaning as well. Better than it are the formulae of blessing narrated from the Prophet ﷺ — the blessing recited in the prayer, for example, is among the most excellent of them. Reciting Durūd-i Tāj is not a sin, but the other transmitted formulae are superior to it. Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah states:

"The merits that certain ignorant people relate for this formula of blessing are entirely false. Its rank cannot possibly be known unless the Lawgiver (peace be upon him) has stated it, and this formula was composed hundreds of years after his time. How, then, can this wording be held to bring reward? To abandon the wordings of blessing that occur in sound hadith, to hope for great reward from this one, and to take it up as a regular litany is misguidance and innovation. And since it also contains expressions of associating partners with Allah, there is a danger that the beliefs of ordinary people will be corrupted; reciting it is therefore forbidden. To teach people Durūd-i Tāj is like handing a deadly poison to the public, because many people fall into the corruption of associating partners with Allah, and it becomes the cause of their ruin." (Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah: p. 143, and Taʾlīfāt-i Rashīdiyyah: p. 149)

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

"It is not known who first devised it. The merits that ordinary people relate for it are simply idle and false. The formulae of blessing that come in the hadiths are certainly superior to Durūd-i Tāj, and it also contains some expressions that amount to associating partners with Allah, so it should be given up. What Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah has written about it is correct." (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 1/222)

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

"The words of Durūd-i Tāj are not from the Noble Qur'an or from hadith, and reciting Durūd-i Tāj is not established from the noble Companions, the Successors, or the pious predecessors. Durūd-i Tāj is an invention of hundreds of years later. No other invented formula can compete with the wording of blessing that the Prophet ﷺ taught his noble Companions, such as Durūd-i Ibrāhīm. Between the blessing in the words that came from the blessed tongue of the master of creation ﷺ and words invented by a member of his community there is the difference of earth and sky. The blessing and the drawing power in the words the Prophet ﷺ himself gave and taught are not found in any other words; and if those other words are also contrary to the sunnah, then no comparison is left at all — the difference then becomes that between light and darkness."

The upshot of the ruling is this. The merits of Durūd-i Tāj that are well known among the ignorant are baseless and unfounded; they are not established from hadith. Merits and the measure of reward cannot possibly be known unless the Prophet ﷺ has stated them. Durūd-i Tāj itself is an invention of hundreds of years later, so who stated the merit and the measure of reward for reciting it, and when? To leave aside the wordings of blessing established in hadith, to hold a belief in vast promised rewards attached to non-sunnah wordings, and then to bind oneself to reciting them as a fixed litany, is an innovation. Moreover, ordinary people do not understand the difference in how expressions such as "the remover of calamity" may properly be applied, so instructing them to recite it amounts to plunging them into associating partners with Allah. Reciting Durūd-i Tāj is neither obligatory nor necessary nor a sunnah; what fairness is there, then, in abandoning the sunnah formula, clinging to this one, and making it the mark of faith and disbelief? (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 2/296)

From the passages quoted above it is clear that reciting Durūd-i Tāj is not correct. It is far better to recite the formulae of blessing that have come down in the hadiths. No information is available about its author.
