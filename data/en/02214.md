### TITLE
Taking a deposit from a tenant

### QUESTION
Is it valid to require a deposit or not? For example, a man wants to rent a house and the landlord says, "Place fifty thousand rand with me as a deposit and I will let you the house." What should be done now? Also, is this deposit money a loan or a pledge? There are then two forms of it: sometimes the rent is reduced because of the deposit and sometimes not — for instance, the rent is three thousand, but because of the deposit it is two thousand. بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
If there is no permission to make use of the deposit money, then it is a pledge (rahn); and the benefit of it is that if the tenant damages something or the house, or does not pay the rent, the amount will be deducted from what has been pledged. If there is permission to use it, then it is a loan; but in that case a reduction in the rent on account of it comes under interest, which would be impermissible.

See ʿAllāmah al-Ḥaṣkafī (may Allah have mercy on him), who says, defining a pledge:

هو حبس شيء مالي أي جعل الشيء محبوساً لأن الحابس هو المرتهن بحق يمكن استيفاؤه أي أخذه منه. (الدر المختار: /، كتاب الرهن، كوئته).

Durar al-Ḥukkām states:

هو حبس المال بحق يمكن أخذه أي الحق منه أي من المال.

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states: The question of money given as a deposit or as security calls for careful thought; on the face of it, it appears to be a kind of pledge. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/217).

Then, giving his own view, he says: I consider that there is no harm in taking security money in this way, and that this money has the status of a loan. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/130; and 1/217).

See further: Īḍāḥ al-Nawādir: 2/20; Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 7/733; Āp ke Masāʾil aur un kā Ḥal: 6/140; and Maḥmūd al-Fatāwā: 3/90.

Where the money is a loan, reducing the rent is not permitted. See the hadith, which states:

وقال عليه الصلاة والسلام كل قرض جر منفعة فهو رباً. إسناده ضعيف مرفوعاً لا موقوفاً. (إتحاف الخيرة المهرة: ۳/۳۹۰. ورواه الحاكم والبيهقي في سننه الكبرى).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
