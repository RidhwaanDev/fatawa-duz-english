### TITLE
The ruling on limited liability

### QUESTION
If someone wishes to register his company as a limited liability company, is that permissible or not? And is it permissible to buy shares in such a company?

A brief introduction to limited liability: limited liability means that the liability of the shareholders is limited to the capital they have put in. That is, if the company runs at a loss, the worst they will suffer is that the capital they invested is lost; and if the company's debts mount up, the shareholders will not be called upon for anything beyond the capital they invested. (Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām: 2/42).

Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil states: in modern legal and economic usage, "limited liability" describes a situation in which a partner or shareholder in a business protects himself against bearing any liability beyond the sum he has put into the limited liability company or partnership. If the business runs at a loss, the most a shareholder will suffer is that he loses his original capital, but the loss will not extend to his personal assets; and if the company's assets are not enough to discharge its liabilities, such as its debts, the creditors cannot claim their outstanding dues from the shareholders' personal assets. (Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil: 5/175). بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the sharia a company and its shareholders stand in the same relation as the owner of the capital and the working partner in a muḍārabah contract, because the shareholders in a company put their capital into it and share in the profit without doing the work themselves. The shareholders will therefore have the status of the owner of the capital.

The ruling on limited liability:

Most contemporary scholars are of the view that a partnership cannot be called defective merely because of the concept of limited liability. In fiqh there is a most interesting parallel to the limited company, one that comes very close to it: the slave who is permitted by his master to trade. Such a man is his master's property and trades with his master's permission, and whatever he trades in belongs to the master as well. If debts fall due upon him, they are limited to the value of the slave; nothing beyond that can be claimed either from the slave or from the master, and here too the creditors are left with unrecoverable dues. This parallel is the closer one to the limited company because, just as in a company the dues become unrecoverable while the shareholders are still alive, so here too the creditors' dues become unrecoverable while the master is still alive. (Islām awr Jadīd Maʿīshat o Tijārat, p. 100, Maktabah Maʿārif al-Qurʾān, Karachi).

Ghayr Sūdī Bankārī states:

Our respected teacher Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muftī Walī Ḥasan Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him), the founder of the Dār al-Iftāʾ of Jāmiʿat al-ʿUlūm al-Islāmiyyah, Binnori Town, also held the buying and selling of shares to be permissible subject to these conditions. In Pakistan the NIT unit invests almost entirely in the stock market, and all these companies are limited liability companies. In our Dār al-Iftāʾ there is a fatwa of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Walī Ḥasan Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) declaring investment in the NIT unit to be permissible, and that fatwa also bears the endorsing signature of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Dr ʿAbd al-Razzāq Sikandar Ṣāḥib (may his shade endure). (Ghayr Sūdī Bankārī, p. 356).

It also states:

The truth is that the bank's liability as a company being limited makes no difference to the relationship, or to the mutual rights and duties, that exist within the bank between the owner of the capital and the working partner. The owner of the capital will still carry exactly the liability the sharia places upon him, and the working partner will still carry exactly the liability the sharia places upon him. For the rule of the muḍārabah is that if a genuine loss occurs without any transgression on the working partner's part, it falls upon the owner of the capital; the working partner's only loss is that his labour has gone for nothing. So when the bank becomes the working partner and the depositors are treated as the owners of the capital, then if a genuine loss occurs without any transgression by the bank, the owners of the funds will bear it as a matter of sharia ruling — not because the bank's liability is limited. (Ghayr Sūdī Bankārī, p. 352).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وما هلك من مال المضاربة يصرف إلى الربح لأنه تبع فإن زاد الهالك على الربح لم يضمن ولو فاسدة من عمله لأنه أمين. وفي رد المحتار: قوله ولو فاسدة أي سواء كانت المضاربة صحيحة أو فاسدة، وسواء كان الهلاك من عمله أولا، قوله من عمله يعني المسلط عليه عند التجار، وأما التعدي فيظهر أنه يضمن. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۵/٦۵٦، سعيد).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Taqī Ṣāḥib (may his shade endure) has discussed the above question at length and has also written out answers to the objections raised against it. For the details see: (Islām awr Jadīd Maʿīshat o Tijārat; Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil; and Ghayr Sūdī Bankārī).

An objection and its answer:

One further objection does remain, namely that if the company goes bankrupt then, under the principles of muḍārabah, all the profit that the owner of the capital and the working partner had earned from the beginning of the contract would have to be given back.

See what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

إن قسم الربح وبقيت المضاربة ثم هلك المال أو بعضه ترادا الربح ليأخذ المالك رأس المال وما فضل بينهما وإن نقص لم يضمن. (الدر المختار: ۵/٦۵٦، سعيد).

The answer to this is that the limited company should draw up a fresh contract with its shareholders every year. Then, if the company goes bankrupt, only the profit earned after the new contract will be liable for the debts.

See what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وإن قسم الربح وفسخت المضاربة والمال في يد المضارب ثم عقداها فهلك المال لم يترادا وبقيت المضاربة لأنه عقد جديد وهي الحيلة النافعة للمضارب. وفي رد المحتار: قوله النافعة للمضارب أي لو خاف أن يسترد منه رب المال الربح بعد القسمة بسبب هلاك ما بقي من رأس المال وعلم مما مر آنفاً أنه لا يتوقف صحة الحيلة على أن يسلم المضارب رأس المال إلى رب المال وتقييد الزيلعي به اتفاقي كما نبه عليه أبو السعود. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۵/٦۵٦، سعيد).

In sum, registering a company with limited liability, and buying its shares, is permissible and valid subject to the conditions set out above.
