### TITLE
The ruling on sacrificing an animal with a broken horn

### QUESTION
Nowadays it is common practice among farmers to pull out the horns of their animals from the root, the reason being that they keep animals in large numbers and there is a strong fear of their attacking one another — and in particular a good deal of difficulty is faced in taking them to the abattoir. So is the sacrifice of such hornless animals valid or not?

### ANSWER
Animals that have no horns at all, or whose horns have broken, may validly be sacrificed. Yes, if the horns have broken off at the root, or have been pulled out from the root, and the effect of this has reached the brain, then the sacrifice of such animals is not permissible or valid.

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

وتجزئ الجماء وهي التي لا قرن لها خلقة وكذا مكسورة القرن تجزئ لما روي أن سيدنا علياً رضي الله عنه سئل عن القرن فقال: لا يضرك، أمرنا رسول الله ﷺ أن نستشرف العين والأذن وروي أن رجلاً من همذان جاء إلى سيدنا على رضي الله عنه فقال: يا أمير المؤمنين البقرة عن كم قال: عن سبعة ثم قال: مكسورة القرن قال: لا ضير

إلى قوله: فإن بلغ الكسر المشاش لا تجزيه والمشاش رؤوس العظام مثل الركبتين والمرفقين. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/۷٦، سعيد). (وكذا في رد المحتار: ٦/۳۲۳، سعيد، والفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۲۹۷).

Fatāwā Bayyināt states:

If an animal's horns have broken off entirely at the root, in such a way that the brain has been affected by it, then the sacrifice of such an animal is not permissible; and if they are only slightly broken, or there are no horns at all — as with a camel — then it is permissible without any dislikedness. (فتاوی بینات، جلدِ چہارم، ص ۵٦۲، از قلمِ حضرت مفتی ولی حسن صاحب رحمہ اللہ) ۔

It is mentioned in another place:

If the horn has broken but its basic root remains and the protruding bone can be seen, then its sacrifice is valid; otherwise not. (فتاوی بینات: ٤/۵۷۱، از قلمِ مفتی عبد السلام صاحب) ۔

Faqīh al-Ummah Ḥaḍrat Muftī Maḥmūd Ḥasan Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him), after reconciling the passages of Al-Hidāyah and Ḥujjat Allāh al-Bālighah, writes:

In sum, three things are distinct: (1) jammāʾ: one that has no horns from birth. (2) maksūrah: one whose horn has broken. (3) aʿḍab: one whose horn has been pulled out from the root. The sacrifice of the first two is valid; that of the last is not. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۷/۳۷۸، جامعہ فاروقیہ) ۔
