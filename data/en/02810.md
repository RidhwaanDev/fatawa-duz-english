### TITLE
The ruling on keeping the name Ṣābir

### QUESTION
My name is Ṣābir. Some people object to my name, saying that Ṣābir amounts to inviting affliction, whereas in the shariah it is not right to pray for affliction or to invite it. A noble hadith states:

عن معاذ بن جبل رضي الله عنه قال:

سمع النبي ﷺ رجلاً وهو يقول: اللهم إني أسألك الصبر، فقال: سألتَ الله البلاء فاسئله العافية. (أخرجه الامام الترمذي، رقم: ۳۵۲۷، وقال: هذا حديث حسن). وأيضا أخرجه الامام أحمد في مسنده، رقم: ۲۲۰۱۷، وقال الشيخ شعيب: إسناده حسن.

Mirqāt states that asking for patience before an affliction comes is blameworthy, whereas asking for it afterwards is recommended. Is it correct, then, to keep such a name or not? بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
1. It is permissible to keep the name Ṣābir. Patience is of several kinds:

(1) صبر علی الطاعات.
(2) صبر عن المعاصی و المنکرات.
(3) صبر عن البدعات.
(4) صبر عن الشہوات.
(5) صبر عن الشبہات.
(6) صبر علی الآفات و البلیات.

The first five kinds of patience are called for at all times; the sixth is called for after an affliction has come and is held back before it. So this name should be kept with the first five meanings in view.

2. One of the meanings of ṣabr is also courage, and in the words of Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) it is the strength to bear. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 4/497). So one should pray for the strength to bear — that is, "O Allah, if ever something uncongenial befalls me, grant me the strength to bear it." Consider what al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd states: al-ṣābir: one who endures hardship, courageous, patient, forbearing, hard-working. (Al-Qāmūs al-Waḥīd: 1/908).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
