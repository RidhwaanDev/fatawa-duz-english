### TITLE
Answers to objections concerning the establishment of Radio Islam

### QUESTION
What do the noble scholars and the muftis of the firm Sacred Law say concerning the matters set out below?

The Muslims here wish to establish their own radio station, the entire management of which will be in the hands of the scholars and on which no programme contrary to the Sacred Law will be broadcast. A difference of opinion has arisen among the scholars over this. Most of them are in favour of establishing the radio station, but some are strongly opposed to it and raise the following objections:

(1) There is no need to convey the call to Islam by this new method. Through the prayer-niche and the pulpit, through sermons and books, and through the tremendous worldwide effort of invitation and propagation, the call has already reached everywhere.

(2) Will adopting it not lead on to every kind of lawful and unlawful medium, right up to television and the video recorder?

(3) Can a radio programme be as beneficial as the printed word?

(4) No change is to be seen coming about in people's lives through such a programme, is it?

(5) If Islamic teachings are presented on it in the style of fictitious programmes, tales and stories, will people's confidence in them not be destroyed?

(6) Will the enemies of Islam not come to learn our confidential matters from our programmes on the radio and the like?

(7) Where will so large a sum of money come from?

(8) When we discuss our own particular issues, such as divorce, will the non-Muslims not treat them as a joke?

(9) Will the government not compel us to broadcast the programmes of the Barelwīs and others too, so that qawwālī, innovations and superstitions will also be disseminated? Answers to them will then have to be given, and the whole thing will take the form of a public disputation.

(10) Will discord not arise within our united and harmonious Jamʿiyyah, and might not a second Jamʿiyyah come into existence?

(11) On the radio there will be no reverence or regard for the recitation of the Qurʾān, for religious addresses and the like, and it will end up as a mere spectacle. And will the noble scholars not learn from others how to pronounce words in a particular accent, and so become the very people described in the words of the hadith:

إن من أبغضكم إلى وأبعدكم مني مجلساً يوم القيامة الثرثارون والمتشدقون

الخ.

Along with answers to these objections, the matters on which a ruling is sought are as follows:

(1) Is it permissible to broadcast religious programmes by radio or not?

(2) Is it permissible for the Muslims to establish their own radio station for the good of the Muslims and for the propagation of Islam, or not?

(3) In the Muslim countries, recitation of the Qurʾān, Qurʾānic commentary, hadiths, the recorded sayings of the spiritual masters and religious lectures are broadcast on the radio. Is this practice of theirs permissible in the Sacred Law or not? Is it unlawful, or disliked, or recommended, or merely permitted, or commendable? An explanation is requested.

(4) Please also state your own personal opinion, so that it may be easier for us to reach a decision.

We hope that you will guide us and place us under obligation and thanks. اجیبوا توجروا

Answer to objection (1):

In our view the establishment of a radio station and the presenting of Islamic programmes on it is permissible and beneficial. Our elders Ḥaḍrat Qārī Muḥammad Ṭayyib Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) and Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) broadcast lectures and Qurʾānic commentary precisely because they regarded it as beneficial. The radio lectures of Ḥaḍrat Qārī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) have perhaps come to the notice of those who oppose this. It is clear from this that, just as one of our elders, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ilyās Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him), placed a particular scheme of religious invitation before the community, so too our elders (may Allah have mercy on them) broadcast lectures over the radio and made the radio station a means of disseminating their discourses; and we know of no statement of any of the pious elders opposing them.

Answer to objection (2): The claim that the radio can lead on to television and the video recorder is beyond our comprehension. It is rather like someone saying that the permissibility of marriage will pave the way for temporary marriage, or that listening to the radio will open the way to listening to songs — whereas in fact everybody listens to the radio, but only some listen to impermissible programmes while others do not. So what is the questioner's view about listening to the radio itself?

Answer to objection (3): Is a radio programme as beneficial as print? That is not the claim being made. The point is rather whether there is benefit in it or not; and so long as one remains within the bounds of the Sacred Law its benefit cannot be denied. Hundreds of thousands of people in this country and in neighbouring countries listen to the radio while going about their work; if accounts of our elders and sound religious programmes fall upon their ears, that will not be devoid of benefit.

Answer to objection (4): Does it bring about a change in people's lives or not? That is hard to determine — just as it is hard to determine whether opening a religious school and teaching the religious sciences brings about a change in people's lives, even though running a school is regarded as a matter of pride and is indeed a genuine and important need. Benefit certainly does come about, however: Muftī Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib's (may Allah have mercy on him) radio lectures brought about a change in people's thinking, and it was because people found them beneficial that they asked for more, so that so great a treasury came before the Ummah. Avoiding undue length, I advise the questioner at the very least to read Maʿārif al-Qurʾān, vol. 1, p. 64, in which the interest taken by the Muslims of the African countries is mentioned. As for the claim that this work, which is being carried on in all the Muslim countries, is not beneficial or is not a cause of change in people's lives — that is a matter of the unseen which we do not know. This much we do know, however: that when non-Muslim Christians spend hundreds of millions of dollars on this work, there must be benefit in it, and it is for that very reason that they spend.

Answer to objection (5): If true events are presented in a dramatised and distorted colouring, that would be wrong. As for occasionally presenting an imaginary story — if that is impermissible, then what is one's view about the reading and teaching of the Maqāmāt of al-Ḥarīrī and the writing of glosses and annotations upon it?

Answer to objection (6): Those who have voiced these apprehensions have done a most valuable service, for by voicing them they have provided a collyrium of insight for those who will do the work and for those who come to the radio station, and a barrier standing in the way of evil. ### ANSWER
(1) Presenting and broadcasting religious programmes by radio is permissible so long as one remains within the bounds of the Sacred Law.

Answer: (2) It is permissible to establish one's own radio station, provided that one has the competence and the leisure to make the programming beneficial. Otherwise one should not become involved in it at all; rather one should put some religiously committed body forward and request some airtime from them.

Answer: (3) If a radio station in the Muslim countries operates within the bounds of the Sacred Law, then it is merely permitted (mubāḥ) and good by reason of something else — just as eating food with the intention of gaining strength for worship is good by reason of something else.

Answer: (4) This humble one's view is that if they can properly discharge everything that such a radio station requires of them, if they have the means to bear the expenses, and if the right men for the work are available, then it should be acquired; otherwise they should bid it farewell.
