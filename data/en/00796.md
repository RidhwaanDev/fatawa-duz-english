### TITLE
Performing the Eid prayer in a city ground or park

### QUESTION
If the Eid prayer is performed in a city ground or park instead of out in the open country, is the sunnah of the Eid ground fulfilled or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
It is sunnah to go out beyond the built-up area and perform the Eid prayers at an Eid ground or in an open field. The Prophet ﷺ and the Rightly Guided Caliphs (may Allah be pleased with them) set aside the merit of the Prophet's Mosque and made a point of praying at the Eid ground. So the Eid prayers should not be prayed in mosques without some valid excuse; that is contrary to the sunnah. However, if the Eid prayers are prayed in a city ground or park or the like, the sunnah is fulfilled. Consider what a hadith states:

عن أبي سعيد الخدري رضي الله عنه قال كان النبي ﷺ يخرج يوم الفطر والأضحى إلى المصلي فأول شيء يبدأ به الصلاة ثم ينصرف فيقوم مقابل الناس والناس جلوس على صفوفهم فيعظهم ويوصيهم ويأمرهم

(رواه البخاري: ۱/۱۳۱/۹٤٦ باب الخروج إلى المصلي بغير منبر)

In his commentary on this hadith, ʿAllāmah al-ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

أن البروز إلى المصلي والخروج إليه، ولا يصلي في المسجد إلا عن ضرورة، وروي ابن زياد عن مالك رحمه الله قال: السنة الخروج إلى الجبانة إلا لا هل مكة ففي المسجد، وقال الشافعي رحمه الله "في الأم": بلغنا أن رسول الله ﷺ كان يخرج في العيدين إلى المصلي بالمدينة وكذا من بعده إلا من عذر مطر ونحوه، وكذا عامة أهل البلدان إلا مكة، شرفها الله تعالى. (عمدة القاري شرح صحيح البخاري: ٥/۱۷۱، باب الخروج إلى المصلي، دار الحديث ملتان)

This hadith shows that praying the Eid prayers in an open ground is sunnah. This was the practice of the Prophet ﷺ and of his Rightly Guided Caliphs (may Allah be pleased with them). A narration in Ibn Mājah Sharīf also records that the Prophet ﷺ performed the Eid prayer in an open field, and a spear was planted in front of him to serve as a barrier, since there was neither a wall nor a roof there.

Consider what Ibn Mājah Sharīf states:

عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه أن رسول الله ﷺ كان يغدو إلى المصلي في يوم عيد والعنزة تحمل بين يديه فاذا بلغ المصلي نصبت بين يديه فيصلي إليها وذلك أن المصلي كان فضاء ليس فيه شيء يستتر به. (رواه ابن ماجه: ۹۲، باب ما جاء في الحرية يوم العيد)

Iʿlāʾ al-Sunan states:

وأخرج الطبراني في الكبير بضعف عن علي رضي الله عنه قال: الخروج إلى الجبان في العيدين من السنة كذا في جمع الفوائد: (۱/۱۰۷) وانجبر ضعفه بماله من الشواهد

وفي الدر المختار والخروج إليها أي الجبانة لصلاة العيد سنة، وان وسعهم المسجد الجامع. (إعلاء السنن: ۸/۱۱۲/۲۱۱۰، باب الخروج يوم الفطر والأضحى إلى المصلي إلا لعذر، إدارة القرآن. كذا في حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح: ٥۳۱ باب أحكام العيدين، قديمي. وعمدة الفقه: كتاب الصلاة حصه دوم: ٤٦۰. وفتاوى محمودية: ۸/٤۰٤ باب العيدين، جامعه فاروقيه)

Objections raised against praying the Eid prayer in open grounds:

(1) Such a place is usually not outside the city.

(2) Such a place is not endowed.

(3) After the Eid prayer is over, sightseers and women, and sometimes animals, move about there; animal shows are even held there and impermissible things take place, so the ground also becomes impure.

Answers drawn from the writings of the senior scholars (may Allah have mercy on them):

In the past, some local people submitted objections of this kind in writing to Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) and to Muftī Ismāʿīl ibn Muḥammad "Bismillāh", the muftī of Jāmiʿah Islāmiyyah Dābhel, Samlak, and both of them gave a detailed reply. A copy of it was in our possession, so we thought it fitting to present a summary of it here for the blessing it carries. Consider what Ḥaḍrat Muftī Ismāʿīl Bismillāh (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote:

(1) It is true that the Eid ground should be outside the city. But if the Eid ground lies in the middle of the city, or comes to lie there as the city grows, or if some open ground in the middle of the city is set aside for the Eid prayer and the Eid prayers are held there regularly, then even in that situation the sunnah of going out for the Eid prayers is fulfilled, and the prayer will certainly be said to have been performed in the sunnah manner. If evidence is wanted, see: (وفاء الوفاء: ۳/۷۸۰).

Consider Fatḥ al-Bārī as well:

وحكي ابن بطال عن ابن حبيب أن مصلى الجنائز بالمدينة كان لاصقا بمسجد النبي ﷺ من ناحية جهة المشرق. انتهى، فإن ثبت ما قال والا فيحتمل أن يكون المراد بالمسجد هنا المصلى المتخذ للعيدين والاستسقاء. (فتح الباري شرح صحيح البخاري: ۳/۱۹۹، باب الصلاة على الجنائز بالمصلى والمسجد، دار نشر الكتب الإسلامية)

This shows that the Prophet's ﷺ Eid ground was not entirely outside the city. In such a case, performing the Eid prayers at a designated place is without any doubt permissible; indeed it is valid as the sunnah practice. When the government is non-Muslim and gives no land for an Eid ground, and this fine open ground is available free of charge for the Eid prayers, why should the opportunity be let slip? People should certainly go there together and perform the Eid prayers, so that the collective standing of the Muslims is seen and an impression is made on other communities. In such a situation it is not permissible or correct to raise groundless objections and propose that this be stopped.

(2) To say that the place must be endowed for the Eid prayer to be valid is wrong. The Eid prayer is not obligatory: the two disciples (may Allah have mercy on them) call it an emphasised sunnah, while Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) calls it necessary, which is the correct position and the one on which fatwa is given. And it is obvious that a place does not have to be endowed even for a prayer that is individually obligatory:

لقول رسول الله ﷺ "جعلت لي الأرض كلها مسجد ًا (مشكوة: ٥٤)

So how could this be a condition and a requirement for any other prayer? Besides, the Ḥanafī jurists generally state, by consensus and agreement, that apart from the sermon the Eid prayers have all the same conditions as the Friday prayer.

ملاحظه: شامي مع در مختار ۲/۱٦٦، بحر شرح كنز ۲/۱٤۰، هداية أولين وغيرهم.

And among the conditions of the Friday prayer, besides several others, is that it be held in a miṣr — that is, a city — or in the fināʾ (the outskirts) of a city. The author of al-Baḥr, the commentator on al-Kanz, says in the second volume:

أو مصلاه أي مصلي المصر لأنه من توابعه فكان في حكمه والحكم غير مقصور على المصلي بل يجوز في جميع أفنية المصر. (البحر الرائق: ۲/۱٤۰ باب الجمعة)

This shows that just as the Friday prayer is valid in every part of the outskirts of a city, so too is the Eid prayer; and it also shows that the outskirts do not need to be endowed.

والفناء في اللغة سعة أمام البيوت وقيل ما امتد من جوانبه كذا في المغرب (البحر الرائق: ۲/۱٤۰)

Furthermore, the jurists state that it is permissible and correct to pray the Friday prayer in a fort and the like, provided there is general permission for people to attend.

See:

شامي مع در مختار ۲/ ومراقي الفلاح وغيره.

In these circumstances it is neither permissible nor correct to declare that the place must be endowed for the Eid prayer to be valid, and so to stop ordinary people from fulfilling an emphasised sunnah. To declare necessary, by one's own invented reasoning, something the sharia has not declared necessary is an affront to the sharia — an affront of the gravest kind, which can never be excused.

(3) It is an agreed ruling among the Ḥanafīs that when ground which has become impure dries out, that place becomes pure for the purpose of prayer, and praying there is permissible and correct without any dislike:

وتطهر الأرض بيبسها وذهاب أثرها كلون وريح لا جل صلاة على ها، وفي الشامي: قوله بيبسها لما في سنن أبي داود باب طهور الأرض إذا يبست وقوله أي جفافها المراد به ذهاب الندوة. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ۱/۳۱۱ باب الأنجاس، سعيد)

Marāqī al-Falāḥ states:

وإذا ذهب أثر النجاسة عن الأرض وجفت جازت الصلاة على ها، قوله وقد جفت ولو بغير الشمس على الصحيح طهرت وجازت الصلاة عليها لقوله ﷺ: أيما أرض جفت فقد زكت. (مراقي الفلاح مع نور الإيضاح: ٦٥ باب الأنجاس والطهارة عنها، مكة المكرمة)

In such a case it is not correct to say that the ground is impure because animal shows are held there; once it has dried out, the place is pure for prayer. فقط واللہ تعالی اعلم و علمہ اتم ۔ Written by the humblest of creatures, Ismāʿīl ibn Muḥammad Bismillāh. (Jāmiʿah Islāmiyyah Dābhel, Samlak, District Surat, 14 Ramadan 1378 AH / 24 March 1959 CE. The answer is correct: ʿAbd al-Ghafūr, may Allah forgive him, and ʿAbd al-Ḥayy Bismillāh, may he be pardoned.)

Now consider the answer of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ:

اقول و باللہ التوفیق — Mawlānā Muftī Bismillāh Ṣāḥib's answer concerning the Eid ground is entirely correct and fully sufficient. He has, moreover, given ample explanation and complete evidence, so there is no need for any further addition. I shall only quote one short passage from ʿAllāmah Ibn al-Qayyim's Zād al-Maʿād:

كان ﷺ يصلي العيدين في المصلي وهو المصلي الذي على باب المدينة الشرقي وهو المصلي الذي يوضع فيه محمل الحاج، ولم يصل العيد بمسجده إلا مرة واحدة أصابهم المطر فصلى بهم العيد في المسجد. (رواه أبو داود وابن ماجه كذا في زاد المعاد ۱/٤٤۱ فصل في هديه صلي الله عليه وسلم في العيدين، مؤسسة الرسالة)

Several points are established by this narration, which ʿAllāmah Ibn al-Qayyim (may Allah have mercy on him) quotes from Abū Dāwūd and Ibn Mājah:

(1) The Eid ground of the Prophet ﷺ was nowhere far from the city; it lay right beside the eastern gate of Madīnah Ṭayyibah, and it has now for a long time been within the middle of the city. So even if it was not in the middle of the city in the Prophet's time, it was certainly immediately adjacent to it.

(2) The Eid ground of the Prophet ﷺ was an open field in which the pilgrims' camels used to be seated and their litters placed, which makes it plain that this land was not endowed for the Eid prayer.

(3) Throughout his life the Prophet ﷺ left aside a mosque such as his own and performed the Eid prayer in that open ground; only once, on account of rain, did he perform it in his mosque — and this despite the fact that the reward of a single prayer in the Prophet's Mosque equals fifty thousand.

The points set out above answer every question raised by those who object, and they establish that the Prophet ﷺ did not regard it as correct to pray the Eid prayers in a mosque.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالیٰ اعلم — the servant Muḥammad Shafīʿ, may Allah pardon him, Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi, 6 Ramadan 1379 AH / 4 March 1960 CE.
