### TITLE
Gifts on non-Islamic festive occasions

### QUESTION
What is the ruling on giving and receiving presents on the occasion of Christmas and other non-Islamic festivals? May they be given to such people for the sake of business relations or not? For they too give us presents on our Islamic occasions, and in giving to them the aim is to maintain relations, not to honour their religion. What is the ruling?

### ANSWER
In the case described, if the aim is not to honour their religion but merely to keep up an outward friendship or outward relations, then there is room for exchanging presents; it is better, however, not to give on the day of Christmas itself but a little before or after it.

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

ولو أهدي لمسلم ولم يرد تعظيم اليوم بل يجري على عادة الناس لا يكفر وينبغي أن يفعله قبله أو بعده نفياً للشبهة. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۷۵٤، سعيد).

In Imdād al-Fatāwā, Mawlānā Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him), after quoting passages from the books of fiqh, writes:

From these reports the details of the rulings on the gift-exchanges asked about have become clear: that if there is no religious harm involved, the giving and taking of presents with non-Muslims who are at peace with us is permissible, and with this most of the questions have been answered. Only two particular points remain that call for comment. The first is that a Diwali gift may perhaps be for the purpose of honouring that festival, which the jurists have written is strictly forbidden. The second is that such gifts also contain pictures, and keeping them and treating them with respect entails regarding them as having value and putting them to use, whereas in some subsidiary rulings any value in pictures has been denied; so there is a conflict here with that ruling of the sharia. The answer to the first is that it is known from custom that the reason for such a gift is respect for the person to whom it is given, not respect for the festival. The answer to the second is that what is intended in the giving is not the image but the material; it is, however, obligatory that the recipient break up the pictures at once. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 3/482).

Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

Question: If Hindus present sweets and the like to respected Muslims on the day of Holi or Diwali, is it correct for the Muslims to accept them or not?

Answer: It is correct; but one should not join in the joy and delight of the unbelievers on the day of their festival. In Majmaʿ al-Barakāt it is stated that when, on the day of Nawrūz, the Magians bring articles of food to men of standing and to the houses of their friends and acquaintances, it is lawful to accept them. Is there then any religious harm to the one who accepts them? Some say that the one who accepts them by way of sharing in their joy does suffer religious harm, while for one who accepts them in any other way there is no harm — but even so it is better to keep away from it. The same is stated in Maṭālib al-Muʾminīn. (Muʿallim al-Fiqh, the translation of Majmūʿat al-Fatāwā: 2/363; and likewise at 358 and 382).
