### TITLE
A female teacher going on an excursion with students

### QUESTION
A matter has come up for us these days: students from our institution go on educational excursions and cover such a distance that they become travellers in the technical sense. May a female teacher go with these students without a mahram or not? Bear in mind also that they return in less than a day. بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
Once a person sets out intending a journey in the sharia sense — that is, of about 82 kilometres or more — the rules of travel come into force: for example, shortening the prayer, and a woman's having her husband or a mahram as a travelling companion. This is so even if the journey is completed in a few hours, and whether it is a journey for Hajj, for trade, or for sightseeing and recreation. For a woman such a journey without a mahram recognised by the sharia is impermissible.

See what a hadith states:

عن أبي سعيد رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: لا يحل لامرأة تؤمن بالله واليوم الآخر أن تسافر سفراً فوق ثلاثة أيام فصاعداً إلا ومعها أبوها أو أخوها أو زوجها أو ابنها أو ذو محرم منها. (رواه أبو داود، رقم: ۱۷۲۸).
قال في مسئولية المرأة المسلمة: ومن أنواع الاختلاط المحرم سفر المرأة من غير محرم قال ﷺ: "لا تسافر المرأة إلا مع ذي محرم" متفق عليه، لان ذلك من وسائل الفتنة والفساد، والمحرم هو زوجها أو من تحرم عليه على التأبيد بنسب كأخ مسلم أو سبب كأخ رضاع. (ص 25).

So in this matter the better course is for a male teacher to go with the students, or for a mahram to accompany the female teacher. After all, if we are not prepared to make even this much sacrifice in order to act upon a ruling of the sharia, then it is hard to expect Paradise for nothing — especially as a sightseeing trip is not even necessary; it is merely a custom that has come in through imitating others. The evils of travelling without a mahram are hidden from no sensible person; and even if we can see no harm in it, a ruling of the sharia should be accepted without argument.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
