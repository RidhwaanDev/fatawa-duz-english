### TITLE
Treating ordinary slaughter as emergency slaughter

### QUESTION
Some passages dealing with slaughter from Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib's (may Allah have mercy on him) book Jawāhir al-Fiqh are set out below. In the light of these passages my question is this: can the method of slaughter used by Rainbow Chicken be brought under the heading of emergency slaughter, so that it would not be necessary to cut all the vessels, nor to face the qiblah, nor to observe the other requirements? بینوا توجروا

The passages from Jawāhir al-Fiqh are as follows:

The rulings on non-voluntary slaughter — hunting and the like — are as follows:

Ten verses of the Noble Qurʾān, together with hadiths concerning Islamic slaughter, have been presented above. One of these sets out the ruling on ordinary slaughtering, which relates to domestic and reared animals; for these to be lawful, three conditions have been stated with complete clarity, namely that the slaughterer be a Muslim, that the name of Allah be pronounced at the time of slaughter, and that the vessels of slaughter be cut with a sharp instrument.

The second ruling, that of hunting, is mentioned in verse 4 of Sūrat al-Māʾidah: if a person says "Bismillāh" and sets a trained dog or the like upon the game, and it wounds the animal and brings it back without eating any of it itself, then that game is lawful. The authentic hadiths state the same ruling for shooting an arrow at game after saying "Bismillāh", and so on. (Jawāhir al-Fiqh: 2/381, "Islāmī Dhabīḥah").

### ANSWER
Under the law of the sacred sharia, as long as a person is able to perform ordinary slaughter he may not turn to emergency slaughter. By ordinary slaughter is meant the slaughtering of animals that are reared at home, such as goats, cows, oxen and buffaloes; and if a wild animal such as a deer is reared at home and becomes tame, it too comes under the same ruling. In the light of the Qurʾān and the sunnah, the following three conditions must be observed for such ordinary Islamic slaughter:

(1) That the slaughterer be a Muslim or one of the People of the Book.

(2) That the name of Allah be pronounced at the time of slaughter.

(3) That the windpipe, the gullet and the blood vessels be cut in the manner laid down by the sacred law. If even one of these is left out, it will not be an Islamic slaughter.

In the case in question, since the chickens in the Rainbow Chicken abattoir are under human control, ordinary slaughter is the only option; it is not correct to include this under non-voluntary, that is emergency, slaughter.
