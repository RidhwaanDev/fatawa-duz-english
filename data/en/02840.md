### TITLE
The ruling on playing basketball and volleyball

### QUESTION
It is well known that a Muslim must prepare for jihād, according to his capacity and means, by adopting the methods that have come in the aḥādīth — for example horsemanship, archery, swimming, running and the like. But if in our own times a person plays a modern game, for example football, basketball and so forth, and makes the intention of exercise for jihād, will that be correct or not?

### ANSWER
The pure Sacred Law has permitted the playing of such games as give bodily exercise, have a beneficial effect upon health, and are beneficial from the medical point of view — provided that the principles and controls mentioned earlier are observed: for example, that no shortcoming occurs on their account in the obligatory duties and the rights that must be discharged; that the covering of the ʿawrah is observed; that no wager is laid upon winning and losing; that time is not squandered; that resemblance to the disbelievers is not deliberately adopted; and so forth. With the observance of these conditions, basketball, football, volleyball and the like are permissible and correct. Moreover, there is bodily exercise in them, and with the intention of preparing for jihād one will deserve reward and recompense, in accordance with the purport of the ḥadīth: إنما الأعمال بالنيات.

Furthermore, it is stated in the noble ḥadīth:

المومن القوي خير وأحب إلى الله من المؤمن الضعيف، وفي كلٍ خير. (رواه مسلم، رقم: ۲٦٦٤)

That is, the strong believer is superior to the weak believer and more beloved in the sight of Allah Most High; yes, no believer is deprived of good. In the light of this ḥadīth too, reward and recompense will be obtained for the intention of exercise.

In Kifāyat al-Muftī it is said:

If there is no fear of the ʿawrah being uncovered and of the prayer being missed, then there is scope in the Sacred Law for playing volleyball with the intention of exercise. Bodily exercise in which there is nothing contrary to the Sacred Law is permissible. There are many methods of exercise, and among them are some methods that are peculiar to a particular disbelieving nation — for example cricket, football, hockey and the like — in which there is dislikedness on account of resembling the European disbelievers. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 9/269, published by Dār al-Ishāʿat).

In former times there was the matter of resemblance to the disbelievers in these games; nowadays, however, they are so widespread among the Muslims that resemblance no longer remains. Yes, in deliberately adopting resemblance there will be dislikedness — just as Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) has said concerning football: these pursuits are not the national emblems or the religious rites of the Christians, and therefore if they are played for the purpose of exercise there is no harm in it; if it is done with the intention of resembling the Christians, it will be disliked. (Imdād al-Aḥkām: 4/367, the chapter on play, singing and images).

However, those means of preparation for jihād that are established from the ḥadīth are better to adopt.
