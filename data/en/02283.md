### TITLE
Some questions relating to partnership

### QUESTION
(1): Is a sealed document (a contract) valid in the sharia or not?

Question (2): If one of the contracting parties acts contrary to this agreement, will such a breach be counted as a breach in the light of the sharia or not?

Question (3): If the capital of the trading partnership — that is, its amount — is not stated in a specified manner in the agreement, but for some particular reason its specified amount is stated in the annual statement of accounts, will the partnership be valid or not?

Question (4): If a partner stops working in accordance with the agreement, when working in person is a stipulated condition of the partnership agreement, and he goes away and remains absent in this manner for 15 years, will such an absent partner be regarded in the sharia as a partner in the business or not? And does his partnership come to an end on that account or not?

Question (5): If, after a partner has resigned, the remaining partners give no reply to his resignation, is such a resignation valid in the sharia or not?

Question (6): It is also written in the partnership agreement that if a partner gives written notice of withdrawal from the partnership, his partnership will come to an end of itself six months after the date of the resignation. Will the partnership come to an end accordingly or not?

Question (7): If, in spite of a partner having resigned in this manner, the partners continue to give him his share when the profits are distributed, does his partnership remain in force, or will it be considered ended?

Question (8): If a partner wrote out a resignation and no one else came to know of it, and afterwards the document happened to come to light from somewhere although no one had presented it, will it be given effect or not?

Question (9): If a partner presents his resignation to the remaining partners contrary to a particular clause of the agreement, is it valid or not?

Question (10): On the death of a partner a meeting is convened to make the deceased's children partners in the business, in which some of the heirs give their consent while some remain silent. Will all the heirs be regarded as partners in the business on that account or not? (Apart from this, the agreement and its clauses and so forth have been omitted on account of their length.)

This request for a fatwa was sent by a family in South Africa to various fatwa departments. These questions came to us as well. The same questions had been sent to Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi, and answers came from there too, and those answers were then also published in Fatāwā ʿUthmānī. These questions came to us as well; and since the answers of Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi were comprehensive and concise, our Dār al-Iftāʾ regarded those very answers as a beacon of light.

### ANSWER
(1): In the case in question the partnership is valid. Although in the Ḥanafī school a partnership in goods (sharikah bi-l-ʿurūḍ) is not valid, yet since in the case in question the sons of the person mentioned bought all the properties from their father jointly, a partnership of ownership (sharikat al-milk) was established between them; and once a partnership of ownership is established, a contractual partnership (sharikat al-ʿaqd) in goods is also valid.

لما في الهندية: والحيلة في جواز الشركة في العروض وكل ما يتعين بالتعيين أن يبيع كل واحد منهما نصف ماله بنصف مال صاحبه حتى يصير مال كل واحد منهما نصفين، وتحصل شركة ملك بينهما، ثم يعقدان بعد ذلك عقد الشركة فيجوز بلا خلاف كذا في البدائع. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۲/۳۰۷، باب اول، فصل ۳).

There therefore remains no difficulty about the validity of the partnership on account of its being in goods.

As for the partnership agreement that is set down in writing in the annexed document, most of its conditions are sound, but some of the conditions in it are also invalid. For example, the condition mentioned in the agreement that no partner may carry on any separate business of his own, directly or indirectly, is contrary to what a sharikat al-ʿinān requires, and the conditions of a sharikat al-mufāwaḍah are not present here; this condition is therefore invalid. But if an invalid condition is attached to a partnership, the partnership is not thereby nullified; rather only that condition becomes invalid, and it is not necessary to act upon it.

لما في رد المحتار: لأن الشركة لا تفسد بالشروط الفاسدة. (فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۳۱٦، ط: سعيد).

Another invalid condition mentioned in the agreement is that, in the event of the death of one of the partners, for every month that has elapsed from 30 June until the death, twenty-five pounds a month shall be paid to his children along with the value of his trading share. The English agreement states expressly that this sum of twenty-five pounds a month is to be regarded as standing in place of whatever profit fell to the deceased's share from 30 June until the date of his death.

Another invalid condition of the same kind is that if a partner terminates the partnership on 31 December he shall be entitled to the amount that stood to his share on 30 June, together with one hundred and fifty pounds which shall be reckoned as his share of the profit from 30 June to 31 December. This condition too is invalid for the reason given above. Had a fixed sum of this sort been settled by way of profit for one particular partner in the original partnership itself, the partnership itself would have become invalid.

لما في الدر المختار: وتفسد باشتراط دراهم مسماة من الربح لا حدهما لقطع الشركة لا لأنه شرط، لعدم فسادها بالشروط. (الدر المختار مع فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۳۱٦، سعيد).

However, since in clause 5 of this same agreement the general method of dividing the profit between the partners is in accordance with the sharia, and in it, instead of any fixed sum being settled for any one party, equal participation in profit and loss has been agreed; and since the original partnership contract rests upon that very clause; and since the procedure laid down in clauses 11 and 12 conflicts with clause 5 and has been laid down merely in order to settle a method of accounting at the time of dissolving the partnership; and since in the English text the fixed sum has been declared to stand in place of the profit — which means that the profit will be whatever is determined under clause 5, while this fixed sum will merely be reckoned as standing in its place — for this reason the original contract of partnership will not be nullified by this invalid condition; only the condition itself will be nullified.

The upshot is that the partnership agreement annexed to the request for a fatwa is valid in the sharia to the extent that a partnership was validly established on its basis; but the invalid conditions stated in it, which have been mentioned above, are not binding in the sharia.

الجواب (2): It is not permissible for any partner to act contrary to those conditions of the agreement which are valid in the sharia.

لأن المسلمين على شروطهم إلا شرطاً حرم حلالاً أو أحل حراماً. (صحيح البخاري).

الجواب (3): If it was known in a specific way, or was preserved in writing somewhere, what the properties were that the four brothers had bought from their father and where they were situated, then it was not necessary for the validity of the partnership to state their particulars and quantities in the partnership agreement. There are two reasons for this:

(a) The first reason is that fixing the amount of the partnership capital at the time of the contract is not necessary for the validity of the contract.

لما في البدائع: وأما العلم بمقدار رأس المال وقت العقد فليس بشرط لجواز الشركة بالأموال عندنا وعند الشافعي رحمه الله شرط

ولنا أن الجهالة لا تمنع جواز العقد لعينها، بل لا فضائها إلى المنازعة وجهالة رأس المال وقت العقد لا تفضي إلى المنازعة، لأنه يعلم مقداره ظاهراً وغالباً لأن الدراهم والدنانير توزنان وقت الشراء فيعلم مقدارها فلا يؤدي إلى جهالة مقدار الربح وقت القسمة. (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/٦۳، سعيد).

(b) The second reason is that in the case in question a partnership of ownership had already been established between the four brothers before the contract of sharikat al-ʿinān, as has been written in the answer to question (1); their shares were also equal, and so was the profit. There was therefore no fear of any uncertainty leading to dispute through the amount not being known. Regarding the device for a partnership in goods that was mentioned in the answer to question (1), ʿAllāmah Ibn al-Humām writes:

وهذا لأن المانع من كون رأس مال الشركة عروضاً كل من أمرين: لزوم ربح مالم يضمن، وجهالة رأس مال كل منهما عند القسمة، وكل منهما منتف، فيكون كل ما ربحه أحدهما ما هو مضمون عليه، ولا تحصل جهالة في رأس مال كل منهما، لأنه لا يحتاج إلى تعرف رأس مال كل منهما عند القسمة حتى يكون ذلك بالحذر فتقع الجهالة لا نهما مستويان في المال شريكان فيه فبالضرورة يكون كل ما يحصل من الثمن بينهما نصفان. (فتح القدير: ۵/۳۹٦).

الجواب (4): A partnership does not come to an end merely by a partner leaving off work and becoming absent.

لما في الهندية: وإن عمل أحدهما ولم يعمل الآخر بعذر أو بغير عذر صار كعملهما معاً، كذا في المضمرات. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۲/۳۲۰، كتاب الشركة، باب ۳، فصل ۲).

If the other partners were not content to remain in partnership with him once he had left off working, they ought to have dissolved the partnership with him expressly.

الجواب (5): For a unilateral dissolution of a partnership it is not necessary in the sharia that the other party accept the dissolution.

لما في الدر المختار: وتبطل أيضاً بإنكارها وبقوله لا أعمل معك وبفسخ أحدهما. (فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۳۲۷، سعيد).

Moreover, in the partnership agreement drawn up in the case in question, the effectiveness of a resignation has not been made dependent upon the acceptance of the other partners. Therefore, when a partner has tendered his resignation in accordance with the agreement, his partnership will be regarded as ended from the period stated in the agreement, whether the other partners accepted the resignation or not.

الجواب (6): The partnership will come to an end. The evidence for this has already been given under (5).

الجواب (7): It has already been stated above that, by tendering a resignation in accordance with the agreement, the resigning partner's partnership came to an end. Now, in order to reinstate him as a partner it is necessary that a fresh contract of partnership be made with him. So if, after that, the other partners plainly concluded a fresh contract of partnership with that person, whether in writing or orally, and on that basis continued to give him a quarter share, then he will be regarded as a partner once more; otherwise not. On the contrary, if no new contract of partnership was made and the other partners merely continued to give him a quarter share, then there are several possibilities in the matter: one is that the partners continued to give him this money as a gratuity; another is that, in consequence of the dissolution of the partnership, they continued to pay this money as the resigning partner's share, the payment of which was due from the other partners. So long, therefore, as no express agreement of a new contract of partnership was made between the parties, the mere payment of a quarter share, given these possibilities, cannot be called a contract of partnership, لأن الشركة لا تثبت بالاحتمال ۔

الجواب (8): In the sharia too it is necessary for the dissolution of a partnership that the one who dissolves it inform the other partners of his dissolution; without his informing them the partnership is not dissolved.

لما في الدر المختار: وتبطل أيضاً بإنكارها

وبفسخ أحدهما

ويتوقف على علم الآخر لأنه عزل قصدي، وفي رد المحتار: قوله لأنه عزل قصدي لأنه نوع حجر فيشترط علمه دفعاً للضرر عنه، فتح. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٤/۳۲۷، سعيد).

In the annexed agreement too it has been laid down that a resignation must be presented to the other partners. Therefore, if a partner wrote out a resignation and kept it with himself and did not present it to any other partner, the partnership will not be considered dissolved by it. Yes, whenever that partner himself brings out that resignation and presents it to the other partners, the partnership will be dissolved six months from the date of presentation, in accordance with the agreement.

الجواب (9): This question is not clear. What is the form of presenting a resignation contrary to a particular clause of the agreement? If it is written out with an explanation, an answer will be possible.

الجواب (10): This particular case has not come to our notice explicitly, but from analogy with numerous other particular cases it appears that in the case in question the children of the deceased partner will be regarded as partners in the business. The principle is that on the death of the deceased the partnership with him came to an end, and it then became binding upon his fellow partners to pay the deceased's share to his heirs; and if they wish to keep them as partners in the business, the consent of all the partners is necessary.

لما في الدر المختار: لا يملك الشريك الشركة إلا بإذن شريكه، جوهرة. (الدر المختار: ٤/۳۱۷، سعيد).

Now, in ordinary circumstances this consent ought to be given in explicit words, لأن الساكت لا ينسب إليه قول ۔

(فتاوى الشامي: ۳/٦۲، سعيد. والأشباه: ۲/۳۸۲، القاعدة الثانية عشر، إدارة القرآن).

But when two of the partners convened a meeting for this very purpose and in it decided to make the deceased's children partners, that decision was taken on behalf of all the partners. At that time one partner, although present, remained silent and raised no objection to it; and afterwards the deceased's children continued to deal in the business as partners for a length of time and still he raised no objection. This whole pattern of conduct is sufficient to establish consent.

نظيره ما في الأشباه: سكوته عند بيع زوجته أو قريبه عقاراً إقرار بأنه ليس له، على ما أفتي به مشايخ سمرقند

وفيه بعد ذلك رآه يبيع أرضاً أو داراً فتصرف فيه المشتري زماناً وهو ساكت يسقط دعواه.

Commenting on this, ʿAllāmah al-Ḥamawī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

فيه عما قبله زيادة تصرف المشتري بعد الشراء زماناً وهو ساكت فهو قيد في الأجنبي لا في الزوجة والقريب كما يفهمه إطلاقه. (شرح الأشباه والنظائر: ۱/۳۸۷، القاعدة الثانية عشر، إدارة القرآن).

The above questions, together with the partnership agreement and its clauses, are set out in detail in Fatāwā ʿUthmānī, volume three, from page 57 to page 71, and may be consulted there. Answers to these questions reached us from several fatwa departments — for example the Dār al-Iftāʾ of Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi, the Dār al-Iftāʾ of Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, and the answers of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Sayyid ʿAbd al-Raḥīm Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) from Rander. We expressed our agreement with all of them; only the answers of the Dār al-Iftāʾ of Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi, however, have been presented to the reader, the rest being omitted on account of their length.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
