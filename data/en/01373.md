### TITLE
Some questions from Canada concerning the sighting of the new moon

### QUESTION
I respectfully submit that a large number of Muslims from every corner of the world have come and settled in Canada. They live intermingled, and people from different countries gather in the mosques; yet there is disagreement over the dates of Ramadan and the two Eids, with the result that Eid is celebrated on two different days within a single household. To put an end to this problem, a number of scholars, several mosques and several Islamic centres have set up a body, and this committee — treating the whole world as having a single horizon — has drawn up a set of principles and rules in which an attempt has been made to open a way of easing matters for the community. The principles and rules are written out below:

(1) For the sighting of the new moon to be established, arrangements must be made to look for the moon on the 29th.

(2) The decision that a sighting has been established will rest on eyewitness testimony.

(3) Testimony of the sighting will be taken from those who saw the moon themselves. When the sky is clear, at least ten men sighting the moon will be considered sufficient for establishing the sighting. When the sky is overcast, the decision will be made on the testimony of two upright men.

(4) A detailed report that the sighting has been established, obtained through reliable and trustworthy channels from any country in the world, will be acceptable.

(5) The announcement of those countries whose centres, religious institutions and moon committees we have an agreement and a working link with will also be acceptable to us.

(6) If, in a region where the astronomers have said on the basis of their data and calculations that seeing the crescent is impossible, someone claims to have seen the moon and gives testimony to that effect, his testimony will not be accepted.

(7) Where astronomical calculation shows a strong likelihood of the moon being visible, this will be used to inform the public beforehand so that they can make their arrangements, but the announcement will depend on eyewitness testimony.

(8) These guiding principles will apply uniformly to all twelve months of the year.

Please let us know your valued opinion on treating the whole world as one horizon ("Global Moon Sighting") and on the principles and rules given above. Your approval, or your advice on any amendment, is requested.

Please also give us guidance on this: countries that are five or six hours ahead of Canada, such as South Africa and the like — if the sighting of the moon can be established there after their sunset, and the scholars there make their decision and inform us, may we announce it before our own sunset?

We hope you will favour us with a prompt reply. و السلام ۔

### ANSWER
We have written in Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā that in distant lands the difference of horizons is taken into account. This is the position of the later scholars, and the evidence for it has been quoted there. The other position, however, is the ẓāhir al-riwāyah — the standard transmitted position of the school — and there is no harm in adopting that for the sake of unity within a country; indeed, it is better and preferable. In South Africa the scholars here have adopted the first position in view of countless considerations of benefit, and you may adopt the ẓāhir al-riwāyah position for the sake of many such considerations. This is not a patching together of positions, since both positions exist within the school.

The ẓāhir al-riwāyah position on nearby and distant lands comes to this: a reliable sighting in another country is binding proof for the people of Canada. There is no need to set out the evidence for this before scholars such as yourselves, since it is already plain to you; still, we shall write down two or three proofs:

وعن شمس الأئمة الحلواني: أنه لا عبرة لاختلاف المطالع، وهو الظاهر. (شرح تحفة الملوك لمحمد بن عبد اللطيف المعروف بابن الملك: ۲/۱۲٦٤).

The notes upon it state:

وهو المذهب، وعليه الفتوى كما في أكثر المعتبرات، كذا في مجمع الأنهر. (۱/۲۹۱).

Ḥāshiyat al-Ṭaḥṭāwī ʿalā al-Durr states:

فيلزم أهل المشرق برؤية أهل المغرب إذا ثبت عندهم رؤية أولٰئك بطريق موجب كما مر، كذا في الدر، وأطلق المصنف رحمه الله فيشمل ما إذا كان بينها تفاوت بحيث يختلف المطالع أو لا. (حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ۱/٤٤۹).

Keeping this point of view in mind, you may decide Ramadan and Eid on the basis of sound, authenticated information from other countries. Brief answers to each numbered point are now given:

(1) Arrangements to look for the moon on the 29th must certainly be made.

(2) In the light of the hadiths and of fiqh this point too is correct: the decision about the month of Ramadan and about Eid should rest on eyewitness testimony.

(3) When the sky is clear, the report must be widespread. If the members of the moon committee are satisfied with the testimony of ten men, and the testimony is not contrary to what is apparent, it will be correct to accept it and make the announcement. Imam Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) put the figure at fifty men, and Khalaf ibn Ayyūb said that in Balkh even five hundred men are too few; but the judge's own view is what counts here.

قدر ذلك أبو يوسف رحمه الله بخمسين رجلاً، وقال خلف بن أيوب رحمه الله: خمس مئة ببلخ قليل والأولي أن يفوض إلى راي القاضي. (الفتاوى السراجية، ص۱٦۸).

(4) Having given the ruling that the difference of horizons is not taken into account, you may accept a widespread report from other countries, provided that country's decision is not contrary to what is apparent. For in some countries the moon is not visible even on the following day after the sighting has been announced, and sometimes a claim is made to have sighted a moon only six or seven hours old.

(5) You may also accept the sighting of those countries with which you have an agreement, provided it is not contrary to what is apparent and falls within the limits of what can be seen.

(6) A report of a sighting should not be accepted from a place where seeing the crescent is impossible, unless the sky is clear and a great many people have genuinely seen it.

Ibn al-Shiḥnah al-Ḥalabī says in Sharḥ Manẓūmat Ibn Wahbān:

ولمحقق متأخري الشافعية الشيخ الإمام تقي الدين السبكي رحمه الله في هذه المسألة تصنيف، مال فيه إلى اعتماد قولهم (الموقتين) لأن الحساب قطعي. (شرح منظومة ابن وهبان لا بن الشحنة الحلبي، ص۹۲).

That is, Shaykh Taqī al-Dīn al-Subkī (may Allah have mercy on him) (683–756 AH) held the calculation to be definitive. Most jurists do not accept the statements of the astronomers, but nowadays their word should be accepted in the negative: when they say that a sighting is not possible, then no sighting takes place.

(7) This clause too is entirely correct: on a day when a sighting is more likely, greater efforts should be made to look for the moon.

(8) Arrangements to sight the moon should be made for all twelve months of the year, so that making such arrangements for Shaʿbān, Ramadan and the two Eids becomes a settled habit. As for countries that are five or six hours ahead of Canada, once their sun has set and the moon has been sighted there, you may announce Ramadan or Eid for the following day. But if the announcement of Eid al-Fitr is made at the time of ʿasr, that day's fast is to be completed.

إذا رأوا هلال الفطر في النهار أتموا صوم ذلك اليوم. (الفتاوى السراجية، ص۱٦۹).

After this fatwa was sent, some objections were raised by the Chicago moon committee, in reply to which the few suggestions below were written. They are given here for further benefit:

(1) If our ruling were to cause further disagreement between the Deobandi scholars and the general public, we would advise the scholars of Canada to keep to their previous practice and not to widen the gulf of disagreement by embarking on new experiments.

(2) If the moon committee's jurisdiction is wide enough to take in the whole temperate northern portion, then the sighting within the North American region should be taken as the standard.

(3) If the people of Canada were to accept Saudi Arabia's decision one year because a sighting was possible that year, and then reject Saudi Arabia's word the next year because it was not possible, the scholars would lose their standing among the public — for ordinary people do not know these fine distinctions, or what it means for a sighting to be possible or impossible. If they take some other country as the standard, it will not carry enough weight for scholars and public alike to agree upon it; and if they accept the report of any country at all, it will then be hard to investigate each country to learn what its sighting procedure is and what arrangements it has.

(4) The Canadian committee should find out the view of the majority of the imams and scholars throughout the country; if they consider their previous practice beneficial, they should keep to it. فقط و السلام ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
