### TITLE
The provident fund

### QUESTION
A monthly sum in a fixed proportion is deducted from the salaries of government employees. The government does this compulsorily and the employees have no choice in it. Then, after the employment comes to an end, it is returned with a further increase. Is it permissible in the light of the sharia to take this money and use it, or not?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, taking the provident-fund money on the completion of employment and using it is permissible and valid, because the sum that the government deducts from the salary falls within the category of an entitlement and does not come into the employee's ownership. Hence the interest that the government pays upon that sum is not interest, since interest is the name for a stipulated increase upon a sum that one owns, whereas this sum is not owned.

Consider what Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

The sum that the department pays on the provident fund, and likewise whatever is added under the name of interest upon the total of the two, is all permissible money. In the sharia it is not interest, even though the department designates it by the name of interest. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 8/97).

Fatāwā ʿUthmānī states:

The increase that is paid by the departments upon the principal sum of a compulsory provident fund is not interest in the sharia, and therefore receiving it is permissible. (Fatāwā ʿUthmānī: 3/307).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

The government has instituted this arrangement in view of its own law, and the welfare of the employee is intended by it. As long as the employee has not taken possession of it, it is not his property; therefore whatever increase is received upon it will not be interest either. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 16/394, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

The government compulsorily deducts a sum every month from the salaries of government employees, which is called the G.P. fund. At the end of employment (retirement) the government pays the whole of this accumulated sum to the employee, and, in the event of his death, to his heirs; and it also adds to it an additional sum under the name of interest. Now, what is the ruling of the sharia on this additional sum that the government pays? The answer is that this additional sum is not interest in the sharia, because interest is settled between two persons by way of a contract in which there is wealth on both sides and that wealth is owned by them. In the case in question, the sum that is deducted from the employee's salary is not owned by the employee, since for an employee's salary to enter his ownership it is necessary that it be in his possession:

قال العلامة ابن نجيم رحمه الله: قوله بل بالتعجيل أو بشرطه أو بالاستيفاء أو بالتمكن أي لا يملك الأجرة إلا بواحد من هذه الأربعة. (البحر الرائق: ۷/۳۰۰).

Since neither the employee nor his agent has taken possession of this sum, the employee has not become its owner, and therefore his dispositions in it will not take effect. Now whatever the government pays — whether the principal sum, or an additional sum upon it, or an additional sum under the name of interest — all of it is a part of the wage, which the government is paying in a deferred lump sum. Thus Ḥaḍrat Muftī Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) says: the sum that is received under the name of interest on a compulsory provident fund is not interest in the sharia; rather it is a part of the wage (the salary). (Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām: 1/169, citing Prōvident Fanḍ par Zakāt awr Sūd kā Masʾalah, p. 4).

Āp ke Masāʾil states:

It is permissible to take the additional sum that the department pays on the provident fund. (Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥall: 6/212).

For further detail, see: (Muntakhabāt-i Niẓām al-Fatāwā, vol. 1, p. 208; Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 7/50; Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil, vol. 1, p. 249; Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 6/290; and the treatise Prōvident Fanḍ par Zakāt awr Sūd kā Masʾalah by Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him)).
