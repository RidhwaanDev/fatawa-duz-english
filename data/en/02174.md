### TITLE
Renting out a house for the sale of liquor or for a cinema

### QUESTION
Is it permissible for a Muslim to rent out his house, land and so on to a man who sells liquor? And where should such rent be used? What is the ruling now on rent that was received in the past?

### ANSWER
This question is disputed among the Hanafi jurists. Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) says that in a place where the disbelievers are in the majority it is permissible to rent out a house or land for the sale of liquor and the like, and that it is also correct to take the income and rent derived from it. The two disciples (may Allah have mercy on them), however, say that it is disliked.

Thus Fatāwā Qāḍīkhān states:

ولو استأجر الذمي من مسلم بيتاً يبيع فيه الخمر جاز عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله ولا بأس لمسلم أن يؤاجر داره من ذمي ليسكنها وإن شرب فيه الخمر. (فتاوى قاضيخان على هامش الهندية: ۲/۳۲٤).

Likewise al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

وإجارة بيت ليتخذ بيت نار أو بيعة أو كنيسة أو يباع فيه خمر بالسواد وهذا قول الإمام رحمه الله، وقالا: يكره كل ذلك لقوله تعالى: ﴿وتعاونوا على البر والتقوى ولا تعاونوا على الإثم والعدوان﴾. (البحر الرائق: ۸/۲۰۲، كوئته).

Fatāwā Bazzāziyyah states:

ولو آجر نفسه لحمل الخمر قال الإمام: لا يكره وعلى قولهما يكره، لأن التصرف في الخمر حرام، وكذا كل موضع تعلقت المعصية بفعل فاعل مختار، كما إذا آجر منزله ليتخذ بيعة أو كنيسة أو بيت نار يطيب له. (الفتاوى البزازية على هامش الهندية: ۵/۱۲۵، العاشر في الحظر والإباحة).

The two disciples (may Allah have mercy on them) hold it disliked because it amounts to helping in something sinful. Then, if a man sells something which is not in itself an act of disobedience but is a proximate cause of disobedience — as when he knows that wine will be made from the grape juice — the dislike will be of the milder kind:

بأنه لا تقوم المعصية بعينه بل بعد تغيره. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۱۹۲، سعيد).

But if it is something in whose very substance the sin subsists — liquor, for example — then the dislike will be of the strict kind, provided this is known beforehand.

Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) says that the hire is upon the use of the house, and for this reason, once the house has been handed over, the rent falls due by the mere handing over. There is nothing of sin in the hire itself: the sin is the tenant's own act, and he acts of his own free will, so it will not be correct to attribute that sin to the landlord.

Thus al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

وله أن الإجارة على منفعة البيت ولهذا تجب الأجرة بمجرد التسليم ولا معصية فيه وإنما المعصية بفعل المستأجر وهو مختار فيه فقطع نسبة ذلك إلى المؤجر. (البحر الرائق: ۸/۲۰۲، كتاب الكراهية، كوئته).

Taʾlīfāt-e Rashīdiyyah states:

Question: If a house or the like is rented to people who sell liquor and other unlawful things in it, or who themselves commit forbidden acts contrary to the sharia in it, or to disbelievers who worship idols in it — is this forbidden, and does it come under helping in sin, or not?

Answer: Renting a house to such people is not correct according to the view of the two disciples (may Allah have mercy on them), while from the view of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) it appears to be permissible, since renting out a house is not itself a sin — the sin lies in the tenant's own voluntary act. The fatwa, however, is upon this: that he should not rent it out, because this is helping in sin.

لا تعاونوا على الإثم والعدوان

۔ (تالیفاتِ رشیدیہ، ص ٤۲۱، اجرت کے مسائل) ۔

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

If the seller of liquor is not a Muslim, and the Muslim population of that settlement is not large but the inhabitants are for the most part disbelievers, then it is permissible to rent a shop to the disbelieving liquor seller. (کفایت المفتی: ۷/۳٤۰).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

In this humble servant's view there is a difference between hiring out to a disbeliever and hiring out to a Muslim. The statements of the jurists (may Allah have mercy on them) relate to a disbeliever, and in that case, both by reasoning and by transmitted evidence, the milder dislike appears to be the preferred view; whereas in hiring out to a Muslim the strict dislike appears to be the preferred view. (احسن الفتاوی: ٦/۵٤٦).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
