### TITLE
The ruling on trading in gold through a bank

### QUESTION
We trade in gold through a bank. The method is that when the price of gold is low we buy gold through the bank, and when there is a rise in the price we sell it. But after buying it we can neither derive any benefit from the gold nor withdraw it from the bank; we can only sell it onward. After buying, only this much is shown in our account: that you are the owner of such-and-such a quantity of gold; and for the rest, in our view the bank does not even hold the gold. Is it permissible for us to enter into such a transaction or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question such a transaction is not permissible and correct. The reason for this is that this transaction falls under selling before taking possession, which is forbidden by the noble hadith; for when you buy gold from the bank, the bank does not hold the gold — only that quantity is shown in your account — and then you sell it onward, whereas in reality the gold has not been transferred into your account. Therefore such a contract is not permissible. Consider what is in the noble hadith:

عن حكيم بن حزام رضي الله عنه قال: يا رسول الله يأتيني الرجل فيريد مني البيع ليس عندي أفأبتاعه له من السوق؟ فقال: لا تبع ما ليس عندك. (سنن أبي داود: والترمذي، رقم: ۱۲۳۲، وابن ماجه، رقم: ۲۱۸۷).

It is in another noble hadith:

عن نافع عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه أن رسول الله ﷺ، قال: من ابتاع طعاماً فلا يبعه حتى يستوفيه. (صحيح مسلم: ۲/۵، باب بطلان بيع المبيع قبل القبض، وصحيح البخاري، رقم: ۲۱۳٦).

قال في الهداية: ومن اشتري شيئاً مما ينقل ويحول لم يجز له بيعه حتى يقبضه، لأنه نهى عن بيع مالم يقبض ولأن فيه غرر انفساخ العقد على اعتبار الهلاك. (الهداية: ۳/۷٤).

It is recorded in Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith:

The prohibition of selling before taking possession has come in numerous hadiths. The mujtahid imāms have held these hadiths to be grounded in a specific legal cause: if the cause is present, the ruling of prohibition remains; otherwise not. Reflection upon the discussions of the mujtahid imāms shows that there are three causes for the prohibition of sale before taking possession: 1. uncertainty (gharar); 2. profiting from what one does not bear the risk of; 3. the possibility of interest arising from deferment. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 15/141).

(For more, see: Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil: 4/81; and Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 5/114).
