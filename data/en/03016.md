### TITLE
The ruling on reciting the national anthem

### QUESTION
In some countries national anthems are in vogue that contain idolatrous themes. In India itself people are told to recite "Vande Mataram", in which the notion of the land of one's homeland being an object of worship is found. Will it be permissible for Muslims to recite anthems of this kind, or not? Kindly explain and be rewarded.

### ANSWER
In the case in question, the pure sharīʿah does not permit the recitation of anthems whose themes carry the scent of idolatry and contain the notion of servitude to anyone other than Allah. In the religion of Islam the concept of worship and servitude is confined solely to the most holy Being of Allah, who is one and has no partner. Accordingly, every word that carries the scent of idolatry, or that comprises idolatry, may not be uttered in ordinary circumstances.

It is related in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī that in one place, on the occasion of a wedding, some little girls were singing some verses, and one of them recited this line:

وفينا نبي يعلم ما في غد"

The noble Prophet ﷺ immediately forbade her and admonished her:

دع هذه"

(بخاری شریف: ۲/۷۷۳).

"Vande Mataram" is a sentence of the Sanskrit language whose meaning is: "I adore my motherland and worship her." (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۱/٤۷٤).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil also states:

Love of one's homeland is not a bad thing, and if it remains within the bounds of justice Islam approves of it; it is a natural sentiment, entrusted by God Himself to every human being. But in Islam none may be worshipped besides God, and servitude belongs to God alone. Therefore, from the Islamic point of view, reciting verses of this kind and accepting them is absolutely impermissible. What is needed is that our fellow countrymen be made to understand that for the Muslims this is not merely a national and political issue, nor are we making it a question of ego and prestige; rather its roots are embedded in faith and creed, and to compel a Muslim to say such a thing is, as it were, to compel him to give up his religion and creed. It is evident that any serious and fair-minded citizen of the country who wishes to preserve the country's colourful religious and cultural character will look upon such an effort with nothing but disapproval. (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۱/٤۷٤).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
