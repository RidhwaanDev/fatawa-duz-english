### TITLE
Accepting the invitation of a bank employee

### QUESTION
A person is employed in a bank or an insurance company. If he invites someone, will accepting his invitation be permissible and correct, or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
Employment in a bank or an insurance company that relates purely to interest-based accounting and dealings is impermissible, and its salary too is not lawful. Eating at the home of such an employee, accepting his invitation, or accepting a gift from him is therefore not correct. If, however, it becomes known that the hospitality or the gift is from lawful wealth, then it is correct.

But if his employment in the bank does not involve purely interest-based dealings — rather it relates to non-interest work such as guarding, sweeping or driving, or he works in some Islamic department — then eating at his home is permissible.

Moreover, nowadays banks handle imports and exports and the payment of electricity, telephone and other bills besides interest-based dealings; that is, lawful business is also carried on. It is therefore difficult to regard a bank as a place of purely interest-based business.

Even so, it is preferable to avoid accepting the invitation of a bank employee, although it is not absolutely unlawful and impermissible either.

Consider what comes in the noble hadith:

عن جابر رضي الله عنه قال: لعن رسول الله ﷺ آكل الربا وموكله وكاتبه وشاهديه وقال: هم سواء. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۲۷، كتاب المساقات والمزارعة، باب الربا).

It is narrated from Sayyidunā Jābir رضي الله عنه that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ cursed the one who consumes interest, the one who pays it, the one who records it and its two witnesses, and said that they are all equal in sin.

Consider what ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

آكل الربا وكاسب الحرام أهدي إليه أو أضافه وغالب ماله حرام لا يقبل ولا يأكل ما لم يخبره أن ذلك المال أصله حلال ورثه أو استقرضه وإن كان غالب ماله حلالاً لا بأس بقبول هديته والأكل منها كذا في الملتقط. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳٤۳).

Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil states:

Accepting a gift or an invitation from a bank salary or from unlawful income. The basic principle in this is that if most of the income is unlawful, then taking anything at all from that wealth is unlawful, whether it be a salary or money. But the money held in a bank is mostly not unlawful; it is the capital itself. It is the money of the bank's owners and of the other depositors, and they form the majority; the majority of the wealth is therefore not unlawful. So if one does some permissible work and takes money for it, that is permissible. (Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil: 4/136).

For more, see: (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā, vol. 5, pp. 673–676).

Jawāhir al-Fatāwā states:

A person whose source of income is unlawful — for example employment in a bank, employment in insurance, income from cinemas, the income of singers or the income of photographers — if he has no lawful source of income, then eating and drinking at his house and accepting and using his gifts and presents is impermissible and unlawful, unless the recipient is himself deserving of zakāt and charity, in which case there will be scope. As for a person whose sources of income are of both kinds, lawful and unlawful, there is a distinction. If his lawful income and his unlawful income are kept separate, and he uses the lawful income for his expenses such as food, drink and clothing, and his word can be relied upon, then eating and drinking at his house is permissible; and if he gives a gift or present, stating that it is from lawful wealth, then accepting it is also permissible. If the two are mixed, the predominant part will be taken into account. (Jawāhir al-Fatāwā: 3/296).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

The mixing in of interest has an effect on prohibition: so if half or more is interest, all of it is unlawful; and if it is less than half, it is lawful. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 4/119).
