### TITLE
Whether zakat is discharged by the methods of transferring ownership used in some madrasahs

### QUESTION
Some careful madrasahs run the following system for transferring ownership of zakat:

They give a needy student 400 rand of zakat money. Another administrator then takes 300 rand from him for his lodging, electricity, tuition and food, and 100 rand remains with the student. On the face of it this system is very attractive and appealing, but some scholars raise the following objections to it:

(1) If a student is absent, the cost of the days of his absence should not be taken from him; yet there is no accepted procedure for returning any part of the money deducted.

(2) If the student dies before the stipends are handed out, the money is held in trust and so should be returned to his heirs.

(3) If a full year passes before the stipends are distributed, zakat ought to become due on that money, since it is still the owner's property.

(4) Furthermore, payment for food is a sale, while the provision of lodging, electricity and so on is a lease; and combining a sale and a lease in a single contract is "صفقة في صفقتين", which is forbidden.

### ANSWER
Giving zakat money to needy students is in reality a transfer of ownership. Then, when the student pays the fee of 300 rand, that falls under a conditional donation: in return for the donation, the student is provided with food, lodging and the like as a stipulated condition. A conditional donation is a gift made for a return. So if the student is absent for a few days, his money does not have to be returned; and if a student has paid the fee for a whole year and leaves in the middle of the year, the fee for the remainder of the year does not have to be returned either, because in a gift made for a return the donor cannot ask for the gift back — and here the money has already been spent or mixed with other funds, which counts as its being used up.

A conditional donation is permissible. Consider what Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

Question: Is it permissible for madrasahs to charge students an admission fee and a monthly fee, or not?

Answer: It is permissible, because this is not a wage but a donation, and a condition may be attached to a donation, since no compulsion arises from it: anyone who does not accept the condition is free not to enrol.

ودليله: أنه ﷺ قال لمن أضافه وعائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها قال: لا، قال: فلا إذن حتى قال في الثالثة: وعائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها قال: نعم. مسلم شريف: ۲/۱۷٦.

(Imdād al-Aḥkām: 3/606, Kitāb al-Ijārah).

(2) Since in certain rulings the principal or the administrator of a madrasah comes under the ruling of an official appointed to collect charity, if such a person dies and his money has been set aside separately, that money is still not returned once the principal or administrator has accepted it on behalf of the needy students. In most cases, moreover, the money has been mixed together, so it is not even known whose money was spent.

(3) If a full year passes before the stipends are distributed, zakat is not due, because zakat does not become obligatory merely through the zakat money lying with the official, since the principal or the administrator is the students' agent.

Niẓām al-Fatāwā quotes the words of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Khalīl Aḥmad Sahāranpūrī: in this humble man's view the money of madrasahs is not a religious endowment; rather the people of the madrasah, like the officials of the public treasury, are agents both of the donors and of the recipients. So zakat is not due on it, nor can the donors take it back. (Niẓām al-Fatāwā: 1/116).

(4) In this arrangement a sale and a lease are not combined either; rather the fee paid in return for the madrasah's facilities is a donation and a gift.
