### TITLE
The definition of basic needs and their scope

### QUESTION
The jurists say that zakat is necessary on wealth that is surplus to a person's basic needs. What, then, is the scope of these basic needs? What things are included in them?

### ANSWER
Basic needs include those things without which it becomes difficult for a person to live, whether the difficulty is real or presumed.

Basic needs are therefore of two kinds:

(1) Real basic needs. These take in everything without which a person would be in danger of perishing: for example, the maintenance he must spend, his living costs, the house he lives in, weapons, and the clothing for hot and cold weather that he needs at all times according to the season.

(2) Presumed basic needs. These take in everything about which a person is genuinely and constantly anxious: for example, a debt he must repay, the tools and instruments of his trade or craft, the necessary furnishings and goods of the house, riding animals, and, for scholars, their religious books. All of these are counted among basic needs. So if a person has cash in hand but also has a debt upon him, zakat will not be necessary on him.

From the particular rulings set out by the jurists this much is clear: there is no fixed definition of basic needs that leaves no room for adding or subtracting; there is latitude in the matter. Provided the sense of the words "need" and "basic" is preserved, and provided one keeps within lawful limits and avoids ostentation, there is room for the category to be widened. For example: a brick house in place of a mud one; a water tank in place of a tap; a motorcycle, jeep or car in place of a riding animal; a rifle or a gun in place of bow and arrow; machines in place of handwork among the tools of industry and craft. In the same way, among the necessities of life: a lift in a large building, a telephone, a refrigerator, a cooler, and — according to the season — a heater, an air conditioner or a fan; likewise servants and maids, or a driver who can drive the car, if the household is well off; and likewise the means for the children's schooling and upbringing. In short, all such modern things as fall within the everyday necessities of life, and whose basis is present in principle in the statements of the jurists, are included in basic needs. Indecent and impermissible things such as a television or a video recorder, however, are not included in basic needs. (Summarised from Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 6/292, 396, Idārat al-Qurʾān; Īḍāḥ al-Nawādir, part two: 33–34; and Imdād al-Fatāwā: 2/42).

The marriage of one's children is not counted among basic needs, because if they are adults the responsibility for marrying is theirs, and if they are minors marriage is not necessary. The father owes only the maintenance of his minor children. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۹/۳٤۳، مبوب و مرتب) ۔

Owning one's own house is likewise not counted among basic needs; a rented house is enough to live in. So if a year passes over money saved up for a house, zakat is necessary on it.

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

فارغ عن الحاجة الأصلية، لأن المشغول بها كالمعدوم، وفسره ابن ملك بما يدفع عنه الهلاك تحقيقاً كثيابه، أو تقديراً كدينه. وفي الشامي: وفسره ابن ملك، وذلك حيث قال: وهي ما يدفع الهلاك عن الإنسان تحقيقاً كالنفقة ودور السكنى وآلات الحرب والثياب المحتاج إليها لدفع الحر أو البرد، أو تقديراً كالدين، فإن المديون محتاج إلى قضاء ه بما في يده من النصاب دفعاً عن نفسه الحبس الذي هو كالهلاك، وكآلات الحرفة وأثاث المنزل ودواب الركوب وكتب العلم لا هلها، فإن الجهل عندهم كالهلاك، فإذا كان له دراهم مستحقة بصرفها إلى تلك الحوائج صارت كالمعدومة، كما أن الماء المستحق بصرفه إلى العطش كان كالمعدوم. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ۲/۲٦۲، سعيد. وكذا في البدائع: ۲/۱۱، سعيد).
