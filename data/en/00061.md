### TITLE
The technical, customary and general use of the word taqlīd

### QUESTION
When did the use of the word taqlīd in its technical sense become current, and when did its general use become widely known? Was the word taqlīd current and in use in the time of the Prophet ﷺ and in the time of the Companions?

### ANSWER
Although the substance of taqlīd (following the ruling of a qualified jurist) is indeed found in that period, the word taqlīd was not in currency then. It is much like the technical terms of hadith — a hadith being muḍṭarib, ḥasan or ḍaʿīf, for example: these terms are not attested in the age of the noble Companions, but were fixed later and became common. In the same way the word taqlīd came into use later. The meaning of taqlīd, however, is certainly attested in the age of the Companions and the Successors, for the words iqtidāʾ (following a leader) and ittibāʿ (following) were in use even in that age. For example:

"اقتدوا بالذين من بعدي أبي بكر وعمر". (رواه الترمذي وحسنه، رقم: ۳٦٦۲).

And as the Prophet ﷺ said about the noble Companions:

"بأيهم اقتديتم اهتديتم". (رواه رزين، مشكاة: ۲/ ۵۵٤، باب مناقب الصحابة، ورواه عبد بن حميد في "مسنده" من حديث ابن عمر مرفوعاً، كذا في "التلخيص الحبير": ٤/ ٤٦۲، باب أدب القضاء).

And there is this to consider: the technical terms of hadith methodology did not exist in that period either, yet later, by unbroken consensus, they became accepted and well known. Looked at in this way, among the classes of hadith scholars we find Ḥanafī classes, Shāfiʿī classes, and Mālikī and Ḥanbalī classes, but nowhere do we find any trace of a class of ghayr muqallidīn (those who reject following a school of law).

The correct and accurate position, then, is that taqlīd is simply the name for relying on the statement of an imam where the texts conflict or are unclear. Otherwise, following is in reality following the sharia and the Lawgiver, not the person of the mujtahid. If someone is irritated by the very word taqlīd and dislikes it, we shall not call him one who has abandoned an obligation merely because he will not use the word. But this much is settled: there is no escaping acceptance of the reality of taqlīd.
