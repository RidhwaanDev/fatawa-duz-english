### TITLE
Ablution and the ritual bath when nail polish has been applied

### QUESTION
What is the ruling on ablution and the ritual bath when nail polish is on the nails?

### ANSWER
In the view of some scholars, if removing it causes hardship, the ablution will be valid. Still, one should avoid applying things whose status is doubtful, and henna and the like are enough for adornment. Most scholars, however, say that neither the ablution nor the ritual bath will be valid.

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ويجب أي يفرض غسل كل ما يمكن من البدن بلا حرج مرة ولا يمنع ما على ظفر صباغ ولا طعام بين أسنانه أوفي سنه المجوف به يفتي. وقيل: إن صلبا منع وهو الأصح.

Shāmī states:

(قوله أن صلبا) أي إن كان ممضوغاً مضغاً متأكداً، بحيث تداخلت أجزاؤه وصار له لزوجة وعلاكة كالعجين شرح المنية. (قوله وهو الأصح) صرح به في شرح المنية وقال: لا متناع نفوذ الماء مع عدم الضرورة والحرج. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ۱/۱۵۲/۱۵٤).

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

والصرام والصباغ ما في ظفرهما يمنع تمام الاغتسال وقيل كل ذلك يجزيهم للحرج والضرورة ومواضع الضرورة مستثناة عن قواعد الشرع كذا في الظهيرية. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۳).

Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah states: the well-known scholars of our own day hold that nail polish is not permissible, because it covers the body of the nail so that water can no longer reach it in ablution or the ritual bath; like kneaded dough, it therefore prevents ablution and the ritual bath. Some other scholars, however, hold that if nail polish is accepted as an adornment for a woman, then, in that case, if there is no difficulty in removing it, removing it is necessary for ablution and the ritual bath. If removing it causes hardship but it has not formed a layer, then its ruling is like that of henna. And if a layer has formed and there is hardship in removing it, then, because of that hardship, it is not necessary for water to reach the nail. (Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah 2/501)

Fatāwā Farīdiyyah states: if the nail polish cannot be removed without a knife or some similar instrument, then, because of the hardship, it does not prevent ablution and the ritual bath. (Fatāwā Farīdiyyah 2/52)

In another place he says: the cautious course is to remove it, though there is room for leaving it on as well. (Fatāwā Farīdiyyah 2/54)

Other scholars, however, hold that nail polish does prevent ablution and the ritual bath.

For further detail see: (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā 2/26, and Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil 1/87)
