### TITLE
The ruling on interpreting the Qur'an by personal opinion

### QUESTION
Here in Laudium a trial has been going on for a long time, namely interpreting the Qur'an by personal opinion (tafsīr bi'l-raʾy). University students, male and female, along with businessmen and working people, gather for a Qur'an lesson. One person reads a verse of the Noble Qur'an and then translates it from some translation or commentary. After that he goes round the gathering asking each person in turn, "Tell us, what is your opinion about this verse? In your view, what does this verse establish?" In this way a whole commentary has grown up, and these people call it a Qur'an lesson.

I respectfully ask you to explain what the ruling of the sharia is on interpreting the Qur'an by personal opinion. Is it correct to interpret the Qur'an by one's own understanding and reasoning in the way described in the question? Please give a detailed, well-substantiated answer.

### ANSWER
Muftī ʿAbd al-Raḥīm says:

The method of teaching the Qur'an described in the question is in reality not a Qur'an lesson at all. It is an improper pastime of distorting the Qur'an, and Ḥaḍrat Muʿādh ibn Jabal (may Allah be pleased with him) foretold it.

In Badhl al-Majhūd, the commentary on Abū Dāwūd, Ḥaḍrat Muʿādh ibn Jabal (may Allah be pleased with him) said: "After you a time of trials is coming. Wealth will grow abundant and the Qur'an will become common, so that the believer and the hypocrite, man and woman, young and old, slave and free will all begin to read it (and will begin to think of themselves as masters of the Qur'an). A man will say, 'Why do people not follow me, when I have read the Qur'an?' They will not follow me until I invent something new (that is, until I interpret the Qur'an by my own opinion)." Ḥaḍrat Muʿādh ibn Jabal (may Allah be pleased with him) then said: "Keep guarding yourselves against newly invented practices, for any innovation that is introduced will be misguidance." (بذل المجهود شرح أبو داود: ۸/۱۹۱).

So whoever uses the strength of his own mind to invent meanings of the Qur'an that suit him, and interprets it as he pleases, is bent on distorting the Qur'an. He is misguided of the worst sort, and is a true instance of the words of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, "ضلوا فأضلوا" — they went astray and led others astray. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ٤/۱۲۰).

This is the way of the Parwezis and the deniers of hadith. It is not permissible to take part in such a gathering or to attend it. If this noble verse comes before them:

﴿فلا جناح عليكم إن تقصروا من الصلوة إن خفتم إن يفتنكم الذين كفروا﴾

they all say that the shortening of the prayer is not because of travel but because of the fear of trouble — whereas the Prophet ﷺ always shortened the prayer while travelling.

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

To write a commentary on the Qur'an, complete mastery of fifteen sciences is required: (1) lexicography, (2) syntax, (3) morphology, (4) derivation of words, (5) the science of meanings (maʿānī), (6) the science of figurative expression (bayān), (7) the science of literary embellishment (badīʿ), (8) the science of the Qur'anic readings, (9) the principles of creed, (10) the principles of jurisprudence, (11) the occasions of revelation and the historical accounts, (12) the abrogating and abrogated texts, (13) jurisprudence, (14) hadith, (15) the knowledge that Allah bestows directly (ʿilm al-mawhibah). (احسن الفتاوی ۱/۵۰۱).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muhammad Taqī writes:

﴿هو الذي بعث في الأميين رسولاً منهم يتلوا عليهم آياته ويزكيهم ويعلمهم الكتاب والحكمة﴾

Translation: He it is who raised up among the unlettered people a Messenger from among themselves, who recites to them the verses of Allah, purifies them, and teaches them the Book and wisdom. (۲/٦۲).

These are the very purposes which Sayyidunā Ibrāhīm (peace be upon him) prayed would be entrusted to Rasūlullāh ﷺ. The Noble Qur'an records that supplication in these words:

﴿ربّنا وابعث فيهم رسولاً منهم يتلوا عليهم آياتك ويعلمهم الكتاب والحكمة ويزكيهم﴾

Translation: Our Lord, raise up among this community a Messenger from among themselves, who will recite Your verses to them, teach them the Book and wisdom, and purify them. (۲/۱۲۹).

In the verses quoted above, four clear and distinct duties are entrusted to Rasūlullāh ﷺ:

(1) Reciting the verses of the Book of Allah to them (يتلوا عليهم آياته).

(2) Teaching them the Book (يعلمهم الكتاب).

(3) Teaching wisdom (والحكمة).

(4) Purifying them (ويزكيهم).

The Wise Qur'an has therefore left no room for any doubt. It is not the case that Rasūlullāh ﷺ was only to recite the verses and then leave people free to explain them as they wished and to act upon them as they wished. On the contrary, he was also sent to teach the Book of Allah. Then, since teaching the Book alone was not enough, it was also made his responsibility to teach people wisdom, which is something over and above the Book. Nor does it end there: the Messenger ﷺ was also charged with purifying people. That means that alongside the theoretical teaching of the Book of Allah and of wisdom, practical training was to be arranged as well, so that people could act upon the teachings of the Book and of wisdom in the way that pleases Allah.

This verse of the Noble Qur'an sets out the following four functions for the blessed person of the Messenger ﷺ:

(1) In the recitation of the Holy Qur'an and the manner of reading it, the practice of the Prophet ﷺ is authoritative.

(2) In the explanation of the Book of Allah, his word is the final word.

(3) For learning the wisdom on which religious guidance rests, his person is the only source.

(4) The duty of training people practically to put his teachings into action was entrusted to him.

For the Prophet ﷺ to carry out these duties and purposes, it was essential that his teachings — whether spoken or practical — be binding on those who follow him, and that the Muslims under his training be obliged to accept his word and act upon it. Of the duties listed above, the second and third — teaching the Book and teaching wisdom — necessarily require that his statements be binding on his followers, while the fourth, the duty of practical training, requires that his actions be a model for the ummah and that the ummah be bound to follow them.

This is not merely a logical inference drawn from the verses of the Noble Qur'an quoted above. It is the clear ruling of countless verses of the Noble Qur'an, which make obedience to the Prophet (peace be upon him) and following him binding on the Muslims. In giving these rulings the Wise Qur'an uses two different terms: (1) obedience, that is, accepting what he says, and (2) following, that is, imitating what he does. The first term relates to the commands and statements of Rasūlullāh ﷺ, while the second relates to his actions and deeds. By commanding the Muslims both to obey and to follow, the Noble Qur'an has made his statements and his actions alike a decisive proof. (حجیت حدیث ص ۱۰۔۱۲).

In short, the Noble Qur'an is a comprehensive and complete book. Yet surely no one holds that the comprehensiveness of the Qur'an means it has no need of teaching and explanation, and that people may grasp its meanings and intent by their own understanding and reason without the instruction of a Messenger. Had that been so, the Noble Qur'an would have been sent down into the homes of the Muslims with the command, "Read it and earn My pleasure!" The reality is exactly the opposite. The Prophet ﷺ was sent first and the Qur'an was revealed afterwards; that is, the teacher was sent first and the lesson came after. This is why the revelation of the Qur'an took twenty-three years. The teacher's responsibility was not simply to recite it to people, but to have them put the teachings of the Qur'an into practice — and this too is why the revelation took twenty-three years. The Companions (may Allah be pleased with them all) became rightly guided and became a model for the whole world. That is why the Lord of Glory issued in His Qur'an a certificate for the obedient students of His beloved ﷺ, so that alongside His Book and the words of His beloved, which will live on until the Day of Judgement, this certificate too would stand. That certificate was:

﴿رضي الله عنهم ورضوا عنه﴾

Behind this certificate lay twenty-three years of the Prophet ﷺ's labour and the sacrifice of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them all). The first circle of learning was established in the Prophet's Mosque, and it stands to this day. If knowledge of the Arabic language and a little intelligence were enough to understand the Qur'an, then those Arabs would have had a far better claim to it than today's misguided non-Arabs — Arabs whose command of Arabic was such that they could compose hundreds of lines of poetry on the spot, and whose intelligence and memory were such that they knew thousands of lines of poetry by heart. Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) knew 5,374 hadiths by heart, yet on no page of history has anyone been able, even with the best will in the world, to record that he ever said, "The Qur'an is enough for me; I will understand it myself and find guidance." No statement of this kind, nor any statement carrying such a sense, is established from any Companion.

In reality this is an extremely dangerous plot by the enemies of Islam. From the very first day they have tried to wipe out Islam, and they failed. Then they kept trying to twist and corrupt it: sometimes under the slogan of love for the Prophet's family, sometimes under the slogan of devotion to the Messenger ﷺ, and sometimes under the slogan of rejecting adherence to a school of law. When even this did not bring them to their goal, they made a final attempt: instead of chipping away at the lamp of Islam from one side and another, dash the whole of it to the ground and scatter it — scatter it so widely that no one can gather it up again. The slogan and claim of being "people of the Qur'an" is a basic and very important link in this plot. Simple-hearted Muslims are drawn together with the words: come, let us read the Qur'an and take guidance from it; we have no need of any scholar. Yet the one raising the slogan does not himself know the basic principles of teaching the Qur'an. The result that finally appears is that those who have begun this work will never accept the truth. The verse

﴿ختم الله على قلوبهم﴾

proves entirely true of such people. As for those who have only recently started sitting with them, if the matter is explained to them they do repent.
