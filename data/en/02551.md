### TITLE
Eating the sweets distributed on ʿĀshūrāʾ

### QUESTION
In India, processions are taken out in the month of Muḥarram in the name of Sayyidunā Ḥusayn رضي الله عنه. These generally consist of Shīʿah, but nowadays our own people also take part in them. These people walk along with the procession carrying a large quantity of food items, and they give and distribute these edibles to everyone, rich and poor alike. They say that in this distribution we intend only to convey reward (īṣāl-i thawāb) to Sayyidunā Ḥusayn رضي الله عنه. Will it be permissible to eat these items or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
If these edibles are offered in the name of Sayyidunā Ḥusayn (may Allah be pleased with him), then eating them is impermissible. If they are purely for conveying reward, then conveying reward is in itself a recommended act, and there is no harm in conveying reward to whomever one wishes and whenever one wishes without binding oneself to a particular date or month — indeed it is better. [Singling out the Day of ʿĀshūrāʾ for it, however, is without proof.] In the view of the Ahl al-Sunnah wa'l-Jamāʿah the deceased do benefit from it. There is greater reward in feeding the poor; the wealthy may also eat it, but the reward will be less, since in their case there is the aspect of a gift.

Specifying times and modes in acts of worship on one's own, however, is an innovation, and most people today are afflicted with this. It is therefore preferable to shun and avoid the distribution of sweets and the like specifically on these days.

Furthermore, most of the beliefs of the Shīʿah have reached the level of disbelief, so no weight is given to any conveying of reward on their part, and one should avoid eating their food. Likewise, taking part in processions of this kind is also impermissible.

Consider the narration of Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī concerning the conveying of reward:

عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنه أن سعد بن عبادة رضي الله عنه توفيت أمه وهو غائب عنها، فقال: يا رسول الله! إن أمي توفيت وأنا غائب عنها أينفعها شيء إن تصدقت به عنها قال: نعم، قال: فإني أشهدك أن حائطي المِخْرَافَ صدقة عليها. (بخاري شريف: ۱/۳۸٦).

قال السيوطي في شرح الصدور في باب ما ينفع الميت في قبره: عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: إذا تصدق أحدكم بصدقة تطوعاً فليجعلها عن أبويه فيكون لهما أجرها ولا ينقص من أجره شيء. (شرح الصدور، ص ۳۰۰، بيروت).

قال في البحر: والأصل فيه أن الإنسان له أن يجعل ثواب عمله لغيره صلاة أو صوماً أو صدقة أو قراء ة قرآن أو ذكراً أو طوافاً أو حجاً أو عمرة أو غير ذلك عند أصحابنا بالكتاب والسنة. (البحر الرائق: ۳/۱۰۵، باب الحج عن الغير، وكذا في فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۲٤۳، سعيد، والهداية: ۱/۲۹۲).

Specifying particular days and modes in acts of worship on one's own, along with a particular reward, is an innovation. Consider:

أخرج مسلم عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه عن النبي ﷺ قال: لا تختصوا ليلة الجمعة بقيام من بين الليالي ولا تختصوا يوم الجمعة بصيام من بين الأيام إلا أن يكون في صوم يصومه أحدكم. (رواه مسلم: ۱/۳٦۱/۱۱٤٤).

In al-Iʿtiṣām, Imām al-Shāṭibī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

ومنها التزام العبادات المعينة في أوقات معينة لم يوجد لها ذلك التعيين في الشريعة. (الاعتصام: ۱/۲۹، دار الكتب العلمية).

For further detail, see: (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā, vol. 1, pp. 244–254).

The ruling on the wealthy eating food of īṣāl-i thawāb:

There is greater reward in feeding food of īṣāl-i thawāb to the poor; the wealthy may also eat it, but the reward will be less, since in their case there is the aspect of a gift. Consider what Ṭaḥṭāwī ʿalā al-Durr states:

الصدقة على الغني هبة والهبة للفقير صدقة. (حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ٤/۳۳٦).

Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah states:

Question: One wishes to convey reward to the souls of one's elders. Can one convey reward by feeding something to one's own people or to wealthy friends, or not? And will the wealthy not be at fault for eating such food?

Answer: Such food of voluntary charity is makrūh tanzīhī (mildly disliked) for a wealthy person, and the reward does reach [the deceased], but less than when the poor eat it. (Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah, p. 280).

And Bāqiyāt-i Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah states: Voluntary charity — whether it be the conveying of reward to a deceased person, or to the souls of the venerable mashāʾikh and the prophets (peace be upon them), or voluntary charity for any other reason — all of these carry a single ruling: for a wealthy person they are makrūh tanzīhī, not unlawful.

قال في الهداية: ولا تدفع إلى غني الخ.

Translation: Al-Hidāyah says: charity is not to be given to a wealthy person. What is meant by this is zakāt and obligatory charity.

قال في فتح القدير: وكذا يجوز النفل للغني

So if a person of means is wealthy, food of reward is not unlawful for him, although it is makrūh tanzīhī. (Bāqiyāt-i Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah, p. 205).

In summary, food of īṣāl-i thawāb is permissible for a wealthy person, though it is less in reward, and it is this that has been called makrūh tanzīhī. That is, since the aim is to convey reward to the deceased, and there is less reward in feeding the wealthy, it is therefore contrary to what is preferable.

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

The Shīʿah are heretics (zindīqs), so association with them of any kind is impermissible. Eating anything from their homes is contrary to the honour of faith and impermissible. At a time of severe necessity, however, there is scope. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 8/122).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Complete caution should be exercised with these Rāfiḍīs; they regard it as a source of reward and a necessity to feed impure things to the Sunnīs. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 18/54, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Question: In some countries it is customary that on the tenth of Muḥarram sweets and other edibles are brought to the mosque or distributed in the home. Is this permissible or not?

Answer: This is not a matter of the Sharīʿah, nor is it established from the Qurʾān and hadith. To regard it as a matter of the Sharīʿah is wrong. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 3/264, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
