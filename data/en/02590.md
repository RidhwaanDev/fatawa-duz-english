### TITLE
Storing semen

### QUESTION
A man is suffering from cancer, and treatment by radiation has been prescribed for him. The doctors say that because of this treatment his semen will be affected and he will become infertile. On this basis the doctor's advice is that, before the treatment, sound semen should be stored in a sperm bank so that it may be of use in the future. Is such a procedure permissible or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, storing a man's semen for the sake of a need and placing it in the future in his wife's womb — where there is no mixing with anyone else's semen — is, although bad because it is contrary to nature, nevertheless something that some scholars have permitted on grounds of need.

A summary of the writings of these scholars is set out below:

By way of introduction it may be submitted that the desire to have children is a natural desire, and an extraordinarily strong one, particularly in the case of women. Being deprived of childbirth makes a woman a prey to various gynaecological, mental, cardiac and bodily illnesses, and often these things become a cause of severe discord and tension between husband and wife, on account of which chastity and honour too are sometimes greatly affected. For this reason this matter has taken on the rank of a need for some women; and in the case in question, because of the cancer, attaining it in the customary manner is not possible. For this reason obtaining a child in the manner mentioned ought to be sound. Some authorities, however, have declared it impermissible. Their arguments, together with the answers to them, may be seen below:

The jurists who have declared it unsound have three points before them:

(1) The man will have to extract the semen by masturbation, and masturbating is not sound.

(2) The man and the woman, or at least the woman, will have to be uncovered; and being uncovered even before doctors is not sound and permissible without dire compulsion.

(3) This method is contrary to nature, and the general temper of the sharia is that it forbids matters contrary to nature.

The answers to these objections may be seen below:

(1) The semen will be extracted by masturbation or by withdrawal, and masturbation is prohibited:

In ordinary circumstances the jurists have forbidden masturbation, but where a need presses they have permitted it. See what Shaykh Ṭāhir ibn ʿAbd al-Rashīd al-Bukhārī writes:

"إذا عالج ذكره حتى أمني

ولا يحل هذا الفعل خارج رمضان إن قصد قضاء الشهوة وإن قصد تسكين شهوة أرجو أن لا يكون عليه وبال". (خلاصة الفتاوى: ۱/۲٦۰، الفصل الثالث فيما يفسد الصوم، المكتبة الرشيدية).

The real wisdom behind the prohibition of masturbation:

In masturbation the substance of life is wasted; but if the act of masturbation is performed for artificial reproduction, then in it the essence of life is being made effective and fruitful. For this reason this case does not fall among the prohibited cases of masturbation.

(2) Without dire compulsion it involves the uncovering of what should be veiled. The answer to this is that the jurists have tolerated being uncovered even in certain cases which are not in themselves any severe illness, but which may potentially become the cause of severe illnesses — just as being deprived of children becomes the cause of certain severe illnesses. Note what ʿAllāmah Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

"وقد روي عن أبي يوسف رحمه الله أنه إذا كان به هزال فاحش وقيل له إن الحقنة تزيل ما بك من الهزال فلا بأس بأن يبدئ ذلك الموضع للمحتقن وهذا صحيح فإن الهزال الفاحش نوع مرض تكون آخره الدق والسل". (المبسوط للإمام السرخسي رحمه الله: ۱۰/۱۵٦، كتاب الاستحسان، النظر الي العورة).

Let alone necessity, the sharia has sometimes granted permission for being uncovered — which is forbidden — even for the performance of a sunnah or of a permissible act. For instance, circumcision is a sunnah for a man and permissible for a woman, and even so the jurists have counted it within the sphere of need and excuse and permitted the uncovering.

Note what ʿAllāmah ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn Samarqandī says:

"ولا يباح النظر والمس إلى ما بين السرة والركبة إلا في حالة الضرورة بأن كانت المرأة ختانة تختن النساء". (تحفة الفقهاء: ۳/۳۳٤، كتاب الاستحسان).

Again, putting on weight is neither a need nor a necessity, yet even here the jurists have permitted the use of an enema.

Note what Khulāṣat al-Fatāwā states:

"لا بأس بالحقنة لا جل السمن هكذا روي عن أبي يوسف رحمه الله". (خلاصة الفتاوى: ٤/۳٦۳، كتاب الكراهية، الفصل الخامس في الأكل، ط: المكتبة الرشيدية).

This method is in reality a means of treatment for the childless, and there is no doubt that the jurists have divided human affairs into three categories — necessity, need and refinement — and have held prohibited things to be permissible only when necessity or need demands that they be permitted. But an examination of the particular rulings of fiqh shows that in the chapter of medical treatment the jurists have exercised a good deal more latitude. For this reason, for a husband and wife deprived of children, obtaining a child is a natural desire and an innate urge, for the sake of which the uncovering of the husband before a male physician and of the woman before a female physician may be tolerated.

(3) It is a method contrary to nature. The answer to this is that, in order to fulfil a natural need and demand (the obtaining of a child), it will be permissible to adopt such an unnatural means as there is no text forbidding. For example, the real path for the birth of a child is the woman's private part, but where there is a need an operation is permitted. For this reason, here too, in the degree of compulsion, this unnatural act ought to be permitted.

The ruling on the establishing of lineage:

For the establishing of lineage it is enough that the wife should become pregnant from the husband's sperm; it is not necessary that he should have physical intercourse with her. So even without intercourse, once the pregnancy is established the child's lineage will be established from the husband and wife. A parallel for this is set out below:

"رجل وطي جارية في ما دون الفرج فأنزل فأخذت الجارية ماء ه في شيء فاستدخلته في فرجها فعلقت، عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله أن الولد ولده وتصير الجارية أم ولده كذا في فتاوى قاضيخان". (الفتاوى الهندية: ٤/۱۱٤، الباب الرابع عشر في دعوي النسب).

The upshot is that storing a man's semen is permissible and sound under the need mentioned above, provided there is no mixing with anyone else's semen; and this facility exists nowadays in a sperm bank, in that there is a separate place for each person.
