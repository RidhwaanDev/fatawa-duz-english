### TITLE
Transferring ownership when giving unlawful wealth in charity

### QUESTION
Money obtained by impermissible means in non-Muslim countries, such as interest money, must be given away in charity. But is it necessary to give it to the poor, or may it be used for some other good cause without transferring ownership to a poor person?

### ANSWER
Some muftis hold that wealth in which there is taint and unlawfulness must be given to the poor. Other muftis, however, say that it may also be spent on other good causes, such as building a lavatory or making paths and roads. These scholars draw a distinction between an obligatory charity and wealth that must be given away in charity: with an obligatory charity such as zakat or ṣadaqat al-fiṭr, they say, transferring ownership to a poor person is necessary, whereas with wealth that must be given away in charity it is not. When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ reached the dwellings of the people of Thamūd and the Companions kneaded dough with water from their well, he ordered that this dough be fed to the camels:

عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه أن الناس نزلوا مع رسول الله ﷺ أرض ثمود الحجر فاستقوا من بئرها واعتجنوا به فأمرهم رسول الله ﷺ أن يهريقوا ما استقوا من بئرها وأن يعلفوا الإبل العجين وأمرهم أن يستقوا من البئر التي كانت تردها الناقة. (رواه البخاري: ۱/٤۷۸).

In one narration the order was to throw the dough away. Whether the dough had become makrūh taḥrīmī or makrūh tanzīhī, in either case the order to feed it to the camels means that when something has become tainted and a person cannot use it himself, there is room for it to be used in some good cause. Wine, on the other hand, is ordered to be poured away, and transferring its ownership to anyone is not allowed. In his commentary on al-Bukhārī, al-ʿAynī has transmitted both positions about this dough — that it was makrūh taḥrīmī and that it was makrūh tanzīhī. (ʿUmdat al-Qārī: 11/99).

If a wealthy person finds a lost article, it must be given away in charity and he may not use the article himself; but the jurists have written that there is room to use it for the shrouding and burial of a deceased person.

وأما الرابع فمصرفه المشهور هو اللقيط الفقير والفقراء الذين لا أولياء لهم فيعطي منه نفقتهم وأوديتهم وكفنهم وعقل جنايتهم. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۳۳۸، سعيد).

The fourth category here includes lost property.

ʿAllāmah Bazdawī (may Allah have mercy on him) also said:

أنه يصرف إلى المرضي والزمني واللقيط وعمارة القناطر والرباطات والثغور والمساجد وما أشبه ذلك. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۳۲۸، سعيد).

Mufti Kifāyatullāh (may Allah have mercy on him) writes concerning money deposited in banks that the interest on money deposited in banks should be collected from the bank, so that one does not incur the sin of assisting the propagation of the Christian religion and the making of Muslims into apostates. Once the money has been received, it may be spent on good works connected with public welfare, or on works of use in meeting the needs of the poor and destitute — for example, on stipends for orphans, the destitute and the students of Islamic madrasahs, on providing them with books and the like, or on building a travellers' lodge, a well or a road, or on lighting the roads. All these forms are permissible. It should not, however, be spent on a mosque, since that is against the sanctity of the mosque. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 8/67).

Fuller detail may be seen in Fatāwā ʿUthmānī, and it appears that the position of Mufti Muḥammad Taqī ʿUthmānī, Mawlānā Rafīʿ ʿUthmānī and the other muftis there is the same: that tainted wealth must be given away in charity, but not in the manner of an obligatory charity, since the word "charity" in itself is used also for what need not be transferred into someone's ownership:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: إذا مات الإنسان انقطع عمله إلا من ثلاث، صدقة جارية، أو علم ينتفع به، أو ولد صالح يدعو له. (رواه مسلم).

The word "charity" is applied to an endowment as well.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
