### TITLE
Claiming that honour and respect were meant in a case of ẓihār

### QUESTION
A man said to his wife, "You are like my mother or my sister." His wife then went back to her parents' home. The husband says that he did not intend divorce but respect and honour — although a quarrel was going on at the time. What is the ruling in sharia?

### ANSWER
In the case described, his intention is accepted as a matter of conscience between him and Allah; but as a matter of judgement this is ẓihār (likening one's wife to a woman permanently unlawful to marry), and at a time of quarrelling and dispute a claimed intention of honour and respect is not accepted. In court, therefore, an expiation is due in sharia.

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

وينبغي أن لا يصدق قضاءً في إرادة البر إذا كان في حال المشاجرة وذكر الطلاق. (فتاوى الشامي: ۳/٤۷۰، باب الظهار، سعيد. وفتح القدير: ٤/۲۵۱، باب الظهار، دار الفكر).

وفي البدائع: ولو قال لها: أنت على كأمي أو مثل أمي يرجع إلى نيته فإن نوى به الظهار كان مظاهراً وإن نوى به الكرامة كان كرامة وإن نوى به الطلاق كان طلاقاً وإن نوى به اليمين كان إيلاء لأن اللفظ يحتمل كل ذلك إذ هو تشبيه المرأة بالأم فيحتمل التشبيه في الكرامة والمنزلة (بدائع الصنائع: ۳/۲۳۱، شرائط الظهار، سعيد).

Majmūʿah-e Qawānīn-e Islāmī states:

If the comparison is made with the whole person of a woman who is permanently unlawful to marry — for example, if a man says, "You are like my mother" — then this is implicit ẓihār, and it may equally mean ẓihār, divorce, īlāʾ, or a likeness in honour and esteem. The ruling follows whatever he intended, so that if the husband meant a likeness in honour and esteem, that too is accepted. But if the same sentence is used while divorce is under discussion, or during a quarrel between the couple, then a claimed intention of honour and esteem will not be accepted in court. (مجموعہ قوانین اسلامی: ۱٦۲، دفعہ ٤۸) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
