### TITLE
The status of canal-irrigated land in Pakistan and India

### QUESTION
Some of the canals in Pakistan and India were built by the British. Is land irrigated by these canals ʿushrī (liable to the tithe) or kharājī (liable to land tax)?

### ANSWER
In the case described, the British made a gift of these canals to the Muslims when they left. The Muslims did not buy them from them, nor did they take them by force. So this land is not liable to kharāj (land tax); rather, half of the ʿushr is due on it.

Jawāhir al-Fiqh states:

Lands that were uncultivated before Pakistan came into being and were not owned by anyone, which the British government then distributed to people as outright owners after providing them with means of irrigation — whatever land the Muslims acquired of this kind, whether free of charge or for a price, is ʿushrī. (جواہر الفقہ: جلد دوم: ۲۵۸).

This passage shows that even though the means of irrigation were provided by the British, they handed the land over in full ownership, and so it has become ʿushrī land.

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

ويجب أي العشر في مسقي سماء أو سيح كنهر إلى قوله ويجب نصفه في مسقي غرب أي دلو كبير ودالية أي دولاب لكثرة المؤنة

This shows that land watered by rain is liable to the full ʿushr, while land irrigated from a well or a pond is liable to half of the ʿushr. Where land is watered in both ways, whichever predominates is what counts; and if the two are equal, the full ʿushr is due on half the produce and half the ʿushr on the other half. (امداد الفتاوی: ۲/٦۰، فصل فی العشر و الخراج).
