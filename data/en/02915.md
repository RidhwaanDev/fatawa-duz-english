### TITLE
The ruling on Khatm-e Khwājagān

### QUESTION
What is the ruling on reciting Khatm-e Khwājagān? Some people call it an innovation. Is that correct?

### ANSWER
Khatm-e Khwājagān is a practice tried and tested by the spiritual guides for obtaining blessing and for having one's supplication accepted, and it is recited for some religious need or lawful worldly need. Reciting it at times of need is permissible; but it should not be made binding — that is, it should be left out from time to time, or at the very least those who do not recite it should not be reproached. Indeed, those who recite Khatm-e Khwājagān are few and those who do not are many, yet those who recite it do not consider those who do not recite it blameworthy.

قال الاستاذ الكامل السيد محمد حقي النازلي: اعلم أن الإمام جعفر الصادق وأبو يزيد البسطامي وأبو الحسن الخرقاني ومن دونهم إلى شاه النقشبندية قدس الله أسرارهم أنهم اتفقوا في قضاء الحاجات وحصول المرادات ودفع البلاء وقهر الأعداء والحساد ورفع الدرجات ووصول القربات وظهور التجليات قد استعملوا هذه الفائدة الجليلة والأسرار الغريبة وهي الاستغفار مئة مرة والفاتحة سبع مرات الخ. (خزينة الاسرار الكبري للحقي، ص ۲۲۰، باب خواص ختم خواجكان نقلا ًعن تعليقات فتاوى فريديه: ٤/۵۰۸، مسائل شتى).

Fatāwā Farīdiyyah states:
Khatm-e Khwājagān, when free of objectionable elements (for instance یا شیخ عبد القادر جیلانی شیئاً للہ), is a practice tried and tested by the spiritual guides for obtaining blessing; and with any recommended and commendable act, doing it regularly is recommended while making it binding is disliked.
والمعروف هو الدوام لا الالتزام فافهم، وهو الموفق
۔ (فتاوی فریدیہ: 4/507، مسائل شتی) ۔

The footnote of Fatāwā Farīdiyyah records:
Between making a thing binding (iltizām) and doing it regularly (dawām) there is a relation of partial overlap. So a person who performs some recommended act permanently and performs it as something binding has committed both regularity and binding adherence. A person who performs some recommended act only occasionally but does so with the belief that it is binding has made it binding without performing it regularly. And a person who performs some recommended and commendable act permanently, but neither regards it as binding nor objects to the one who omits it, has performed it regularly without making it binding, and has kept clear of innovation. (حاشیہ فتاوی فریدیہ: ٤/۵۰۹، مسائل شتی) ۔

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Khalīl Aḥmad Sahāranpūrī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in one place in Barāhīn-e Qāṭiʿah:
What is an innovation is binding adherence in the sense of insistence, and what is recommended is regular practice without binding adherence. (ص ٦۷) ۔
In another place he writes: regular practice does not entail binding adherence. (ص ٦۵) ۔

Here we quote a few practices from Siyar Aʿlām al-Nubalāʾ of Imām Dhahabī (may Allah have mercy on him) which were left out by the Prophet ﷺ but were not forbidden by him. Dr. ʿAbd al-Ilāh has quoted them in his book Mafhūm al-Bidʿah, and we quote a few of these accounts from it.

(It should be borne in mind that we do not agree with every matter in that book.)

(1) Ḥaḍrat Abū al-Dardāʾ (may Allah be pleased with him) used to recite a hundred thousand glorifications daily. (۲/۳٤۸) ۔

(2) Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) used to fast on the first three days of the month. (۲/٦۰۹) ۔

(3) Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) used to recite twelve thousand glorifications daily. (۲/٦۱۰) ۔

(4) Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) had a string with twelve thousand knots in it, and he would recite the glorifications on it before sleeping. (۲/٦۲۳) ۔

(5) ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Abī Laylā used to recite the Noble Qur'an after Fajr until sunrise. (٤/۲٦۵) ۔

(6) ʿAlī ibn Ḥusayn Zayn al-ʿĀbidīn used to pray a thousand rakʿahs in twenty-four hours. (٤/۳۹۲) ۔

(7) Ṭāʾūs ibn Kaysān, together with his students and associates, used to set aside the time from after ʿAṣr until Maghrib for supplication. (۵/٤۸) ۔

(8) ʿAlī ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) used to pray a thousand rakʿahs daily. (۵/۲۵۳) ۔

(9) Imām Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal used to pray three hundred rakʿahs in a day and night; and when his trials had weakened him, he would then pray a hundred and fifty rakʿahs. (۵/۲۱۲) ۔

(10) Baqī ibn Makhlad used to complete the Noble Qur'an in thirteen rakʿahs, prayed a hundred rakʿahs during the day, and fasted continually. (۱۳/۲۹۲) ۔

(11) Junayd ibn Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him) used to pray three hundred rakʿahs daily and recite several thousand glorifications. (۱٤ / ٦۷) ۔

(12) ʿAllāmah Ibn al-Qayyim (may Allah have mercy on him) has written concerning ʿAllāmah Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) that Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) used to say that whoever recites یا حی یا قیوم، لا الہ الا انت forty times daily and regularly between the sunnah of Fajr and the obligatory prayer of Fajr, Allah will, through these words, keep his heart fresh and alive. (مدارج السالکین للعلامۃ ابن القیم رحمه الله : ۳/۲٦٤) ۔

All of these are practices left out by the Prophet ﷺ. Those who made them their regular practice kept to them themselves, but did not object to anyone who left them.

Ḥaḍrat Sahāranpūrī (may Allah have mercy on him) said: binding adherence in the sense of insistence is an innovation, and regular practice without binding adherence is recommended. (براہین قاطعہ، ص ٦۷) ۔ The detail of this question is given in the treatise of Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him),
"إقامة الحجة على أن الإكثار في التعبد ليس ببدعة"
