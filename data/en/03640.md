### TITLE
The ruling on saying "If I have relations with you of my own accord, you are unlawful to me like my mother"

### QUESTION
A man said these words to his wife: "If I come to have relations with you of my own accord, then you are unlawful to me like my mother; if you come of your own accord, there is no problem." Did ẓihār occur by these words, or divorce? If divorce occurred, when, and which kind? Please explain in detail with evidence.

### ANSWER
In the case in question the husband's words quoted above, "you are unlawful to me like my mother," carry the possibility of all three: ẓihār, divorce and īlāʾ. But when there is no intention of divorce, then because of the particle of comparison they will be taken to mean ẓihār. Accordingly, when the man in question comes to have relations of his own accord, ẓihār will be established, and its ruling is that he must free a slave; if none is available, he must fast for two consecutive months; and if he is not able to fast, he must feed sixty poor people two meals. Note what Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ records:

ولو قال أنت علي حرام، كأمي حمل على نيته، لأنه إذا ذكر مع التشبيه التحريم لم يحتمل معني الكرامة فتعين التحريم، وهو يحتمل تحريم الظهار ويحتمل تحريم الطلاق والإيلاء فيرجع إلى نيته فإن لم تكن له نية يكون ظهاراً، لأن حرف التشبيه يختص بالظهار فمطلق التحريم يحمل عليه. (بدائع الصنائع: ۸/۱۹۲، سعيد).
قال في المحيط البرهاني: ولو قال لها: أنت علي حرام كأمي، فإن نوى الطلاق كان طلاقاً، وإن نوى الظهار أو نوى التحريم لا غير أو لم تكن نية فهو ظهار، لأن معني البر انتفي بقرائن التحريم بحرف التشبيه، فبقي احتمال الظهار والطلاق، فأي ذلك ينوي صحت نيته، وعند عدم النية يحمل على الظهار؛ لأنه أدني. (المحيط البرهاني: ٤/۲۷، دار إحياء التراث العربي). وينظر للمزيد: (العناية رح الهداية: ٤/۲۵۳، دار الفكر، وتبيين الحقائق: ۳/۵، ط: القاهرة).

Majmūʿah Qawānīn-e Islāmī states:

Divorce does not take effect by ẓihār; but until the expiation for ẓihār has been paid, relations with the wife and embracing her are unlawful. The expiation for ẓihār is to fast for two consecutive months, in such a way that the fasts of Ramadan and those five days on which fasting is forbidden are not included in them. If the blessed month of Ramadan begins in the middle of the fasts of expiation, or the tenth of Dhū al-Ḥijjah arrives, then the two months of fasting will have to be kept afresh. And for the person who is not able to fast, the expiation is to feed sixty poor people their fill at both meals with food of a middling quality, or to give each one of the sixty poor people half a ṣāʿ of wheat or its value. (Majmūʿah Qawānīn-e Islāmī, p. 163).
