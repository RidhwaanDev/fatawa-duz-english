### TITLE
In the sacrifice it is the place of the agent and of the animal that counts

### QUESTION
If someone appoints another person as his agent for the sacrifice, and the principal lives in Pakistan while the agent is in Madīnah Munawwarah, and the principal tells the agent to perform the sacrifice — Eid generally falls in Saudi Arabia one or two days earlier and in Pakistan one or two days later — then if the agent slaughters the animal on the day of Eid, has the principal's sacrifice been valid or not, seeing that the day of Eid al-Aḍḥā has not yet even arrived where the principal is? In other words, in the time of the sacrifice is it the principal that counts, or the agent who performs the sacrifice?

### ANSWER
On this question the people of knowledge and the scholars fall into two groups. Some say that what counts is the days of sacrifice where the principal is; on this basis, in the case asked about, the sacrifice performed on behalf of the principal will not be fulfilled. Fatwas to this effect have been published by the Dār al-Iftāʾ of Jāmiʿah ʿUlūm al-Islāmiyyah ʿAllāmah Binnorī Town, the Dār al-Iftāʾ of Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi, the Dār al-Iftāʾ of Jāmiʿah Islāmiyyah Dabhel, and Shāhī Murādābād, and others.

Others say that what counts is the place of the agent and of the sacrificial animal, and the days of sacrifice there; so in the case asked about the sacrifice on behalf of the principal has been fulfilled. Ḥaḍrat Muftī Sayyid ʿAbd al-Raḥīm Lājpūrī, later of Rānder (may Allah have mercy on him), gave a fatwa to this effect a long time ago; and in the recent past Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muftī Rashīd Farīdī Ṣāḥib of Madrasah Miftāḥ al-ʿUlūm Tarāj, Surat, Gujarat, has written six research papers shedding detailed light on the question mentioned above. The names of the papers are as follows:

(1) تحقيق الكلام في بيان السبب لوجوب الأحكام.
(2) رفع الارتياب من سببية الوقت للموقتات.
(3) تمييز الطرقات لتحقق الشرائط للقربات.
(4) نور السَّني لمن يجب عليه الأضحية بالغني.
(5) تعقب الفريد على تخصيص الوجوب بصبح العيد.
(6) كشف الغطاء عن اعتبار الوقت لمحل الأداء.

This collection of treatises was published in two instalments in the monthly "Dār al-ʿUlūm". In the light of these treatises the following few submissions are set out:

(1) The basic obligation of the sacrifice attaches to a person's liability by virtue of his being of means; and this obligation upon the liability is from Allah, and it does not depend upon time.

See Multaqā al-Abḥur, which states:

هي واجبة، وإنما تجب على حر مسلم مقيم موسر. (ملتقى الأبحر: ٤/۱٦٦).
في مجمع الأنهر: قوله موسر" لأن العبادة لا تجب إلا على القادر وهو الغني دون الفقير. (مجمع الأنهر: ٤/۱٦٦).
وفي بدائع الصنائع: أما شرائط الوجوب

منها: الإسلام

ومنها الحرية

ومنها الإقامة

ومنها الغناء والغناء شرط الوجوب في هذا النوع، لأنه حق مالي متعلق بملك المال. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/٦۳، سعيد).
لأن الموسر تجب الأضحية في ذمته. (البدائع: ۵/۷٦، سعيد).

That the ruling of obligation upon the liability is attached to the man of means is proof that being of means is the cause of the ruling.

لأن ترتيب الحكم على المشتق نص على علِّية مبدأ الاشتقاق. (فتح الغفار، ص ٦۰).

The jurists have stated expressly that the obligation of the sacrifice arises from being of means, and there is no disagreement whatever about this. See Al-Bināyah Sharḥ al-Hidāyah, which states:

وشرط اليسار لقوله ﷺ: "من وجد سعة ولم يضح فلا يقربن مصلانا"، يدل على أن الوجوب بالسعة ولا سعة للفقير. (البناية: ۱۱/٤).

Takmilah Fatḥ al-Qadīr (تكمله فتح القدير) states:

إذ لا نزاع لاحد أن علة وجوب الأضحية على الموسر هي القدرة على النصاب. (تكملة فتح القدير: 9/۵۰۷).

See the rational proof for the obligation upon the liability before the day of sacrifice:

The time (the days of sacrifice) is a condition for performance, and the obligation to perform remains altogether bound up with performance. So if a man of means who is a resident offers his sacrifice in a village without any delay as soon as the dawn of the day of sacrifice rises, this sacrifice is valid by the agreement of all. It must therefore inevitably be accepted that the sacrifice was obligatory upon his liability before the day of sacrifice, because before the obligation to perform (which arises within the time by the address of Allah Most High) the existence of the obligation itself is necessary, and the basic obligation precedes the obligation to perform.

فثبت أن أصل الوجوب قد وجد بالغنا قبل صبح يوم النحر" فافهم وتدبر.

In the light of the proofs mentioned above it becomes as clear as daylight that the obligation of the sacrifice upon the liability arises from being of means and in no way depends upon time. Time is only a condition of performance.

Then, in the view of Imām Aʿẓam (may Allah have mercy on him), the time itself begins, with respect to all those under obligation, as soon as the dawn of the day of sacrifice rises; though for slaughtering within a city there is the further condition of finishing the Eid prayer.

انظر: (المبسوط: ۱۲/۱۰، وبدائع الصنائع: ۵/۷۳، سعيد، وشرح الوقاية: ٤/۳۹).

Further, just as with the time of prayer and fasting, the time of the sacrifice has been treated as being, along with its being a condition of performance, also the cause of the obligation to perform. The time therefore combines both aspects.

See Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ, which states:

لا تجوز قبل دخول الوقت لأن الوقت كما هو شرط الوجوب فهو شرط جواز إقامة الواجب. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/۷۳، سعيد، وكذا في البناية: ۱۱/۳، والعناية على هامش فتح القدير: ۱/۲۱٦).

And just as the performance of time-bound acts of worship is not valid before their time, so too it is not valid after their time; rather making them up becomes binding. So a man of means cannot offer the sacrifice before the days of sacrifice, for the reason that the time is a condition of performance and "تقدم المشروط على شرط الصحة لا يجوز" ۔ The reason for its impermissibility is not that the liability of the man of means is not yet charged with the obligation. فافهم ۔

Yes: after being of means (the obligation upon the liability), if it is Eid in the place where the animal is, the sacrifice will be permissible; because the cause — that is, the owner's being of means — is present, and the Eid (the condition of performance) is present in the place of the sacrificial animal or in the place of the agent. Therefore, in the case asked about, the sacrifice is valid and correct. For the sacrifice is a financial act of worship, and the cause of the obligation itself is, by agreement of all, being of means, which relates to the person under obligation; whereas the obligation of performance relates to the sacrificial animal, not to the person under obligation. It is thus established that the time (the days of sacrifice) is taken into account with respect to the place of performance, not with respect to the person under obligation.

See Al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq, which states:

ولأنها (الأضحية) تشبه الزكاة فيعتبر في الأداء مكان المحل وهو المال لا مكان الفاعل. (البحر الرائق: ۸/۱۷۵، كوئته).

(2) Discussion of the modern position:

The position of some muftis of the present age — for example Muftī Muḥammad Taqī ʿUthmānī Ṣāḥib, Muftī Shabbīr Aḥmad Murādābādī Ṣāḥib, Muftī Aḥmad Khānpūrī Ṣāḥib, Muftī ʿUmar Fārūq Desāʾī Ṣāḥib of London (may their shade be extended) and some others — is that "for the obligation of a person upon whom the sacrifice is necessary to be discharged, it is necessary that the dawn of the day of sacrifice should have risen where he is; otherwise the sacrifice will not be valid."

Proof: those scholars say that being of means is only a condition of the obligation, and that the cause of the basic obligation itself is the time, which begins with the rising of the dawn of the day of sacrifice and continues until sunset on the twelfth. This means that before the dawn of the day of sacrifice rises the obligation of the sacrifice has not come at all; and therefore, just as a man of means cannot offer the sacrifice at his own place before the day of sacrifice has dawned, so too he cannot have it offered through an agent at some other place (where the dawn of the day of sacrifice has already risen).

Answer: (1) Being of means has been called a condition of the obligation, کما فی کتب الفقہ و ہو الصحیح ۔ But the respected muftis are taking it to be a condition merely of the obligation of performance rather than a condition of the capacity for obligation, that is, of the basic obligation; and they are treating the time as the cause of the obligation of the sacrifice — whereas in the view of all the jurists the cause of the obligation of the sacrifice is the ability to possess the nisab.

(3) The grave harm in the modern position. There is a strong fear that acting upon this modern position will land the ummah in hardship, constraint and distress. For this question is not merely one of carrying out the sacrifices of Western countries in Eastern countries; it concerns the performing of the sacrifice of one place in another place in all the cities and villages of the world. And on the other hand it is entirely accepted and beyond doubt that, in terms of solar times, there is a difference in the times of true dawn, sunset and so on between the different provinces of a country and between the different districts of a province.

Now, when a sacrifice performed in another place on behalf of the one upon whom the sacrifice is due, before true dawn where he is, is also to be declared null, and when it is said that a city-dweller's sacrifice can be performed in a village only once the dawn has risen in the city, then the harm and hardship of this will be hidden from no one.

Moreover, the practice by which the wealthy of Africa, America, Britain and other such countries, and of the Arab countries, have their sacrifices performed in India, Pakistan, Bengal and other Asian and non-Asian countries has been going on since olden times and is current among the people. Now the well-to-do or poor man living in a city or a village of an Eastern country who acts as agent for the sacrifice on behalf of the people of the West does not know who the principal is or where he lives. If he knows in a general way, he does not know the difference of times in his principal's country; and even if he knows the difference of times on a national scale, he still has no real knowledge of the times of true dawn and the like; and to ascertain these times is certainly hard and difficult for everyone.

(4) The nullification of a continuous practice on the modern position: another harm of this modern position is that there has been, since the best of generations, a continuous practice of sending sacrificial animals or money from all corners of the world for sacrifices to be performed in the Ḥaram. Since there is a great difference between their times and the time of slaughter in the Ḥaram — indeed generally there is a difference of date as well — this would entail the nullification of this common continuous practice of the ummah, whereas in the light of the principles of the sharia this practice of the ummah is entirely valid.

The position of our Dār al-Iftāʾ:

This question has been written on from our Dār al-Iftāʾ several times. The gist of what was written the last time is that in agency it is the time and place of the agent that count: leaving aside the time and place of the principal, when the time has come where the agent is, he may perform the sacrifice on behalf of the principal — just as, in a ḥajj performed on behalf of someone excused or ill, the agent will perform the standing at ʿArafah and at Muzdalifah and the other rites of ḥajj according to the date in Saudi Arabia, even though these days come one or two days later in India or Pakistan; and in spite of that all these acts will be valid.

In the same way, if someone appoints another as his agent for a marriage and the agent contracts the marriage, then, although the unity of the session of offer and acceptance is necessary for the marriage to be contracted, it is the agent's session that counts, not the session of the principal.

See Al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq, which states:

وكل وكيلاً بأن يذبح شاة له وخرج إلى السواد فأخرج الوكيل الأضحية إلى موضع لا يجد من المصر وذبحها هناك فإن كان الموكل في السواد جازت الأضحية وإن كان عاد إلى المصر وعلم الوكيل بقدومه لم تجز الأضحية عن الموكل بلا خلاف وإن لم يعلم بعود الموكل إلى المصر فكذا عن محمد وعند أبي يوسف يجوز وهو المختار. (البحر الرائق: ۸/۱۷٦، كوئته). (وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۲۹۷، والفتاوى التاتارخانية: ۱۷/٤۲۳، وخلاصة الفتاوى: ٤/۳۱۳).

A clear and unclouded argument from the words of the jurists concerning this question:

The jurists (may Allah have mercy on them) have written that if a city-dweller wishes to perform his sacrifice early, he should send it to some village; and if the villager slaughters the animal immediately at daybreak, the sacrifice is valid. There is no mention in this that a city-dweller's sacrifice can be performed in a village only once the dawn has risen in the city.

See what is stated in Al-Hidāyah:

وحيلة المصري إذا أراد التعجيل أن يبعث بها إلى خارج المصر، فيضحي بها كما طلع الفجر. (الهداية: ٤/٤٦٦).

For example, if someone sends his sacrifice to a village lying two or four hours to the east of Johannesburg, true dawn occurs there some ten or fifteen minutes earlier; and if the villager slaughters the animal at daybreak, this sacrifice is valid in the light of the words of the jurists — whereas in the light of the modern position the obligation of the sacrifice has not yet come upon the man resident in Johannesburg, so how could a sacrifice on his behalf be valid?

A subtle corroboration from the date of the Prophet's ﷺ demise:

Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written in "Al-Ṭarāʾif wa al-Ẓarāʾif" (ص ۱۲۸) that in the year of the Prophet's ﷺ demise the date in Makkah Mukarramah was one day ahead of that in Madīnah Munawwarah. See:

فائدة تاريخية تتعلق بتاريخ وفاة النبي ﷺ. [عن جمع الوسائل في شرح الشمائل للقاري الحنفي رحمه الله] باب ما جاء في وفاة رسول الله ﷺ

إلى قوله: وحله أن يقال يحتمل اختلاف أهل مكة والمدينة في رؤية هلال ذي الحجة، عند أهل مكة يوم الخميس وعند أهل مدينة يوم الجمعة وكان وقوف عرفة واقعاً برؤية أهل مكة ولما رجع إلى المدينة اعتبروا التاريخ برؤية أهل المدينة وكان الشهور الثلاثة كوامل فيكون أول ربيع الأول يوم الخميس ويوم الاثنين الثاني عشر منه، هذا، انتهي.

Keeping the above difference of dates in view, we may say that in ۹ ؁ھ the Prophet ﷺ sent sacrificial animals with Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him). At that time too there could have been the possibility of the people of Makkah being a day ahead; and in spite of that it is nowhere established that the Prophet ﷺ gave any instruction to perform the sacrifice a day later. From this too it becomes clear that the Prophet (upon him be blessings and peace) must have taken into account the place of the agent and of the sacrificial animal.

In short, the gist and outcome of this whole investigation is that the continuous practice in the matter of slaughtering sacrificial animals is exactly in accordance with the sharia:

وهو أن المعتبر في الأضحية مكان الأضحية، لا من عليه الأضحية".

So the sacrifice of the inhabitants of Africa, Britain, America and other Western countries, performed in Eastern countries according to the time here, is entirely valid and completely in accordance with the express statements of fiqh.

See the fatwa of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Sayyid ʿAbd al-Raḥīm Ṣāḥib:

Question: Brother ʿAbd al-Rashīd has written from Madras for a sacrifice to be performed here (in Hyderabad). There Eid is on Monday and here on Sunday. May we perform his sacrifice here on Sunday or not? Or will it have to be done on Monday?

بينوا توجروا.

Answer: What counts is the place where the sacrificial animal is; the place of the one offering the sacrifice does not count. Thus if the man offering the sacrifice is in a city and sends his sacrificial animal to a village where the Eid prayer is not held, and his sacrificial animal is slaughtered there after true dawn, the sacrifice of that city-dweller will be valid.

Hidāyah Ākhirayn states:
والمعتبر في ذلك مكان الأضحية حتى لو كانت في السواد والمضحي في المصر يجوز كما انشق الفجر، ولو كان على العكس لا يجوز إلا بعد الصلاة وحيلة المصري إذا أراد التعجيل أن يبعث بها إلى خارج المصر فيضحي بها كما طلع الفجر. (الهداية: ٤/٤۳۰).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

والمعتبر مكان الأضحية لا مكان من عليه فحيلة مصري

(الدر المختار مع الشامي: ٦/۳۱۸، كتاب الأضحية، سعيد).

In the case asked about, brother ʿAbd al-Rashīd has written to you from Madras for his sacrifice to be performed in Hyderabad, and in Madras Eid al-Aḍḥā is on Monday while where you are it is on Sunday; so you may without any hesitation perform his sacrifice on Sunday, and his sacrifice will be valid. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ۵/٤۱۸) ۔

(Note: Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah has been published by Maktabat al-Iḥsān Deoband with the annotations of Muftī Saʿīd Pālanpūrī Ṣāḥib. In it Ḥaḍrat Muftī Saʿīd Ṣāḥib, after mentioning the question above, has published in the text itself, under the heading of an addition, a detailed fatwa of Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband in support of the modern position and against the fatwa of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Ṣāḥib, with the signatures of several further muftis. But annotation belongs in a footnote: what sense is there in mentioning Deoband's detailed fatwa in the text alongside the fatwa of Ḥaḍrat Muftī ʿAbd al-Raḥīm Ṣāḥib?)

See the fatwa of Muftī Ẓafīr al-Dīn, mufti of Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband:

Answer: ہوالموفق ۔ What counts is the place where the sacrifice is performed. So if the sacrifice is to be performed in India, the dates of that same country — the 10th, 11th and 12th of Dhū al-Ḥijjah — will count, and the sacrifice will be performed on those dates. Africa, London and other countries will not count.

The upshot is that it is permissible and valid for an agent to perform the sacrifice according to the time of his own place; although, acting upon caution in matters of worship, it is preferable and better to wait for the days of sacrifice to begin where the principal is, so that the obligation of the sacrifice on behalf of the principal may be discharged by the agreement of the scholars and no kind of doubt may remain. The jurists, moreover, have a maxim: "الخروج من الخلاف مستحب" ۔ On this basis one should hold to the reins of caution in future. Yes — it is not binding to repeat sacrifices already performed in the past.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
