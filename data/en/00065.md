### TITLE
Answers to fifty-four questions from a ghayr muqallid

### QUESTION
Questions:

(مقدمة الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۳)

Question no. 50: Knowledge of the unseen

### ANSWER
Answers:

(1) Hardly anyone would deny this accepted historical fact: that the rulings of sacred law which the Jews and the Christians received from their respective prophets, and the books which Allah sent down to guide them, were openly altered and changed as time went on by the scholars of the Jews and the Christians, who kept their own convenience and ease in view and so committed the enormous crime of distorting the texts. The real cause and motive of that crime was love of comfort and ease: whichever ruling they found difficult they would change, and by tampering with the sacred heavenly books they would insert whatever suited them. The Noble Qur'an describes this shameful act of theirs in these words:

﴿يكتبون الكتاب بأيديهم ثم يقولون هذا من عند الله﴾ (سورة البقرة: ۷۹).

Translation: "They (the People of the Book) write the book with their own hands and then say, 'This is from Allah.'"

The Noble Qur'an mentions this shameful act and enormous crime of theirs in many places. And those who followed them and walked according to the word of these corrupt scholars — who taught wrong rulings and led people towards evil — such followers were declared idolaters, because they turned away from the commands of Allah and acted on distorted commands. By putting the command of Allah behind their backs and following distorted rulings, and by obeying corrupt scholars, they associated partners with Allah.

But, respected sir, to say that those who follow the four imams today are likewise idolaters is entirely wrong. The reason is that the Jews and Christians were declared idolaters because they set aside the commands of Allah, accepted and acted upon the rulings their scholars had invented, and abandoned the path of truth for the path of error.

Let us now consider fairly whether those who follow the four imams are committing this same enormous crime or not. To settle that, we must first examine the work of the jurists and the imams: was what they did a service to religion, or were they, like the scholars of the Jews and Christians, engaged in distorting the religion? Did the jurists simply invent rulings of their own and teach them to the people, or did they carry out the great task of making plain what the sharia intends?

ʿAllāmah Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) has praised the jurists of this ummah in most striking words. Consider:

"ويفهمونهم مراده بحسب اجتهادهم واستطاعتهم".

(الكلام المفيد، ص: ۱۳۵ بحواله فتاوى ابن تيميه: ۲/۲۰۲).

That is, the jurists explain to ordinary Muslims, according to their own reasoning and capacity, what the Prophet ﷺ intended in his hadiths. Now let us consider whether the scholars of the Jews and Christians did the same. Did they explain to people what the prophets intended, or did they make people follow their own selves and desires? The Noble Qur'an settles the matter. Allah says:

﴿يلوون ألسنتهم بالكتاب لتحسبوه من الكتاب وما هو من الكتاب﴾ (سورة آل عمران: ۷۸).

In another place they are described in these words:

﴿يكتبون الكتاب بأيديهم ثم يقولون هذا من عند الله﴾ (سورة البقرة: ۷۹).

In the light of these blessed verses the difference between the work of the scholars of the Jews and Christians and that of the jurists of the community of Muḥammad ﷺ is plain. They committed sheer distortion in the religion of Allah, while the jurists of the community of Muḥammad ﷺ made plain to the ummah what the Prophet intended and conveyed the true religion to people with complete sincerity and honesty. Such was the honesty of these jurists that Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) declared in plain words:

"اتركوا قولي بخبر رسول الله ﷺ" ۔ (شرح عقود رسم المفتي، ص: ۲۰).

Translation: that is, if you find my statement to conflict with a hadith of the Messenger, leave my statement and act on the hadith.

These men are benefactors of the ummah; how then could those who follow them be idolaters? The jurists and scholars of the ummah serve as a lamp on the road, by whose light understanding the religion becomes easy. Moreover, in the divine ordering of things the work of serving the religion has always been taken from the scholars and jurists of the ummah. That is why Allah says:

﴿وأطيعوا الله وأطيعوا الرسول وأولي الأمر منكم﴾ (سورة النساء: ۵۹).

Most of the commentators have taken ﴿أولي الأمر﴾ to mean the scholars and jurists of the ummah, and from this they have established that taqlīd is obligatory. The well-known ghayr muqallid scholar Nawwāb Ṣiddīq Ḥasan Khān identifies them in the same way in his book "الجنة". Consider:

"قال ابن عباس وجابر والحسن وأبو العالية وعطاء والضحاك ومجاهد والإمام أحمد: هم العلماء"

The same interpretation is reported from Imam Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ, ʿAllāmah Maḥmūd al-Ālūsī, Imam al-Rāzī and other commentators: that what is meant is the scholars, and that obeying them is necessary. Nawwāb Ṣiddīq Ḥasan Khān Ṣāḥib writes that even if rulers are also meant by the phrase, there is no contradiction. Consider:

"والتحقيق أن الأمراء إنما يطاعون إذا أمروا بمقتضى العلم فطاعتهم تبع لطاعة العلماء كما أن طاعة العلماء تبع لطاعة الرسول".

Budūr al-Ahillah states:

The basic rule with a command is that carrying out what is commanded is obligatory. (الكلام المفيد ص ۵۷ بحوالة بدور الأهلة ص ۲۲).

From all these details it follows that obeying and following the scholars and jurists of this ummah is something we have been commanded to do, not shirk. That is why most scholars have declared taqlīd to be necessary, and a consensus on it has even been transmitted. It should also be kept in mind that those who practise taqlīd do not regard the mujtahid as free from error; they follow him on this understanding: (المجتهد يخطئ ويصيب).

The followers of every one of the imams proclaim openly that in matters settled by an explicit text no one may be followed against the Qur'an, the hadith and consensus. That is why the jurists have stated plainly in many places that if a statement of ours conflicts with a hadith of the Messenger of Allah, it is not to be accepted.

The statements of the great scholars on the importance and necessity of taqlīd are so numerous, and the verses and hadiths pointing to it so many, that it is hard even to gather them in this short piece. But the brief account given makes it clear that to learn one's religion from the scholars of the ummah and to act after asking them is not shirk; on the contrary, in the words of Nawwāb Ṣiddīq Ḥasan Khān Ṣāḥib, it is the carrying out of what we have been commanded.

Answer (2): There is no doubt that love for the noble Prophet ﷺ is not shown by the tongue alone; what really counts is following the Messenger and acting on his teachings and commands. This casts no blame at all on those who follow the imams. If what is meant is that the muqallids make a great claim of love for the Messenger but do not follow the Messenger, following their own imams instead, then there is no question that this is obviously false. We wrote clearly under number (1) that the jurists and the noble imams serve as a lamp on the road: their aim is not to have people follow them, but rather they call people to follow the Messenger ﷺ.

Not only that: Allah, Mighty and Sublime, has in His ordering of affairs confined guidance to the following of these imams. Thus we find the statements of most scholars of the ummah to the effect that people's guidance has now been placed in following these imams, and that there is great good in this. Ḥaḍrat Shāh Walīullāh (may Allah have mercy on him) — who himself possessed a high capacity for independent legal reasoning, and some of whose early statements people have cited in refutation of taqlīd — relates a dream of his in his book Fuyūḍ al-Ḥaramayn. Consider:

"واستفدت منه ﷺ ثلاثة أمور، خلاف ما كان عندي وما كانت طبعي تميل إليه أشد ميل فصارت هذه الاستفادة من براهين الحق تعالى إلى قوله. وثانيها الوصاة بالتقليد بهذه المذاهب الأربعة لا أخرج منها إلى آخره

(Taqlīd-e Sharʿī kī Ḍarūrat, p. 11, quoting Fuyūḍ al-Ḥaramayn, pp. 64–65).

This passage shows that taqlīd was at first contrary to Ḥaḍrat Shāh Walīullāh Ṣāḥib's own thinking, but the noble Prophet ﷺ instructed him to adopt it. These passages are also enough to silence those who cite Ḥaḍrat Shāh Ṣāḥib's words in condemnation of taqlīd. Since it is now clear that this was Shāh Ṣāḥib's early view, and that his retraction of it is likewise established, let us show how this passage invites reflection. These passages found in the books of the muqallids are in fact an invitation to the ghayr muqallidīn to reflect: merely adopting the name "Ahl-e-Ḥadīth" in speech and in writing cannot make good your claim of love, unless there is genuine following; and genuine following will only come about when you take your guidance from those from whom the Noble Qur'an and the hadiths command that guidance be taken. It is hardly following the Messenger that men from whom the great mass of Muslims take their guidance, and whom they follow after finding their word to agree with the Book and the Sunnah — imams who drew directly from the Companions and the Successors and who lived in the best of generations — should be dismissed as mere "people of opinion", while you, for all your deficient knowledge and crooked understanding, act on your own deficient opinion and are still to be counted as followers of the Messenger. That is impossible. A hadith states:

"اتبعوا السواد الأعظم". (رواه الحاكم في "المستدرك" (۱/۱٤۷، رقم: ۳۹۱).

Let people consider on which side the great mass of the ummah stands today; and then let them take the hadith "من شذ شذ في النار" and reflect on their own condition — are you not exactly the ones it describes? It is perfectly plain that the truth will always be with the great mass, because, according to the hadith, the majority of the ummah cannot come together upon error and misguidance. And Shāh Walīullāh says:

"ولما اندرست المذاهب الحقة إلا هذه الأربعة كان اتباعها اتباعاً للسواد الأعظم".

(Taqlīd-e Sharʿī kī Ḍarūrat, p. 5, published in Surat, quoting ʿIqd al-Jīd).

Answers (3), (4), (5) and (6): Nobody has any hesitation about this, that whoever abandons a hadith of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ because he thinks it of no account is a disbeliever. If the purpose of quoting this is to object that the Hanafis and other muqallids set aside some hadiths in favour of others, and that this amounts to belittling hadith, then it should be understood that setting aside some hadiths in favour of others is not belittling them. The explanation is this. Hadiths are of every kind: weak, strong, fabricated and so on. In themselves there is no defect in hadiths, and nobody claims there is. The fact is that in the ranks of the narrators of hadith there are narrators of every kind — some possessing the finest qualities, and some forgers and liars. The jurists of the ummah, who guide people towards the religion and the firm sacred law, have a duty, before accepting any hadith, to examine carefully by what routes and intermediaries it has reached us, so that something false is not made a proof and attributed to the Prophet (peace be upon him). Honesty therefore demands that a hadith be thoroughly sifted before it is made a proof. Whatever hadith satisfies the principles, rules and conditions is taken, and whatever does not is set aside. A hadith that is set aside is not set aside out of contempt; rather, honesty and trustworthiness require that the sound report be taken and made the basis of the ruling. The proof that this is not done out of contempt is that nobody attacks a weak hadith in respect of its text; it is always the narrators who are examined and the narrators who are criticised. However weak a hadith may be, nobody speaks ill of it. It is precisely because of this regard for hadith that the Hanafi books state in many places that to belittle a hadith and reject it is disbelief.

Answer (7): To seek out hadith without knowledge is, in its consequences, utterly ruinous and harmful. Just as the mistakes of the ghayr muqallidīn came to light under the previous numbers, so here too, if we reflect, we find the ghayr muqallidīn in the very front rank of those who seek out hadith without knowledge. It is astonishing — the hunter walks straight into his own net.

Let us now show how this fault is found among the ghayr muqallidīn. It is this: in order to make people disgusted with following the imams and to turn them against it, the idea is spread that the imams state rulings out of their own opinion and that "you should look into it yourself". For this purpose translated books of hadith are put into the hands of ordinary, unlettered people. Consider: is it right for such people, without any knowledge of Arabic and the other necessary sciences, to study hadiths and derive rulings as they imagine? For the derivation of rulings requires, along with thorough mastery of the sciences, the faculty of ijtihād. So the rulings which the noble imams put forward after their derivation are supposedly "mere opinion" — though the noble imams possessed complete mastery together with the highest degree of the faculty of ijtihād and derivation — while whatever these people decide, with defective understanding and from studying hadith without knowledge, is not opinion-mongering? Glory be to Allah! Whereas concerning such ordinary people, who have no share in knowledge, the consensus of the ummah has always been that their safety lies in attaching themselves to someone and not meddling in religion themselves; otherwise there is no doubt about their ruin. Thus Imam al-Ghazālī (may Allah have mercy on him) (450–505 AH) says:

"وإنما حق العوام أن يؤمنوا ويسلموا ويشتغلوا بعبادتهم ومعايشهم ويتركوا العلم للعلماء فإن العامي لو يزني ويسرق كان خيراً له من أن يتكلم في العلم فإنه من تكلم في الله وفي دينه من غير إتقان العلم وقع في الكفر من حيث لا يدري كمن يركب لجة البحر وهو لا يعرف السباحة. (إحياء علوم الدين: ۳/۳٤، بيان تفصيل مداخل الشيطان إلى القلب، مطبعة البابي الحلبي).

This passage shows that for an ordinary person to meddle in religion is more dangerous for him than adultery and theft, which are very great sins. Now judge fairly: can it be right to urge ordinary people in this way to study hadith without knowledge and to give opinions in religion? This is why the muqallids relied on the understanding of the noble imams — because their understanding was better than ours, they lived in the best of generations, and they acquired their learning from the Companions and the Successors. Finally, consider this remarkable sentence of Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah, who says:

"الحديث مضلة إلا للفقهاء". (الفتاوى الحديثية، ص ۱/۲۰۲، دار الفكر، والمدخل: ۱/۱۲۸، في ذكر النعوت، دار الفكر، والرسالة الباهرة للإمام ابن حزم، عن ابن وهب).

That is, for anyone other than the jurists, hadith is a cause of going astray, because grasping the fine points and secrets of hadith is not within everyone's power. It is quite possible that the Prophet intended one thing while a man understands something else, and that will be the cause of his going astray. This is why it is reported from the greatest hadith scholars that their own task was to pass on this treasury, while understanding it and deriving rulings from it is the work of the jurists. This too is why most of the greatest hadith scholars were followers of one imam or another. If it were proper for everyone to meddle in religion, these men would have had the greatest right of all to do so, since vast treasuries of hadith were in their possession and in scholarly ability they stood far above today's laymen and the ghayr muqallidīn.

Answer (8): Just as every hadith found in the books of hadith is examined and tested, so too the scholars have examined and tested the hadiths mentioned in the books of fiqh. When a question is discussed and hadiths are cited as evidence, whether they are strong or weak is also brought under discussion; no hadith is accepted as evidence with the eyes closed. Indeed, independent works on verification and cross-checking have been written in order to examine the hadiths found in the books of fiqh and to assess their standing. For example, the referencing of the hadiths mentioned in al-Hidāyah was carried out by al-Zaylaʿī in Naṣb al-Rāyah, which Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī (may Allah have mercy on him) abridged under the title Talkhīṣ al-Dirāyah. It should also be understood that a proof, or a hadith cited within a proof, need not itself be reported from the imam who founded the school; indeed there are often many hadiths serving as evidence on a single question. Sometimes the founding imam takes a narration by a sound and elevated chain and makes it his proof, while the author of a book records that same hadith by some weak route; and when several hadiths serve as evidence on one question, the author of a book may, by his own choice, mention a defective narration. It is not necessary that every proof be ascribed to the founding imam. In any case, no proof is accepted without discussion and critical examination.

Answer (9): The statement that a hadith takes precedence over the word of an imam points to the highest degree of trustworthiness and honesty in the imams who are followed. Had these men wished to have people follow them out of self-interest, they could have said, "Hold fast only to the word of the imam." But, giving proof of the utmost honesty, the noble imams always said that hadith should be given precedence over the word of the imam. In this connection a historic sentence of al-Imām al-Aʿẓam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) has made his honesty and sincerity as clear as broad daylight, and has also silenced those who condemn taqlīd and level the charge that the imams place their own personal opinion above the hadiths. Consider the imam's historic words:

"اتركوا قولي بخبر رسول الله إذا صح الحديث فهو مذهبي". (شرح عقود رسم المفتي).

And for those who describe Hanafi fiqh as no more than one man's opinion, it is enough to note that al-Imām al-Aʿẓam (may Allah have mercy on him) did not compile fiqh from his own opinion. Rather, the rulings which a whole consultative body extracted after reflection, study and debate were all set down exactly as they were, differences included. Later scholars and muftis were therefore not bound to take only the imam's word; the ruling is given according to whichever statement is closest to the hadith. Thus in a great many questions the imam's word is left aside and the ruling is given according to the statement of the two companions of the imam and others.

Consider also the testimony of Ḥaḍrat Shāh Walīullāh (may Allah have mercy on him). He says:

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ conveyed to me that in the Hanafi school there is a very good method, one that agrees closely with the method of the sunnah as it was refined in the age of al-Bukhārī and his contemporaries. It is this: that of the three statements — those of al-Imām al-Aʿẓam and his two companions — whichever is closest should be taken; and after that the Hanafi jurists who are also scholars of hadith should be followed, for there are many matters which the imam and his two companions neither stated in the principles nor denied, and which the hadiths indicate, so these must certainly be affirmed. And all of this is the Hanafi school. (Fuyūḍ al-Ḥaramayn, Urdu, p. 58).

This testimony comes from Ḥaḍrat Shāh Walīullāh Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him), who at the outset did not regard taqlīd as correct. And it should be borne in mind that this statement carries the confirmation of the court of prophethood. "سبحان الله".

Answers (10) and (11): The claim of the ghayr muqallidīn that wherever the term "Ahl-e-Ḥadīth" appears in the older books and in the writings of the great scholars it refers specifically to their own group is pure imagination and nothing more.

What has dust to do with the world of purity?

An apt comparison: it is like a man whose body is overcome by bile, so that everything appears to him in that colour, though the reality is otherwise. It is a great deception practised on the general public that they are shown the words "Ahl-e-Ḥadīth" in the books of the great scholars and are thereby satisfied and made to believe that "Ahl-e-Ḥadīth" means our own group in particular. Let us now consider whether those whom the ghayr muqallidīn make such vain attempts to enrol in their sect used to practise taqlīd. The first thing to bear in mind here is that wherever the term Ahl-e-Ḥadīth occurs in the books, what is meant is the body of hadith scholars — a fact so clearly established that to prove it would be a pointless exercise. What must be examined is whether that body of hadith scholars, whom the ghayr muqallidīn attach to themselves, practised taqlīd or not. The prized scholar of the ghayr muqallidīn, Nawwāb Ṣiddīq Ḥasan Ṣāḥib, writes of Imam al-Nasāʾī in his book "al-Ḥiṭṭah fī Dhikr al-Ṣiḥāḥ al-Sittah":

"كان أحد أعلام الدين وأركان الحديث إمام أهل عصره ومقدمهم بين أصحاب الحديث وجرحه وتعديله معتبر بين العلماء وكان شافعي المذهب".

(Taqlīd-e Sharʿī kī Ḍarūrat, p. 13).

Of Imam al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him), quoting Abū ʿĀṣim:

وقد ذكره أبو عاصم في طبقات أصحابنا الشافعية نقلا عن السبكي

Of Imam Abū Dāwūd he says:

فقيل حنبلي وقيل شافعي.

(Taqlīd-e Sharʿī kī Ḍarūrat, p. 13).

After all this detail a question arises of itself: if the ghayr muqallidīn are nowhere to be found from the beginning until now, then in what age did they come into being, and how did they acquire this name? Briefly: before the arrival of the British in the subcontinent nobody had even heard of this name; everyone was an adherent of Hanafi fiqh. Then the British, in order to weaken the Muslims, cultivated a number of sects, and the sect of the ghayr muqallidīn is one fruit of those efforts. British imperialism gained many advantages from it. For instance, when the Muslims of India came into the field and raised the banner of jihad to free themselves from British subjugation, these sects not only supported the British government with tongue and pen but also kept providing financial help in the various battles, in return for which they were rewarded with favour and estates. The ghayr muqallidīn have recorded this with great pride in their own books, so there is hardly any need to cite a reference; anyone who is curious may look at the services of the Shaykh al-Kull in his biography and in the accounts of his life. And how did this newly born sect come by the title "Ahl-e-Ḥadīth"? In reality that too was a favour of the British government. Thus Mawlawī ʿAbd al-Majīd Sohdrawī, a ghayr muqallid, writes:

Mawlawī Muḥammad Ḥusayn Baṭālawī rendered great service to the ghayr muqallidīn through Ishāʿat al-Sunnah. It was by his efforts that the word "Wahhābī" was erased from government offices and papers and this group was named "Ahl-e-Ḥadīth". He also served the British government, and received an estate as a reward. It is to be hoped that you will now have your family tree well in mind.

(Ahl-e-Ḥadīth awr Angrez, p. 87, quoting Rasāʾil-e Ahl-e-Ḥadīth, p. 19).
