### TITLE
Taking interest money from a non-Muslim

### QUESTION
If interest money comes into the hands of a non-Muslim, does it become his property or not? If a non-Muslim offers a Muslim a gift out of interest money, is the Muslim allowed to accept it or not? And what is the ruling if a Muslim gives a gift out of interest money?

بينوا بالتفصيل تو جروا باجر جزيل.

### ANSWER
If interest money comes into a Muslim's hands, the Muslim does not become its owner; he must return it. If the owner of the money is known, he should give it back to him, and if the owner is not known, it should be given away in charity without any intention of earning reward — it should be given away as a way of ridding oneself of a burden and a calamity. The money may then rightly and lawfully be given to a deserving poor or needy person who does not own the zakat threshold. Accepting a gift of interest money, however, is not permissible or correct.

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

ويردونها على أربابها إن عرفوهم وإلا تصدقوا بها، لأن سبيل الكسب الخبيث التصدق إذا تعذر الرد على صاحبه. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۳۸۵، كتاب الكراهية، فصل في البيع، سعيد).

If, on the other hand, interest money comes into the hands of a non-Muslim, he becomes its owner and it is permissible and correct to accept a gift from him — so long as he has not committed something that reason itself declares forbidden and repugnant, such as fraud, theft or robbery. An Islamic government will, of course, not permit non-Muslims to deal in interest within the abode of Islam.

The whole question really turns on whether non-Muslims are addressed by the subsidiary rulings of the sharia or not, and the jurists differ over this. Some scholars hold that at the outset they are addressed only by the command to believe and by the penal laws, and not by the laws governing transactions, while others hold that non-Muslims are addressed by the laws of transactions as well. In any case, non-Muslims do own money acquired through interest; this is what appears to be correct.

Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote in Aḥkām al-Qurʾān:

تحقيق أن الكفار مخاطبون بالفروع أم لا؟
الكفار مخاطبون بالإيمان إجماعاً، وكذا بالمشروع من العقوبات والمعاملات، وكذا بالفروع وعامة الشرائع في حكم المؤاخذة في الآخرة بلا خلاف، ذكره في المنار وغيره، وأما في وجوب الأداء في أحكام الدنيا فالصحيح أنهم غير مخاطبين به، وما نسب إلى أهل العراق من مشايخنا وإلي الأكثر من أصحاب الشافعي رحمه الله من كونهم مخاطبين بوجوب الأداء في الدنيا فهو مؤول بأنهم مأمورون بأن يومنوا ثم يصلوا كما في عامة كتب الأصول، وذهب البخاريون إلى أنهم مكلفون بالفروع في حق الاعتقاد فقط والصحيح المؤيد بالنصوص والآيات هو ما ذهب إليه الجمهور أنهم مخاطبون باعتقاد الشرائع وكذا بأدائها باستجماع شرائطها منها تقديم الإيمان. (أحكام القرآن: ۱/۱۱، ۱۲. وكذا في الشامي: ٤/۱۲۸، سعيد).

After quoting the statements of the jurists, Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

The following points emerge from the passage quoted above:

(1) In the view of Imam Zufar (may Allah have mercy on him), all the general commands laid down in the sharia concerning transactions include non-Muslims as well, whether they are at war with the Muslims or are protected subjects; and because the address is general, the ruling too is established generally. So any transaction that non-Muslims — whether belligerents or protected subjects — carry out contrary to the sharia will be declared invalid, even though belligerents will not be interfered with, since there is no authority over them, nor will protected subjects, because of the treaty made with them.

(2) In the view of the two companions (may Allah have mercy on them), not every transaction of belligerents that is contrary to the sharia will be declared invalid, because they have not undertaken to abide by the rulings of Islam. In other words, in their view a general address is not enough for a ruling to be established: an undertaking to abide by the law is also a condition, and that is absent in the case of belligerents. But those transactions of protected subjects that go against the agreed rulings of Islam will be declared invalid, because they have undertaken to abide by the rulings of Islam in matters of transactions. (In the present age, however, most non-Muslim countries are described as lands of security, and in those countries the non-Muslims have given no undertaking to abide by the rulings binding on Muslims. This ruling will therefore not apply in those countries, and so in every case a non-Muslim will be held to be the owner of interest money.)

(3) In the view of Imam al-Aʿẓam (may Allah have mercy on him), even the transactions of protected subjects that are contrary to the sharia will not be declared invalid, provided those transactions accord with their own religion; for the protected subjects did not undertake to abide by the rulings of Islam in matters that go against their own beliefs. If, however, a condition regarding some particular transaction was written into the treaty itself, they are bound by that undertaking. As for the remaining transactions laid down by the sharia, which do not accord with their beliefs and about which no condition was stipulated with them, no undertaking was given by the protected subjects; so when they carry out such transactions in accordance with their own religion, those transactions will be treated as valid and will not be declared invalid. (Imdād al-Aḥkām: 4/390, the section on Muslims' dealings with the People of the Book and the polytheists).

Some objections and their answers:

Objection 1: Since in the view of the jurists non-Muslims are addressed by the subsidiary rulings and by the laws of transactions as well, their ruling regarding interest ought to be the same as ours, as appears from the wording of al-Manār and other works.

حيث قال: والكفار مخاطبون بالأمر بالإيمان وبالمشروع من العقوبات والمعاملات. وفي نور الأنوار: وأما المعاملات فهي دائرة بيننا وبينهم فينبغي أن نعامل معهم حسب ما تعاملنا بيننا في البيع والشراء والإجارة وغيرها سوى الخمر والخنزير فإنهما مباحان لهم، لا لنا. (نور الأنوار، ص ۵۹، ٦۰).

So if they acquire money by carrying out a transaction that is contrary to the sharia, that money ought not to be lawful, should it?

Answer: Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) gave the answer to this in the passage that follows. Consider it:

The gist of the answer is that although non-Muslims are indeed addressed by the subsidiary rulings in matters of penalties and transactions, a general address is not enough to establish a ruling of prohibition and invalidity: an undertaking to abide by the law is also a condition. Belligerents have given no undertaking whatever to abide by the rulings of Islam, neither in what agrees with their beliefs nor in what goes against them. So however they earn money — whether through interest, through seizure, or through void and defective sales, and whether by a method that accords with their religion or one that does not — in every case that money enters their ownership, and it is permissible for a Muslim to receive it as wages. (Imdād al-Aḥkām: 4/399, the section on Muslims' dealings with the People of the Book and the polytheists).

Objection 2: The texts concerning the consuming of interest money are general, applying to Muslim and non-Muslim alike. How, then, can it be permissible to take interest money from a non-Muslim?

Answer: The answer is that interest means taking an excess without a counter-value in a contract dealing in goods of the same kind measured by weight or volume. For example, a man lends 100 rand and stipulates that after one month 150 rand will be taken back; this is interest.

Sayyid Sharīf al-Jurjānī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

الربا: هو في اللغة: الزيادة، وفي الشرع: هو فضل خالٍ عن عوض شرط لأحد العاقدين. (التعريفات، ص ۱۱۲).

This is not permissible in anyone's view; it is forbidden by consensus. But the case in question is not of that kind. Here the non-Muslim has become the owner of the money and is giving a Muslim something that belongs to him, and there is no apparent reason for this to be impermissible, since a person may dispose of what he owns in whatever way he wishes.

ʿAṭr al-Hidāyah states:

Interest is any increase, real or constructive, that is made a condition in a contract without a counter-value. A loan is ultimately an exchange, so taking a profit for lending is forbidden. In the case of a gift and the like, no counter-value is mentioned at all, so if the person who accepted the gift returns something more than the gift, that excess is lawful. Whatever is taken or given without any stipulation has nothing to do with interest; it is rather a kindness and a favour. (ʿAṭr al-Hidāyah, p. 165).

Objection 3: Someone may object that this makes earning interest easy: simply take the interest from a non-Muslim and it becomes lawful. For instance, the owner of a company might engage a non-Muslim as his exclusive employee and tell him to carry on the transactions and go on earning interest, and then that interest would be permissible for him. What is the answer to this?

Answer: It is not permissible to procure interest money through a non-Muslim, as has already been explained. But if the non-Muslim has himself taken interest and become its owner, and he then wishes to give a Muslim a gift without any counter-value, or to pay it as the salary of a Muslim employee, this is permissible; for it is a Muslim who is forbidden to own interest money, whereas there is no ground for prohibition in the case of a non-Muslim. Once he has become the owner of that money, he may either use it himself or give it away as a gift to someone else.

Objection 4: The words of the author of al-Hidāyah show that dealing in interest is excepted from their transactions, so it cannot be called lawful for non-Muslims either. What is the answer to this?

Al-Hidāyah states:

والربا مستثني عن عقودهم لقوله عليه السلام: الا من أربي فليس بيننا وبينه عهد. (الهداية: ۲/۳۳۸).

Muṣannaf Ibn Abī Shaybah reports:

عن الشعبي قال: كتب رسول الله ﷺ إلى أهل نجران وهم نصاري "أن من بايع منكم بالربا فلا ذمة له". (مصنف ابن أبي شيبة: ۲۰/۵۵۷).

Answer: The answer is that the head of an Islamic state should not permit protected subjects to conduct business dealings in interest. In itself, however, if interest is considered valid in their own religion and they take it, the non-Muslims will be held to own that money. Today there is no Islamic government that prevents anyone from dealing in interest, so if non-Muslims deal in interest among themselves they will own that money.

Abū ʿUbayd al-Qāsim ibn Sallām says in his book al-Amwāl:

قوله: ومن أكل منهم الربا فذمتي منه بريئة لا نراه غلظ عليهم أكل الربا خاصة من بين المعاصي كلها ولم يجعله لهم مباحاً وهو يعلم أنهم يرتكبون من المعاصي ما هو أعظم من ذلك من الشرك وشرب الخمر وغيره إلا دفعاً عن المسلمين وألا يبايعوهم به فيأكل المسلمون الربا، ولولا المسلمون ما كان أكل أولئك الربا إلا كسائر ما هم فيه من المعاصي، بل الشرك أعظم. (الاموال: ۱/٤٦٦، بيروت. والأموال لابن زنجوية: ۲/۱۰۳. وذكره عن أبي عبيدة الزيلعي في نصب الراية: ۳/۲۰۳).

Iʿlāʾ al-Sunan states:

ولنا أن حرمة الربا ثابتة في حقهم، وهو مستثنى من العهد فإن النبي ﷺ كتب إلى نصاري نجران من أربي فليس بيننا وبينه عهد وكتب إلى مجوس هجر: إما أن تدعوا الربا أو تأذنوا بحرب من الله ورسوله، فالتعرض لهم في ذلك بالمنع لا يكون غدراً بالأمان كذا في المبسوط (۱٤/۵۸) وقد تقدم في شروط أهل الذمة من كتاب الجهاد ما يدل على نهيه ﷺ والخلفاء الراشدين أهل الذمة عن الإرباء في دار الإسلام وأيضاً: فإنما تثبت العصمة في حق الأحكام بالإحراز والإحراز بالدار لا بالدين، لأن الدين مانع لمن يعتقده حقاً للشرع دون من لا يعتقده ولقوة الدار يمنع عن ماله من يعتقد حرمته ومن لم يعتقده كما في المبسوط: (۱٤/۵۸) أيضاً فالاعتبار بالدار، هو الصحيح. (إعلاء السنن: ۱٤/۳۵۲. والمبسوط: ۱٤/۵۸).
