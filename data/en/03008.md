### TITLE
The ruling on Muslims taking part in elections

### QUESTION
Are Muslims permitted to take part in elections or not? And is this obligatory in the sharīʿah, or is it a matter of choice? Kindly explain and be rewarded.

### ANSWER
It is permissible and correct for Muslims to take part in elections, to stand as candidates, to vote, and to run an election campaign for a candidate. Indeed, in democratic countries, because innumerable religious and communal benefits and objectives are bound up with participation in elections, it is better and commendable to do so.

Before voting, however, it is necessary to keep the following few matters in view:

(1) Testimony. The one who votes is bearing witness that the person in whose favour he is voting is fit for the office and that there is no fear of harm to the Muslims from him.

(2) Intercession. The voter is interceding for his representative, and it is necessary for one who intercedes that the person be worthy of intercession. Otherwise, voting for an unfit, oppressive, tyrannical person is to share in his oppression.

قال الله تعالى: ومن يشفع شفاعة حسنة يكن له نصيب منها ومن يشفع شفاعة سيئة يكن له كفل منها. (النساء: ۸۵).

(3) Agency. The one who votes is making the candidate his agent. (Derived from جواہر الفقہ: ۲/۲۹۷۔۲۹۹).

(4) Trusteeship and counsel. That is, the electoral board consults all the people of the country as to which of the various candidates in the electoral field is, in their opinion, more suited to serve the country as ruler or as an aide of the government; and the voter informs the electoral board of his opinion. In this respect it is necessary to observe the rule that governs counsel and opinion, for in the hadith counsel and opinion have been declared a trust. It is narrated from Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (raḍiyallāhu taʿālā ʿanhu) that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

المستشار مؤتمن. (رواہ الترمذی)

That is, one whose counsel is sought is a trustee.

Now, to vote for a person of ability and competence is a cause of immense reward, and it is also necessary, because the Glorious Qur'an has made truthful testimony binding. Allah says:

كونوا قوامين لله شهداء بالقسط.

And in another place He says:

كونوا قوامين بالقسط شهداء لله،

In both these verses Allah has made it obligatory upon the Muslims not to shirk truthful testimony, but to stand up to give testimony for the sake of Allah.

If in some constituency no candidate at all appears to be genuinely competent and honest, but one of them, judged by the standard of capability and fear of God, is preferable to the others, then it is permissible — indeed commendable — to vote for him too, with the intention of lessening the evil and lessening the oppression, just as the jurists (raḥimahumullāh) have prescribed lessening the impurity where one is unable to remove it entirely, and lessening the oppression where one has no power to repel it altogether.

On this same principle, a person who votes in democratic elections is, in the eyes of the sharīʿah, better and more worthy of praise than one who does not. For example: if the ideology of one party is not founded on enmity towards Islam, he should vote for it; and if the ideology of another party is contrary to that, he should not vote for it.

The second issue is that of a Muslim standing as a candidate.

In this connection it should be borne in mind that he is making two claims:

(1) Ability. That is, the candidate possesses the ability, the capacity and the competence for this work, and is ready to shoulder the burden of responsibility for all the people.

(2) Honesty. That is, the claimant will bear this burden with trustworthiness. Accordingly, if a candidate considers himself fit for the office and finds no one more competent than himself, he should seek it; otherwise he will be held responsible for the corruption and injustices that follow when an unfit person comes to power.

It was for this very reason that Ḥaḍrat Yūsuf (ʿalayhis salām) said:

اجعلني على خزائن الأرض، إني حفيظ عليم. (يوسف).

Papers were also written on the question under discussion at a session of the Islamic Fiqh Academy of India. A summary of them is as follows:

(1) Islam has an independent system of governance of its own; but in present-day world conditions, as compared with the other non-Islamic systems of government, it is the prevailing democratic system that is preferable for Muslim minorities. Therefore, under this system it is permissible for Muslims to take part in elections, to stand as candidates, to vote, and to run an election campaign for a candidate.

(2) The communal and religious interests of the Muslims require that they exercise their legal right to vote to the fullest.

(3) Those political parties that have openly made opposition to Islam and the Muslims the very purpose of their party — it is not permissible for Muslims to join them, nor is it permissible to vote for any candidate of theirs, even if he is personally of good character.

(4) Agreements may be concluded with democratic secular political parties on the basis of communal interests.

(5) One may work together with non-Muslims for the benefit of the country and of humanity and to establish an atmosphere of justice, fairness, peace and security in society; and organisations may also be set up in partnership with them. (غیر مسلم ممالک میں آباد مسلمانوں کے کچھ اہم مسائل، ص ۴۹، ط: ایفا پبلیکشنز).

Joining parties opposed to Islam, and assisting them, is not permissible:

Political parties that have fanned the sentiments of nationalism and sectarianism, bigotry and hatred, that strive to win over the majority of the country to their side by propagating hatred against Islam, the Muslims and the Christians, and that harbour the foul design of gaining a majority in the government by kindling the fire of genocide, bloodshed and riots — it will never be permissible to vote for such parties or to join them. Allah, blessed and exalted, says:

﴿ولا تعاونوا على الإثم والعدوان واتقوا الله إن الله شديد العقاب﴾. (المائدة: ۲).

Do not assist one another in sin and transgression, and fear Allah; indeed Allah is severe in punishment.

It may be that voting for such a candidate brings the Muslims some benefit at the local level, but at the national level it will bring national harm to the Muslims. A well-known maxim of fiqh states:

"درء المفاسد أولي من جلب المصالح". (الأشباه والنظائر).

At the time of elections there is room in the sharīʿah for reaching an understanding with non-Muslim secular political parties, for forming an alliance with them, joining them, supporting them and concluding agreements with them on the basis of Muslim interests. After migrating to Madīnah al-Ṭayyibah, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ concluded an agreement with the Jews of Madīnah and the non-Muslim tribes round about, which is remembered by the name of the Covenant of Madīnah: that they would jointly resist outside invaders, and that the adherents of every religion would have the freedom to follow their own religion. It was, as it were, an agreement of a political and defensive nature. In view of present-day conditions this is necessary in the sharīʿah. (Taken from غیر مسلم ممالک میں آباد مسلمانوں کے کچھ اہم مسائل، ص ۲۸۸، ۲۸۹).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
