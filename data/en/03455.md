### TITLE

Changing certain letters in recitation and its effect on the prayer

### QUESTION

In some countries, when senior shaykhs recite the Noble Qur'an they change certain letters — for example, they say "Allāhu ajbar" in place of "Allāhu akbar" — and, astonishingly, they recite in this way in the prayer as well. What is the ruling on a prayer performed behind such a person? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER

If someone has become habituated to reciting incorrectly, and the mistake is of a kind that does not change the meaning, then the prayer does not become invalid, and a prayer performed behind him is valid. Consider what is transmitted in Fatāwā Shāmī:

وإن كان الخطأ بإبدال حرف بحرف، فإن أمكن الفصل بينهما بلا كلفة كالصاد مع الطاء بان قرأ الطالحات مكان الصالحات فاتفقوا على أنه مفسد وإن لم يمكن إلا بمشقة كالظاء مع الضاد والصاد مع السين فأكثرهم على عدم الفساد لعموم البلوى. (فتاوى الشامي: ۱/٦۳۱، سعيد).

Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Ashraf al-Aḥkām:

It is the view of Imām Faḍlī (may Allah have mercy on him) that if a person recites a word incorrectly again and again until it becomes his habit, then that becomes his own dialect; so the prayer of a person who recites the Qur'an correctly will be valid behind such a man. Thus, on one occasion in Makkah, Mawlānā Muḥammad Qāsim and a number of other scholars prayed behind a Turkish imam. Turks pronounce kāf as chīm, and the imam too recited "iyyācha naʿbudu" in place of "iyyāka naʿbudu". Everyone repeated the prayer except Mawlānā Qāsim (may Allah have mercy on him), who did not repeat it, and who gave this same ruling. (Ashrāf al-Aḥkām, p. 59, quoting Anfās-e-ʿĪsā, vol. 2, p. 434, published by Idārah Islāmiyyāt, Karachi.)

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
