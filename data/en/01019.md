### TITLE
Deducting expenses from zakat money

### QUESTION
Suppose a man sends money from his zakat to build a house for someone, or sends books that a person needs, or sends someone to deliver the zakat money to the poor. Can the cost of transport be deducted from the zakat or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, one should first try to meet such expenses from funds other than zakat. But if no other arrangement can be made, there should be room to meet them from the zakat money.

قال الله تعالى: ﴿إنما الصدقات للفقراء والمساكين والعاملين عليها﴾ (التوبة: ٦۰).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

There is room to pay those who collect and bring in zakat money a wage for their work out of that money, even if they are well off. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 4/286).

Mawlānā Khālid Sayfullāh says:

Just as the officials appointed over zakat and ʿushr keep themselves busy protecting the rights of the poor, so further staff may be needed for the accounting and the distribution of zakat and ʿushr. The jurists have generally not discussed this question, but ʿAllāmah al-Qurṭubī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written on it too:

الحادي عشرة: ودل قوله تعالى: ﴿والعاملين عليها﴾ على أن كل ما كان من فروض الكفايات كالساعي والكاتب والقسام والعاشر وغيرهم فالقائم به يجوز له أخذ الأجرة عليه. (الجامع لا حكام القرآن: ۸/۱۱۳، دار الكتب العلمية).

If one reflects, then in its broad sense the term "officials" does not include only the collectors; it includes all the workers involved in gathering and distributing zakat. (Islām kā Niẓām-e ʿUshr o Zakāt: 115).

Islāmī Fiqh states:

In many places goods are sent by train, bus or aeroplane. Should the fare for that transport be paid from the zakat fund or not? Some scholars hold that the fare should not be paid from this fund (as stated in Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm: 6/335; Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 9/483; Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 2/10; Fatāwā Farīdiyyah: 3/397). In the view of this writer, however, it is permissible to pay the cost of transport from the zakat fund. If we call in a workman to build a poor person's house, or send him to deliver zakat goods, and we do not pay his wage from the zakat, then the houses will not get built and the goods will not reach the people. Nor does this go against the requirement of transferring ownership, because after meeting all these expenses we shall be handing the item itself over to someone entitled to it; so the payment counts not as a fare but as part of the price. Likewise, if we send our zakat somewhere else by money order or insured post and the money order and insurance fees are paid from the zakat money, that does not go against the transfer of ownership either, since all of it is done in order to get the money to the poor person. (Islāmī Fiqh: 1/473).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
