### TITLE
A creditor receiving zakat money on the debtor's behalf

### QUESTION
Suppose a man is in debt and is also entitled to receive zakat. Someone tells him, "I will pay off your debt," and the zakat money is then handed to the creditor on the debtor's behalf. Has the zakat been discharged or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, the debtor should inform the creditor: "So-and-so will pay my debt on my behalf, so please receive the money for me." In this situation the creditor first takes possession on behalf of the debtor, so the zakat is discharged; and when he then uses the money, that becomes qabḍ ḍamān (possession entailing liability), so the debt is settled as well.

Consider what Shāmī states:

والأصل أن القبضين إذا تجانسا ناب أحدهما عن الآخر، وإذا تغايرا ناب الأعلى عن الأدنى لا عكسه، فقبض الوديعة مع قبض الهبة يتجانسان لأنهما قبض أمانة ومع قبض الشراء يتغايران، لأنه قبض ضمان فلا ينوب الأول عنه. (شامي: ۵/٦۹٤، كتاب الهبه، سعيد).

But possession as a trust turns into possession entailing liability once the holder uses the property. Consider what al-Hidāyah states:

وإن خلطها المودع بماله حتى لا يتميز ضمنها. (الهداية: ۳/۲۷۳).

Sharḥ al-Majallah states:

ولو أنفق الوديع يعني الوديعة ثم رد مثله وخلطه بالباقي خلطاً لا يتميز معه ضمن الكل (تنوير) أي فيضمن البعض بالإنفاق والبعض بالخلط (طحطاوي). (شرح المجلة: ۱/٤۳۸، بيروت).

It also states:

لوكان المبيع في يد المشتري عارية أو وديعة أو رهناً ثم اشتراه من مالكه لا يصير قابضاً بمجرد العقد لأن قبض العارية والوديعة والرهن قبض أمانة ولا ينوب عن قبض الشراء لأن قبض الشراء مضمون بنفسه ولكن لو فعل المشتري في فصل الوديعة والعارية ما يكون قابضاً منه ثم أراد البائع أخذ المبيع ليحبسه بالثمن لم يكن له ذلك. (شرح المجلة: ۱/٤۳۸).

Shāmī states:

ومنه لو غصب شيئاً ثم اشتراه صار قابضاً بخلاف الوديعة والعارية إلا إذا وصل إليه بعد التخلية. (شامي: ٤/۵٦۱، مطلب في حبس المبيع لقبض الثمن، سعيد).

This passage of Shāmī shows that if a deposited item is sold to the very person who is holding it in trust, and the seller clears the way for him to take it, and then, once it has reached his hands, he deals with it as an owner, his possession is valid. The same applies here: when the creditor uses the money after receiving it, his possession becomes valid.

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states:

It is true that in some situations a single taking of possession serves for two, one of them in a person's own right and the other on behalf of someone else; but this is not a general rule. If we keep to the outward form of the matter here, what emerges is that the man who obtains the goods from the market is the buyer for the institution and then also the seller on the institution's behalf, and he is the buyer from his own hand, since the purchase from the institution has still to take place. One may, however, put it this way: the purchase agreement he made with the institution before the goods were obtained from the market was no more than a promise, so at the time he takes the goods from the market he is only the institution's agent for the purchase. Afterwards, when he has obtained the goods, keeps them in his possession and deals with them as an owner, he becomes — in line with that earlier promise — the buyer of those goods from the institution. In this way the earlier possession, which he held as an agent for another, now becomes possession in his own right, that is, possession for himself. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 3/426, Idārat al-Qur'ān).

So in the case under discussion too, the creditor's possession is at first taken as an agent on behalf of the poor man. When he keeps the money in his possession and deals with it as an owner, then, in line with the earlier promise, he becomes the one recovering his loan, and that earlier possession as an agent now becomes possession in his own right, that is, possession for himself. Thus the wealthy man's zakat is discharged and the poor debtor's loan is repaid as well.

It is better, however, for the creditor's wife to act as the debtor's agent to receive the money and to hand it over to her husband after she has taken possession of it.

Al-Ashbāh wa'l-Naẓāʾir states:

أو يوكل المديون خادم الدائن بقبض الزكاة ثم بقضاء دينه فيقبض الوكيل صار ملكاً للمؤكل. (الأشباه والنظائر: كتاب الحيل ص ٤۲۸).

A second way of settling the loan:

The wealthy man may become the debtor's agent to receive the money and then pay the loan to the creditor on his behalf. In that case both the wealthy man's zakat and the poor man's loan are validly discharged. Consider what Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

ولو قضي دين حي فقير بغير أمره لم يجز، لأنه لم يوجد التمليك من الفقير لعدم قبضه وإن كان بأمره يجوز عن الزكاة لوجود التمليك من الفقير؛ لأنه لما أمره به صار وكيلاً عنه في القبض فصار كأن الفقير قبض الصدقة بنفسه وملكه من الغريم. (بدائع الصنائع: ۲/۳۹، سعيد).

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

ولو قضي دين الفقير بزكاة ماله إن كان بأمره يجوز؛ وإن كان بغير أمره لا يجوز وسقط الدين. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۹۰).

An objection may be raised here: possession has to be taken by another party, yet in this case the giver and the receiver have become one and the same person. How can that be valid?

The answer is that in reality it is indeed another party's possession. Once the wealthy man has obtained the debtor's permission, he becomes the debtor's agent to receive the money, and an agent's possession is the possession of the one who appointed him — which is possession by another party. This is clear from the passage of Badāʾiʿ.

Consider a further parallel. The jurists have written that one and the same man cannot be both seller and buyer; but if the one who appointed him gives permission, such a transaction is sound and valid. Thus Al-Fatāwā al-Bazzāziyyah records:

الوكيل بالبيع لا يملك شراءه لنفسه ٖ لأن الواحد لا يكون مشترياً وبائعاً فيبيعه من غيره ثم يشتريه منه وإن أمره الموكل أن يبيعه من نفسه ٖ أو أولاده الصغار أو ممن لا يقبل له شهادته فباع منهم جاز. (الفتاوى البزازية بهامش الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۷٤۵، الرابع في البيع).

See further: Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā, vol. 5, p. 741.

There is also a present-day parallel to this. According to the preferred view, with the zakat money that comes to religious schools the principals act as agents both for the donors and for the school's needy students; that is, one and the same man is both the giver and the receiver.
