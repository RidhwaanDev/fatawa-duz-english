### TITLE
The garage card and its ruling

### QUESTION
A company gives people a garage card on this basis: when they buy petrol with the card, the company itself pays the bill to the garage; then at the end of the month the company works out the account and charges the purchaser a further 15 per cent, and paying this extra sum is also obligatory. Is there any room for a transaction of this kind or not?

### ANSWER
The usual way a garage card works is that one goes on filling up with petrol by means of it, and at the end of each month one receives a full statement and pays an amount corresponding to the petrol taken; nothing further has to be paid. This arrangement is sound, because no interest or other addition has been imposed: as much as was borrowed has been repaid. But in the case asked about there is mention of taking a further 15 per cent; if that is the position, there is no room for it — it is plain interest. If, however, the company grants a period of grace, so that if payment is made within, say, one month no extra sum need be paid, while if payment is made after the month a further 15 per cent must be paid, then there will be room for it, provided the holder never lets matters reach the point of interest and keeps paying within the fixed period.

Fatāwā ʿUthmānī states:

When the card is used, arrangements must be made to pay the bill within the fixed period so that no interest is incurred. The better course for this is to adopt the Direct Debit method — that is, the card company takes the amount of the bill directly from your bank — so that interest is not imposed through delay even unintentionally. (Fatāwā ʿUthmānī: 3/353).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وما يصح ولا يبطل بالشرط الفاسد لعدم المعاوضة المالية

القرض والهبة. وفي الشامية: (قوله القرض) كأقرضتك هذه المائة بشرط أن تخدمني سنة، وفي البزازية: وتعليق القرض حرام والشرط لا يلزم. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۵/۲٤۹، باب ما يبطل بالشرط الفاسد، سعيد).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم

Giving a female animal out on a half-share basis:

Question: Some people give an animal to another person on the condition that the responsibility for its fodder and keep will rest on him and that the milk and the offspring will be shared half and half. Is a transaction of this kind valid or not?

Answer: Some scholars declare this transaction permissible on the ground of custom. Its impermissibility rested on an analogy with the miller's measure; now, because of custom, it is permissible, since custom prevails over analogy. Other scholars declare it impermissible on the ground that it has not become a general custom, and they devise the following expedient to make it lawful: the owner sells half of his animal to the man who is to keep it and waives the price, so that the animal becomes jointly owned; thereafter the milk and the offspring will all be jointly owned. (Drawn from Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil, p. 158.) But there is a difficulty with this expedient: if the man who rears the animal refuses to rear it, saying that he wishes to sell the animal and divide the price, or asks the original owner for half the cost of rearing it, the owner will evidently not be prepared for that. It is therefore more appropriate to declare the arrangement permissible on the ground of custom.
