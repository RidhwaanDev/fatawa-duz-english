### TITLE
Dealings with a non-Muslim whose business is unlawful

### QUESTION
A man has dealings and transactions with a non-Muslim (a Hindu or a Christian), or accepts a gift from him, or occasionally eats a meal at his invitation, and this non-Muslim does work that is not permissible for Muslims in the sharia. For example:

(1) He commits invalid sales: he buys goods from abroad and sells them before taking possession of them.

(2) He sells liquor, and this is the greater part of his earnings.

(3) He sells cloth bearing pictures of living beings, and this is his earning.

(4) He is a barber and cuts people's hair in ways contrary to the sharia and shaves beards.

(5) He is involved in speculation or gambling — "betting".

(6) He sells musical instruments and cassettes, and so forth. Now if a Muslim sells something to this non-Muslim and takes the price, or takes a gift from him and so on, is this permissible or not? Cases like this often arise in non-Muslim countries. بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
It is permissible for a Muslim to have sound dealings with a non-Muslim whose earnings come from the transactions listed in the question.

Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad ʿUthmānī says concerning selling before taking possession:

If the buyer is an unbeliever, there is no objection to his making such a sale. (امداد الاحکام: ۳/٤۱۰) ۔

In another place he says:

There is no harm in selling musical instruments to unbelievers:

وفي بيعه أي المزمار مع الكفار لم تقم الحرمة بالعين ولا بالفعل فإن الكفار ليسوا مخاطبين بحرمة الغناء ولا هو حرام في الأديان كلها
۔ (امداد الاحکام: ۳/۳۸٤) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
