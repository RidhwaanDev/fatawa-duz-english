### TITLE
The ruling on a driver's exclusion from inheritance when someone dies in an accident

### QUESTION
A young man was travelling in a car with his father. On the way there was an accident: the young man, who was driving, survived and his father died. Will this son receive a share of his father's inheritance or not? Does this fall within the definition of accidental homicide or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, if there was negligence or fault on the part of the young man, or he broke the rules of the road and the accident resulted from that, then this has the ruling of accidental homicide: expiation will be due from the driver and blood money from his ʿāqilah, and he will be excluded from his late father's inheritance.

If, however, the driver was driving in observance of the government's laws and the accident occurred suddenly, so that the driver was helpless, then such a case does not have the ruling of accidental homicide but is a killing by indirect cause; and in it the son will not be excluded from his father's inheritance. Consider the following.

Al-Sharīfiyyah, the commentary on al-Sirājiyyah, states:

وأما القتل الذي يتعلق به وجوب الكفارة فهو إما شبه عمد

وإما خطأ كأن رمي إلى صيد فأصاب إنساناً وانقلب في النوم عليه فقتله أو وطئته دابته وهو راكبها أو سقط من سطح عليه أو سقط حجر من يده فمات، وموجبه الكفارة والدية على العاقلة ولا إثم فيه فعندنا يحرم القاتل عن الميراث في هذه الصور كلها؛

لأن المخطئ مباشر للقتل بفعله فيلزمه الكفارة والحرمان. (الشريفية شرح السراجية، ص ٤۸، ط: الرياض).

In some circumstances the driver is entirely helpless, and in such cases no liability for compensation arises. Consider the following cases:

1. A man was driving a car and someone suddenly ran out in front of it and the driver could not stop; no compensation is due from him.

2. A car is standing at a signal waiting for the green light, and someone comes from behind and strikes it so that his car collides with the car in front. In this case it is difficult to attribute the direct act to the man in the middle, so no compensation is due from him.

3. The car was in perfect order and he was driving it in the customary manner when a fault suddenly developed and the car went out of control; here too no compensation is due.

4. A man was driving in accordance with the law when the vehicle in front suddenly braked and damage resulted; in this case too no compensation is due. Derived from (Buḥūth fī Qaḍāyā Fiqhiyyah Muʿāṣirah: 1/311, 315).
