### TITLE
Madrasah fundraisers come under the ruling of zakat collectors

### QUESTION
Do the fundraisers of madrasahs come under the ruling of the collectors of zakat or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, according to the preponderant view, the fundraisers and donation collectors of madrasahs do come under the ruling of the collectors of zakat.

Consider what Jawāhir al-Fatāwā states:

The considered position of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah illuminate his resting place) was at first that the principals of madrasahs do not come under the ruling of the collectors of charity, but are rather the agents of those who give zakat. Later, on the strength of the research of Ḥaḍrat Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him), Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) and Ḥaḍrat Sahāranpūrī (may Allah have mercy on him), Ḥaḍrat Muftī Ṣāḥib withdrew from his earlier position and held that the madrasah principals of today, and the donation collectors appointed by them, do come under the ruling of the collectors of charity and are the agents of the poor. (جواہر الفتاوی: جلد اول ص ۷٤، اسلامی کتب خانہ، کراچی) ۔

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states:

According to the preponderant view, the fundraisers and donation collectors of madrasahs come under the ruling of the collectors of zakat. Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Kifāyatullāh (may Allah have mercy on him) held from the very beginning that fundraisers and collectors come under the ruling of the collectors of zakat. Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him), in Imdād al-Muftīn and Maʿārif al-Qurʾān, and Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him), in Imdād al-Fatāwā, did not at first regard fundraisers and collectors as coming under the ruling of the collectors, but in the end these scholars changed their view. For details see Jawāhir al-Fiqh, volume four; Fatāwā Khalīliyyah, volume one; and Imdād al-Fatāwā, volume six. (جدید فقہی مباحث: ۷/۵۸٤، ادارۃ القرآن) ۔

Mawlānā Khālid Sayfullāh says:

Where no sharia system of government exists, religious institutions and schools may organise the collective gathering of zakat and its delivery to those entitled to it, since the students of religious madrasahs are themselves among the best recipients of zakat. In such a case those who do the work of collecting zakat on behalf of these institutions come, in part, under the ruling of the collectors. Although they are not appointed to this work by the leader of the Muslims, the real object of the sharia in entrusting the right of collecting zakat to the leader — namely that the collection and distribution of zakat should be organised collectively — is achieved in this way too, and what truly counts is the underlying purposes:

الأمور بمقاصدها

۔ (اسلام کا نظامِ عشر و زکوٰۃ: ص ۱۱۷، عاملین اور موجود ہ محصلین) ۔

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Kifāyatullāh (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

There is scope for paying those who collect and bring in zakat money a wage out of that money, even if they are wealthy; but under no circumstances may they be given more than half of what they have collected. Zakat money may not be given to a person entitled to zakat as payment for work — apart from the collection and gathering of zakat itself — because transfer of ownership without any return is a condition for discharging zakat. Employees in the departments of teaching and preaching are paid a salary under a contract of hire, and that is not a transfer of ownership without return. If, however, they are given a monthly sum as a stipend, and their work is not assessed as that of a hired employee, and they are not called to account as a hired worker is, then it will be permissible to give them a monthly stipend out of zakat. (کفایت المفتی: ٤/۲۸٦، مصارفِ زکوٰۃ، دار الاشاعت) ۔
