### TITLE
Giving the name Naṣr Allāh

### QUESTION
A man has given his child the name Naṣr Allāh, and people object to it, asking what "the help of Allah" is supposed to mean. The question now is whether this name should be changed or not. بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
In the case asked about there is no need to change this name. Its sense is Nāṣir li-Dīn Allāh, that is, this person is a helper of the religion of Allah Most High. Allah Most High says in the Noble Qurʾān:

﴿إن تنصروا الله ينصركم ويثبت أقدامكم﴾ [سورة محمد].

Rūḥ al-Maʿānī records:

إن تنصروا الله أي دينه ورسوله

فنصرته سبحانه نصرة رسوله ودينه. (روح المعاني: ۲٦/ ٤۳).

Bayān al-Qurʾān renders it: you will help the religion of Allah. (Bayān al-Qurʾān).

That is, the verbal noun naṣr carries the sense of the active participle, and the word "religion" is omitted. In the science of rhetoric this is called figurative usage by omission. Al-Balāghah al-ʿArabiyyah records:

القسم الرابع: المجاز المرسل القائم على التوسع في اللغة دون ضابط معين، ومنه المجاز بالحذف أو بالزيادة، فالحذف يكون للإيجاز، كحذف كلمة يوجد ما يدل عليها، أو حذف جملة أو أكثر. (البلاغة العربية، ص ٦٦٦).

In the same way Naṣr Allāh can also carry the sense of the passive participle, that is, manṣūr min Allāh — as with ʿAwn al-Maʿbūd, meaning muʿān wa manṣūr min Allāh. The sense will then be that the help of Allah Most High will remain with this child. It is a good omen.

The upshot is that there is no need to change this name.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
