### TITLE
Ruling on making a written ṭalāq conditional

### QUESTION
There is constant quarrelling between my wife and my parents. My parents complain that my wife does not do the housework and so on, and they do not want to keep her in the house. My father has written me a letter telling me to give my wife a conditional ṭalāq, and saying that if I do not do so my parents will remain displeased with me. Yet there is no quarrel between me and my wife; on the contrary, we live together well and we have children. My father has himself written out the wording of the conditional ṭalāq and given it to me so that I may copy it out and deliver it to my wife. I set out my father's document below:

A letter of admonition

My wife Shamīmah Khātūn bint Naṣīr al-Dīn, listen carefully. You are my wife, and I shall provide your maintenance wherever you live. But in my absence you took charge of my parents' house for some weeks as though you owned it, and caused my parents mental and physical distress and suffering. Driven by this, they are cutting me off from their property, and I am therefore compelled to accept what they say and the condition they lay down: that if at any time in your whole life you set foot in the house or on the land owned by my parents, you are divorced at that very moment — ṭalāq, ṭalāq, ṭalāq.

My question now is this: is there any room for me, in order to satisfy my parents, to copy out this document for them and tell them that I have written it out and sent it to my wife, while saying nothing about it to my wife at all? Would this conditional ṭalāq then be suspended, and would it take effect if the condition were fulfilled? Kindly favour me with an answer.

### ANSWER
Just as a spoken ṭalāq may be made conditional, so may a written one, and the ṭalāq takes effect when the condition is fulfilled. Accordingly, if you write out the divorce document for your parents, the ṭalāq will be suspended upon the condition even though you neither write to your wife nor mention it to her; and if your wife then goes to your parents' house in their lifetime, three ṭalāqs will take effect. You should therefore not write out the divorce document even for your parents. If, however, you resort to tawriyah — using words that are true in themselves but understood otherwise — and tell your parents only that you have indeed written a letter to your wife, without telling them the contents of the letter and without saying that you have given her a conditional ṭalāq, and if by way of precaution you also have witnesses to that letter, then no ṭalāq will take effect, because you will not have written a divorce document at all.

Al-Hidāyah states:

وإذا أضافه إلى شرط وقع عقيب الشرط مثل أن يقول لا مرأته إن دخلت الدار فأنت طالق. (الهداية: ۲/۳۸۵).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

كتب الطلاق إن مستبيناً على نحو لوح وقع إن نوى وقيل مطلقاً. وفي الشامية: قوله كتب الطلاق) قال في الهندية: الكتابة على نوعين: مرسومة وغير مرسومة، ونعني بالمرسومة أن يكون مصدراً ومعنوناً مثل ما يكتب إلى الغائب وغير المرسومة أن لا يكون مصدراً ومعنوناً، وهو على وجهين مستبينة وغير مستبينة، فالمستبينة ما يكتب على الصحيفة والحائط والأرض على وجه يمكن فهمه وقراء ته، وغير المستبينة ما يكتب على الهواء والماء وشيء لا يمكن فهمه وقراء ته. ففي غير المستبينة لا يقع الطلاق وإن نوى، وإن كانت مستبينة لكنها غير مرسومة إن نوى الطلاق يقع وإلا لا، وإن كانت مرسومة يقع الطلاق نوى أولم ينو، ثم المرسومة لا تخلو إما أن أرسل الطلاق بأن كتب: أما بعد فأنت طالق، فكما كتب هذا يقع الطلاق وتلزمها العدة من وقت الكتابة، وإن علق طلاقها بمجيء الكتاب بأن كتب: إذا جاء ك كتابي فأنت طالق فجاء ها الكتاب فقرأ ته أولم تقرأ يقع الطلاق كذا في الخلاصة. قوله مطلقاً المراد به في الموضعين نوى أولم ينو. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۳/۲٤٦، مطلب في الطلاق بالكتابة، سعيد. وكذا في حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ۲/۱۱۱، كوئته).

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states: A ṭalāq takes effect by writing it out oneself or by having it written. In the matter of ṭalāq, earnest and jest are alike: that is, if a ṭalāq is given as a pretence or as a joke, or a man tells someone else to write out a ṭalāq, the ṭalāq takes effect. (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: ۹/۱٤٦، مدلل مکمل) ۔

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states: For a ṭalāq to take effect it is not a condition that the divorce document reach the woman; the ṭalāq takes effect by the writing alone, and whatever kind of ṭalāq has been written down is the kind that takes effect. (احسن الفتاوی: ۵/۱٤۸) ۔
