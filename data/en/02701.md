### TITLE
Greeting a man who shaves his beard

### QUESTION
Is it correct to greet a man who shaves his beard or not?

### ANSWER
It appears from the statements of the jurists that a man who shaves his beard is a sinner and a transgressor, so it is better not to greet him, by way of reproof. If, however, the greeting is not meant to show him honour but to win his heart and draw him towards the religion, then there is hope of reward in it.

See what Rūḥ al-Maʿānī states:

ولا يجب رد سلام فاسق أو مبتدع زجراً له أو لغيره وإن شرع سلامه. (روح المعاني: ۳/ ۱۰۱، النساء: ۸٦).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states: arrogance is forbidden, and one who commits it — particularly one who persists in it — is a transgressor; and it is permissible, indeed preferable, not to be the first to greet a transgressor. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 4/279).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states: it is not necessary to return the greeting of a transgressor, but returning it is permissible and not disliked. Those who shave their beards, or trim them so short that they look shaven, fall under the definition of a transgressor. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 9/106).
