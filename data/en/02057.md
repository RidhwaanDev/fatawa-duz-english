### TITLE
Medical insurance and mutual assistance

### QUESTION
Consider this form of medical insurance. A few friends and colleagues in an office set up a regular fund, so that every person deposits a few rupees into the fund out of each salary, and then, whenever a member needs treatment, financial assistance is provided to him. If more of the fund is spent on members who fall ill, the contribution is increased for all the members, and if less is spent, the contribution is reduced. Is this form permissible or not?

### ANSWER
Since at no stage of this form of medical insurance is there any interest or gambling, and since there is nothing else in it contrary to the sharia, this form of mutual assistance is permissible without any dislike — indeed it is recommended. It is one of the permissible forms of insurance and mutual assistance that the scholars have proposed on various occasions. Muslims should abandon the forbidden forms of insurance current today and adopt the permissible forms. (Summarised from Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥall: 6/257).

Consider the decision of the Islamic Fiqh Academy of the Muslim World League, Makkah Mukarramah, concerning mutual assistance (cooperative insurance):

The session of the Academy unanimously endorses decision no. 51 of the Council of Senior Scholars of Saudi Arabia, dated 4/4/1397 AH, concerning the permissibility of cooperative insurance and the prohibition of commercial insurance, on the basis of the following arguments:

First: cooperative insurance is a type of gratuitous contract whose primary aim is the sharing of losses and the sharing of liability when accidents occur. For this purpose a number of persons pool sums of money and assist whoever suffers the loss. The aim of a cooperative insurance group is neither trade nor gaining profit from other people's wealth; rather its aim is to distribute losses among themselves and to make them good.

Second: cooperative insurance is free of both kinds of interest, ribā al-faḍl and ribā al-nasāʾ (interest of excess and interest of deferment). The contract of its participants is not an interest-bearing one, nor are the instalments collected used in interest-based dealings.

Third: the participants' not knowing the extent of the benefit they will receive in cooperative insurance is likewise not harmful, because these people are donors, and in such a case there is no risk, uncertainty (gharar) or gambling. Commercial insurance, by contrast, is a financial and commercial exchange.

Fourth: the participants or their representatives fulfil the objects of this cooperation by investing the instalments collected; and there is no harm in this, whether those who carry out the investment do so voluntarily or for a wage.

It is the view of the session that the following principles should be observed in drawing up the detailed regulations for cooperative insurance:

1. The cooperative insurance organisation should have a central office with branches under it in every city, and within the organisation there should be separate departments for the different kinds of loss and for people belonging to different professions — for example, a department for health insurance, a department for insurance of the disabled and the aged, and so on.

2. The cooperative insurance organisation should be as simple and easy as possible and entirely free of complicated procedures.

3. The organisation should have a supreme council to settle its procedures and to propose the necessary regulations, which are to be put into effect after being brought into harmony with the rules of the sharia.

4. If losses exceed the organisation's income, so that they become a cause for raising the instalments, the government and the participants should bear that increase.

The session of al-Majmaʿ al-Fiqhī endorses the proposal of the Council of Senior Scholars and its sense that the detailed regulations for a cooperative company should be drawn up by experts and specialists. (ʿAṣr-i Ḥāḍir ke Pēchīdah Masāʾil kā Sharʿī Ḥall, compiled by Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām Qāsimī (may Allah have mercy on him), pp. 37–40).

For more, see: (Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil: 7/292, and Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/102).
