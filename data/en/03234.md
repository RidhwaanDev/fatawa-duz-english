### TITLE
The ruling on bequeathing that a religious book be placed in the grave

### QUESTION
A student of religious knowledge was deeply attached to Shāh Walīullāh's (may Allah have mercy on him) book Ḥujjat Allāh al-Bālighah. He was suffering from cancer, and he left instructions that after his death Ḥujjat Allāh al-Bālighah should be placed in his grave. Is this bequest binding to act upon or not? And if someone acted upon it, would he be sinful or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question such a bequest is not to be acted upon; and if someone did act upon it, he would be guilty of grave disrespect, for the book would become soiled with the blood and other matter of the corpse. Nor would it be correct to set the book in a niche, because the notion at work in that is that the dead man will read the book in the grave, and this is not sound.

Consider what is recorded in Fatāwā Bazzāziyyah:

أوصي بأن يدفن كتبه معه لا يجوز إلا أن يكون فيها شيء لا يفهمه أحد أو فيها فساد فينبغي أن تدفن. (الفتاوى البزازية بهامش الهندية: ٦/٤٤۰، كتاب الوصايا).
قال في البحر الرائق: وإذا أوصي أن تدفن كتبه لم يجز إلا أن يكون فيها شيء لا يفهمه أحد ويكون فيه فساد فينبغي أن يدفن. (تكملة البحر الرائق: /٤۵٤، كتاب الوصايا).

Āp ke Masāʾil aur un kā Ḥal states:

It is impermissible to bury the Noble Qur'an, or any part of it, in the grave along with the deceased, because the corpse swells and bursts in the grave, and to place the Noble Qur'an in such a spot is disrespectful. The same ruling applies to other sacred words. There was no such practice among the pious predecessors. (Āp ke Masāʾil aur un kā Ḥal: 3/236).
