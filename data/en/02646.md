### TITLE
An internal medical examination for joining the army

### QUESTION
If it is a condition for employment in a country's army that an internal examination will be carried out, in which the private parts must be uncovered, is it permissible to take up such employment or not?

### ANSWER
Raising an army and maintaining it is an important department of a country, especially in the present age, and inquiries and tests are carried out for entry into the army so that the country's army may be established in the best possible way. The jurists have also written that uncovering the private parts is not permissible without a pressing need; so if this examination amounts to a genuine need, there is room for it. See what Fatāwā al-Shāmī states:

مطلب: المواضع التي يحل فيها النظر إلى عورة الأجنبي. (قوله لا باحته لتحمل الشهادة) ومثله نظر القابلة والخافضة والختان والطبيب وزاد في الخلاصة من مواضع حل النظر للعورة عند الحاجة الاحتقان والبكارة في العنة والرد بالعيب فتح، قلت: وكذا لو ادعي الزاني بكارتها. ونظمتها بقولي:
ولا تنظر لعورة أجنبي

٭

بلا عذر كقابلة طبيب
وختان وخافضة وحقن

٭

شهود زنا بلا قصد مريب
وعلم بكارة في عنة أو

٭

زنا أو حين رد للمعيب
(فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۳٦، كتاب الحدود، سعيد).
وفي شرح فتح القدير: والنظر إلى العورة عند الحاجة لا يوجب فسقاً كنظر القابلة

الخ. (فتح القدير: ۵/۲۹۸، باب الشهادة على الزنا، دار الفكر، وكذا في المبسوط للامام السرخسي رحمه الله: ۹/۱۳۱، دار الفكر).

Faqīh al-Ummah Hazrat Mufti Mahmūd Sahib (may Allah have mercy on him), however, held that this need is not recognised by the sharia. See what he says:

Question: These days, when a man is recruited into the police or admitted to a college, he is stripped and given a medical examination. Is this permissible, given that uncovering the private parts is unlawful?

الجواب حامداً و مصلیاً: This need is not recognised by the sharia as a ground for uncovering the private parts, for neither health nor life depends upon it, nor does any other religious necessity rest upon it. In police service a man is on many occasions compelled by the law to act contrary to the sharia. Nor does his livelihood depend on this, for without it a great many people live comfortably and in accordance with the sharia. So in the case asked about there is no room whatsoever for committing something unlawful; rather, keeping away from it is necessary. فقط واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم

(فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۹/۲۴۵، جامعہ فاروقیہ) و (کفایت المفتی: ۱۲/۲۸۰، ط: جامعہ فاروقیہ).

In sum: undergoing an internal examination of this kind in order to take up such employment is something that falls under what the sharia forbids. To all appearances it can be called neither a religious necessity nor a necessity of livelihood, so one should keep away from it, since there are many other means of earning a living besides this.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
