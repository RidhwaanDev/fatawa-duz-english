### TITLE
Zakat due on a cheque

### QUESTION
If a person already owns the nisab and, before his zakat year is complete, he receives a cheque for a sum of money, and he has still not drawn the money from the bank by the time the year passes — when does zakat become binding on him? As soon as he receives the cheque, or only after he draws the money from the bank?

### ANSWER
Once the cheque has reached him, this counts as constructive possession — that is, a cheque that is backed by funds and is not uncovered, particularly once the bank has issued it; possession of that money is constructive possession. Zakat therefore becomes necessary as soon as the cheque is received, because he can draw the money from the bank at any time he wishes. The case is like a person making a gift of money kept in a chest: if the chest is open and not locked, that amounts to taking possession, because he can take the money whenever he wants; but if it is locked, it does not count as possession. In the same way here, he can draw the money by means of the cheque whenever he wishes; indeed, in some dealings the cheque itself serves as payment, and cheques are used in large transactions. As for the point that the bank can cancel it, that has nothing to do with possession; it is a cancelling of its value, and the same can happen with a draft or with dollars — if someone's dollars are stolen, the bank may cancel their value. In any case, in the matter asked about, the obligation of zakat attaches as soon as the cheque is received, even if the money then lies in the bank for years.

Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

والتمكن من القبض كالقبض فلو وهب لرجل ثياباً في صندوق مقفل ودفع إليه الصندوق لم يكن قبضاً لعدم تمكنه من القبض، وإن مفتوحاً كان قبضاً لتمكنه منه، فإنه كالتخلية في البيع. اختيار. (الدر المختار: ۵/٦۹۰، كتاب الهبة، سعيد).

Al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

ولو وهب لرجل ثياباً في صندوق مقفل ودفع إليه الصندوق لم يكن قبضاً، وإن كان الصندوق مفتوحاً كان قبضاً لأنه يمكنه القبض، كذا في المحيط. (البحر الرائق: ۷/۲۸٦).
