### TITLE
The ruling on an annulment pronounced by a single mawlawī ṣāḥib

### QUESTION
A woman was married to a man, and disagreement arose between the two of them. In the end the woman went and settled in her parents' home. The husband's demand was: "Hand the children over to me in accordance with the sharia and I am ready to give a divorce." But the woman does not wish to give the children to her husband. In short, without her husband's knowledge the woman obtained a separation from a certain mawlawī ṣāḥib and married another man. Now: (1) Has the marriage been annulled or not? (2) If it has not been annulled, is the parentage of the children born to the second man established or not? (3) On the death of the second husband, will this woman inherit from him or not?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about: (1) The woman's marriage has not been annulled, because an annulment requires the decision of a sharia judge; and where there is no sharia judge, the marriage may be annulled through the Jamāʿat al-Muslimīn, or the Jamʿiyyat al-ʿUlamāʾ, or arbitrators — and for these too there are conditions and regulations which must be observed. A marriage is therefore not annulled merely by a single scholar's pronouncing an annulment. Accordingly the second marriage was not valid, and this woman remains, as before, in the marriage of the first man.

Al-Ḥīlah al-Nājizah states:

All the ways in which a woman may be released have this in common: that neither the woman nor her guardians act on their own authority; rather, the judgement of a qāḍī is a condition. That is, it is necessary for the woman to file her case in the qāḍī's court and for the qāḍī, after a proper investigation according to the sharia, to order a separation or whatever else is due. If in some place there is no sharia judge, then the ruling of a body of Muslims will also take the place of a qāḍī's judgement; and the way of this is that the matter should be laid before a body of religious and influential Muslims of the neighbourhood or settlement, numbering at least three, and that body should investigate the facts and give a ruling in accordance with the sharia. (الحیلۃ الناجزۃ: ۲٤۸، امدادیہ ۔ و چند اہم فقہی مسائل: ٦۰) ۔

Note: the conditions and regulations governing a body of Muslims and arbitrators have been mentioned here only in brief; a fuller discussion of them will be given at the end of the chapter.

(2) Since the marriage was not annulled, the second marriage is invalid; nevertheless the parentage of children born within the second marriage will be established.

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

ويثبت نسب الولد المولود في النكاح الفاسد وتعتبر مدة النسب من وقت الدخول عند محمد رحمه الله وعليه الفتوي قاله أبو الليث رحمه الله كذا في التبيين. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۳۳۰، باب في النكاح الفاسد).

(3) On the death of the first husband the woman will be entitled to inherit, because she is his wife; but on the death of the second husband she will not be entitled to inherit.

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

قوله ويثبت النسب (في النكاح الفاسد) أما الإرث فلا يثبت فيه. (فتاوى الشامي: ۳/۱۳٤، مطلب في النكاح الفاسد. ط: سعيد).
ومثله في الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ۲/٦۰، باب المهر، ط: كوئته).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
