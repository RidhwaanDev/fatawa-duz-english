### TITLE
Using medicine to hold back menstruation

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to use medicine to stop menstruation during the Hajj or in Ramadan? Nowadays women have to endure difficulty because of irregular menstruation.

### ANSWER
Menstruation is not something that has to continue; it is a temporary matter. Taking medicine to hold it back is therefore not forbidden but permissible, and using such medicine can be particularly beneficial during the Hajj or in Ramadan, so that a woman can carry out her duties easily and conveniently, since menstruation at those times usually brings hardship with it. It is not appropriate, however, to use medicine to stop menstruation permanently, since there is a risk of harm to health, so caution is better.

Radd al-Muḥtār states:

وقال في السراج سئل المشايخ عن المرضعة إذا لم تر حيضاً فعالجته حتى رأت صفرة في أيام الحيض قال هو حيض تنقضي به العدة. (رد المحتار: ۱/۳۰٤).

It also states:

لو انقطع دمها فعالجته بدواء حتى رأت صفرة في أيام الحيض أجاب بعض المشايخ بانه تنقضي به العدة. (رد المحتار: ۳/۵۵).

Just as a woman may take treatment to bring on her menstruation where there is a benefit in doing so, such as completing her ʿiddah (the waiting period), so too she may take medicine to stop or hold back menstruation for the sake of performing her religious duties, since neither the arrival of menstruation nor its prevention is required by the sharia.

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

There is no harm in women using medicines that temporarily stop menstrual bleeding during the days of the Hajj, so that all the rites of the Hajj may be carried out as usual and at their appointed times, and so that they can complete those rites within the fixed period. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/243).
