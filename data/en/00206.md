### TITLE
Holding a weekly gathering for blessings on the Prophet ﷺ and supplication

### QUESTION
In our city, in our mosque, we want to hold a programme of blessings on the Prophet ﷺ one night a week after the Isha prayer. It would run as follows.

(1) The imam will first relate some of the virtues of sending blessings and salutations on the Prophet ﷺ to those present.

(2) Then blessings and salutations will be recited aloud.

(3) After that there will be a collective supplication.

Is it permissible to hold a gathering of this kind or not? Is it correct to call it an innovation? Some people here say that it is an innovation. Is what they say correct or not?

### ANSWER
Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

Question: What is the ruling on people gathering in one place every Thursday to complete 125,000 recitations of blessings on the Prophet ﷺ for the welfare of the Muslims and then making a supplication? Should one join this gathering and recite, or not?

Answer: Binding oneself to such a gathering has no basis. There is great reward in reciting blessings on the Prophet ﷺ individually.

Question: What is the ruling on a group of people gathering and reciting blessings on the Prophet ﷺ, or making remembrance of Allah, in loud voices?

Answer: Reciting blessings in a congregation in this way is not established, so such a fixed form should not be made a binding practice. Whatever a person recites of blessings on the Prophet ﷺ on his own brings reward, and reciting aloud is also permissible, provided that it does not disturb anyone who is praying or anyone who is ill.

It also says on page 119:

Making it binding to recite blessings on the Prophet ﷺ every day after the Isha prayer is likewise not correct. Those who are free and wish to recite gladly and sincerely should recite, and no pressure or compulsion should be placed on the one who does not wish to recite. (كفايت المفتي: ۲/۱۰۰).

From the fatwa works quoted above it is clear that binding oneself to such gatherings, and arranging them in such a way that pressure and compulsion are used on people to attend, is impermissible and an innovation. However, if it is not made a binding practice — rather, those who wish to attend gladly attend, and no blame or pressure is placed on the one who does not attend — then there is no harm in it. It is better also not to fix a particular time, but to recite at other times as well from time to time; and rather than one man alone reciting, the better arrangement is for all those present to recite quietly, and for a collective supplication to be made at the end.

At this point one should also understand what innovation really is, so that it becomes easy to avoid matters of innovation.

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

"An innovation is an act that did not exist in the first three generations, which are testified to be good, for which no basis is found, and which is done, or left, in the belief that it is a religious act. To do such a thing, or to leave it believing that leaving it is a religious act, is innovation." (کفایت المفتی: ۱/۱٦٦).

Maʿrakah-e Sunnat wa Bidʿat states:

"Besides this there is a further matter: making binding what the sharia has not made binding, or restricting to a particular time what the sharia has not restricted to a particular time, and fixing it to a particular day or date. This is what is technically called "التزام مالا يلزم" — that is, making binding what is not binding." (معرکۂ سنت و بدعت: ص ۱۵۸)

From this definition it is clear that performing an act that has no basis in the generations testified to be good, and treating it as part of the religion and a means of reward, and attaching to such acts binding practices, restrictions and specifications that the sharia has not made binding — all of this falls under innovation. And these are precisely the features found in the ṣalāt and salām as practised by the Barelwīs. The details are as follows.

(1) Fixing a particular time for the recitation of blessings on the Prophet ﷺ.

(2) Fixing a particular place.

(3) Fixing a particular congregational form.

(4) Fixing the form of someone leading it.

(5) Fixing that it be recited standing.

(6) Fixing that it be recited aloud.

(7) Binding oneself to all of these matters.

(8) Then the belief that the Prophet ﷺ comes and attends.

Each one of these acts is an innovation in its own right, because there is no proof in the pure sharia for these restrictions and specifications regarding blessings on the Prophet ﷺ. It is therefore clear that this customary practice is something invented, and to regard invented things as part of the religion and to hope for reward through them is innovation. This customary practice is not established from the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), nor from the Successors, nor from the Successors of the Successors, nor from the pious elders and the righteous predecessors. However, if a gathering for blessings on the Prophet ﷺ is held in which nothing contrary to the sharia takes place, which is free of the restrictions, specifications and binding practices mentioned above, and in which people are not forced to take part, then there is room for it.

In short, if fixing the time after the Isha prayer is for the convenience of the people, and not because more reward is obtained after the Isha prayer or because this is a part of the religion; and if reciting together is likewise done to encourage people and awaken their interest, and for ease — just as a particular day may be set aside for preaching and admonition, the aim not being that more reward is gained on that day but only that it is convenient — then this is not innovation. Likewise the litanies of the Sufis are not innovation, because they are prescribed as a remedy, to build the habit of abundant remembrance of Allah, and that particular form is not regarded as an end in itself or as part of the religion.
