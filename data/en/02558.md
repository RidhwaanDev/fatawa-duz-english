### TITLE
Removing life-support apparatus from a patient

### QUESTION
Sometimes artificial apparatus is attached to persons who are near death and their heartbeat is thereby kept going. If this apparatus is removed, the heartbeat stops and the person becomes like a corpse — indeed, he dies. Is it permissible to remove this artificial apparatus or not? Does it fall under the ruling of killing or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, consider the details of whether removing the artificial apparatus from the patient is permissible and correct in the light of the following research:

(1) According to physicians, when respiration and the movement of the heart cease completely, or when the cerebrum and the brain stem cease their functions, this is called brain death.

Among the principal organs the brain holds a most important place; the brain not only controls the entire nervous system, but respiration and the heartbeat are also subject to it.

The nervous system has four parts:

(a) المخ (cerebrum): the greater part of the nervous system consists of this. From here proceed thought, memory, feeling, sensation, the movement of the various limbs of the body, and other such actions as a person performs of his own will while awake.

(b) المخيخ (cerebellum): ten per cent of the nervous system consists of this; it maintains the balance of the body.

(c) جذع المخ (brain stem): this joins the brain to the spinal cord; its function is to control respiration, the heartbeat and the circulation of the blood.

(d) The spinal cord: the messages of sensation and movement of the human body are transmitted from here to the brain.

Thus, if the death of the cerebrum or the cerebellum occurs, it is possible for a person to remain alive in an abnormal manner; but if the death of the brain stem occurs, this will be termed real death, some of whose outward signs are as follows:

(1) The pupils of the eyes become insensible and motionless and their light departs.

(2) The cycle of respiration stops completely.

(3) The movement of the heart stops completely; besides this, death is determined and confirmed by modern medical instruments; and so on.

In the view of the jurists, however, death is the name for the separation of the soul from the body, that is, that life no longer remains in the limbs of the body.

An indication of this is found in the hadiths in which the states of the death of the believer and of the disbeliever are mentioned — for example, that the soul of the believer leaves the body with ease and the soul of the disbeliever amid severe pain. It becomes clear from this that death is the name for the parting of the soul from the body. Keeping this before us, then, in the view of the jurists there are the following three cases in the light of the patient's condition:

(1) The patient returns to his natural state through the breathing apparatus, that is, his normal functioning returns; in this case the breathing apparatus should be detached, since the patient is now safe from danger.

(2) Despite the apparatus his heart stops and his breathing also stops; since the patient's death has occurred completely, the doctor will direct that the breathing apparatus be removed.

(3) The signs of brain death appear — for example complete unconsciousness, insensibility and immobility — but the movement of the heart and breathing are set going artificially by means of the breathing apparatus. This third case is the one under consideration.

Nevertheless, if the matter is viewed from the standpoint of the Sharīʿah, a person in whom any one of the following signs appears will be regarded as dead:

(a) The movement of the heart and breathing stop completely and expert physicians state that their return is now impossible.

(b) All the functions of the brain cease, and according to the statement of expert physicians his brain death has occurred.

In such a case removing the breathing apparatus is permissible and correct; that person will be regarded as dead and the rulings of the dead will apply to him. کذا فی فقہ النوازل ۔

(2) The second reason: an expert physician has advised that the patient cannot remain alive of himself and that his functions continue only by means of the apparatus. In that case removing the artificial apparatus is correct, and it does not fall under the ruling of killing either, because artificial apparatus and the like come under treatment, and treatment in itself is neither farḍ nor wājib. This, however, is when recovery through it is not certain or strongly probable; otherwise it is obligatory and one will be sinful for abandoning it, as has just been mentioned above. In the case in question there is no treatment at all; rather, death is almost certain.

Al-Muḥīṭ al-Burhānī states:

وفي النوازل: الرجل اذا ظهر به داء، فقال له الطبيب: قد غلبك الدم فأخرجه، فلم يخرجه حتى مات لا يكون مأخوذاً؛ لأنه لا يعلم يقيناً أن الشفاء فيه، وفيه أيضاً: استطلق بطنه أو رمدت عينه، فلم يعالج حتى أضعفه ومات بسببه لا إثم عليه؛ فرق بين هذا وبينما إذا جاع ولم يأكل مع القدرة على الأكل حتى مات فإنه يأثم، والفرق: أن الأكل قدر قوته فيه شفا يتعين، فإذا تركه صار مهلكاً نفسه، ولا كذلك المعالجة. (المحيط البرهاني: الفصل التاسع عشر في التداوي من كتاب الاستحسان: ٦/۱۱۷). (وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳۵٤، الباب الثامن عشر في التداوي). وللاستزادة انظر: (فتاوى قاضيخان على هامش الهندية: ۳/٤۰۳، والفتاوى السراجية، ص ۳۳۲).

وفي الكيسانيات في الجراحات المخوفة والقروح العظيمة والحصاة الواقعة في المثانة ونحوها إن قيل: قد ينجو وقد يموت أو ينجو ولا يموت يعالج وإن قيل لا ينجو أصلاً لا يداوي بل يترك كذا في الظهيرية. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳٦۰).

Fiqh al-Mushkilāt states:

أما الصورة الأخيرة وهي التي يبقي فيها التنفس بأجهزة النعاش الصناعية فالظاهر أنه تكلف محض وحياة اصطناعية، فلسنا مكلفين بإبقائها شرعاً، فيجوز نزع مثل تلك الأجهزة والآلات. (فقه المشكلات، ص ۱۸۵، مرتبه: قاضي مجاهد الإسلام قاسمي صاحب رحمه الله).

(3) The third reason: the way of the Sharīʿah with one who is near death is that Sūrah Yā Sīn should be recited beside him so that death may be made easy and he may be relieved as soon as possible from the agonies of death. It is thus known that creating the means of easing the death of one who is near death is desirable, and removing the apparatus is of the same nature.

Consider what Ibn Kathīr (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

قال بعض العلماء: من خصائص هذه السورة أنها لا تقرأ عند أمر عسير إلا يسره الله تعالى، وكان قراءتها عند الميت لتنزل الرحمة والبركة وليسهل عليه خروج الروح. والله أعلم. قال الإمام أحمد: حدثنا أبو المغيرة حدثنا صفوان كان المشيخة يقولون: إذا قرء ت يعني يسٓ عند الميت خفف الله عنه بها. (تفسير ابن كثير: ۳/٦۱۹).

Al-Durr al-Manthūr states:

أخرج ابن مردويه والديلمي عن أبي الدرداء رضي الله عنه عن النبي ﷺ قال: ما من ميت يقرأ عنده يسٓ إلا هون الله عليه. وأخرج أبو الشيخ في فضائل القرآن والديلمي من حديث أبي ذر رضي الله عنه مثله. وأخرج ابن سعد وأحمد في مسنده عن صفوان بن عمرو قال: كانت المشيخة يقولون

وأخرج البيهقي في شعب الإيمان عن أبي قلابة قال: من قرأ يسٓ غفر له، ومن قرأ ها عند طعام خاف قلته كفاه، ومن قرأها عند ميت هون الله عليه، ومن قرأها عند امرأة عسر عليها ولدها يسر عليها، ومن قرأها فكأنما قرأ القرآن إحدى عشرة مرة، ولكل شيء قلب وقلب القرآن يسٓ

(الدر المنثور: ۷/۳۸، ۳۹).

In Maʿārif al-Qurʾān, Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

It is narrated from Abū Dharr (may Allah be pleased with him) that when Yā Sīn is recited beside a dying person, ease comes to him at the time of his death. (Narrated by al-Daylamī and Ibn Ḥibbān, Maẓharī). (Maʿārif al-Qurʾān: 7/363).

Fatāwā al-Shāmī states:

هو استحسان بعض المتأخرين (أي قراء ة سورة الرعد) لقول جابر رضي الله عنه أنها تهون عليه خروج روحه. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۱۹۱، سعيد).

Al-Talkhīṣ al-Ḥabīr states:

في فتح العزيز شرح الوجيز

استحب بعض التابعين قراء ة سورة الرعد

والمبهم المذكور هو أبو الشعثاء جابر بن زيد صاحب ابن عباس رضي الله عنه، أخرجه أبوبكر المروزي في كتاب الجنائز له وزاد فإن ذلك تخفيف عن الميت. (التلخيص الحبير: ۵/۱۱۳).

(4) The fourth reason: attaching the artificial apparatus is itself an act of kindness, and removing it is the abandonment of kindness, and the principle is: "ترك الإحسان لا يكون إساء ة" — that is, abandoning kindness is not an evil.

Consider what Qawāʿid al-Fiqh states:

ترك الإحسان لا يكون إساء ة: فمن مر بامرأة وصبي في المفازة وهو يقدر على نقله إلى العمران فلم يفعل لم يكن ضامناً شيئاً من بدله. (قواعد الفقه، ص ۷۰).

The decision of al-Majmaʿ al-Fiqhī al-Islāmī:

Al-Majmaʿ al-Fiqhī al-Islāmī considered this subject: whether death can be confirmed by means of definite medical signs, and what the ruling is on removing the life-sustaining apparatus attached to the body of a patient in intensive care.

At the session, the oral and written statements and opinions presented on behalf of the Ministry of Health of Saudi Arabia and by expert physicians were considered. Decision no. (5), dated 3/7/1986 CE, taken on this matter at the session of the Islamic Fiqh Academy, Jeddah, held in Amman, Jordan, was also kept in view.

After deliberating on all aspects of the subject, al-Majmaʿ al-Fiqhī reached the following decisions:

If the functioning of the brain of a patient to whose body life-sustaining apparatus is attached ceases completely, and three expert and well-informed doctors agree that this functioning can no longer be restored, then it is correct to remove the apparatus attached to that patient's body, even though the movement of the heart and the respiratory system are maintained in the patient by reason of that apparatus. The patient's death, however, will be reckoned in the Sharīʿah from the time when, after the removal of the apparatus, the heart and the breathing cease to function. (ʿAṣr-i Ḥāḍir ke Pechīdah Masāʾil kā Sharʿī Ḥal, compiled by Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām Qāsimī (may Allah have mercy on him), p. 185, Idārat al-Qurʾān).
