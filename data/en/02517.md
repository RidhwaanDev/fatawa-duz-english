### TITLE
Eating soy sauce

### QUESTION
Will soy sauce made in the following way be halal or not? Please note that it is used in sauces and the like.

The ingredients of soy sauce:

(1) Wheat. (2) Salt. (3) Water. (4) Soya beans.

The method of manufacture:

(1) The soya beans are first soaked in water and then steamed at a high temperature.

(2) The wheat is roasted at a high temperature and is then ground.

(3) The salt is dissolved in water.

(4) The steamed soya beans and the wheat are mixed together and left for three days so that the base of the soy sauce forms. This base is called shoyu koji.

(5) This base is then mixed with the salt water, and the mixture is called moromi.

(6) The moromi is left for several months so that it ferments of its own accord, as a result of which a soft, solid mass comes into being.

At this stage, when the moromi ferments by itself and turns into alcohol, the alcohol becomes part of that soft solid mass, and in the end (after filtering and so on) as much as three per cent of it may be present in the soy sauce.

(7) After the fermentation, pressure is applied to this soft mass and it is filtered through layers of cloth, so that the soft solid matter is separated out and only the liquid soy sauce remains.

(8) This liquid soy sauce is then boiled at 120 degrees so that any germs in it are destroyed, and it is then sealed in bottles.

What we wish to know is whether such a sauce is halal or not.

### ANSWER
In the case in question, the soy sauce that is finally produced contains three per cent alcohol; but this alcohol has come into being from the mixing of soya beans and wheat, so according to the position of Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) there will be room to consume it. This is because in his view it is permissible to use an intoxicating drink made from something other than the four prohibited drinks so long as it does not intoxicate, and the later scholars have given the fatwa on this position because of the widespread affliction people face in this matter.

Mawlānā Fatḥ Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him) has written in ʿAṭr Hidāyah: the argument that most Western medicines are compounded with alcohol is not sufficient, because wine is that which is designed to produce delight and exhilaration, not something that is lethal like poison. In my own research, the strong essence that is mixed into Western medicine, and which in their terminology they call alcohol, can in no way be wine; for it has been reported to me that it is not something drunk like other beverages, and that if it is consumed it is lethal like poison. Such a thing, then, does not take the ruling of wine. As for the fact that mixing a few drops of it into water and drinking it produces intoxication, that does not make it unlawful before it has been compounded — just like those syrups which can be turned into wine by a slight process of compounding: they are neither unlawful nor impure. (ʿAṭr Hidāyah, p. 96, Maktabah ʿAṣriyyah).

On the face of it this is hard to accept, because what is generally put into medicine is called ethyl alcohol, whose other name is ethanol, and this is what is normally meant when the word "alcohol" is used. In medicines it is used as a solvent, and it is this very substance that produces the intoxication in wine and similar drinks.

Ethanol is defined in the dictionary in the following words:

A COLORLESS VOLATILE FLAMMABLE LIQUID C2 H5 OH THAT IS THE INTOXICATING AGENT IN FERMENTED AND DISTILLED LIQUOURS (AS BEER WINE WHISKY) CALLED ALSO ETHYL ALCOHOL.
(Websters High School Dictionary)

In short, ethanol is a colourless liquid that is able to burn, that quickly turns to vapour and evaporates, and that produces the intoxication in alcoholic drinks.

The Encarta Encyclopedia states:

ETHYL ALCOHOL OR ETHANOL C2 H5 OH, IS A CLEAR COLORLESS LIQUID, WITH A BURNING TASTE AND CHARACTERISTIC AGREEABLE ODOUR. (Microsoft Encarata 2008)

This definition adds one further point: just as ethanol is able to burn, its taste too has a burning quality.

The ethanol that is made for drinking is produced by mixing rice, barley or the like with yeast so that fermentation takes place, which then produces ethanol; the quantity produced, however, is very small, the ethanol content amounting to between 7 and 12 per cent.

Commercial ethanol is 95 per cent ethanol. It is produced by heating and cooling the substance mentioned above in a particular way (distillation) so that the other components separate out, leaving 95 per cent ethanol and 5 per cent water. The water is then removed as well, so that it becomes 100 per cent ethanol.

Research has shown that 100 per cent ethanol cannot be drunk. There is a danger of it destroying the human intestines, and a strong likelihood that the lungs will stop working.

Similarly, even when it is not 100 per cent, the greater the quantity of it in a drink, the greater the harm.

Some scientists have written that using alcohol in small amounts is beneficial and even good for one's health, and that it protects against coronary heart disease. Using it in large quantities over a long period, however, gives rise to a great many illnesses, among them damage to the liver and damage to the heart muscle, and there is a risk of death as well.

The research set out above makes it clear that pure 100 per cent ethanol is harmful to the body and cannot be drunk.

The intoxication found in wine and similar drinks, however, is precisely the effect of this ethanol, without anything else being mixed into it, as its definition shows. It does not have to be compounded with anything else in order to intoxicate. It is therefore not sound to compare it with syrups, because the capacity to intoxicate is present in it even before any compounding. The alcohol used in medicines and in some drinks, on the other hand, is present in a very small quantity and for that reason does not intoxicate, so in the view of Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) there is room to use it; and the later scholars have also given the fatwa accordingly.

Takmilat Fatḥ al-Mulhim states:

وأما غير الأشربة الأربعة، فليست نجسة عند الإمام أبي حنيفة رحمه الله.

وبهذا يتبين حكم الكحول المسكرة (Alcohols) التي عمت بها البلوي اليوم، فإنها تستعمل في كثير منا لأدوية والعطور والمركبات الأخرى، فإنها إن اتخذت من العنب أو التمر فلا سبيل إلى حلتها أو طهارتها، وإن اتخذت من غيرهما فالأمر فيها سهل على مذهب أبي حنيفة رحمه الله، ولا يحرم استعمالها للتداوي أو لأغراض مباحة أخري ما لم تبلغ حد الإسكار، لا نها إنما تستعمل مركبة مع المواد الأخرى، ولا يحكم بنجاستها أخذاً بقول أبي حنيفة رحمه الله.

وإن معظم الكحول التي تستعمل اليوم في الأدوية والعطور وغيرها لا تتخذ من العنب أو التمر، إنما تتخذ من الحبوب أو القشور أو البترول وغيره، كما ذكرنا في باب بيع الخمر من كتاب البيوع، وحينئذٍ هناك فسحة في الأخذ بقول أبي حنيفة رحمه الله عند عموم البلوي. والله أعلم. (تكملة فتح الملهم: ۳/٦۰۸، ط: دار العلوم كراتشي).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

If spirit has been obtained from grapes, raisins or dates, it is impure by the agreement of all. If it has been made from something else, it is pure according to the two shaykhs (may Allah have mercy on them) and impure according to Imam Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him). Research has established that nowadays grapes and dates are not used to make spirit and alcohol, so according to the position of the two shaykhs (may Allah have mercy on them) it is pure. Although the jurists (may Allah have mercy on them) declared the position of Imam Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him) to be the one on which fatwa is given, on account of the corruption of the age, today, in view of the need for medical treatment and the general affliction, the fatwa of purity is given in accordance with the position of the two shaykhs (may Allah have mercy on them). In any case, by the principles of issuing fatwa the position of the two shaykhs (may Allah have mercy on them) is given preference unless something intervenes. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 2/95).
