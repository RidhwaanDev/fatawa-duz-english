### TITLE
The ruling on the heirs of those martyred in an air crash recovering blood-money from an insurance company

### QUESTION
Last year an aircraft crashed in a certain country and a great many people were martyred. According to international law the heirs are to receive 50 lakh rupees for each person martyred. This sum is not paid by the owners of the aircraft; rather the aircraft company will obtain it from an insurance company and pay it over (the insurance company is non-Muslim and the owners of the aircraft are Muslims), and under international law it is obligatory for every aircraft company to take out insurance. The question now is whether it is lawful in the Sacred Law to take this money or not. After the death of the head of the household, this money is a means of livelihood for the heirs.

### ANSWER
In the case in question, being killed by falling from an aircraft carries the ruling of unintentional homicide (qatl khaṭaʾ), and in unintentional homicide the responsibility for the blood-money falls in principle upon the ʿāqilah. But in our time, since the system of the ʿāqilah no longer exists, the insurance company that bears liability for the compensation stands in the place of the ʿāqilah, because it has accepted the responsibility and a contract has been concluded between the owners of the aircraft and the company; under that contract the company has become responsible for the compensation. It is true that the following objection may be raised against this: that the dealing between the aircraft owners and the insurance company is an interest-based one, and that therefore, if the aircraft owners recover the money from them and give it to Muslims, it should not be correct to take more than the amount the aircraft owners have paid in. The answer to this is that if the insurance company pays the heirs of the martyrs directly, there ought to be room for it, since no dealing has taken place between them and the insurance company. In any case, the opinion of other honourable muftis should also be sought.

Liability is established by reason of the contract. Consider what the author of Hidāyah says:

فالضمان عليه أي على الوكيل بالصلح كالوكيل بالنكاح إلا أن يضمنه لأنه حينئذٍ هو مؤاخذ بعقد الضمان لا بعقد الصلح. (الهداية: ۳/۲۵۰، باب التبرع بالصلح).
والخطأ على نوعين:

وموجب ذلك الكفارة والدية على العاقلة. (الهداية: ٤/ ۵٦۱، ط: شركة علمية).

Al-Jināyāt fī al-Fiqh al-Islāmī records:

يشترط أن يقع من الجاني أو بسبب منه، فعل على المجني عليه، سواء أكان الجاني أراد الفعل وقصده، كما لو أراد أن يرمي صيداً، فأصاب إنساناً، أو وقع الفعل نتيجة إهماله وعدم احتياطه، دون أن يقصِده، كمن ألقي ماء ساخناً من مكان عالٍ، ولم يري المجني عليه، فوقع عليه فمات. والعقوبة الأصلية الأولي: الدية

واتفق الفقهاء على أن دية القتل الخطأ على العاقلة، مؤجلة في ثلاث سنين

(الجنايات في الفقه الإسلامي، ص ۲۰۳).
والعاقلة أهل الديوان ان كان القاتل من أهل الديوان

واهل الديوان أهل الرأيات

لأن العقل كان على أهل النصرة وقد كانت بأنواع بالقرابة والحلف والولاء والعد

ولهذا قالوا لوكان اليوم قوم تناصرهم بالحرف فعاقلتهم أهل الحرفة. (الهداية: ٤/۶۴۵، شركة علمية).
قال الإمام أبو حنيفة رحمه الله: إن العاقلة هم الذين يتناصر بهم القاتل

ولنا قضية عمر رضي الله عنه، فالحاصل أن قضاء عمر رضي الله عنه بمحضر من الصحابة دل على أن الحكم كان مناطه النصرة فيتغير بتغيره ويمكن أن يقال في عصرنا: أن التناصر أصبح للعمال بوفاتهم الذي يسمي "تريد يونين" فينبغي أن تكون عاقلة عامل وفاقه

(تكملة فتح الملهم: ۲/۳۷۹، كراچي).

Islāmī Fiqh states:

If a motor car or a train should collide by chance, or an aircraft should meet with an accident, and one person or a great many people lose their lives, the responsibility for that loss of life will not fall upon the driver or the pilot but upon the company or the government. It is for this reason that the jurists place the responsibility for the blood-money (diyah) in such chance accidents upon the ʿāqilah. In the earliest period of Islam the people of the dīwān — that is, those entered in the register kept for a single class of people — were all one another's ʿāqilah. After that the responsibility was placed upon the family, the tribe, the clan and so forth. But now this state of affairs too has changed, and the responsibility should now fall upon the company, the factory or the government. The jurists have treated this as unintentional homicide. The company, the factory owner or the government may indeed punish the driver or the pilot, but they cannot place upon them the responsibility for the diyah, that is, the blood-money. In any case these are new questions, and the scholars need to give them further consideration. (Islāmī Fiqh, vol. 2, p. 643, under the heading "The ruling on inanimate conveyances".)

Fatāwā ʿUthmānī states:

In principle the compensation was binding upon the truck driver, and there was a right to claim only as much as the blood-money and no more; but if the unintentional killer himself pays more, it is permissible to take it. As for recovering it from an insurance company: up to the limit of whatever premium the insured has so far paid in under any head, recovering it is permissible without any shadow of doubt. As for anything beyond that, this interpretation is possible: that the insurance company be regarded as standing in the place of his ʿāqilah. The impermissible contract that was made in order to make it the ʿāqilah is the act of the two parties themselves, for which they themselves are answerable; but once it has become, by custom and by law, the substitute for the ʿāqilah, then there appears to be room for recovering from it as well. And although the money of an insurance company is, on account of its many invalid contracts, strongly presumed to be unlawful, there nevertheless appears to be room for it on the ground that unbelievers at war are not addressed by the subsidiary rulings. Still, there is no complete certainty on this point; let it be given further thought, and let other scholars also be consulted. (Fatāwā ʿUthmānī: 3/544.)

The proper use of unlawful wealth is in principle to convey it to its owners; but if the owner's whereabouts are not known, it should be given away in charity on his behalf.

قال العلامة الشامي رحمه الله: ويردونها على أربابها إن عرفوهم وإلا تصدقوا بها لأن سبيل الكسب الخبيث التصدق إذا تعذر الرد على صاحبه. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۳۸۵، سعيد).

But if interest money should come into the hands of an unbeliever, the unbeliever becomes the owner of that money. (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 5/393.)

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states: Is it permissible or not to take money in the case of an accident?

The blood-money is, in the Sacred Law, binding upon the ʿāqilah; but in this age it is very difficult to impose it upon the ʿāqilah, for that would require a qadi appointed under the Sacred Law who has the power to enforce his rulings. In our own time, so far as we know, the courts have such money paid by the insurance company. If the insurance company does pay the money, then — since the income of an insurance company is derived for the most part from interest-based business — if the heirs are extremely needy and in want, there is room for them to take the money from the insurance company and use as much of it as they need, and to give whatever is over and above their need to the poor; and if they are not needy, then the entire sum should be given to the poor and the destitute. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 9/31, Dār al-Ishāʿat.) But Muftī ʿAbd al-Raḥīm Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) has written the opposite of what has been set down above.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
