### TITLE
Rent from a liquor shop and a cinema in a shopping mall

### QUESTION
If there is one liquor shop in a mall and the remaining shops deal in other goods, may a Muslim take the rent from it or not? It should also be borne in mind that most of the mall's income — 80 or 90 per cent, say — is lawful, and only 10 per cent is rent from liquor, a cinema and the like. Is this 10 per cent of the rent lawful or unlawful? May the Muslim owner take it or not? بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, our imams differ over renting out a shop or a building for liquor, a cinema and other such sinful purposes, and over taking the rental income from it. The two disciples (may Allah have mercy on them) say that it is not correct, since it is helping in sin, while Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) says that such a contract of hire is sound and that taking the rent for it is permissible as well, because the contract of hire is concluded upon the use of the shop or building, and there is no sin in that. The selling of liquor is the act of one who acts by his own free choice; the owner of the property did not tell the tenant to sell liquor in it. So if it was known beforehand that the tenant would sell liquor, then to let it out for that purpose is strictly disliked and is cooperation in sin; but if the shop was let out for some other purpose and the tenant then sells liquor in it, it is mildly disliked.

See what al-Mabsūṭ states:

ولا بأس بأن يؤاجر المسلم داراً من الذمي ليسكنها، فإن شرب فيها الخمر أو عبد فيها الصليب أو دخل فيها الخنازير لم يلحق المسلم فيه إثم في شيء من ذلك لأنه لم يؤاجرها لذلك والمعصية في فعل المستأجر دون قصد رب الدار فلا إثم على رب الدار في ذلك. (المبسوط للإمام السرخسي رحمه الله: ۱٦/۳۰۹، بيروت).

Fatāwā Bazzāziyyah states:

ولو آجر نفسه لحمل الخمر قال الإمام: لا يكره وعلى قولهما يكره، لأن التصرف في الخمر حرام، وكذا كل موضع تعلقت المعصية بفعل فاعل مختار، كما إذا آجر منزله ليتخذ بيعة أو كنيسة أو بيت نار يطيب له. (الفتاوى البزازية على هامش الهندية: ۵/۱۲۵، العاشر في الحظر والإباحة).

Khulāṣat al-Fatāwā states:

رجل آجر بيتاً ليتخذ فيه نار أو بيعة أو كنيسة أو يباع فيه الخمر فلا باس به، وكذا كل موضع تعلقت المعصية بفعل فاعل مختار. (خلاصة الفتاوى: ٤/٤۷٦، كتاب الكراهية).

(كذا في الهداية: ۳/٤۷۲، كتاب الكراهية، والكفاية: ۸/۳

وفي العيني شرح الكنز: ۲/۳۸٤، وكذا في الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٦/۳۹۲، سعيد، وعزيز الفتاوى: ۱/٦۳۸).

On this matter there is an apparent difference among the fatawa of our elders (may Allah have mercy on them).

Hazrat Mawlānā Rashīd Aḥmad Gangohī says:

To give a house to such a person is not correct according to the view of the two disciples (may Allah have mercy on them). From the view of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah it appears to be permissible, since renting out a house is not a sin; the fatwa, however, is that he should not rent it out, because it is helping in sin. (فتاوی رشیدیہ، ص ۵۰۲).

Hazrat Mufti Kifāyatullāh (may Allah have mercy on him) says that if the seller of liquor is not a Muslim, and the Muslim population of that settlement is not large but the inhabitants are mostly disbelievers, then it is permissible to rent a shop to the disbelieving liquor seller. (کفایت المفتی: ۷/۳٤۰).

Hazrat Mawlānā Mufti Rashīd Aḥmad Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) says that giving a shop to a disbeliever is mildly disliked, while giving one to a Muslim is strictly disliked. (احسن الفتاوی: ٦/۵٤٦).

The words of Hazrat Mawlānā Rashīd Aḥmad Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) bring to light a way of reconciling the fatawa of the elders (may Allah have mercy on them).

In short, if a Muslim runs the liquor shop, the rent of that shop should be given away in charity; but if a non-Muslim runs the liquor shop, there is room to act upon the view of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him).
