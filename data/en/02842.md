### TITLE
The ruling on the learned scholars playing cricket and listening to the commentary

### QUESTION
Is it correct for the noble scholars and the eminent muftīs to play cricket, to watch it on television and to travel for it, or not? And what is the ruling on listening to the commentary? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
For the noble scholars to play cricket and to travel for it is not at all fitting; they ought to keep away from it. Watching the game of cricket on television is not correct. Likewise it is not correct for the religious class to involve themselves in vain things such as the commentary and thereby to adopt a resemblance to the heedless. Over and above this, there is in it an encouragement of the present-day cult of sport, which is forbidden.

عن عبد الله بن عبد الرحمن بن أبي الحسن أن رسول الله ﷺ قال: إن الله ليدخل بالسهم الواحد ثلاثة الجنة صانعه يحتسب في صنعته الخير والرامي والممد به قال: ارموا واركبوا ولأن ترموا أحب إلى من أن تركبوا، كل ما يلهو به المسلم باطل إلا رميه بقوس وتأديبه فرسه وملاعبته أهله فإنهن من الحق. (رواه الترمذي: ۱/۲۹۳).

(وأيضا رواه أبو داود، ص ۳٤۰، وابن ماجه: ۲/۲۰۲).

In Imdād al-Muftīn it is said:

Question: What is the position on playing the English games of tennis, football, cricket and other games of this kind?

The answer:

قال في الدر المختار من الكراهية: وكره كل لهو لقوله عليه الصلاة والسلام: كل لهو المسلم حرام إلا ثلاثة

الخ. قال الشامي: أي كل لعب وعبث إلى قوله والمزمار والصبخ والبوق فإنها كلها مكروهة، لأنها زي الكفار. (شامي: ۵/۲۷۵، ط: مصري) وفي القهستاني عن الملتقط: من لعب بالصولجان يريد الفروسة يجوز وعن الجواهر قد جاء الأثر في رخصة المصارعة لتحصيل القدرة على المقاتلة دون التلهي فإنه مكروه، وفي الدر المختار: والمصارعة ليست ببدعة إلا للتلهي فتكره.

وفي الشامي: أقول: قدمنا عن القهستاني في جواز اللعب بالصولجان وهو الكرة للفروسة وفي جواز المسابقة بالطير عندنا نظر وكذا في جواز معرفة ما في اليد واللعب بالمأثم فإنه لهو مجرد، (شامي)

From the aḥādīth that have come concerning this matter, and likewise from the juristic passages set out above, the following details regarding games are derived. (a) A game from which no considerable religious or worldly benefit is intended is impermissible, and it is that which the ḥadīth refers to. (b) A game from which a considerable religious or worldly benefit is intended is permissible, on condition that no matter contrary to the Sacred Law is mixed with it — and among the matters contrary to the Sacred Law is resemblance to the disbelievers. (c) A game from which some religious or worldly benefit is intended, but with which some impermissible matter contrary to the Sacred Law becomes mixed, likewise becomes impermissible; or a game that is regarded as peculiar to a particular nation will also be impermissible, التشبه ممنوع. Nowadays, since these conditions are generally not met in the existing games, they are declared impermissible. (Imdād al-Muftīn: 2/831, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

In Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal it is said:

There are three conditions for a game to be permissible. (1) The first is that what is intended by the game should be merely exercise or recreation, and that the game itself should not be made an independent objective.

(2) The second is that the game should also be permissible in itself, there being nothing impermissible in that game. (3) The third is that no shortcoming or heedlessness in the religious obligations should arise from it.

If this standard is kept in view, the great majority of them will appear impermissible and wrong. For our young men who are fond of sport, sport has become such a beloved occupation that, set against it, they have neither any thought for the religious obligations nor any attention for their studies; and such a taste for sport has been created that our youth seem to have been born only to play, and as though there were no other purpose to life at all. Who can call such a game permissible? (Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal: 8/407, new edition).

In Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah it is said:

In the present age cricket has become such a game that matters contrary to the Sacred Law are generally found in it: letting the prayers be missed; wagering and gambling upon it; its being taken up by the openly sinful, the wicked and heedless sort of people. Heedlessness has reached such a limit that not only the days but even the nights are now spent absorbed in it. At the time of a cricket match young girls and women gather in the ground, and who knows how many moral and legal evils have entered into it. Experience shows that the more the passion for it and absorption in it increase, the more heedlessness goes on increasing; night and day only the concern for it is uppermost, so that even after coming to the mosque, while performing wuḍūʾ and after finishing wuḍūʾ — and many enthusiasts even in the congregation hall — remain occupied in talking about it. It has gone so far that if on some occasion the commentary of a match is being broadcast during the tarāwīḥ in the blessed month of Ramaḍān, many of its enthusiasts sacrifice the tarāwīḥ for it. Fireworks are set off over winning and losing, in which, along with resembling another nation, there is also a wasting of wealth; and often this behaviour becomes the cause of communal strife, and the Muslims suffer loss of life and property. Seeing all these circumstances, how can such a game now be called permissible? (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 7/277, published by Maktabah Raḥīmiyyah).

In Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah it is said:

In cricket, hockey and the like there is permission in view of a sound purpose; all of these are not simply forbidden. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 19/534, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

In sum, in the game of cricket in itself there is nothing objectionable; if it is played with the intention of exercise, while adhering to the conditions mentioned above, then there is no harm in it and it is permitted. But the scholars ought to keep away from games of this kind — especially since in our own times shortcomings in the obligatory duties are occurring on account of it, rights are being wasted, the players are being presented as heroes, love of them has seeped into the hearts of the common people, who are devoted to their every gesture, and great quarrels arise over winning and losing. Moreover, nowadays the one-day (single-day matches) has also become the custom; these are mostly played on Friday, and the whole of the blessed day of Friday is wasted in play and in watching it. The game goes on at the very time of the Friday prayer, and not only the players but thousands of spectators abandon the Friday prayer and take upon their own heads the ruin of this world and the next. Therefore, on account of these external factors, one ought to keep away from this game entirely.

Note: some scholars do not even concede that there is exercise in this game. Thus they say that some remain seated and their turn does not come in the game, while some become so exhausted that they are left fit for no work, religious or worldly.

In the same way, watching cricket on television is also impermissible. All these evils are present in it too, and over and above that the objectionableness increases because of the advertisements of naked and half-naked women.

In Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah it is said:

For a person who occupies the lofty office of the imāmate, to occupy himself with a notorious, useless and vain game of this kind, to take an interest in it and to listen to the commentary, is utterly unbecoming of his station; a resemblance to the heedless is also entailed, and the imām's dignity is diminished in the eyes of the people. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 7/278, published by Maktabah Raḥīmiyyah, Rānder).
