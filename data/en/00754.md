### TITLE
A man's claim that his parents' town is also his home town

### QUESTION
A man has moved with his family to the place where he works, but his parents live somewhere else. He regards his parents' town as his home town as well. Is this correct or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, if the man has not made his place of work his home town but merely intends to stay there, and he treats the place where his parents live as his home town, then that is correct, because a home town is not cancelled by a place of temporary residence. If he counts both places as home towns, certain conditions apply: for example, that he married in the second place and his wife lives there; or that he has a house in both places with his belongings there, and that he intends to go on living there and has not moved away, and so on.

As the question stands, however, it appears that for this man the place of work has the status of a home town, and his parents' place is not his home town, because the mere presence of his parents is not enough to make a place a home town.

Consider what Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

ثم الوطن الأصلي يجوز أن يكون واحداً أو أكثر من ذلك بأن كان له أهل ودار في بلدتين أو أكثر ولم يكن من نيته الخروج منها. (بدائع الصنائع: ۱/۱۰۳، سعيد)

Al-Hidāyah states:

ومن كان له وطن فانتقل منه واستوطن غيره ثم سافر فدخل وطنه الأول قصر لأنه لم يبق وطناً له ألا تري أنه عليه الصلاة والسلام بعد الهجرة عد نفسه بمكة من المسافرين. (هداية: ۱/۱٦۷)

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

فالوطن الأصلي ينتقض بمثله لا غير وهو أن يتوطن الإنسان في بلدة أخري وينقل الأهل إليها من بلدته فيخرج الأول من أن يكون وطناً أصلياً له حتى لو دخل فيه مسافراً لا تصير صلاته أربعاً وأصله أن رسول الله ﷺ والمهاجرين من أصحابه ثكانوا من أهل مكة وكان لهم بها أوطان أصلية ثم لما هاجروا وتوطنوا بالمدينة وجعلوها داراً لا نفسهم انتقض وطنهم الأصلي بمكة حتى كانوا إذا أتوا مكة يصلون صلاة المسافرين حتى قال النبي ﷺ حين صلى بهم أتموا يا أهل مكة صلاتكم فإنا قوم سفر. (بدائع الصنائع: ۱/۱۰۳، سعيد. وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱٤۲. والبحر الرائق: ۲/۱۳٦، باب المسافر، الماجدية)

Imdād al-Aḥkām states:

(1) A home town is one in which a person lives with his family and from which he does not intend to move away or to move his family.

(2) When a person intends to settle in another place, his first home town is not cancelled unless he moves his family there.

(3) A person may have several home towns. Indeed, if a man contracts four marriages in four towns and keeps each wife in her own town, he will have four home towns.

(4) In a town where a man's family lives permanently, whether in a rented house or in a house of his own, he will no longer shorten his prayers when he arrives there as a traveller; he will have to pray in full.

(5) Simply contracting a marriage in a town does not make it one's home town; the condition is that one keeps one's family there and does not move them away. (امداد الاحکام: ۱/٦۹٥، فصل فی صلاۃ المسافر، کراچی)

In sum: these points show that if a person calls a place his home town while his family does not live there, then to all appearances it is not his home town. Parents are not included in "family" here; "family" means one's wife and children.
