### TITLE
Ruling on stipulating that the first ṭalāq belongs to the husband, the second to the wife and the third to the husband

### QUESTION
At the time of the nikāḥ a husband and wife stipulated that the right to give the first ṭalāq would belong to the husband, that the second ṭalāq would be in the wife's hands, and that the right to the third ṭalāq would belong to the husband. Is a delegation valid in this order or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question the delegation of one ṭalāq to the woman is sound and valid, and she may now bring one ṭalāq into effect upon herself. But this by no means implies that only two ṭalāqs are left with the husband; rather, as before, the husband holds all three ṭalāqs, so that if he pronounces three ṭalāqs, all three will take effect upon his wife.

Since, however, a particular order was stipulated in the case described, that order must be observed: the wife will have the right of one ṭalāq only after the husband has given one ṭalāq, and before that she will have no right to bring a ṭalāq into effect.

Majmūʿah Qawānīn Islāmī states: If the husband, while mentioning the number of ṭalāqs or their description, tied the delegation or the appointment of an agent to the condition of the woman's or the agent's wish, then the number or description he stated must be adhered to; if it is departed from, no ṭalāq will take effect.

قال في الدر: طلقي نفسك ثلاثاً إن شئت فطلقت واحدة وكذا عكسه لا يقع فيهما لاشتراط الموافقة لفظاً (قوله لاشتراط الموافقة لفظاً
ان اشتراط الموافقة لفظاً خاص بالمعلق بالمشيئة فيكون تعليقاً للإتيان بصورة اللفظ. "الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۳/۳۳٤، فصل في المشيئة، سعيد. وهكذا في الهداية على فتح القدير: ۳/۱۱۸".
(مجموعہ قوانین اسلامی: ۱۵۳، دفعہ ٤۰) ۔

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states: One form of delegation is that the ṭalāq is delegated in the offer and acceptance of the marriage itself, and this is valid. It is necessary, however, that the offer be made by the woman and be tied to the delegation of ṭalāq to her, and that the man accept it. If the offer comes from the man and he makes the delegation along with his offer, and the woman accepts, it is of no effect. Khulāṣat al-Fatāwā states:

وعلى هذا لو تزوج امرأة على أنها طالق أو على أن أمرها بيدها تطلق نفسها كلما تريد لا يقع الطلاق ولا يصير الأمر بيدها ولو بدأت المرأة فقالت: زوجت نفسي منك على أني طالق أو على أن أمري بيدي أطلق نفسي كلما أريد فقال الزوج: قبلت، وقع الطلاق وصار الأمر بيدها. (خلاصة الفتاوى: ۲/۲۹، ط: المكتبة الرشيدية).

Ibn Nujaym quoted this from al-Khulāṣah (البحر الرائق: ۳/۳۱۸), and ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) quoted it in turn from Ibn Nujaym in (رد المحتار). Fatāwā Bazzāziyyah also mentions this same form of delegated ṭalāq, subject to certain restrictions and limits, as follows:

إذا خافت المرأة أنه إذا تزوجها لا يجعل الأمر بيدها بعد التزوج تقول زوجت نفسي منك بكذا على أن أمري بيدي، أطلق نفسي منك بائناً متي شئت كلما ضربتني بغير جناية أو تزوجت على أخري أو اشتريت أو غبت عني سنة. (البزازية: ٤/۲۳٤)

This is binding upon the husband. (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۳/٤٦، اشتراط فی النکاح) ۔

Majmūʿah Qawānīn Islāmī states: A delegation or an appointment of an agent does not put an end to the husband's own right of ṭalāq. (مجموعہ قوانین اسلامی: ۱۵٤) ۔

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states: The right of ṭalāq that the Sharia has given to men passes to the woman as a result of a delegation; but there is no danger of the purposes of the Sharia being lost through this transfer of the right, because even after the woman has been given the right, the husband's own right to bring a ṭalāq into effect is not extinguished but remains exactly as it was before. (جدید فقہی مباحث: ۱۱/ ۳۵۳) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
