### TITLE
Passing the mīqāt without iḥrām when intending to stop in Jeddah for a few hours

### QUESTION
If a person decides that he will stop for a few hours in Jeddah and only then enter the Sacred Precinct, may he pass the mīqāt without iḥrām or not? That is, will it be valid for him to put on iḥrām in Jeddah, or will a dam be due from him?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, since the aircraft passes over the mīqāt or level with it, iḥrām must be put on before coming level with the mīqāt. If someone forms the intention of staying a day or a few hours in Jeddah and so puts off his iḥrām until Jeddah, that is not correct; it is merely a legal device. A device may be resorted to at a time of need in order to avoid something forbidden or disliked, but a device by which a ruling of the sharia is nullified is not correct. It is like the device of the people of the Sabbath, against which a severe warning is recorded in the Noble Qur'an. Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muftī Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) has written in معارف القرآن: ۷/۵۲۳: "It must be remembered, however, that devices of this kind are permissible only so long as they are not made a means of nullifying the purposes of the sharia."

As for a person who goes from here to Madinah and then, forming the intention of Umrah, puts on iḥrām from there, that is no device at all. In the first place, the journey to Madinah is an aim in its own right; indeed there are two aims in Madinah. (1) The Prophet's Mosque,

كما قال النبي ﷺ لا تشد الرحال إلا إلى ثلاثة مساجد

الخ. (2) Visiting the blessed grave of the Prophet ﷺ,

كما روي عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: "من زار قبري وجبت له شفاعتي". (رواه ابن خزيمة في صحيحه والدارقطني والبيهقي وآخرون وإسناده حسن كما في آثار السنن للعلامة النيموي ص ۳۳۹)

And even supposing that Madinah is not made the aim, and that only Umrah or only Hajj is intended, the upshot is still that this person wishes to travel to Mecca by way of Madinah and to set out from a mīqāt. Two mīqāts will then lie on his route: one farther from Mecca and one nearer to Mecca; and such a person may choose the mīqāt that is nearer to Mecca, کما ہو مذکور فی کتب الفقہ ۔

As for the ruling the jurists have written, that if a person intends to go to some place in the Ḥill he may travel there without iḥrām, this device is of use only to a person who genuinely intends that place in the first instance.

كما قال العلامة الشامي: لكن لا تتم الحيلة إلا إذا كان قصده لموضع من الحل قصداً أولياً كما قررناه. (الشامي: ۲/٤۷۷، سعيد).

In any case, devices of this sort should be avoided on this important and blessed journey. If, however, some Hanafi driver has to travel there every day and can find no opportunity for Umrah, then that is indeed a question that deserves consideration.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
