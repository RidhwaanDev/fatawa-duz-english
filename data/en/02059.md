### TITLE
Taking out insurance

### QUESTION
What do the scholars of the religion say about this matter: a jamāʿat owns a number of houses. Is it permissible to insure those houses, and likewise the buildings of a school, a madrasah and mosques, or not? If it is not permissible, what is the reason for its impermissibility? And if someone has already taken out such insurance, what is the ruling on that?

### ANSWER
Given the foundations upon which the transaction of insurance rests in our time, it is not permissible, in the light of the sharia perspective and its principles and rules, to insure property or an estate of any kind. The ruling of impermissibility is due to the presence of two forbidden elements whose prohibition is established and plainly manifest from the decisive texts of the Book and the Sunnah and from the consensus of the ummah — namely, the presence of ribā (interest) and qimār (gambling). It is on account of these that this transaction is impermissible.

Ribā, because from the point of view of fiqh the money that is paid to the insurance company has the status of a loan, and the profits that come with it when it is returned are, as it were, a consideration for the term that was fixed for the repayment of that loan. Although the profit that the insurance company pays along with the principal is called a "bonus", its reality is that of interest, and changing the name cannot change the reality. Concerning this very point Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

"It is obvious that the reality of a transaction does not change merely by changing its name; the profits of an insurance company undoubtedly fall within the definition of interest and ribā." (Jawāhir al-Fiqh: 2/181).

And there are countless decisive texts establishing the prohibition of ribā.

The second corrupting element in it is the presence of qimār. This is because the reality of gambling is that there is wealth on both sides and that gain and loss are suspended upon something undetermined and unknown; this is what the jurists (may Allah have mercy on them) express as khaṭar and mukhāṭarah (risk and risk-taking). The principle that the scholars of the ummah have laid down concerning gambling is this:

تعليق الملك على الخطر والمال في الجانبين. (جواهر الفقه: ۲/۲۲۷، وإمداد الفتاوى: ۳/۱٦۱).

Thus Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ al-Rāzī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

لا خلاف بين أهل العلم في تحريم القمار وإن المخاطرة من القمار قال ابن عباس رضي الله عنه: إن المخاطرة قمار وإن أهل الجاهلية كانوا يخاطرون على المال والزوجة وقد كان ذلك مباحاً إلى أن ورد تحريمه. (أحكام القرآن: ۱/۳۲۹، باب تحريم الميسر، وكذا في أحكام القرآن للتهانوى رحمه الله: ۱/۳۹۳).

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

وسمي القمار قماراً لأن كل واحد من المقامرين ممن يجوز أن يذهب ماله إلى صاحبه، ويجوز أن يستفيد مال صاحبه، وهو حرام بالنص. (رد المحتار: ٦/٤۰۳، كتاب الحظر والإباحة، سعيد).

Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Maʿārif al-Qurʾān:

All the Companions and the Successors are agreed that every form of qimār, that is, gambling, is included in maysir and that all of it is forbidden. Ibn Kathīr (may Allah have mercy on him) in his commentary, and al-Jaṣṣāṣ (may Allah have mercy on him) in Aḥkām al-Qurʾān, have transmitted that the commentator of the Qurʾān Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him), Ibn ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him), Qatādah (may Allah be pleased with him), Muʿāwiyah ibn Ṣāliḥ (may Allah be pleased with him), ʿAṭāʾ (may Allah have mercy on him) and Ṭāwūs (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

الميسر القمار حتى لعب الصبيان بالكعاب والجوز،

That is, gambling of every kind is maysir, even the play of children with knucklebones and walnuts and the like. (Maʿārif al-Qurʾān: 1/532).

The jurist Abū al-Layth al-Samarqandī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in explaining maysir:

وقال عطاء ومجاهد: الميسر القمار كله حتى لعب الصبيان بالجوز والكعاب. (تفسير السمرقندي: ۱/۲۰۳).

In an insurance company likewise, whether or not the benefit accruing on the money in the event of an accident is obtained hangs upon risk, so this too falls under gambling; and there are decisive texts establishing the prohibition of gambling.

Consider what the Noble Qurʾān states:

﴿إنما الخمر والميسر والأنصاب والأزلام رجس من عمل الشيطان فاجتنبوه لعلكم تفلحون﴾. (المائدة، الآية: ۹۰).

The Imām of Dār al-Hijrah, Ḥaḍrat Mālik ibn Anas (may Allah have mercy on him), gives an example of a transaction of this kind and records its ruling:

أن يعمد الرجل إلى الرجل قد ضلت راحلته أو دابته أو غلامه وثمن هذه الأشياء خمسون دينار فيقول: أنا آخذها منك بعشرين ديناراً فإن وجدها المبتاع ذهب من مال البائع بثلاثين ديناراً وإن لم يجدها ذهب البائع منه بعشرين ديناراً وهما لا يدريان كيف يكون حالهما في ذلك ولا يدريان أيضاً إذا وجدت تلك الضالة كيف تؤخذ وما حدث فيها من أمر الله مما يكون فيه نقصها وزيادتها فهذا أعظم المخاطرة. (المدونة الكبرى: ۳/۲۵٤، كتاب الغرر، دار الفكر).

That is, a man goes to another man whose riding camel or other animal or slave has gone missing, the value of these things being, say, fifty dinars, and says to him: "I will take it from you for twenty dinars." Now if the buyer finds the lost item, the owner has lost thirty dinars from his wealth; and if he does not find it, the owner has taken twenty dinars from him for nothing. Neither of the two knows, at the time of the transaction, what their situation will be — whether the item will be found or not; and if it is found, they do not know in what state it will be received, nor what increase or decrease has befallen it by the decree of Allah. To enter into such a transaction is the gravest kind of risk and deception.

What this example teaches is that a transaction in which the outcome may turn out either well or badly is not valid in the sharia; and the transaction of insurance is exactly of this kind.

In sum, an insurance company is an interest-based company. Depositing money with it, and insuring the property of a mosque or a madrasah or any building belonging to the jamāʿat, is not permissible in the sharia. If a jamāʿat has mistakenly taken out insurance, it should take back only the money it deposited and must on no account take anything in excess of that.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
