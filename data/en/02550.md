### TITLE
Serving the ʿĀshūrāʾ meal at night

### QUESTION
People generally fast on the Day of ʿĀshūrāʾ, so the meal is served at night. If food is served on the Day of ʿĀshūrāʾ after Maghrib, will the blessings and merit be attained or not?

### ANSWER
The wording of the noble hadith is as follows:

"من وسع على عياله يوم عاشوراء وسع الله عليه سائر السنة"

Since fasting during the day is recommended, and in matters of eating and drinking the night follows the day that precedes it — for the ifṭār meal is the food of that day, though it is eaten after Maghrib — the night meal follows the preceding day. If someone were to say:

"والله أكلت العشاء يوم الخميس"

and he had eaten after Maghrib on Thursday, he would not by custom be held to have broken his oath. A meal after Maghrib is therefore also included among the recommended acts of the day.

Moreover, there are two meals in a day: ghadāʾ and ʿashāʾ. Ghadāʾ is the name for the morning meal and ʿashāʾ for the evening meal, even if the latter is eaten after Maghrib.

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī states:

وإن أبابكر تعشي عند النبي ﷺ ثم لبث حيث صليت العشاء ثم رجع فلبث حتى تعشي النبي ﷺ. (رواه البخاري: ۱/ ۸۵).

The hadith above mentions that Sayyidunā Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) ate the evening meal — which is normally eaten after ʿAṣr — after the ʿIshāʾ prayer. This makes it clear that even when the evening meal is eaten at night it is still counted as the food of that day. That merit is therefore present in eating after Maghrib as well.

An objection and its answer:

Some say that the noble hadith —

"من وسع على عياله يوم عاشوراء وسع الله عليه سائر سنته"

— means that whoever is generous with his family on the Day of ʿĀshūrāʾ, Allah Most High will be generous with him for the whole year. The implication of this hadith is that the Day of ʿĀshūrāʾ is a day of eating and drinking, whereas in the light of authentic hadith the Day of ʿĀshūrāʾ is a day of fasting. What form of reconciliation, then, is possible between these two narrations?

The answer: (1) The first answer is that some of its narrations mention generosity in maintenance (nafaqah), which one may give to one's family in cash or in kind. Consider:

من وسع على عياله في النفقة يوم عاشوراء

الخ. (مشكاة المصابيح، باب فضل الصدقة: ۱/۱۷۰، ط: قديمي كتب خانه)

Here the word nafaqah occurs, and the best gift is that one give something in cash or in kind, which can be of use at any time.

(2) The second answer is that "day" in "the Day of ʿĀshūrāʾ" carries two meanings: (a) from sunrise to sunset; (b) the full twenty-four-hour day. In the view of the Sharīʿah, Friday does not begin at sunrise but runs from sunset on Thursday to sunset on Friday. Hence whoever passes away on the night of Friday or on the day of Friday attains the merit of Friday in either case, and the same holds for Monday. So if a person feeds his family on the night of the tenth, which precedes ʿĀshūrāʾ, that too falls under this hadith, and the promise of expansion applies to him as well.

This meaning is supported by the following verses of ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Ḥabīb al-Mālikī:

لا تنس لا ينسك الرحمن عاشوراء

٭

واذكره لا زلت في الأخيار مذكوراً

قال الرسول صلاة الله تشمله

٭

قولاً عليه وجدنا الحق والنوراً

من بات في ليل عاشوراء ذا سعة

٭

يكن بعيشه في الحول محبوراً

فارغب فديتك فيما فيه رغبنا

٭

خير الوري كلهم حياً ومقبوراً

Translation: Do not forget ʿĀshūrāʾ — the Most Merciful, who is full of mercy, will not forget you.

Remember Allah Most High, and you will always be renowned and remembered among the God-fearing.

The Messenger of Allah, whom the mercy of Allah Most High encompasses,

uttered a saying in which we found truth and light.

Whoever is generous on the night of ʿĀshūrāʾ,

his livelihood will be adorned and pleasant for the whole year.

May I be sacrificed for you: desire that in which we have been encouraged

by that Prophet who is the best of all people, in life and in death.

In the above verses generosity is spoken of as being at night, and the poet is one of the imams of the Mālikīs.

(3) The third answer is that even if generosity means providing food and drink, and "day" means the daytime itself, then for those who have not reached puberty or who are not fasting, generosity has no connection with fasting at all.

(4) The fourth answer is that generosity means arranging the means of generosity, even if the eating and drinking take place on the following night.

(5) The fifth answer is the one given at the outset, that the night follows the day that precedes it.

In short, the Sharīʿah is a mercy. So in the light of these answers, whether a person gives cash or goods, or serves food on the night of ʿĀshūrāʾ, or gives to a child and the like during the day, or cooks on the Day of ʿĀshūrāʾ and serves it at night, the hadith will encompass all of these. For more, see: (al-Yawāqīt al-Ghāliyah, p. 207, discourses of Shaykh Muḥammad Yūnus, Shaykh al-Ḥadīth at Jāmiʿah Saharanpur).
