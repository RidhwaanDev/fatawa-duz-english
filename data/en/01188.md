### TITLE
Iḥrām for those who come to Jeddah by sea

### QUESTION
If a person travels from India or Pakistan to Jeddah by ship, and Yalamlam is left far off to one side so that the ship does not actually pass parallel to it, may such a person enter iḥrām at Jeddah?

### ANSWER
On this question there is some difference of opinion among our senior scholars (may Allah have mercy on them). The considered position of Ḥaḍrat Mufti Muhammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) is that iḥrām may be delayed until Jeddah, whereas Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Yūsuf Binnorī (may Allah have mercy on him) used to say that it is not permissible to delay iḥrām until Jeddah. Even so, Ḥaḍrat Mufti Muhammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) also wrote that the more cautious course is to enter iḥrām before reaching the point parallel to Yalamlam.

See what is stated in Jawāhir al-Fiqh:

In circumstances such as these, where the scholars differ over the question, caution lies in entering iḥrām on the ship at Yalamlam itself, or else in entering iḥrām before disembarking on the shore at Jeddah, because, as the jurists have made clear, in a matter of disagreement it is better to adopt the more cautious side, so that no one is left disputing the validity of one's act of worship. Besides, entering iḥrām before the mīqāt is superior in everyone's view; indeed, some narrations of hadith mention the merit of setting out in iḥrām from one's very home. The condition is that there be no danger of falling into the things forbidden while in iḥrām. Anyone who fears that avoiding the prohibitions of iḥrām throughout that whole period will be difficult for him is better advised to delay it to the last limit, and such a person should be careful enough at that final limit that his iḥrām is free of the scholars' disagreement. (جواہر الفقہ: ۱/۴۸۹، مواقیت احرام، دار العلوم کراچی).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

On the route that was used in former times for travelling by sea, iḥrām used to be entered on reaching the point parallel to Yalamlam, and this remained the practice of the senior scholars and jurists of India; even today this is the more cautious course. Present-day geographers do say that the route now passes neither by Yalamlam nor parallel to it, and that iḥrām must therefore be entered at Jeddah; but the demand of caution is what has been stated above. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۰/۳۷۹، مبوب و مرتب).
