### TITLE
The authority to appoint a judge and the qualities required in him

### QUESTION
What are the qualities required in a judge? If there were an Islamic government in South Africa, who would appoint the judge? Further, since there is no Islamic government today, is it necessary for the Muslims to appoint a judge or not?

### ANSWER
In the Islamic sharīʿah the office of judge is a most important and most delicate office. Not every Tom, Dick and Harry has a right to it; rather only that person in whom the required qualities are present will be entitled to become a judge.

An explanation concerning the office of judge:

Qaḍāʾ is to decide the disputes of people with truth in accordance with the law sent down by Allah Most High. (بدائع الصنائع: ۷/۲، کتاب آداب القاضی، سعد)۔ Some jurists say that qaḍāʾ is the name for settling disputes and putting an end to quarrels. (الدر المختار: ۵/۳۵۲، سعید، و لسان الحکام: ۱/۲۱۸، الفصل الاول فی آداب القضاء)۔

It is necessary for the decision of a judge that it should not be contrary to consensus.

The decision of a judge is not a report; rather it has the status of the creation of a binding ruling.

The conditions necessary for eligibility for the office of judge:

(1) Being of sound mind; no insane or mentally disordered person is fit for the office of judge.

(2) Being an adult; the appointment of a minor is likewise not valid.

(3) Being a Muslim; a non-Muslim cannot be made a judge, and if a judge is a non-Muslim and gives a decision, it will not be enforceable.

(4) Being free.

(5) Being sighted; a blind man will not be made a judge.

(6) He should be able to speak and not be dumb, and able to hear and not be deaf.

(7) He should not be one who has been punished for the offence of false accusation.

The qualities required in a judge are as follows:

٭ He should be a man of knowledge and learning, with an informed grasp of the lawful and the unlawful and of the other necessary rulings.

٭ He should be acquainted with the Book and the Sunnah and with the method of ijtihād, so that he may give sound decisions in the events and cases that arise.

٭ He should have the necessary knowledge of the Arabic language, of its various expressions and idioms, and of its literature.

٭ He should be familiar with the language, social usage and custom, idioms and vocabulary of the country and region in which he is.

٭ He should not feel it beneath him to take counsel from the scholars.

٭ He should be characterised by uprightness. In the technical usage of the jurists an upright person is one who abstains from major sins and does not persist in minor ones.

٭ His good deeds should outweigh his minor lapses.

٭ He should keep away from places of suspicion.

٭ He should not be hasty by temperament.

٭ He should not be of bad character.

٭ He should be firm in character, wise, understanding and righteous.

(Summarised from Islāmī ʿAdālat by Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām Qāsimī (may Allah have mercy on him), and from Īḍāḥ al-Nawādir by Muftī Shabbīr Aḥmad Qāsimī.)

The authority to appoint a judge in the present age:

Consider the detailed treatment of this in Islāmī ʿAdālat:

Now the question arises: when the Islamic caliphate has come to an end and the central authority that strung the Muslim world together upon a single thread has perished, what path is open to the Muslims, after the loss of leadership and caliphate, for discharging the duty of appointing judges? Should they suspend the Islamic rulings connected with collective life and cheerfully allow the system of justice of Islam to be effaced, or has the Islamic sharīʿah also given some rulings for these varied circumstances?

The answer to this question is that Islam does not in any circumstance approve for its followers a life of anarchy and disorder, nor does it in any circumstance consider it correct to turn away from the rulings of the sharīʿah. Therefore this much is settled in any case: whatever the circumstances may be, the duty of binding the Muslims together into a community remains in place, and there is no way of avoiding the establishment among the Muslims of a sharʿī department that enforces the laws of the sharīʿah; the ummah is charged with this in any case to the extent of its ability.

Now, in this connection it is necessary that, in view of the political conditions of different countries, the various categories into which the Muslim lands have fallen should be determined, and the manner of delegating judicial authority in each of them ascertained.

1. The first and original Islamic form, which is what the sharīʿah requires, is that the government be established in the form of a leadership and caliphate founded upon "consultation", which will be the practical embodiment of the true collective system of Islam. In such a case the authority to delegate the office of judge will belong to the leader and caliph.

2. The second case is that the country is under the authority of some Muslim king or absolute Muslim ruler, or that a democratic government is established in which power lies in practice in the hands of the Muslims. In this case the delegation of the office of judge by the Muslim king or the Muslim ruler will be regarded as valid.

3. The third case is that there is in the country a Muslim government which, despite its internal freedom, is subordinate to some other non-Muslim government. In this case the delegation of the office of judge by the Muslim ruler will be valid, for although he is under a non-Muslim authority, he is autonomous in internal affairs.

4. The fourth case is that the disbelievers have gained dominance over the country, whether power lies with some non-Muslim king or absolute ruler, or with a democratic government in which power lies in practice in the hands of non-Muslims. In this case it will be obligatory upon the Muslims to elect a leader of their own so that they may live a collective life, and the delegation of the office of judge by this leader will be valid. In this way, too, the Islamic sharīʿah will be able to be applied among the Muslims as far as possible, and the sin of suspending the rulings of the sharīʿah, as well as of dissension and division, will be avoided.

And if — may God forbid — the Muslims do not agree upon the election of a leader, but the people of binding and loosing agree upon appointing a particular person as judge, this too will be valid. (اسلامی عدالت، جلدِ اول، از ص ۱۷۷ تا ص ۱۷۹، ط: قاضی پبلشرز)۔

Consider the proofs: 1.

ثم رأيت في الفتح قال: والذي له ولاية التقليد الخليفة والسلطان الذي نصبه الخليفة وأطلق له التصرف وكذا الذي ولاه السلطان ناحية وجعل له خراجها وأطلق له التصرف فإن له أن يولي ويعزل كذا قالوا. (رد المحتار: ۵/۳۵۵، سعيد).
قال المازري في شرح التلقين: القضاء ينعقد بأحد وجهين أحدهما عقد أمير المؤمنين أو أحد أمراء ه، الذين جعل لهم العقد في مثل هذا، والثاني: عقد ذوي الرأي وأهل العلم والمعرفة والعدالة لرجل منهم كملت فيه شروط القضاء وهذا حيث لا يمكنهم مطالبة الإمام في ذلك

الخ. (ابن فرحون، ص ۲۱).

2.

ويجوز تقلد القضاء من السلطان العادل والجائر. (الدر المختار: ۵/۳٦۸، سعيد).

3.

وبلاد الإسلام التي في أيدي الكفرة لا شك أنها بلاد الإسلام لا بلاد الحرب لأنهم لم يظهروا فيها حكم الكفر والقضاة مسلمون والملوك الذين يطيعونهم عن ضرورة مسلمون ولو كانت عن غير ضرورة ففساق وكل مصر فيه والٍ من جهتهم تجوز فيه إقامة الجمع والأعياد وأخذ الخراج وتقليد القضاة وتزويج الأيامي لاستيلاء المسلم عليه وأما إطاعة الكفر فذاك مخادعة. (رد المحتار عن التاتارخانية: ۵/۳٦۸، سعيد).

4.

وإذا لم يكن سلطان ولا من يجوز التقلد منه كما هو في بعض بلاد المسلمين غلب عليهم الكفار كقرطبة الآن يجب على المسلمين أن يتفقوا على واحد منهم يجعلونه والياً فيولي قاضياً ويكون هو الذي يقضي بينهم وكذا ينصبوا إماماً يصلي بهم الجمعة. (رد المحتار نقلاً عن الفتح: ۵/۳٦۹، سعيد).
حتى لو اجتمع أهل بلدة على تولية واحد القضاء لم يصح بخلاف ما لو ولوا سلطاناً بعد موت سلطانهم كما في البزازية وتمامه فيه قلت: وهذا حيث لا ضرورة وإلا فلهم تولية القاضي أيضاً. (رد المحتار: ۵/۳٦۸، سعيد)

It is obvious that this case is one of the utmost compulsion and necessity, for no society of Muslims can in any circumstance dispense with the decision of a judge, and it is obligatory upon the Muslims to strive to the extent of their ability to fulfil this religious need. So if, on account of particular circumstances, it is not possible to establish a system of leadership for a wide region, then the scholars and the people of binding and loosing of a limited and small area should gather together and appoint a judge for themselves, so that this religious need of the Muslims may be met. The mutual agreement of these scholars and people of binding and loosing will be regarded as the mutual agreement of the general body of Muslims, and, according to the sharīʿah, within the limits for which he has been appointed judge, the office of judge will be validly constituted and his decision will be enforceable. (ماخوذ از حاشیہ اسلامی عدالت، جلدِ اول، از ص ۱۷۸ تا ص ۱۸۰، ط: قاضی پبلشرز)۔
