### TITLE
The ruling on using spirit in a stove

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to use spirit in a stove or not? For spirit is derived from wine.

### ANSWER
The spirit in use in our time is generally not made from the four forbidden intoxicating drinks, that is: (1) raw grape wine, (2) cooked grape wine, (3) the wine of raisins, and (4) date wine. Wine made from anything other than these four is pure in the view of the two Shaykhs; and after a change of essence its very reality has changed. There is therefore room for using it.

See what ʿAllāmah Muḥammad ibn Bakhīt al-Muṭīʿī, Mufti of the Egyptian lands, writes in his Fatāwā:

ونفيد ثالثاً: أنهم كانوا يستخرجون الإسبيرتو من الخمر، ومن كل الأشربة المسكرة ولما كثر استعماله في الطب والصنائع اتسعت تجارته صاروا يستحضرونه من الفواكه والخضروات والبقول والحبوب؛ بل ويستحضرونه أيضاً من الأخشاب. وعلي ذلك نقول: ما كان مستحضراً من الخمر أو من الأشربة المسكرة غير الخمر يعطي حكمها نجاسة وطهارة.

وما كان مستحضراً من الثمار والحبوب والأخشاب فهو طاهر، وهذا الصنف هو الرائج والغالب استعماله في المتجر على ما بلغنا ممن بحثوا عنه. (الفتاوى للامام العلامة محمد بن بخيت المطيعي، ص ۱۹، ط: دار الصديق للعلوم، دمشق).

Imdād al-Aḥkām records:

The spirit that is burned in a stove is of the inferior kind. It is certainly not the spirit of the four wines; it will be an ordinary spirit made from some wine other than those. At the present time, because of the widespread affliction, a fatwa is given permitting the use of the inferior spirit, since it is mixed into a great many articles such as dyes and medicinal powders, which are hard to avoid. And once the inferior spirit has been declared permissible to use on account of the general affliction, then the ruling of lawfulness, permissibility and purity will apply to it everywhere.

الشيء إذا ثبت، ثبت بلوازمه.

However, as far as possible it is better and preferable to avoid using it without need.

The footnote records: Ṭibbī Jawhar, printed by Ashraf al-Maṭābiʿ, p. 160 of the new edition, states expressly that there are three kinds of spirit. In India the inferior kind of spirit is produced, which is made from the wine of potatoes, jujubes, wheat, barley and the like; the superior spirit is made in Europe, and it is the spirit of the wine of grapes, apples, pomegranates and raisins; and this superior kind of spirit is costly and is put into medicines. (Imdād al-Aḥkām with the footnotes: 4/507).

Kifāyat al-Muftī records:

Methylated spirit, which is burned in a stove, has had its intoxicating component, alcohol, so compounded that methylated spirit is not an intoxicant; its use for burning is therefore permissible.

Answer by Mawlānā Ḥabīb al-Mursalīn Ṣāḥib: if the spirit passes out of the reality of wine, then using it will be permissible. And so on. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 9/152, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

If the spirit has not been obtained from grapes, raisins, fresh dates or dried dates, then there is room for it. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 4/209).
