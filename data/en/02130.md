### TITLE
The ruling on gifting an undivided share

### QUESTION
Zayd, being of sound mind and in full possession of his senses, gifted his land to his four brothers, and the land is in those brothers' possession; but it was not divided up and given to them separately. Zayd has since died, and now Zayd's sisters are claiming a share of inheritance in this land. Is their claim valid or not?

### ANSWER
The jurists generally write that in the view of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) the gift of an undivided share is not valid unless it is divided up and handed over. The gift of an undivided share means gifting a portion of a whole. This ruling is stated plainly in the standard books of fiqh and needs no reference. In our own day, however, the question calls for fresh consideration, because gifting an undivided share has become the general custom, and under systems of law other than the Islamic law such a gift is regarded as valid and complete. On this basis the jurists have written that when the general custom runs contrary to an explicit text of the jurists, general custom and particular custom are given preference. In this servant's view, therefore, such a gift should take effect. Handing over possession is, however, necessary: possession is necessary in the view of three of the imāms — Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), Imām al-Shāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) and Imām Aḥmad (may Allah have mercy on him). In the gift of an undivided share there will indeed be possession, but it will be defective possession, not complete possession.

The standard books of fiqh state that a partner cannot become a hired employee — that is, if two brothers are partners in a business and they fix a salary for one brother in addition to his share of the profits, this is not permissible. But Ḥaḍrat Muftī Rashīd Aḥmad (may Allah have mercy on him), after a lengthy investigation, wrote at the end: an explicit text of the school is set aside even on the strength of a particular usage, and employing the partners of a company as salaried men is a general usage, so the explicit text of the school will be set aside all the more readily. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 7/328.)

Before that he writes:

For setting aside an explicit text of the Sacred Law, a general usage is a condition; but for setting aside an explicit text of the school, even a particular usage is enough. (Same reference.)

Consider a few instances:

• The earlier jurists counted the expression "سرحتك" among the allusive formulations and so treated it as an irrevocable divorce; the later jurists, however, counted it as an explicit formulation on account of custom, and gave the verdict of a revocable divorce even without any intention.

• In the view of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), only compulsion on the part of the ruler counts as compulsion; but Imām Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him) counted compulsion by someone other than the ruler as compulsion too.

• The jurists generally held the hiring out of books to be impermissible, but some jurists gave a verdict of permissibility on account of custom. كما مر في موضعه.

• Likewise, the verdict imposing liability upon the collector and upon the common hired workman was also issued on the basis of custom.

The upshot is that we cannot set aside an explicit text of hadith on account of general custom or particular custom, though we can set aside analogy on account of general custom. For example, we cannot set aside the report of qafīz al-ṭaḥḥān on account of general custom; but other questions derived from it by analogy — the commission agent, for instance, who works on a percentage — are permissible on account of general custom, since besides the need for it there is also a general custom to that effect. It was for this reason that the mashāʾikh of Balkh (may Allah have mercy on them) declared payment on a percentage basis to be permissible: that is, that a man sews the cloth and takes a half or a quarter of it. They took this to be general custom and declared it permissible, even though ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) had reservations about its being general custom. (In the present age it has become so widespread that there is now no doubt whatever that it is general custom.)

But an explicit text of the school, or a ẓāhir al-riwāyah position, may be set aside both on account of general custom, which prevails in every land, and on account of particular custom, which prevails in particular lands.

This matter is dealt with in Sharḥ ʿUqūd Rasm al-Muftī, in ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī's (may Allah have mercy on him) treatise Nashr al-ʿUrf, and in other works on custom. So if there is a general custom of making a gift of an undivided share by handing over possession without dividing it — and today this is both the custom and the law — then such a gift ought to be upheld. It was probably for this reason that ʿAllāmah Anwar Shāh Kashmīrī (may Allah have mercy on him) said in Fayḍ al-Bārī, his commentary on Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī (3/370):

ثم اعلم أن هبة المشاع لا تتم في أصل المذهب وإن تحقق القبض أيضاً وأفتي المتأخرون بجوازها وبه أفتي وذلك لا ني أتردد في نفس مسألة الشيوع فلست أشدد فيها كالحنفية ولا أوسع فيها كالبخاري بل هي أمر بين الأمرين كما علمت، فإن مرضي الشرع وهو رفع الإبهام والتمييز، والشيوع يخل به، فلا يكون هدراً، كما أهدره البخاري، ولا ضرورياً، كما فهمه الحنفية، بحيث قالوا ببطلان الهبة؛ وبالجملة إذا كان حال الشيوع عندي ما سمعت، فلم أشدد في الحكم ووافقت المتأخرين في جواز هبة المشاع عند القبض. (فيض الباري: ۳/۳۷۵، كتاب الهبة).

Among the earlier jurists, the eminent and illustrious jurist ʿAllāmah Shams al-Aʾimmah al-Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote in al-Mabsūṭ:

وبالإجماع هبة المشاع فيما لا يحتمل القسمة تتم بالقبض وكذلك عندي فيما يحتمل القسمة جائز. (المبسوط للإمام السرخسي: ۲۱/٦۹، ط: إدارة القرآن).

ʿAllāmah al-Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote these words in the Book of Pledges, after the Book of Gifts.

It is, however, necessary that no dispute should arise; and if it does give rise to a dispute over which portion is to go to whom, then it is not valid.

Consider what Fayḍ al-Bārī states:

والذي أراه أن النهي عنه لكونه مفضياً إلى النزاع، وكل أمر يكون النهي عنه كذلك لا يشدد فيه الشارع بنفسه، بل ربما يغمض عنه أيضاً، فلا ينبغي التشدد فيه، ويدل عليه ما أخرجه البخاري

وفيه فقال رسول الله ﷺ لما كثرت عنده الخصومة في ذلك: فإما لا، فلا تبتاعوا حتى يبدو صلاح الثمر، كالمشورة يشيرها، لكثرة خصومتهم. (فيض الباري: ۳/۳۷۲).
