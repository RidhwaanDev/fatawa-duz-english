### TITLE
Whether a judgement given against an absent party is enforceable

### QUESTION
A woman brought a claim against her husband, saying that he has been absent for several years and gives her nothing for her maintenance, and that she therefore wants a separation. If in this case the judge gives some decision against the husband, that is a judgement against an absent party. Is a judgement against an absent party permissible in the view of the Ḥanafīs or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, where a judgement is to be given against an absent party, the judge will appoint someone on behalf of the absent man and will conduct the proceedings treating him as standing in the place of the absent man. In the technical usage of the jurists such a person is called a wakīl musakhkhar, a representative appointed by the court. In this case the decision will be enforceable. But even if the judge has not appointed a wakīl musakhkhar, the decision of the judge will still be enforceable, because the question of judgement against an absent party is one in which juristic effort has been exercised.

قال في المجلة: الوكيل المسخر هو الوكيل المنصوب من قبل الحاكم للمدعي عليه الذي لم يمكن إحضاره بالمحكمة. (المجلة، ص ۳٦۵، المادة: ۱۷۹۱). (وكذا في رد المحتار: ۵/٤۱۵، سعيد).
قال في الهداية: وإذا كان كذلك يتضمن الحكم به قضاء على الغائب وأنه لا يجوز إلا إذا رآه القاضي وقضي به لأنه مجتهد فيه ثم ما كان يخاف عليه الفساد يبيعه القاضي لأنه تعذر عليه حفظ صورته ومعناه فينظر له بحفظ المعني. (الهداية: ۲/٦۲۰، كتاب المفقود).
قال في فتح القدير: إذا رأي القاضي المصلحة في الحكم للغائب وعليه فحكم فإنه ينفذ لأنه مجتهد فيه. (فتح القدير: ٦/۱٤۲، دار الفكر).
قال في الفقه الحنفي في ثوبه الجديد: ولو رأي القاضي مصلحة في الحكم للغائب وعليه فحكم فإن حكمه ينفذ؛ لأن القضاء على الغائب أمر مجتهد فيه لما مر معنا في حديث عائشة رضي الله عنه أن النبي ﷺ قضي على أبي سفيان وهو غائب وقال لزوجته: خذي ما يكفيك وولدك بالمعروف، الخ. (الفقه الحنفي في ثوبه الجديد: ۳/۱٤۲).

Mawlānā Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām Ṣāḥib writes in Islāmī ʿAdālat: If the defendant avoids appearing even after receiving notice of the case, the judge will regard him as unable to clear himself of the charge and will hear the case and give his decision as well. And on behalf of such a defendant as cannot possibly be brought before the court, the judge will summon, to state the case on his behalf, some person of whom it may be hoped that he will protect the interests of the defendant. In the technical usage of the jurists such a person is called a wakīl musakhkhar. (اسلامی عدالت، جلدِ اول ص ۳۸۳، ط: قاضی پبلشرز)۔

Note: in the footnotes of Islāmī ʿAdālat the question of judgement against an absent party has been discussed in detail from p. 383 to p. 395; referring to it is likewise beneficial.

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil records:

If the defendant is absent and no trace of him can be found, or it is not possible to inform him of the plaintiff's claim and to summon him to clear himself of the charge, but some close relative and well-wisher of his is present of whom it may be hoped that he will safeguard his rights, then that person should be summoned; his standing will be that of one pursuing the case on behalf of the defendant. Although the earlier jurists permitted the appointment of a wakīl musakhkhar in only five kinds of case, al-Ḥaṣkafī (may Allah have mercy on him) has reported the inclination of the later scholars in these words:

فالمتأخرون أن القاضي ينصب وكيلاً في الكل وهو قول الثاني.

That is, the view of the later scholars is that the judge will appoint someone as representative in all cases, and this is also the opinion of Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him). So, in the light of this explicit statement of al-Ḥaṣkafī (may Allah have mercy on him) and of present-day circumstances:

1. If the defendant is absent and no trace of him can be found, the claim being made against the defendant should be publicised through the means of publication and communication that are well known in each age, and he should be called upon to clear himself of the charge.

2. If the defendant is present but goes about in hiding and it is not possible to convey the notice of the Dār al-Qaḍāʾ to him, then in such a case too recourse should be had to the wakīl musakhkhar. ʿAllāmah al-Ḥaṣkafī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written that if the defendant hides himself, then in the view of Imām Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) the proceedings of the case will be continued after an oath has been taken from the wakīl musakhkhar; and he has also stated explicitly that the practice of the later jurists is upon this. 3. If the defendant is given prior notice and even so avoids clearing himself of the charge, or does not accept the notice given through the Dār al-Qaḍāʾ, then these forms of avoiding clearing oneself of the charge will carry the ruling of refusing to take the oath, and on that basis the judge will be authorised to give his decision. (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۱/٤۵۰۔٤۵۲)۔
