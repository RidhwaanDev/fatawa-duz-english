### TITLE
The ruling on compiling a solar hijri calendar

### QUESTION
What is the position in the sharia of a solar hijri calendar made up of events from the life of the Prophet ﷺ? In 1990 an international moon-sighting conference was held in Pakistan, and the writers of papers at that conference stressed a lunar hijri calendar for the Muslim community. Some persons stressed a solar hijri calendar in their papers, and Mawlānā Sayyid Shabbīr Aḥmad, in his work Kashf-e Hilāl, compiled a solar hijri calendar in order to preserve the Islamic identity and to give expression to Islamic history and culture, and laid down this principle: that whatever event comes earlier in order in the blessed life of the Holy Prophet ﷺ should be placed earlier in the order of the months.

Furthermore, several of our acts of worship are tied to the lunar hijri calendar. But for administrative and commercial matters the Christian calendar has become a necessity for us, even though the Christian calendar carries the benefits of a solar calendar.

The names of the months of the Christian calendar are made up of the names of Roman gods, goddesses and dictators, and this is a cause of mental distress for the people of Islam.

Like the lunar calendar, the solar calendar too should begin from the migration. In the solar calendar the names of the months should be given after the events that occurred in the blessed life of the Prophet; whichever event occurred in a given Christian month, that Christian month should be named after that event.

On the basis of the above-mentioned reasons, is it permissible to compile a solar hijri calendar and to put it into use, or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, converting the hijri calendar into the Christian calendar is a difficult business; besides, whether or not it would be accepted is also a doubtful matter. Therefore the use of the Christian calendar for prayer times and the like is permissible, and there is no great harm in using it.

The names January, February and so on are indeed originally the names of gods and goddesses, but they have become like an abandoned and disused literal meaning; nobody's mind moves from these names to the gods, and indeed a large class of people is unaware even of their real meaning. Consider the meanings of the names of the months from Wikipedia:

January: it is from the Latin word [mensis Ianuarius], the month of Janus, the Roman god who is the god of doorways and doors and of beginnings and endings.

February: it is from the Latin word [mensis Februarius], the month of Februa, the Roman festival of purification; this and Februus have the same root, and Februus is the name of the goddess of death among some Roman inhabitants (Etruscan).

March: the month of Mars; it is from the Latin word [mensis Martius], the month of Mars, the Roman god of wars.

April: it is from the Latin word [mensis Aprilis]; its meaning is not known for certain. It is commonly derived from the forms of aperire (to open), or from Aphrodite, who is a goddess.

May: the month of Maia; it is from the Latin word [mensis Maius], the month of Maia, the Roman goddess who is the goddess of plants; its root is the same as the root of the Latin word magnus (big) and the English word major (great).

June: it is from the Latin word [mensis Iunius], the month of Juno, the Roman goddess who is the goddess of marriage, childbirth and government.

July: it is from the Latin word [mensis Iulius], the month of Julius Caesar (the name of a king), the month of his birth, which was brought into use in 44 BC under the terms of his calendar.

August: it is from the Latin word [mensis Augustus], the month of Augustus; Augustus brought it into use in 8 BC, because certain important events occurred in this month during his acquisition of power.

September: it is from the Latin word [mensis September], the seventh month of the calendar of Rome, which was in use before 153 BC.

October: it is from the Latin word [mensis October], the eighth month of the calendar of Rome, which was in use before 153 BC.

November: it is from the Latin word [mensis November], the ninth month of the calendar of Rome, which was in use before 153 BC.

December: it is from the Latin word [mensis December], the tenth month of the calendar of Rome, which was in use before 153 BC.

Consider a few examples of an abandoned and disused literal meaning:

1. The meaning of ṣalāt as the moving of the two hip joints; 2. the meaning of Baghdād as the gift and grant of an idol; 3. the literal meaning of

فداك أبي وأمي

and 4. the literal meaning of

تربت يداك

All of these have become obsolete, and the use of these words is permissible and correct — indeed they have also come down in the hadiths. The Prophet ﷺ said فداك أبي وأمي for some of the noble Companions. If someone were to say that this is — Allah forbid — a lie and an impertinence, because the parents of the Holy Prophet ﷺ were not even alive, and because to make one's parents a ransom for anyone is impertinence, then in answer to him this very thing will be said: that the literal meaning has become obsolete and this is in the sense of intense pleasure and the showing of joy and delight.

1.

ـ قال في تاج العروس: الصلاة واوية، مأخوذة من: صلي إذا دعا، قيل: إنها من الصلوين. (۳۸/٤٤۲). قال أبو البقاء الكفومي: إن الصلاة في الأصل انعطاف جسماني لأنها من تحريك الصلوين ثم استعمل في الرحمة والدعاء. (معجم في المصطلحات والفروق اللغوية، ص ۸۷٤، مؤسسة الرسالة).

2.

ـ قال ابن كثير رحمه الله في البداية والنهاية: بغ اسم صنم وقيل: شيطان وداد عطية أي عطية الصنم. (۱۰/۱۰۸، ط: دار إحياء التراث). (وكذا في تاريخ بغداد: ۱/٦۰، وتاج العروس: ۷/٤٤۲، دار الهداية).

Consider: a report in the noble al-Bukhārī states:

3.

ـ عن عبد الله بن شداد قال: سمعت علياً رضي الله عنه يقول: ما رأيت النبي ﷺ يفدي رجلاً بعد سعد سمعته يقول: ارم فداك أبي وأمي. (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۲۹۰۵، باب المجن ومن يترس بترس صاحبه).

عن عبد الله بن الزبير رضي الله عنه قال: كنت يوم الأحزاب جعلت أنا وعمر بن أبي سلمة في النساء فنظرت فإذا أنا بالزبير على فرسه يختلف إلى بني قريظة مرتين أو ثلاثاً فلما رجعت قلت: يا أبت! رأيتك تختلف قال: أو هل رأيتني يا بني! قلت: نعم، قال: كان رسول الله ﷺ قال: من يأت بني قريظة فيأتيني بخبرهم فانطلقت فلما رجعت جمع لي رسول الله ﷺ أبويه فقال: فداك أبي وأمي. (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۳۷۲۰، باب مناقب الزبير بن العوام).

In the same way the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to some of the noble Companions

تربت يداك"

If someone says that this is a supplication for destruction, the answer to it is that the literal meaning has become obsolete and its sense is reproof and rebuke.

4.

ـ عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه عن النبي ﷺ قال: تنكح المرأة لأربع: لمالها ولحسبها، ولجمالها، ولدينها، فاظفر بذات الدين تربت يداك. متفق عليه. (بلوغ المرام، ص: ۲۹۲ رقم: 964).

فقالت أم سلمة: يا رسول الله! وتحتلم المرأة فقال: تربت يداك فبم يشبهها ولدها. (صحيح مسلم، رقم: ۳۱۳).

عن عائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها قالت: أتاني عمي من الرضاعة
ولم يرضعني الرجل قال: تربت يداك، أو يمينك. (صحيح مسلم، رقم: ۱٤٤۵).

There are hundreds of examples of this in the Arabic language. For this reason, in our view there is no ugliness in the use of these months. Some Barelwīs and Salafīs raise a difficulty against us, that you call the Messenger of Allah ﷺ "Ān Ḥuḍūr", which means "the one present", while you do not hold him to be present and watching. The answer to this too is the same: that here "Ān Ḥuḍūr" is in the sense of "the honoured one", "his lordship", and "present in the heart", and the original meaning is obsolete. The upshot is that there is no harm in using the names of the months, because their literal meanings have become obsolete.

قال في الأشباه: تترك الحقيقة بدلالة الاستعمال والعادة. كذا ذكره فخر الإسلام. (الأشباه والنظائر: ۱/۹۳، ط: بيروت).

قال الشيخ أحمد الزرقا: لأن الاستعمال والتعارف يجعل إطلاق اللفظ على ما تعورف استعماله فيه حقيقة بالنسبة إلى المستعملين ويجعل إطلاقه على معناه الوضعي الأصلي في نظرهم مجازاً، ومن المعلوم أن الأمر إذا دار بين الحقيقة والمجاز تترجح الحقيقة وهي هنا العرف والعادة ويترك المجاز وهو المعني الوضعي الأصلي فقد قال صاحب جامع الفصولين: مطلق الكلام محمول على المعتاد. (شرح القواعد الفقهية، ص ۱۳۲، ط: دار القلم).

The gist of the passage of Shaykh Aḥmad al-Zarqā (may Allah have mercy on him) is that, because of people's usage, the customary meaning stands at the level of the literal sense, while the original and literal meaning stands at the level of a figurative sense; and at the time of a clash the customary-literal meaning will be given precedence. The outcome is that the use of the names January, February and so forth is permissible, because among the general public and the elite alike their literal meanings have become like the phoenix. Furthermore, in the purified sharia the rulings on questions do not turn upon obscure investigations, but upon the words and meanings in use in common custom. Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Taqī (may his blessings endure) says: in questions of this kind the temper of the sharia is that it takes account of people's common custom and does not look at technical subtleties. (Fiqhī Maqālāt: 3/219).
