### TITLE
Annulment of a marriage because the husband is insane

### QUESTION
If a man is insane, how can his wife obtain a separation, given that she knew about his condition beforehand but now fears for her honour and is afraid of him as well?

### ANSWER
If a woman's husband is insane — unaware of the world around him, with no notion of fulfilling his wife's rights — then, even though she knew of his insanity beforehand, if it is now difficult and indeed dangerous for her to live with him, if food and maintenance are also a problem for her, and if living a chaste and upright life is beyond her, then in all these cases she should bring her case before a sharia judge, or, where there is no such judge, before a body of scholars. Finding her claim to be sound, that body should allow one year's grace for the husband to be treated. If he has not recovered within the year, she should bring her case before the same body again, and its members should then separate the two. Once her waiting period has passed, she may marry elsewhere. (ملخص از امداد المفتین از مفتی شفیع صاحب ۲/٦۷۳) ۔

Majmūʿah-e Qawānīn-e Islāmī states:

Insanity in a husband that may put his wife's body and life in danger is a ground for separation. The judge will, however, allow the husband a year's grace for treatment; if even after that he has not recovered and the wife wants to be parted from him, the judge will grant the separation.

(الفتاوی الحامدیہ للعلامۃ رکن الدین ناغوری ص ۷٦ نقلاً عن المضمرات، بحوالہ: الحیلۃ الناجزۃ ص ۳۹) ۔

Since, however, no explanation of "supervening insanity" is given here, nor is one to be found anywhere else, the meaning of "unremitting" insanity as its opposite cannot be made fully clear either; and to apply here, by mere analogy, the explanation of "unremitting" as against "intermittent" insanity given elsewhere would be contrary to caution. The cautious course, then, is to set that explanation aside, to allow a full year's grace in every case, and only then to give a ruling.

Likewise, if the husband is unable to fulfil the rights of marriage and it becomes difficult for the wife to live a chaste and upright life — indeed, where there is strong reason to fear that she will fall into sin — she may bring her case and have a judge or a body of Muslims grant a separation.
