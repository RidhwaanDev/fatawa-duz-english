### TITLE

Drinking fizzy drinks

### QUESTION

Carbon dioxide is a gas (a simple gas) which is used in drinks such as Coca-Cola. It can be obtained in the following two ways:

(1) By means of chemicals.

(2) By means of manufacturing liquor.

Generally this gas is obtained from chemicals, but nowadays, because of its scarcity, there is an intention to produce it by the second method.

In order to understand this method, some members of the Jamʿiyyat al-Muftīn and some representatives of the halaal bodies went to inspect it. A brief account of the method that was explained to them, together with such further clarification as is necessary, is as follows:

(1) Wheat is mixed with water.

(2) The solid particles (the chaff) are removed.

(3) It is boiled in a large tank and a kind of herb (hopps) is added to it, which has three functions:

(a) It produces bitterness in it.

(b) It produces fragrance in it.

(c) It destroys harmful particles, such as bacteria and so on.

(4) It is then cooled.

(5) This starch naturally contains sugar; in scientific and chemical terminology it is a compound of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, or these things are present in this starch, which is why this starch is also called a carbohydrate. Yeast is added to this starch and it is fermented in a fermentation machine; this process goes on for about twelve days. The yeast acts gradually on the whole of the starch and in twelve days converts the sugar of the whole starch into two things: (1) alcohol; (2) carbon dioxide (CO2 gas). The alcohol is in liquid or solid form and remains in the vessel, whereas the carbon dioxide flies off above; and since it is in the form of a gas, this gas is captured by other means. This gas is not obtained from the alcohol, nor is it the steam of the yeast; rather it is a product of the sugar present in the starch.

Note: (1) The company intends to use 50 per cent of its carbon dioxide gas in this way.

(2) After purification, 0.000002 per cent of the liquor (two parts in a hundred million) remains in it.

(3) The gas being used at present is the chemically produced gas.

(4) This liquor is made from wheat and barley.

Now what is asked is this: is there room to use this gas or not? And will it be permissible to consume those drinks, such as Coca-Cola and the like, in which this gas will be used, or not?

### ANSWER

For the gas in fizzy drinks that is made from liquor there is a precedent in the discussions of the jurists, namely that the vapours and smoke of an impure thing are, according to the sounder view, not impure. So the gas that has been extracted from liquor will likewise not be impure, and its use will therefore be permissible.

Fatāwā ʿĀlamgīrī states:

إذا مرت الريح بالعذرات وأصابت الثوب المبلول يتنجس إن وجدت رائحة النجاسة وما يصيب الثوب من بخارات النجاسة لا يتنجس بها وهو الصحيح هكذا في الظهيرية، دخان النجاسة إذا أصاب الثوب أو البدن الصحيح أنه لا ينجسه هكذا في السراج الوهاج، وفي الفتاوى: إذا أحرقت العذرة في بيت فعلا دخانه وبخاره إلى الطابق وانعقد ثم ذاب أو عرق الطابق فأصاب ماؤه ثوباً لا يفسده استحساناً ما لم يظهر أثر النجاسة. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/٤۷).

When air passes over filth and excrement and then touches a wet garment, the garment becomes impure if the stench of the impurity is perceptible; but if the vapours of the impurity touch the garment, the garment does not become impure by them, and this is the sound narration. When the smoke of an impurity touches a garment or the body, the sound view is that the garment and the body do not become impure, as stated in al-Sirāj al-Wahhāj. And in the Fatāwā it is stated that if excrement is burned in a house and its smoke or vapour touches a brick or a pane of glass or the ceiling, and after settling it melts or moisture forms on it and it touches a garment, the garment does not become impure so long as no trace of the impurity appears; and this is a ruling of juristic preference.

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

(وبخار نجس) في الفتح مرت الريح بالعذرات وأصاب الثوب إن وجدت رائحتها تنجس لكن نقل في الحلية أن الصحيح أنه لا ينجس وما يصيب الثوب من بخارات النجاسة قيل: ينجسه وقيل: لا وهو الصحيح وفي الحلية: استنجي بالماء وخرج منه ريح لا ينجس عند عامة المشايخ وهو الأصح وكذا إذا كان سراويله مبتلاً. (فتاوى الشامي: ۱/۳۲۵، سعيد).

In the passage above ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) gave preference to the view that if air passes over filth and touches a garment, then even if a stench is perceptible from the garment it does not become impure; and likewise, if the trousers are wet and wind is passed, the garment does not become impure.

Ḥāshiyat al-Ṭaḥṭāwī ʿalā al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

(قوله وبخار نجس) القول بعفوه هو الصحيح

ولو استنجي بالماء ولم يمسحه حتى فسا اختلف المشايخ فيه وعامتهم على أنه لا ينجس

وفيه: دخان النجاسة إذا أصاب الثوب أو البدن فيه اختلاف والصحيح أنه لا ينجسه. (حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ۱/۱٦۱، كوئته).

The ruling on keeping liquor in order to turn it into vinegar:

Making gas or vinegar from liquor is permissible in the view of the Ḥanafīs, and it is also permissible to keep liquor with that intention. Furthermore, in the factories both the workers and the owners of the factory are non-Muslims, who are not bound by the rulings concerning liquor.

Consider what ʿĀlamgīrī states:

ولو أمسك الخمر في بيته للتخليل جاز ولا يأثم. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳۷۳).

Hidāyah states:

كما إذا ورثهما ثم إن كان خمراً يخللها وإن كان خنزيراً يسيبه. (الهداية: ۳/۵۹).

Al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

وخل الخمر سواء خللت أو تخللت يعني خل الخمر فلا فرق في ذلك بين أن يتخلل بنفسه أو يخلل بإلقاء شيء فيه كالملح أو الخل أو النقل من الظل إلى الشمس أو بإيقاد النار بالقرب منها. (البحر الرائق: ۸/۲۱۹، كوئته).

The passages quoted above show that it is permissible to keep liquor in the house with the intention of turning it into vinegar, whether it then turns into vinegar of itself or the various methods of turning it into vinegar are used — such as putting salt into it, moving it from the shade into the sun, or lighting a fire near it, and so on. All these things are permissible.

On this basis the vapour extracted from liquor is not impure. Yes, since it is difficult to find room for it according to the Shāfiʿīs, caution lies in avoiding it. But we advise Muslims that instead of "Coca-Cola" they should drink other drinks whose ingredients are known, since some of the ingredients of Coca-Cola are not even known; such doubtful and suspect things should therefore be avoided.

Some of the muftis have carried out the following research:

Carbon dioxide (CO2) gas is neither impure in itself nor rendered impure by contact, since it is neither liquor (khamr) itself, nor the smoke or vapour of liquor. Moreover, its contact with the alcohol lasts only for a very short period, which is as good as nothing. Then the trace of alcohol that appears at the beginning is also slight — that is, two parts in a thousand (0.2%) — and after purification even that is reduced to two parts in a hundred million (0.000002%), which has no legal consideration at all.

By comparison, the trace of alcohol in vinegar is between one and three per cent, in bread five parts in a thousand, and in the Coca-Cola in circulation one part in a million. Vinegar comes into being from liquor, but it does not have the effects and properties of liquor, and it is therefore pure and lawful.

Consider the milk and honey that are obtained from animals, and where they are obtained from. Allah Most High has mentioned this in the Qur'an:

﴿من بين فرث ودم لبناً خالصاً سائغاً لشربين﴾،

and

﴿يخرج من بطونها شراب مختلف ألوانه فيه شفاء للناس﴾ [النحل: ]

Both of these things are pure and lawful.

The jurists did not declare wind, which breaks the ablution, to be impure; if this wind touches a wet garment, that garment does not become impure. Indeed, even the vapours rising from an impurity do not render anything impure, and this is the sound view. (ʿInāyah and Kifāyah; Durr Mukhtār: 1/238, Saʿīd.)

Bihishtī Zēwar states:

If the steam of impure water touches the body, the body will be called impure only when a drop of the water drips onto the body; otherwise a ruling of impurity will not be given merely because the heat of the steam has touched it, just as no ruling follows from the stench of an impurity reaching the nose. Likewise, if the smell of the smoke or steam of an impurity comes into the body or the clothes, the ruling of impurity will not apply. (Bihishtī Zēwar, Ṭibbī Jawhar, part nine, p. 783.)

Therefore the carbon dioxide obtained during the process of making liquor is not impure, and if it is mixed with pure and lawful food or drink, that food or drink will remain pure and lawful.

It should also be borne in mind that the carbon dioxide under discussion is to be obtained from that liquor factory in which liquor is made from barley; and it is well known among the Ḥanafī jurists that liquor made from barley is not impure in the view of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him). Because of widespread affliction and extensive use, the fatwa that alcohol made from things other than grapes and dates is not impure has been well known and current since the time of Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him).

Accordingly, Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Imdād al-Fatāwā:

If spirit has not been obtained from grapes, raisins, fresh dates or dried dates, then there is room in it, because of the difference of opinion; otherwise there is no room, because of the agreement. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 1/137; and Bihishtī Zēwar, Ṭibbī Jawhar, part nine, p. 102.)

وعن محمد رحمه الله أنه حرام ويحد شاربه

وكان أبو يوسف رحمه الله يقول: ما كان من الأشربة يبقي بعد ما يبلغ عشرة أيام ولا يفسد فإني أكرهه ثم رجع إلى قول أبي حنيفة رحمه الله. (الهداية: ٤/٤۹۵، كتاب الأشربة، ط: شركة علمية، ملتان).
