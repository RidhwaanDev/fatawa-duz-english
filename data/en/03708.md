### TITLE
The ruling on obtaining benefits by paying extra over the medical aid premium

### QUESTION
Those who are attached to Discovery Medical Aid are given the option of the Vitality Programme. Such people have to pay some money over and above the medical aid premium, on account of which they obtain various benefits: for example, they receive discounts on air tickets, gym membership, particular vegetables, particular clothes, particular watches, and so on.

2. Is it permissible to obtain loyalty benefits by paying an annual fee to a bank? For example, eBucks: in this, those who have an FNB account register their credit card with the bank and, by paying two hundred rand annually, obtain various benefits. Is this permissible or not? Explain and be rewarded.

### ANSWER
In the case in question, both matters relate to a conditional gratuity: in the first, the various benefits have been made conditional upon paying something extra on the premium; and in the second, obtaining the benefits has been made conditional upon registering with the bank and paying two hundred rand annually. Since money is not here set against money, nor against something of the same genus, and since there is also no uncertainty leading to dispute, there is therefore scope in it for one whose account is not interest-bearing. Yes, if the bank's income is mostly from unlawful and interest-based dealings, then one ought to keep away from obtaining such benefits. It is recorded in Fatāwā Hindiyyah:

فإن كان مجهولاً جهالة مفضية إلى المنازعة يمنع صحة العقد وإلا فلا. (الفتاوى الهندية: ٤/٤۱۱).

It is mentioned in Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā:

If you do not yourself take the money from the company under the heading of medical treatment, but rather the company itself undertakes the responsibility for treatment in return for that money and has the treatment carried out, then in this case the company has provided you with the facility of treatment in exchange for the money you had paid in. Although in this case the counter-value of the price is unknown, yet since this uncertainty does not lead to dispute, and people do conduct transactions involving such uncertainty, there will therefore be scope for it. (5/435).

The evidences concerning a conditional gratuity have already been given several times earlier, and may be consulted there.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
