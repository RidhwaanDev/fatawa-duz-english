### TITLE
The ruling on a fast when poisonous gas is inhaled

### QUESTION
Will the fast be broken by inhaling the poisonous gas used for killing insects, or not?

### ANSWER
If it went down the throat without the person's choosing it, the fast will not be broken. Yes, if he takes it down his throat by his own choice, the fast will be broken. Consider what is stated in al-Durr al-Mukhtār:

أو دخل حلقه غبار أو ذباب أو دخان، ولو ذاكراً استحساناً لعدم إمكان التحرز عنه، ومفاده: أنه لو أدخل حلقه الدخان أفطر أي دخان كان ولو عوداً أو عنبراً له ذاكراً لإمكان التحرز عنه فليتنبه له كما بسطه الشرنبلالي. (الدر المختار: ۲/۳۹۵، سعيد).

See further: (درر الحكام شرح غرر الأحكام: ۱/۲۰۲، والجوهرة النيرة: ۱/۱۳۸).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states: If a person, while remembering that he is fasting, deliberately takes smoke inside himself by an act of his own (that is, he takes it into his stomach or his brain by way of the mouth or the nose), his fast will be broken, whatever the smoke may be, even if it is only the smoke of aloes-wood or ambergris. And it makes no difference in what manner he takes the smoke inside himself; so much so that if he lit incense and turned it towards himself and drew it up through his nose while remembering that he was fasting, his fast will be broken, because it is possible to guard against this — and most people are heedless of this point. But if it goes inside of its own accord, the fast will not be broken. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 3/304, the Book of Fasting).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
