### TITLE
Removing the doubt that using a loudspeaker invalidates the prayer

### QUESTION
Some people declare praying aloud over a loudspeaker impermissible, or at least doubtful. Is prayer over a loudspeaker sound or not, and what is the reality of the objection raised about it, namely that the congregation is taking its cue from someone other than the imam?

### ANSWER
Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

Is prayer by means of a loudspeaker valid or not? In the beginning the scholars differed over this question. Some were of the view that the sound of a loudspeaker is not the imam's own voice but an echo of that voice. On that view, the movements of the followers would be based on a second sound rather than on the imam's voice, and it is not permissible for a follower to move on the voice of anyone other than the imam.

Against this, others held that the use of a loudspeaker for prayer is nevertheless correct, and that there is a precedent for it in the sharia — a case in which worshippers moved on the word of a man who was outside the prayer. When the Kaʿbah replaced Bayt al-Maqdis as the qiblah, people in some of the mosques on the outskirts of Madinah were praying facing Bayt al-Maqdis. A Companion (may Allah be pleased with him) announced the change of qiblah, and they all turned. Clearly this movement took place on the voice of a person who was outside the prayer.

It has now been established by research that the sound of a loudspeaker is not a copy or an imitation of the imam's voice; it is exactly the same voice that issues from his tongue. Accordingly the scholars are now agreed that prayer and the leading of prayer by means of a loudspeaker are permissible.

Some scholars regard its use as carrying a degree of dislike and hold that it should be resorted to only where there is unavoidable need. Their argument is that the jurists declared it disliked to appoint repeaters to amplify the imam's voice without need, so the same ruling will apply to the loudspeaker. But this argument needs examination. The voice of the repeaters is not the imam's own voice, whereas it has been established that the sound of a loudspeaker is exactly the imam's voice; the two therefore cannot be placed on the same level. What is certainly true is that the loudspeaker should be used only as far as necessary, so that its sound stays within reasonable limits and within the mosque, and that carrying it to people outside the mosque who are busy with their own work should be avoided, since that causes inattention to the Noble Qur'an, and in that lies the danger of disrespect to it. (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۱/۱۳٤، کتب خانہ نعیمیہ دیوبند)
