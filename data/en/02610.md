### TITLE
The ruling on an attractive, decorated burqaʿ

### QUESTION
Nowadays some women wear burqaʿs and ʿabāyahs with attractive designs. What is the ruling on these?

### ANSWER
In order to keep society pure, to close the door to discord and corruption, and to protect the honour, dignity and chastity of women, the sacred Sharīʿah has taught women to go out of the house veiled. It is by the veil that a woman's modesty and shame remain bright and that she is spared the poisoned glances of the openly sinful. This purpose can be achieved only when the veil and the burqaʿ have the following features:

(1) It should cover and conceal the whole body, and when she goes out it should conceal the face and the hands as well.

(2) It should be thick enough and loose enough that the parts of the body that must be concealed, and the shape of the body, are not apparent.

(3) It should not be so beautifully patterned and attractive that it draws men towards her — for the purpose of the burqaʿ is to turn men's attention away, whereas such a burqaʿ has become the very centre of their attention.

A warning has come in the hadith concerning women who draw men towards themselves. Consider:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ صنفان من أهل النار لم أرهما قوم معهم سياط كأذناب البقر يضربون بها الناس ونساء كاسيات عاريات مميلات مائلات رؤوسهن كأسنمة البخت المائلة لا يدخلن الجنة ولا يجدن ريحها وإن ريحها ليوجد من مسيرة كذا وكذا. (رواه مسلم، رقم: ۲۱۲۸، باب النساء الكاسيات عاريات).

In the hadith above the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said that two groups of his Ummah would be people of the Fire, and he declared one of them to be those women who are clothed and yet naked.

ʿAllāmah al-ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) says that one explanation of "clothed yet naked" is that they parade about in the markets dressed in fine and beautiful clothing.

ʿAllāmah Badr al-Dīn al-ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written in his commentary on Abū Dāwūd:

ومنها: مشيهن في الأسواق في ثياب فاخرة، وأنواع طيب فاتحة، مكشوفات الوجوه، مائلات: متبخترات. (شرح أبي داود: ۳/۵٤، ط: الرياض).

In Rūḥ al-Maʿānī, ʿAllāmah al-Ālūsī (may Allah have mercy on him) has likewise declared the decorated and patterned burqaʿ to be forbidden. (See: ۱۸/۱٤٦)۔

Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

Imām al-Jaṣṣāṣ (may Allah have mercy on him) said that when the Qur'an has counted even the sound of jewellery as a display of adornment and has forbidden it, then to go out wearing burqaʿs of decorated colours and patterns will be forbidden all the more. (معارف القرآن: ٦/٤۰٦)۔

Mawlānā ʿĀshiq Ilāhī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in his book Sharʿī Pardah:

Nowadays many women contrive all sorts of ways to draw strange men towards themselves. For instance, a woman goes out wearing a burqaʿ but lets her hands show; or she keeps the face-veil of the burqaʿ so short that both cheeks can be clearly seen; or she fits into the burqaʿ a face-veil so fine that it makes her features and her beauty even more apparent — so that the burqaʿ itself, instead of being a covering, has become a means of attraction. Flowers worked on a burqaʿ, or a burqaʿ of shiny or fine cloth, turns men of corrupt hearts towards the woman wearing it. Burqaʿs have become, instead of a covering, a means of drawing glances, and the proverb applies: even he who would not have looked, looks. We seek refuge with Allah. (فصل الخطاب فی مسئلۃ الحجاب، ص ۱۰۹، ط: ادارۃ المعارف)۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
