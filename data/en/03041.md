### TITLE
The ruling on wishing non-Muslims well on their festivals

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to offer congratulations to a non-Muslim by saying "Happy merry christmas" or not, given that the sum of what it is taken to mean is: "Blessed be the birth of Ḥaḍrat ʿĪsā (peace be upon him)"?

بينوا توجروا.

### ANSWER
In our time these words are used in the same way as "Eid mubārak" and are said to one another on the occasion of Christmas by way of congratulation. Their other meaning has fallen into disuse, and most people are not even aware of it — just as January and February are, in their origin, the names of idols, yet are used simply as the names of months, that idolatrous sense being entirely obsolete. On this basis it is in order for the sake of keeping up friendly relations; but it is not in order to say such words out of reverence for the day. Some scholars do say, however, that these words still turn the mind to the cross, and that for this reason such words should be avoided as far as possible.

قال في الأشباه النظائر: واعلم أن اعتبار العادة والعرف ترجع إليه في الفقه في مسائل كثيرة حتى جعلوا ذلك أصلاً، فقالوا في الأصول في باب ما تترك به الحقيقة تترك الحقيقة بدلالة الاستعمال والعادة

والمراد من الاستعمال نقل اللفظ من موضوعه الأصلي إلى معناه المجازي شرعاً وغلبة الاستعمال فيه، ومن العادة نقله إلى معناه المجازي عرفاً. (الأشباه والنظائر: ۱/۲٦۸، ط: إدارة القرآن والعلوم الإسلامية).

On the origin of the name Baghdad it is written that "Bagh" was the name of an idol they used to worship, and dād in Persian means a gift — that is, "the gift of the idol". But this meaning has fallen into disuse, and so using the name is permissible. Tārīkh Baghdād states:

بغداد وكان لهم صنم يعبدونه بالمشرق يقال له "البغ" فقال: بغ، داد (أعطاني الصنم)، والفقهاء يكرهون هذا الاسم من أجل هذا وسماها أبو جعفر مدينة السلام لأن دجلة يقال لها وادي السلام. (تاريخ بغداد: ۱/۵۸، ومثله في معجم البلدان: ۱/٤۵٦، وتهذيب الأسماء واللغات للامام النووي رحمه الله: ۱/۱۰۱۵).

But to say it out of reverence for that day and out of heartfelt love for it is unbelief:

والإعطاء باسم النيروز والمهرجان لا يجوز وإن قصد تعظيمه كما يعطيه المشركون يكفر. (الدر المختار: ٦/۷۵٤، سعيد).

At a session of the Islamic Fiqh Academy many noble scholars and eminent muftis wrote and presented detailed papers on this subject, and the substance of them was later published in book form. It seems appropriate to give something of a summary of it, so consider the following:

Congratulating non-Muslims on their festivals:

Most of the scholars who wrote papers hold that it is not in order to congratulate non-Muslims on their festivals, because it entails showing reverence for their idolatrous customs and festivals.

There is no harm in offering congratulations:

There are also some paper-writers who hold the view that there is no harm in congratulating non-Muslims on their festivals; indeed, that it is better to do so out of a feeling of communal harmony, with the intention of goodwill, and in order to win hearts.

Some have made a further point, namely that a congratulation is not a supplication — the word āshīrvād is used for a supplication — and that it is more fitting to use the word badhāī in place of mubārak-bād. If there is fear of harm or of a rift in relations, the word badhāī or something similar may be used.

Some of the paper-writers hold it to be in order to congratulate them subject to certain conditions and restrictions, for example:

(1) Social good manners require that if they congratulate us on our festivals, we too should congratulate them on theirs — but on condition that a man himself holds no belief in them. Further, this may fall under the general sense of the verse:

وإذا حييتم بتحية فحيوا بأحسن منها أو ردوها"

(2) It is permissible if it does not clash with the rulings of Islam and does not entail resembling them.

(3) Under necessity and compulsion there is no harm in congratulating him in the manner of

لكم دينكم ولي دين

(4) Congratulating non-Muslims on their festivals is permissible if it is done with the intention that an atmosphere of religious hatred will be brought to an end and that feelings of love and sympathy towards Muslims will grow in non-Muslim society.

(5) Likewise, if someone offers congratulations in order to ward off harm, or for the religious good of that unbeliever — that is, in the hope of his being guided — it is permissible. (Ghayr Muslim Mamālik mēn Ābād Musalmānōn ke Kuchh Aham Masāʾil, pp. 125–128, published by IFA Publications).
