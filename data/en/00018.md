### TITLE
Was Jibrīl (peace be upon him) the teacher of the Prophet ﷺ?

### QUESTION
According to the sounder view, the one referred to in ﴿علمه شديد القوى﴾ is Jibrīl the Trustworthy. So is it correct to call Jibrīl (peace be upon him) the teacher of the Prophet ﷺ, or not?

### ANSWER
In ordinary usage a teacher is someone who, besides passing on words he has received, adds his own reasoning and deductions to them. Jibrīl (peace be upon him) only delivered the speech of Allah, that is, the revelation. This is why most commentators have explained "علمه" in the sense of conveying, delivering or bringing down: ﴿علمه شديد القوي﴾ carries the meaning of "بلغه" and "أنزله".

For this reason Jibrīl (peace be upon him) is not called the teacher of the Prophet ﷺ. One may certainly say "علمه شديد القوي" or "علمه جبريل", but there the customary sense of teaching is not intended; what is meant is conveying and bringing down.

Consider what Tanwīr al-Adhhān states:

﴿علمه شديد القوى﴾ أي: نزل به عليه وقرأه عليه وبينه له. (تنوير الأذهان: ٤/ ۱۷۲).

Taysīr al-Karīm al-Raḥmān states:

﴿علمه شديد القوى﴾ أي: نزل بالوحي علٰي رسول الله ﷺ جبريل عليه السلام. (تيسير الكريم الرحمن في تفسير كلام المنان، ص: ۷٦۰).

Mawāhib al-Raḥmān states:

The revelation that was sent down upon him — the One Mighty in Power taught it to him, that is, delivered it to him. (مواہب الرحمن: ۸/ ۵۲)۔

Furthermore, in the hadith of Jibrīl the Prophet ﷺ said: "فإنه جبرئيل أتاكم ليعلمكم دينكم". (رواه مسلم).

He did not say: "أتاني يعلمني".

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband records:

Question: Was Jibrīl the teacher of the Prophet ﷺ, or merely one who conveyed and carried the message?

Answer: It is established by the definitive texts of the sharia that Allah Most High Himself taught the Prophet ﷺ, and that his nurturer and teacher is directly the hand of divine power. Jibrīl in between was no more than the means by which the message was conveyed — just as in large congregations the worshippers bow and prostrate on hearing the voice of the one relaying the imam's call, yet that man is not called their imam. Again, if a teacher posts the answer to a problem, we do not call the postman a teacher or instructor. This is also why Jibrīl had no authority to alter the words.

The proof of this lies in the verses of Sūrat al-ʿAlaq themselves: alongside the word "أقرأ" the words "باسم ربك" also occur, which points to the fact that Allah Most High is his nurturer and that it is He who will teach him. Then in ﴿اقرأ وربك الأكرم الذي علم بالقلم علم الإنسان ما لم يعلم﴾ Allah has presented Himself as the teacher, making it clear that He alone will teach him. In truth this verse is an answer to what the Prophet ﷺ had said at the beginning — that he was not a reader. To this it was said: by nature you are not a reader, but your Lord is so generous that He teaches the literate by means of the pen; in the same way He can teach without the medium of pen and writing at all.

Rūḥ al-Maʿānī states:

فكما علم سبحانه القاري بواسطة الكتابة بالقلم يعلمك بدونها وحقيقة الكرم إعطاء ما ينبغي لا لغرض (إلى قوله) والإشعار بأنه تعالى يعلمه عليه الصلاة والسلام من العلوم ما لا يحيط به العقول ما لا يخفى. (روح المعاني: ۳/ ۱۸۰).

It is therefore clear that Jibrīl was not the teacher or instructor of the Prophet ﷺ; rather, Allah Himself, Mighty and Sublime, took charge of teaching him. (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند، از مفتی محمد شفیع صاحب رحمہ اللہ: پنجم و ششم، ص ۱٤۹ ۔ ۱۵۱، مکتبہ امدادیہ)۔

In short, Jibrīl (peace be upon him) was not the teacher of the Prophet ﷺ; he held only the rank of one who conveys the message, an envoy and a courier. Indeed, the Noble Qur'an shows that a human being, that is Ādam (peace be upon him), was the teacher of the angels.

قال الله تعالى: ﴿وعلم آدم الأسماء كلها فلما أنبأهم بأسمائهم﴾ (سورة البقرة: ۳۲، ۳۳).
