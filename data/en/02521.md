### TITLE
"آراء المتخصصين والمفتين في تحقيق جلاتين" — the sharia ruling on gelatine in the light of the researches of expert scientists

### QUESTION
In a gelatine factory gelatine is made from various hides, of which about 90 to 95 per cent are the hides of animals that are not slaughtered in the manner of the sharia and all of whose parts are unfit to be eaten. The hides of these very animals are cleaned in different machines in different places, their hair is taken off, and they are kept in limewater until the hide becomes completely clean. Then it is put into great iron tanks and melted down; then it is set and made into strands like threads and then into something like sugar. Now the matter to be enquired into is this: is there permission to use gelatine of this kind in foods or not?

### ANSWER
The whole of the question set out above turns on the transformation of substance and the change of essence. If a change of essence comes about while the hides and bones are being taken through the various stages, then eating it and using it in foods will be permissible; otherwise not. For if the reality and essence of those things which the sharia had declared forbidden is changed, the earlier ruling does not remain. Those particular and fundamental attributes of a thing by which that thing is identified and recognised are the very reality and essence of that thing.

Contemporary scientists differ over the change of essence in gelatine. Most authorities have established through their researches that the reality of the hides and bones of those animals from whose collagen gelatine is made does not remain in the gelatine; rather it is changed into a new thing. For this reason there will be scope for using it.

The research of some scientists, however, is that the transformation of substance is not found in gelatine. On this basis, in view of the difference of opinion, it is better and more cautious to keep away from using gelatine of this kind.

The reality of the transformation of substance and the change of essence:

What the transformation of a reality means according to the jurists is that the first sense and referent of a thing either perishes altogether and is changed into another sense and reality, or that some of the parts of its sense and reality and its particular properties perish.

Note what the erudite Ibn al-Humām (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Fatḥ al-Qadīr:

The writing of Ḥaḍrat Mufti Kifāyat Allāh (may the mercy of Allah Most High be upon him) on the transformation of a reality is an eye-opener. Please consider it:

What is meant by the transformation of a reality is that the thing itself leaves its own reality and is changed into some other reality — as when wine becomes vinegar, or blood becomes musk, or sperm becomes a lump of flesh, and so on. In these cases the wine has itself left its reality of being wine, the blood its reality of being blood, and the sperm its reality of being sperm, and they have been changed into other realities. The ruling that the reality has changed can be given only when the particular properties of the first, transformed reality do not remain in it — as is found in the examples mentioned, since after it has become vinegar the particular properties of the wine are entirely removed.

The removal of some of the properties, or their not being perceptible on account of their small quantity, does not bring about a transformation. The jurists have expressly stated that if some wine is mixed into flour and it is kneaded and a loaf is baked, that loaf is impure; or if a tola or two of wine or urine is mixed into a pitcher or two of water, that water is impure — even though no effect of that tiny quantity of wine will be perceptible in the loaf or in the water. But since in these cases the wine has not itself left its reality, the ruling of impurity remains; and its not being perceptible is on account of the smallness of its parts, for the parts of the wine are few and those of the flour many, and so it is not perceptible in the loaf. This, then, is mixture and not transformation.

In the same way, the survival of certain qualities of the transformed reality that are not particular to it does not prevent transformation — as, even after wine has become vinegar, its thinness remains, or some greasiness of impure oil remains in soap; because thinness is not particular to the reality of wine, nor greasiness to the reality of oil. So when giving a ruling that the rulings have changed on account of the transformation of substance, it is necessary to proceed with much reflection and caution, for confusion often arises between transformation and mixture, and transformation is taken for mixture or mixture for transformation. واللہ الموفق ۔ (کفایت المفتی: ۲/۳۳۳، ط: دار الاشاعت، کراچی) ۔

Ḥakīm al-Ummat Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānawī (may Allah illumine his resting place) writes:

An account of the change of essence: the rulings too change with a change of essence. For example, the juice of grapes is pure, but when it has become another thing, that is, wine, it has become impure; and when the wine has again become another thing, that is, vinegar, it has become pure. The meaning of a change of essence is that from one thing there comes into being another thing whose ruling is quite the opposite of the first thing. For example, an impure thing has been transformed into such a thing as is pure, so the impure thing has become pure — as manure is impure, but when it has become earth, and earth is a pure thing, it has become pure; or an egg is pure, but it has become blood, and blood is an impure thing, so the egg has become impure; and when the clot of that blood has become flesh, and flesh is a pure thing, it has become pure again. And if the transformation is into such a thing whose ruling is the same as its ruling was before the transformation, then that same ruling will remain: if it was pure it remains pure, and if it was impure it remains impure. For example, a pure bone is burnt and becomes ash: a transformation has indeed taken place, but the ruling remained the same, because ash too is pure. And if sperm has become blood, a transformation has indeed taken place, but from impure to impure, and the ruling remained as before; whereas when the clot has become flesh it has become pure, because a clot of flesh is pure. And if the transformation itself is incomplete — that is, another thing distinct from the first thing has not come into being but only a change of one degree has occurred — then the rulings will not change, as when a loaf is baked from impure wheat, so that instead of the form of wheat the form of a loaf has come about; but this is not regarded as becoming another thing. (بہشتی زیور، نواں حصہ، ص ۷۸۲، ط: دار الاشاعت) ۔

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him), however, has restricted the purity of things by the transformation of a reality to cases of general affliction: that is, where there is general affliction in a thing, there the ruling of purity will be given, but where there is no general affliction in a thing, there the ruling of purity will not be given. An explanation of the general affliction in the use of gelatine:

In the present age gelatine is used in great quantity, and it is so exceedingly widespread that everyone, high and low, is confronted with it, and everyone has need to find out the question of its lawfulness or unlawfulness. As to how widespread its use is, the Encyclopaedia Britannica says of it: gelatine is used for making jellied meats and soups, toffees and so forth, for moulded sweet dishes such as jelly and other such foods, for frothy foods such as ice cream, and for making a mixture of various kinds of oils and fats with water.

The statements of other informed people and experts on the matter are these:

A Muslim organisation of South Africa, the South African National Halaal Authority, has written of it that it is used a great deal in various sweets and in things made from milk. Other experts say that gelatine is used in the various kinds of cream applied to the head and the face and in cosmetic products. If we turn to medicines, there are more than six hundred medicines in which gelatine is used, and among these medicines capsules and cough medicines are the most numerous. Besides these, gelatine is also used for photographic film, paper, dyes and lithography (printing from stone or metal). In the information on the internet it is stated that gelatine has countless properties — such as making jelly, thickening a thing and setting it, assisting in its mouldability for making film, and thickening things — on account of which it is used in the following industries:

Food (sweets and things made from milk, meat products and so on)

The pharmaceutical industry.

Photography.

Countless technical uses.

In medicine gelatine is generally used in making capsules; both soft and hard kinds of capsule are made from it. Besides these it is also used in making paste, vinegar and coffee, and it is also used in artificial juice. (Research presented by Dr Asghar Ali of Jāmiʿah Hamdard, New Delhi.)

Another explanation is that, according to the experts, hard capsules are generally made from bone and from the layer of pig meat, because gelatine made from pig meat produces transparency and elasticity in a capsule. So much for how general the use of gelatine is. It is plain that hardly anyone can escape, such that occasion should not arise for him to use gelatine in one way or another; on the contrary, everyone does have to deal with it, or may have to. (جدید فقہی تحقیقات، ص ۱۸۲ تا ۱۸۳) ۔

Please consider a summary of the research of the expert scientists:

(1) Dr Muhammad Aslam Parvez:

Collagen belongs to the family of the proteins, and among the kinds of protein it is the fibrous protein that is found in our muscles. A third of the whole protein present in the body of every vertebrate animal is collagen; it is this protein that is found in the greatest quantity in the bodies of animals. Its fibres are sticky; they do not dissolve in water and are indigestible. Like other proteins it too is made of amino acids. Of the amino acids, two which are named glycine and alanine are found in collagen in a quantity that is found in no other protein. The quantity of the amino acid named proline is also greater in collagen. In collagen there is an amino acid named hydroxyproline which is found only in collagen; this amino acid is found only in collagen and in another fibrous protein named elastin, and it is on account of hydroxyproline that there is a certain hardness in them. The amino acid that is found in collagen has very little nutritional value. Gelatine is made by dissolving collagen, and this dissolving is called hydrolysis, that is, that particular process of dissolution in which water is used — for "lysis" means to break and "hydro" means water, that is, to dissolve a thing with the help of water. The outward structure, shape, colour, taste and smell of gelatine — every one of these — is different from collagen, because after passing through a chemical process and after being dissolved a new thing comes into existence. Gelatine is a chemical substance and a protein of a distinct kind which is different from collagen. It is a scientific fact that the substance of gelatine and gelatine itself are altogether different things: not one of the particular substances found in collagen is present in gelatine.

(2) Dr ʿUmar Afḍal:

The summary of his research is that, after passing through the chemical process and being dissolved, only a fluid substance remains, which can be set when it cools. In the gelatine that is prepared neither any animal's hide nor its trace remains.

(3) Dr Asghar Ali:

There are three methods of making gelatine: (a) After a dried bone or hide has been cleaned, it is kept for ten to fifteen days in 5% HCl (a kind of acid), whereupon it takes the form of a chemical. Then this chemical is kept for four or five hours in 10% lime. After that it is taken out of the lime and put into hot water; then it is dried, and after its moisture has been driven off it is ground and gelatine is made from it.

(b) A calf's hide is washed and kept in 10% lime for six to twelve hours; then it is taken out of the lime and put into hot water, and then gelatine is made from it by the first method given above.

(c) A pig's hide is cleaned and washed and left in 1–5% HCl acid for ten to thirty hours; then gelatine is obtained from it by the method given above. Summarised from (جدید فقہی تحقیقات، ص ۷۱ تا ۹۵) ۔

We have also received the research of another scientist, who holds that there is no change of reality.

(4) Dr Muhammad Anṣār Ḥusain:

(who belongs to the department of hide technology). A summary of his research is set out below:

(1) A hide has three layers. In the first layer are the hair and the pores, and this layer is one per cent of the whole hide.

The second layer is 98 per cent of the thickness of the hide, and it is in this layer that there is a particular substance which is called collagen.

The third layer is of flesh; in it there are also veins, sinews and muscles. This layer too is one per cent of the hide.

(2) Collagen is precisely gelatine. (3) In the process of making gelatine, the use of acid, keeping it in limewater, boiling, heating and the process of evaporation — all this is done only so that the non-collagen parts that are present in the hide as a whole along with the collagen, that is, blood, fat, fibres and so on, may be removed and pure collagen obtained. It is this pure and transparent collagen that is given the name of gelatine.

(4) The process of making gelatine is a chemical process, but the effect of this process is upon the substances attached to the collagen, and these are removed; the collagen itself is not affected by this chemical change. And so on. Summarised from the paper (کولاجن جلاٹین اور تبدیل ماہیت) ۔

The views of contemporary honoured scholars on the researches of the scientists:

(1) Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh: to all appearances this case seems to be one of a change of essence, and there appears to be scope for likening it to the turning of wine into vinegar, since not only its qualities but its shape and form too have changed.

(2) Mawlānā Muhammad ʿUbayd Allāh Asʿadī: if the hide or bone is of a pig, then merely grinding it does not make its use sound. The use of the bone of another animal, or of a tanned hide, is sound. If in gelatine there is any form of extraction from hide, it can be sound after tanning; and if gelatine is made in such a way that the bone dries out, or that after the tanning of the hide these are then dissolved and turned into another form, then this is a change of essence — and in dissolving them and turning them into another form there will be no condition of tanning or of drying either.

(3) Mawlānā Akhtar Imām ʿĀdil: the case of gelatine too, according to the questionnaire, appears to be one of a change of essence.

(4) Mufti Jamīl Aḥmad Naẕīrī: of the methods of making gelatine that have come to light from the writings of the experts, the definition of a transformation of essence does not hold true of any one of them. Even so, despite there being no transformation of essence, certain forms of gelatine are permissible:

(a) Gelatine from the dry bone of an animal other than a pig is permissible, whether it was slaughtered or died of itself.

(b) Gelatine from the moist bone and the hide of a slaughtered animal is permissible.

(c) Gelatine from the intestine of an animal whose flesh is eaten may be used.

(5) Mawlānā Irshād al-Ḥaqq Aʿẓamī: if gelatine is made from an impure thing, then there are two aspects to it. Number (a): gelatine is in origin a sticky and greasy substance which is a sign of life and a locus of impurity, and it has been preserved by a particular process. Keeping this aspect in view, gelatine made from impure things is impermissible.

(b) The position of gelatine is clearer than that of the filth which falls into earth and water and becomes mud, upon which the scholars have given the ruling of purity. For this reason it is not free of the possibility of purity; and then there is general affliction as well, and so permission may be given for its use.

(6) Mawlānā Muhammad Muṣṭafā ʿAbd al-Quddūs Nadwī: the case mentioned in the question will come under a change of essence, because after dissolving, the reality of hide or bone comes to an end; and among the causes of a change of essence is burning as well.

(7) Mawlānā Abū al-ʿĀṣ Waḥīdī: certainly the process that gelatine undergoes will come under a change of essence.

(8) Mawlānā Muhammad Irshād al-Qāsimī: three methods of making gelatine are mentioned in the question. Because the principles and particular rulings of fiqh and the definition of transformation are found in them, they fall under a transformation of essence; and in view of the general need and the general affliction, it is permissible, because its nature, shape, smell, taste, state and condition have changed.

(9) Mawlānā Muhammad Muṣṭafā Qāsimī: if in fact matters are as gelatine has been depicted in the questionnaire, then without doubt this will come under a transformation of substance and a change of essence, and its sale and its being eaten and drunk will be permissible.

(10) Mawlānā Muhammad Burhān al-Dīn Sanbhalī: certain experts in this field showed this writer a gelatine factory in South Africa, so that by this process its reality changes; but certain scholars there say that the reality does not change.

(11) Mawlānā Zubayr Aḥmad Qāsimī: the explanation and depiction of gelatine that has been given may be accepted as a change of essence and a transformation of substance.

(12) Dr Mawlānā Sulṭān Aḥmad Iṣlāḥī: gelatine (in the light of the material of Dr Asghar Ali) is an extremely advanced form of transformation or change of essence.

(13) Mawlānā Iʿjāz Aḥmad Qāsimī: the account of gelatine that has been given in the questionnaire shows that, by the process of tanning, boiling and exposure to the sun, by the method of dissolving, and by a chemical process and so forth, the reality of the thing — the hide or bone of a carcass — changes altogether.

(14) Mawlānā Khurshīd Aḥmad Aʿẓamī: from the account that has been given about gelatine, it appears on the face of it that its reality and essence do change.

Apart from the authorities mentioned, in the view of certain other scholars a change of essence does not take place in gelatine.

(15) The summary of the detailed fatwa of Mufti Taqī is that in his view the use of gelatine from bone is sound, by analogy with rennet; but as to whether a change of essence takes place in gelatine from hide or not, he is undecided. (از فتوی مفتی تقی، ۱٤، شوال ۱٤۱۷؁ھ) ۔

(16) The finding of Mufti Ashraf, mufti of Jāmiʿah Maḥmūdiyyah Springs, is that a change of essence does take place in it. Accordingly a summary of one of his detailed fatwas is set out as follows:

According to the experts, in the making of gelatine from collagen a physical and chemical change comes into existence, and gelatine becomes a new compound which is altogether distinct from its origin, that is, from collagen.

According to the report

"الشفاء الطبي"

of the medicine company B. Tabs, hide and gelatine are not at all alike in their respective physical and chemical properties and in their outward form; gelatine and collagen are two different things.

According to the experts who prepare food materials, in the process by which gelatine is made from collagen a complete change of essence takes place. For this reason this humble one's inclination is that gelatine is pure and lawful, since along with the change of essence there is general affliction as well.

The decision of the Islamic Fiqh Academy:

The fourteenth fiqh seminar of the Islamic Fiqh Academy was held on 20–22 June 2004 at Dār al-ʿUlūm Sabīl al-Salām, the well-known religious school of Hyderabad, the scholarly and historic city of India. About two hundred and fifty scholars, muftis, and experts in medical science and in pharmacy and drug manufacture from the whole country took part in this seminar, in which delegates from every region were present, from Kashmir to Kerala and from eastern India to central India. Besides India, discerning scholars from Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Iran and Nepal also took part. The resolutions that were passed against this background are as follows:
