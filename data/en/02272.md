### TITLE

The basic principles of partnership on the Islamic pattern

### QUESTION

Respected and honoured Muftī Ṣāḥib,
Please consider the following details of an agreement for doing business on the Islamic pattern:
Two partners wish to do business on the Islamic pattern; what agreement should they draw up, given that the position is as follows: one partner only puts in capital, while the other puts in money and also does the work; the first partner wants an annual profit, while the second wants wages for his work in addition to the profit. The matters to be determined are how the following are to be arranged:
(1) How much capital should each of the two partners put in — the one who does the physical work and the one who does not?
(2) Is it permissible to make the work the responsibility of only one partner or not?
(3) Please explain the division of profit and loss, and the ruling and details on putting the profit back into the partnership.
(4) What is the ruling on a time-limited partnership?
(5) What are the details of paying tax under the law of the country and zakāt under Islamic law?
(6) The partners' salaries, and one of the partners taking money for his own needs?
(7) The partners entering into a loan agreement with someone — that is, if a loan is taken for the business, to whose account will it be reckoned?
(8) What will be the method of distributing the profits?
(9) How will the company's assets and capital be divided?
(10) If one of the partners passes away, does the partnership continue or not?

### ANSWER

(1) The partners are free, by mutual consent, for each partner to put in as much capital as he wishes; no fixed proportion is binding or required of anyone. (الهداية: ۲/٦۳۰).

(2) It is permissible to make the work the responsibility of only one partner, but this is a combination of mushārakah (partnership) and muḍārabah (profit-sharing investment). (The details of this may be seen in اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ۵/٤۷, and this question has already been dealt with briefly under Kitāb al-Muḍārabah.) It will, however, make a difference to the division of profit, as will be explained in detail below.
(3) The basic principles for distributing profits:
(a) For the partnership to be valid it is a condition that the profit ratio be settled as a percentage — how many percent of the profit each partner is to receive: 50 percent each, for example, or 40 percent to one partner and 60 percent to the other, and so on.
٭ It is not, however, permissible or correct to settle a fixed sum for anyone, such as that so-and-so will receive one thousand rand a month.
٭ Likewise it is not permissible to fix a percentage of the capital. For instance, if a partner's capital is one hundred thousand rand and he is to be given 20 percent of that capital, namely 20 thousand rand — this is not permissible.

قال في البدائع: ومنها أن يكون الربح معلوم القدر، فإن كان مجهولاً تفسد الشركة، لأن الربح هو المعقود عليه، وجهالته توجب فساد العقد

ومنها أن يكون الربح جزء اً مشاعاً في الجملة لا معيناً، فإن عينا عشرة أو مائة كانت الشركة فاسدة، لأن العقد يقتضي تحقق الشركة في الربح، والتعيين يقطع الشركة لجواز أن لا يحصل من الربح إلا القدر المعين لا حدهما

الخ. (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/۵۹، سعيد. وكذا في الهداية: ۲/٦۳۲. والفتاوى الهندية: ۲/۳۰۲).

(b) If the two partners agree that each partner's profit will be in the same percentage as the capital he has put in, this is permissible — whether the capital is half and half or one has put in less and the other more, and whether both partners have agreed to work or the work has been settled upon one partner alone.

قال في البدائع: فنقول: إذا شرطا الربح على قدر المالين متساوياً أو متفاضلاً فلا شك أنه يجوز، ويكون الربح بينهما على الشرط سواء شرطا العمل عليهما أو على أحدهما. (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/٦۲، سعيد).

(c) For the partner who also does the work, it is correct to fix a profit greater than his proportion of the capital, whether the other partner works or not. For example, Ḥāmid and Maḥmūd each put in one hundred thousand rand, and the work was also made a condition upon Maḥmūd; it is then permissible and correct to fix 70 percent of the profit for Maḥmūd.
Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

وإن كان المالان متساويين فشرطا لأحدهما فضلاً على ربح ينظر: إن شرط العمل عليهما جميعاً جاز والربح بينهما على الشرط في قول أصحابنا الثلاثة

وإن كان المالان متفاضلين وشرط التساوي في الربح فهو على هذا الخلاف أن ذلك جائز عند أصحابنا الثلاثة، وكان زيادة الربح لأحدهما على قدر رأس ماله بعمله وأنه جائز. (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/٦۲، ٦۳، سعيد. والفتاوى الهندية: ۲/۳۲۰).

(d) For a partner who does no work, it is not permissible or correct, in the view of the majority of scholars, to fix more than his proportion of the capital. For example, Ḥāmid and Maḥmūd each put in one hundred thousand rand and the work was made a condition upon Maḥmūd alone; it is then not permissible to fix more than 50 percent of the profit for Ḥāmid.
Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

وإن كان المالان متساويين

شرطا العمل على أحدهما

وإن شرطاه على أقلهما ربحاً لم يجز، لأن الذي شرطا له الزيادة ليس له في الزيادة مال ولا عمل ولا ضمان

(بدائع الصنائع: ٦/٦۳، سعيد).

See further: (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ۵/٤۷، مشارکہ اور مضاربہ کا اجتماع).
(e) If one partner has put in more capital and the work has also been made a condition upon him, then it is not permissible to divide the profit half and half. For example, Ḥāmid put in one hundred thousand rand and Maḥmūd one hundred and fifty thousand, and the work was also made a condition upon Maḥmūd; a 50 percent division of the profit between the two will then not be permissible.
Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

وإن كان المالان متفاضلين وشرطا التساوي في الربح

شرطا العمل على أحدهما

وإن شرطاه على صاحب الأكثر لم يجز، لأن زيادة الربح في حق صاحب الأقل لا يقابلها مال ولا عمل ولا ضمان. (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/٦۳، سعيد).

٭ Bearing the loss in a partnership:
The jurists are agreed that if a loss occurs in the partnership business, each partner bears the loss in proportion to his capital. For example, Ḥāmid and Maḥmūd are partners; Ḥāmid put in 60 percent and Maḥmūd 40 percent. If, say, a loss of one thousand rand occurred, Ḥāmid would bear 600 and Maḥmūd 400.
This is supported by a report in Muṣannaf ʿAbd al-Razzāq:

عن الشعبي رحمه الله عن علي رضي الله عنه في المضاربة: الوضيعة على المال والربح على ما اصطلحوا عليه، وأما الثوري رحمه الله فذكره

عن علي رضي الله عنه في المضاربة أو الشريكين. (مصنف عبد الرزاق: ۸/۲٤۸، المجلس العلمي).

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

والوضيعة على قدر المالين متساوياً ومتفاضلاً، لأن الوضيعة اسم لجزء هالك من المال، فيتقدر بقدر المال. (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/٦۲، سعيد).

٭ The ruling on putting the profit back into the business:
If the partners do not distribute the profit but put it back into the business instead, this is permissible and correct, and their capital will be reckoned accordingly. For example, Ḥāmid and Maḥmūd each put one hundred thousand rand into the business on a half-and-half basis, and a profit of 50 thousand was made; instead of dividing it they put that 50 thousand back into the business, so each one's capital has now become one hundred and twenty-five thousand.
(4) The ruling on a time-limited partnership:
It is permissible for the partners to settle by mutual consent whatever term for the partnership they wish — two years, three years, and so on.

قال في الشامية نقلاً عن الخانية: وإن وقتا لذلك (أي للشركة) وقتاً بأن قال: ما اشتريت اليوم فهو بيننا صح التوقيت، فما اشتراه بعد اليوم يكون للمشتري خاصة، وكذا لو وقت المضاربة لا نها والشركة توكيل، والوكالة مما يتوقت. (فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۳۱۲، سعيد).

See further (و الفتاوی الہندیۃ: ۲/۳۰۲ ۔ واسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ۵/٤٦، مضاربہ کو ختم کرنا).
(5) Zakāt is not obligatory upon the joint company or business as such; zakāt will be obligatory upon each person according to the extent of his own share.
For further detail see: (فتاوی دار العلوم زکریا جلد سوم، کتاب الزکوۃ).
(6) The jurists differ over fixing a salary for a partner. The details of this have already been given in the chapter on muḍārabah and may be consulted there. The gist is that in the view of Muftī Rashīd Aḥmad Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) it is permissible. For details see: (احسن الفتاوی: ۷/۳۲۱ ۔ ۳۲۸).
(7) If a partner takes a loan for the joint business, and it has also been agreed between them that a loan may be taken when the need arises, then its repayment is binding upon both of them.
Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

ولو استقرض مالاً لزمهما جميعاً، لأنه تملك مال بالعقد، فكان كالصرف، فيثبت في حقه وحق شريكه

. (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/۷۲، سعيد).

(8) The method of distributing the profits has been set out in detail under (3).
(9) The method of settling and dividing the assets:
When the partnership has come to an end — the term has run out, or it has been dissolved for some other reason — there are two possibilities:
(a) The assets are in cash form: first divide the profit in accordance with the agreed condition, then divide the capital according to the respective shares.
(b) The assets are not in cash form but in the form of goods: in that case the market value of the goods should be assessed, and if both partners agree, the goods should be sold and the cash divided; then, after the profit has been divided, the capital should be divided in the proportions in which it was put in.
Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil states:
Every partner has the right to terminate the mushārakah at any time by giving notice to the other partner; by such a notice the mushārakah will be considered terminated.
In that case, if all the assets of the mushārakah are in cash form, they will be divided among the partners according to their shares. But if the assets are not in liquid form, the partners may agree upon one of two courses: either they liquidate the assets (that is, sell them and convert them into cash), or they divide them in their existing state. If there is disagreement among the partners on this matter — some wanting liquidation and others wanting the assets themselves divided in their non-cash form — then the latter course (dividing the assets in their existing state) will be given preference, because after the termination of the mushārakah all the assets are the joint property of the shareholders, and each of those who hold joint ownership of a thing has the right to demand division, or the separating out of his own share, and no one can compel him to liquidate. However, if the assets are such that they cannot be divided and their shares separated out — machinery, for example — then those assets will be sold and the proceeds divided. (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ۵/۳۸، مشارکہ ختم کرنا).
(10) If one of the partners passes away, the partnership comes to an end; if the parties wish to carry on, it will have to be renewed afresh.
Al-Hidāyah states:

وإذا مات أحد الشريكين

بطلت الشركة. (الهداية: ۲/٦۳۵).

Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil states:
If one of the partners passes away during the term of the mushārakah, the mushārakah agreement with the deceased comes to an end. In that case his heirs will have the choice: if they wish they may take back the deceased's share, and if they wish they may keep this mushārakah agreement running. (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ۵/۳۸).
