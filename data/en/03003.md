### TITLE
The era of the Rightly Guided Caliphate upon the way of prophethood

### QUESTION
Did the Rightly Guided Caliphate upon the way of prophethood (khilāfat-e rāshidah ʿalā minhāj al-nubuwwah) last until the time of Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him), or did it come to an end with Ḥaḍrat Ḥasan (may Allah be pleased with him)? After that there was still a caliphate, but it was not upon the way of prophethood — so what was the difference between the two, given that the rulings of the sharīʿah were enforced in both and justice and fairness were available? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
The Rightly Guided Caliphate upon the way of prophethood lasted for thirty years, and its era comes to an end with the close of the caliphate of Ḥaḍrat Ḥasan (may Allah be pleased with him). The order of the caliphate and the length of each caliphate are as follows:

The period of the caliphate of Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah the Exalted be pleased with him): 2 years, 3 months.

The period of the caliphate of Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar al-Fārūq (may Allah the Exalted be pleased with him): 10 years, 6 months.

The period of the caliphate of Ḥaḍrat ʿUthmān Dhū al-Nūrayn (may Allah the Exalted be pleased with him): 12 years.

The period of the caliphate of Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī, the Lion of Allah (may Allah the Exalted be pleased with him): 4 years, 9 months.

The completion of the Rightly Guided Caliphate upon the way of prophethood: the period of the caliphate of Ḥaḍrat Ḥasan (may Allah the Exalted be pleased with him): 6 months.

There is, however, a second view: that even after this, rightly guided caliphs continued to come from time to time and to discharge the affairs of the caliphate upon the way of prophethood, such as Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz (may Allah have mercy on him) and others.

The differences between the Rightly Guided Caliphate upon the way of prophethood and the caliphate that came after it are as follows:

In the Rightly Guided Caliphate upon the way of prophethood the caliph passed his life in the utmost simplicity, making do with the barest sufficiency; later this condition did not remain.

They did not hoard wealth and riches for themselves; indeed, they did not even hoard the wealth of the public treasury. Later the situation changed.

In the Rightly Guided Caliphate upon the way of prophethood the Rightly Guided Caliphs were explicitly given the glad tidings of Paradise and were among the first and foremost of the Emigrants.

In the Rightly Guided Caliphate no caliph appointed his son or a close relative as his successor; later, because of the compulsion of circumstances, this condition did not remain.

In the Rightly Guided Caliphate no caliph arranged bodyguards and protection for himself; later, because of circumstances, the need for this was felt.

Consider the hadith concerning the thirty years of the caliphate. It occurs in Abū Dāwūd Sharīf:

عن سفينة قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: "خلافة النبوة ثلاثون سنة ثم يؤتي الله الملك أو ملكه من يشاء". (سنن أبي داود، رقم: ٤٦٤٦).

In the annotation of Badhl al-Majhūd it is recorded:

فإن أبابكر رضي الله عنه بويع له بعد وفاته ﷺ في أولي الربيعين سنة ۱۱هـ، وتوفي رضي الله تعالى عنه في جمادي الأولي سنة ۱۳هـ، كما في التقريب (۳٤۹۰). وجزم السيوطي في تاريخ الخلفاء (ص ۹۳. ۹٤) بجمادي الأخرى، فبويع لعمر رضي الله عنه باستخلاف من الصديق الأكبر، ثم توفي رضي الله تعالى عنه، واستشهد في ذي الحجة سنة ۲۳هـ، وولي الخلافة عشر سنين ونصفاً، كما في التقريب (٤۹۲۲)، فبويع لعثمان رضي الله عنه، ثم استشهد في ذي الحجة سنة ۳۵هـ، "التقريب" (٤۵۳۵)، وولي ثنتي عشرة سنة، فبويع لعلي رضي الله عنه، ثم استشهد رضي الله عنه في رمضان سنة ٤۰هـ، "التقريب" (٤۷۸۷).

وتوفي الإمام الحسن رضي الله عنه شهيداً بالسم سنة ٤۹هـ، وقيل: سنة ۵۰هـ، وقيل بعدها، كذا في التقريب (۱۲۷۰) وقال السيوطي في تاريخ الخلفاء (ص ۲۱۷، ۲۱۸): ولي الحسن الخلافة بعد قتل أبيه بمبايعة أهل الكوفة، فأقام فيها ستة أشهر وأياماً، ثم سار إليه معاوية رضي الله عنه. (تعليق بذل المجهود: ۱۳/۵۸، باب في الخلفاء، ط: دار البشائر الإسلامية).

Kifāyat al-Muftī records:

The narrations mentioning twelve caliphs are authentic, but their meaning is that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ gave the news that the strength and grandeur of Islam would remain established up to twelve caliphs. The meaning is not that the twelve caliphs would be Rightly Guided Caliphs; for the duration of the Rightly Guided Caliphate — the caliphate of prophethood — has been stated to be thirty (30) years. (کفایت المفتی: ۱/۱٤۰، ط: دار الاشاعت)۔

In Islāmī ʿAqāʾid, Dr Muftī ʿAbd al-Wāḥid writes:

If the national and communal system of a government is upon the way of prophethood, such a government is called the Rightly Guided Caliphate, because a government that is entirely upon the way (path) of prophethood will certainly be "rightly guided" — guidance and right direction through and through. And a rightly guided caliph is one who is a model of the Prophet in knowledge, righteous action, abstinence and piety: outwardly a ruler and inwardly a friend of Allah of the highest rank, his sainthood being a reflection of the Prophet's prophethood. So the person in whom rulership and sainthood are both gathered together, that person is a rightly guided caliph. If the ordering and management of a government is not upon the way of prophethood, then, if justice, fairness, trustworthiness and honesty predominate in it, that government will be called a just government; otherwise it will be called an oppressive and tyrannical government. (اسلامی عقائد، ص ۲۰۸، از ڈاکٹر مفتی عبد الواحد)۔
