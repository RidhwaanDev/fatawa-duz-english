### TITLE
The ruling on writing the girl's name on a wedding invitation

### QUESTION
Nowadays the names of the bridegroom and the bride are written on the wedding invitation for the purpose of identification. Some people raise the objection that writing the girl's name is not correct. Is it established anywhere from sound hadiths or from the reports of the Companions that writing the girl's name on an invitation has been forbidden? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the era of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and the noble Companions there were no cards or the like at all. Yes, from the style of the Noble Qur'an it emerges that the matter of women is founded upon concealment; accordingly, wherever it became necessary to mention women, their names were avoided — for example:

امرأة فرعون، امرأة نوح، امرأة لوط،

and

فلما قضي زيد منها وطراً زوجناكها،

and so forth. Yes, only the name of Ḥaḍrat Maryam bint ʿImrān is mentioned. Therefore, publishing women's names on announcements and the like is not a good thing. However, if in a wedding invitation the names of the bridegroom and bride, together with their fathers' names, are written for the sake of identification, there is no great harm in it — especially since these cards are distributed among relatives and near ones.

Furthermore, the women Companions used to give their names for the sake of identification. Once Ḥaḍrat Zaynab, the wife of Ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him), came to the Prophet ﷺ to ask about a matter and gave her name, Zaynab. The Messenger ﷺ asked, "Which Zaynab?" She said, امرأۃ ابن مسعود. This was for identification. Consider the hadith in Bukhārī Sharīf:

عن زينب امرأة عبد الله رضي الله عنه، قالت: كنت في المسجد فرأيت النبي ﷺ فقال: تصدقن ولو من حليكن وكانت زينب تنفق على عبد الله وأيتام في حجرها قال: فقالت لعبد الله، سل رسول الله ﷺ أيجزي عني أن أنفق عليك وعلى أيتام في حجري من الصدقة فقال: سلي أنت رسول الله ﷺ، فانطلقت إلى النبي ﷺ فوجدت امرأة من الأنصار على الباب

فقال: من هما قال: زينب قال: أي الزيانب، قال: امرأة عبد الله قال: نعم، لها أجران أجر القرابة وأجر الصدقة. (رواه البخاري، رقم: ۱٤٦٦).

Yes — despite extensive searching, no prohibition against writing the girl's name on a wedding card was found in any hadith or report. Nevertheless, one should avoid publishing girls' names on announcements without need.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
