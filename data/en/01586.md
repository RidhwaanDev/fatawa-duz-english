### TITLE
The ruling on the wife of a man missing in war

### QUESTION
Because a state of war has arisen in a certain country, some people have gone missing — that is, for several years there has been no trace of them: there is no news of where they are living, nor any news of whether they are alive or dead. Are such men to be counted as falling under the ruling of the missing person (mafqūd al-khabar) or not?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about these people undoubtedly fall under the ruling of the missing person. Concerning the missing person the Hanafi scholars have given their fatwa according to the Mālikī school, and according to the Mālikī school the qāḍī will, after four years, issue a ruling of death upon him subject to certain conditions, and will give the woman permission to remarry.

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah Most High have mercy on him) said: whenever the qāḍī, from the circumstantial indications and the situation, forms a strong conviction that these people who went missing on the field of battle are no longer alive, at that point a ruling of death will be passed upon them, and their wives will be permitted to remarry once the qāḍī has given his ruling. (In our own time, when there is no sharia judge, the body that stands in his place — the Jamʿiyyat al-ʿUlamāʾ or the Jamāʿat al-Muslimīn — will, after investigating the matter, pass the ruling of death and grant permission to remarry.) (مستفاد از امداد المفتین: ص۵۵٦، دار الاشاعت، کراچی)

Majmūʿah Qawānīn-e Islāmī states:

(A useful point) The ruling that the wife of a missing man must wait a further four years is by agreement necessary in the case where the woman is able to pass such a period in patience, endurance and chastity. But if this is not possible — that is, the woman expresses the fear that she will fall into sin, and after waiting a long time for the missing man she has been driven to submit her application in a state in which she is no longer able to bear it patiently — then in this case there is also room, in accordance with the Mālikī school, for the four-year term to be reduced; for when there is a severe fear of the woman falling into sin, then in their view a separation is permissible after a minimum of one year of waiting. This separation will be a revocable divorce, and in this case the wife of the missing man will observe the waiting period of divorce rather than the waiting period of widowhood. (مجموعہ قوانین اسلامی ۱۹٦ بحوالہ الحیلۃ الناجزۃ ٦۹ ۔ ۸۰) ۔
