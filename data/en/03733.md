### TITLE
The ruling on the rights to a translation that was commissioned for a fee

### QUESTION
Zayd said to ʿAmr: "Translate a few books for us, and in exchange we shall pay you a sum" — that is, we shall give the wage for doing the translation. ʿAmr agreed, made the translation for a wage, and Zayd paid him his remuneration. Now Zayd has demanded that ʿAmr hand over to him the copies of all the translations and delete the files of them from his computer. But ʿAmr says: "I cannot do this, because translations of the Qurʾān and ḥadīth are needed." Is Zayd's demand correct, and can ʿAmr refuse it or not? بینوا بالدلیل ۔

### ANSWER
When ʿAmr completed his work and also received the money, the hire contract came to an end. The translation that ʿAmr made has now become the right of Zayd, and rights are like property. Therefore it will not be permissible for ʿAmr to keep this copy, or to benefit from it, or to sell it onward — in all of that there is loss to Zayd. And Zayd's stipulating this and demanding that the files be deleted is valid and correct.

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh says that there are three fundamental elements for something to be property:

(1) First, that it be permissible in the Sacred Law. (2) Second, that the thing be capable of being benefited from. (3) The third element is that whatever thing has become current in custom for buying and selling is property. And it would be no exaggeration to say that all four schools of fiqh are agreed upon this. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/171).

Mawlānā ʿUmar ʿĀbidīn Qāsimī writes:

So the upshot of this entire discussion is: (1) In the view of the majority, usufructs and rights, besides property, may also be bought and sold. (2) If it were indeed necessary for the object of sale to be property, then from a careful consideration of the wordings of the jurists it becomes clear that rights and usufructs are themselves included within property. This is not only the position of the three imāms; rather, parallels to it are found among the Ḥanafīs as well. (3) For a thing to be property, custom and usage carry fundamental importance. (4) There is no single fixed form of being capable of storage; a thing's being legally protected is also sufficient for it to be capable of storage. (Ḥuqūq awr un kī Kharīd o Farokht, p. 141).

In Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā it is stated:

Question: Is it permissible to take remuneration for the right of authorship or not? A publisher or press prepares a project of work on some subject and engages the services of scholars for it, and some remuneration is fixed for them which is given in exchange for that composing and authoring, arranging and researching, or any scholarly work. This practice is current for the most part among internationally renowned presses and publishers.

Answer: It is permissible to take remuneration for the right of authorship. As for the first case mentioned in the question, its ruling is that there is no disagreement about its permissibility; its ruling is the same as that of remuneration for leading the prayer and for teaching. Moreover, the composition will be the property of the publisher or press, because it paid the wage for that work. (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 5/322).
