### TITLE
Zakat on shares in a property company

### QUESTION
A man holds a share in a property company. Should he pay zakat on its income, or on the value of the share, or on both?

### ANSWER
In the case described, if the company trades, zakat is due both on the capital value of the share and on its profit. If the company does not trade and only collects rent, then zakat on its shares means zakat on the profit; there is no zakat on the capital sum.

Consider what Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

If the company trades, zakat is due on the sum invested. If it is a company that collects rent, there is no zakat on the sum invested; zakat is due on the profit received. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 4/257, "Zakat on shares", Dār al-Ishāʿat).

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

Zakat is due on shares if the company trades — for instance, if it sells cloth, iron, purchased goods and the like, or sells cement, or supplies electricity (as an electric company does). In that case zakat is due both on the capital value of the shares (their price) and on the profit from them. But if the company does not trade and only collects rent — as a tram company or a railway company does — then zakat is due on the profit from its shares and not on the capital sum. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 2/14).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

If the land, buildings and machinery of a factory are not themselves for sale but are a means of earning income, no zakat is due on them. Zakat will be due on the income earned from them, treated like cash under the rule of the sharia. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 9/427, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

Consider what al-Hidāyah states:

وليس في دور السكنى وثياب البدن وأثاث المنازل ودواب الركوب وعبيد الخدمة وسلاح الاستعمال زكاة لأنها مشغولة بالحاجة الأصلية وليست بنامية وعلى هذا كتب العلم لا هلها وآلات المحترفين لما قلنا. (الهداية: ۱/۱۸٦، كتاب الزكاة).
