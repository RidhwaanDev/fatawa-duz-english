### TITLE
Using a steel chain to hang a taʿwīdh

### QUESTION
If the string of a taʿwīdh has broken, or breaks again and again, is it permissible to use a steel chain or not? And what is the ruling if the case of the taʿwīdh is made of glass? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
The jurists have written that if the steel chain is not itself the object, then using it is permissible — just as it is permissible to use a steel chain for a watch, because the chain is not the object but is subordinate to it. Furthermore, this ruling concerns the use of it by men; for women it is permissible without any restriction. But if the taʿwīdh together with its case looks like an ornament, then it will not be permissible for a man to use it.

The Prophet ﷺ wore a coat of iron mail. See Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, which states:

عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنه قال: قال النبي ﷺ وهو في قبة: اللهم إني أنشدك عهدك ووعدك، اللهم إن شئت لم تعبد بعد اليوم فأخذ أبو بكر رضي الله تعالى عنه بيده، فقال: حسبك يا رسول الله، فقد ألححت على ربك وهو في الدرع

الخ. (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۲۹۱۵).

Sunan al-Tirmidhī states:

عن الزبير بن العوام قال: كان على النبي ﷺ درعان يوم أحد

الخ. (سنن الترمذي، رقم: ۳۷۳۸).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ولا يكره في المنطقة حلقة حديد أو نحاس وعظم. (الدر المختار: ٦/۳۵۹، سعيد).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā records: There are two ways in which a chain may be used. (1) Wearing it is itself the object, as people wear it for adornment. (2) It is not itself the object, but is used for fastening something else. In the first case its use is impermissible, and in the second it is permissible without any dislike. The back of the watch itself, which rests against the wrist, is likewise made of some metal; but since that too is not itself the object, it is permissible. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 8/213).

A ring of iron, copper or bone may be fitted to a waistband. For the details of the evidences see: (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 7/264–265).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
