### TITLE
Taking back an endowment once the temporary need has ended

### QUESTION
A man endowed a piece of land for a girls' madrasah. Then, because the madrasah needed a way of access, he gave another plot as well. Later it turned out that the madrasah did not need that second plot. We wish to know whether that second plot — which had its own separate number and boundaries — became an endowment or not, and whether it may now be given back or not.

### ANSWER
If he handed the plot over to the madrasah because of the madrasah's need and said the words, "I have given this plot to the madrasah", then the madrasah has been made its owner and he cannot now take it back:

ولو قال: وهبت داري لمسجد أو أعطيتها له صح ويكون تمليكاً فيشترط التسليم كما لو قال: وقفت الخ. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۲/٤٦۰، وكذا في الفتاوى التاتارخانية: ۵/۸۵۳).

And if he used the words of endowment, then too he cannot take this plot back.

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

وجعله أبو يوسف رحمه الله كالإعتاق فلذلك لم يشترط القبض والإفراز أي فيلزم عنده بمجرد القول كالإعتاق بجامع إسقاط الملك. (فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۳٤۹).

والأخذ بقول الثاني أحوط وأسهل بحر، وفي الدرر وصدر الشريعة وبه يفتي وأقره المصنف رحمه الله. (الدر المختار: ٤/۳۵۱، كتاب الوقف، سعيد).

These passages make it clear as daylight that, according to the view on which the fatwa is given, it is not necessary in an endowment to hand possession over to a trustee or to set the endowed land apart. Once an endowment is complete, selling it for a price is altogether forbidden. There is, however, scope in some cases for exchanging one piece of land for another, but the case in question is not one of the cases in which there is such scope.

اعلم أن الاستبدال على ثلاثة وجوه: الأول أن يشترطه الواقف لنفسه أو لغيره أو لنفسه وغيره فالاستبدال فيه جائز على الصحيح، وقيل اتفاقاً. والثاني: أن لا يشترطه سواء شرط عدمه أو سكت لكن صار بحيث لا ينتفع به بالكلية بأن لا يحصل منه شيء أصلاً أو لا يفي مؤنته فهو أيضاً جائز على الأصح إذا كان بإذن القاضي ورأيه المصلحة فيه. والثالث: أن لا يشترطه أيضاً ولكن فيه نفع في الجملة وبدله خير منه ريعاً ونفعاً وهذا لا يجوز استبداله على الأصح المختار. (فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۳۸٤، سعيد).

The gist of this passage is that there are three cases of exchanging an endowment:

(1) The endower lays down in advance, for himself or for someone else, the condition that he will have the right to exchange a particular endowment.

(2) Or the endowment has become altogether useless, so that no benefit of any kind can be derived from it, or it cannot even meet its own upkeep; in that case it may be exchanged by order of the judge.

(3) The condition of exchange was not laid down, but the substitute is better than it, that is, the benefit of the substitute is greater; in this case exchanging it is not permissible. From the foregoing details it is clear that giving this endowed plot back, or exchanging it, or selling it is not permissible; rather it will remain the property of the madrasah as before.

It should be borne in mind that an endowment does not come to an end when a temporary need comes to an end.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
