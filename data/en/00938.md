### TITLE
Zakat on jewellery given as a pledge

### QUESTION
Who owes the zakat on jewellery that has been given as a pledge — the pledgor or the pledgee?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, the owner of the jewellery is the pledgor; with the pledgee it is only held as a trust. Zakat is therefore binding on the pledgor.

The details of when zakat becomes necessary are as follows:

(1) If the loan is 50 thousand and the value of the jewellery is 70 thousand, then after deducting the 50 thousand of debt, zakat is necessary on 20 thousand only.

(2) If the loan is 50 thousand and the value is 52 thousand, then the remaining two thousand is below the nisab, so no zakat is necessary. If, however, adding other cash and the like brings it up to the nisab, zakat is necessary.

(3) If the value of the jewellery is less than the loan, no zakat is necessary.

Consider what Radd al-Muḥtār states:

قلت: لكن أرجع شيخ مشايخنا السائحاني في قول الشارح "بعد قبضه" إلى المرتهن كما رأيته بخطه في هامش نسخته، ويؤيده أن عبارة البحر هكذا: ومن موانع وجوب الرهن إذا كان في يد المرتهن بعدم ملك اليد، وليس فيها ما يدل على أنه لا يزكيه بعد الاسترداد. (الشامي: ۲/۲٦۳، سعيد).

From the wording of ʿAllāmah al-Sāʾiḥānī (may Allah have mercy on him), ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) drew the conclusion that it does not follow from it that the pledgor owes no zakat once the pledge is returned — that is, zakat should be binding on the pledgor once it comes back into his hands.

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

ولا علي الراهن إذا كان الرهن في يد المرتهن، هكذا في البحر الرائق. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۷۲).

This passage shows that as long as the pledged article is in the possession of the pledgee no zakat is necessary, but that it should become binding once it returns to the pledgor.

Fatāwā Farīdiyyah states:

Zakat on jewellery given as a pledge

Question: What do the scholars of religion say about this matter? My wife's jewellery amounts to about twenty tolas of gold. To settle a debt I took the jewellery from her and pledged it with the National Bank. About two years have now passed since I took a loan of three thousand rupees against the security of this jewellery. Is zakat due on jewellery of this kind, which is neither in one's own possession nor free of encumbrance?

Answer: Every year you should subtract the amount of the loan from the value of the jewellery — whether the loan was taken from a bank or from anyone else — and pay zakat straight away on the remaining jewellery, provided the remainder is not below the amount of the nisab. (فتاوی فریدیہ: ۳/٤۱۹) ۔

In short: if Zayd has pledged 10 tolas of gold with ʿAmr, and Zayd owes ʿAmr a debt equal in value to 2 tolas of gold, then Zayd must pay zakat on 8 tolas of gold, because that extra 8 tolas of gold is like a trust of Zayd's held by ʿAmr.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
