### TITLE

The ruling on restricting a muḍārabah to a fixed term

### QUESTION

In a muḍārabah (profit-sharing investment partnership) contract, if someone says, "You may invest the money for whatever period you like, but the term must be a minimum of 5 years and a maximum of 15 years" — is it permissible to lay down such a condition or not?

### ANSWER

In the case described, it is permissible and valid to restrict a muḍārabah contract to a fixed term and to lay down such a condition. It is clear from the statements of the jurists, however, that a condition fixing a minimum term is not valid; the right to dissolve the contract remains even before that term has elapsed.
Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

وإن وقت للمضاربة وقتاً بعينه يتقيد به حتى يبطل العقد بمضيه. (الفتاوى الهندية: ٤/۲۹۸).
(وكذا في شرح المجلة: ٤/۳۵۵، المادة: ۱٤۲۰).

Islāmī Fiqh states:
Where a term has been fixed, each of the two parties has the right to bring the arrangement to an end as soon as that term expires. (اسلامی فقہ: ۲/۳۹۷) ۔
Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil states:
According to the Ḥanafī and Ḥanbalī schools of thought, a muḍārabah may be limited to a particular term — one year, six months, and so on — after which the muḍārabah comes to an end without any notice. Can the two parties also fix a minimum term for the muḍārabah, before which the muḍārabah may not be terminated? A rule that is generally cited here indicates that no such term can be fixed, and that each party has the right to terminate the agreement whenever it wishes.
This unrestricted right of the parties to terminate the muḍārabah can create certain difficulties under present-day conditions, because most business ventures nowadays need some time before they show their fruits; they call for complex and sustained effort. So if the rabb al-māl (the investor) terminates the muḍārabah at the very outset of a business venture, this will cause great difficulty for the project, and it will be a severe blow in particular to the muḍārib (the working partner), who will have earned nothing in spite of all his effort. Therefore, if at the very time of entering into the muḍārabah contract the parties agree that neither of them will terminate the muḍārabah within a specified period except in certain defined circumstances, this does not appear to be contrary to any principle of the Sharīʿah — especially in the light of the hadith which states:

المسلمون على شروطهم إلا شرطاً أحل حراماً أو حرم حلالاً". (رواه الترمذي وصححه، برقم: ۱۳۵۲، في باب ما يذكر عن رسول الله ﷺ في الصلح بين الناس).

The conditions agreed upon among Muslims are to be upheld, except for conditions that make something forbidden permissible or something permissible forbidden. (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ۵/٤٦، ٤۷، بعنوان "مضاربہ کو ختم کرنا " ) ۔
واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
