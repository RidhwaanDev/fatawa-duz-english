### TITLE
Dry ablution when a person cannot use the water himself

### QUESTION
If a person has water for ablution but is unable to use it (because he is disabled or the like), and a stranger or a friend of his is present, what is the ruling if he performs dry ablution (tayammum) and prays without asking them for help? Is it necessary to ask for help in such a case?

### ANSWER
Such a person must ask whoever is present — whether a stranger or a friend — for help. In this situation it is not permissible for him to perform tayammum and pray without asking for help.

Al-Baḥr states:

وإن وجد خادماً كعبده وولده وأجيره لا يجزيه التيمم اتفاقاً كما نقله في المحيط وإن وجد غير خادمه من لو استعان به أعانه ولو زوجته فظاهر المذهب أنه لا يتيمم من غير خلاف بين أبي حنيفة وصاحبيه كما يفيد كلام المبسوط والبدائع وغيرهما. (البحر الرائق ۱/۱٤۰).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states:

If there is some other illness, and no one is found to help him perform ablution, and the sick man cannot perform ablution himself — but a servant is available, or one can be got for the customary wage and he is able to pay the wage of engaging such a worker, or there is someone at hand who would help him if he asked for help — then according to the standard position of the school he should not perform tayammum, because he is able to use the water. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 1/237).
