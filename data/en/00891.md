### TITLE
Walking in a graveyard with shoes on

### QUESTION
What is the ruling on walking in a graveyard with shoes on? The ordinary books write that it is disliked, but that clearly creates difficulty.

### ANSWER
Walking in a graveyard with shoes on is contrary to proper etiquette. However, if there is real difficulty, and the shoes are clean of filth and impurity, and one's walking shows no arrogance but is done humbly and modestly, out of respect for the graves, then there is room for it.

Consider what Badhl al-Majhūd states:

عن بشير مولي رسول الله ﷺ

بينما أنا أماشي مع رسول الله ﷺ

ثم مر بقبور المسلمين

فإذا رجل لم أقف على تسميته يمشي في القبور عليه نعلان فقال يا صاحب السبتيتين ويحك ألق سبتيتيك فنظر الرجل فلما عرف رسول الله ﷺ خلعهما فرما بهما

وأمره بالخلع احتراماً بالنعال بين القبور قلت: لا يتم ذلك إلا على بعض الوجوه المذكورة. (بذل المجهود: ٤/۲۱۳)

In the time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, shoes of tanned leather from which the hair had been removed were regarded as fashionable. The hadith therefore urges a person to adopt a humble bearing in a graveyard and to keep away from things that suggest pride. In our own time, plain hairless shoes are an article of everyday use, so wearing them in a graveyard is correct.

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

والمشي في المقابر بنعلىن لا يكره عندنا كذا في السراج الوهاج. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱٦۷، الفصل السادس في القبر والدفن)

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

Walking over graves, whether with shoes on or without them, is strictly forbidden and disliked. But where there is no grave and the ground is empty, there is no harm in walking with shoes on; it is permissible and not disliked. ʿĀlamgīrī states:

والمشي في القبور لا يكره عندنا

Still, one should take care; in particular, it is better to take one's shoes off when reciting Fātiḥah beside a grave. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 3/97, Kitāb al-Janāʾiz).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
