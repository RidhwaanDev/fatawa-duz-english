### TITLE
Giving other money in charity in place of interest money

### QUESTION
A man's money is deposited in a bank in England and interest accrues on it. If this man pays the poor out of other money in his possession on behalf of the bank's interest, will he be free of liability, or is it binding and necessary that he withdraw that very money from the bank and give it away in charity? بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
In the case in question the superior and better course is that the actual money be given away in charity. But if, in place of the interest money, some other money is given in charity in order to discharge the liability, the liability will be discharged, because currency is not specified by designation. Then too, banks do not hand over the interest money separately; they hand it over mixed with the capital, so that distinguishing between the capital and the interest is impossible.

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

لو خلط السلطان المال المغصوب بماله ملكه، لأن الخلط استهلاك إذا لم يمكن تميزه عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله، وقوله أرفق، إذ قلما يخلو مال عن غصب. وفي الشامية: قوله لأن الخلط استهلاك، أي بمنزلة من حيث أن حق الغير يتعلق بالذمة، لا بالأعيان،

لا نا نقول: إنه لما خلطها ملكها، وصار مثلها ديناً في ذمته، لا عينها. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۲/۲۹۰، مطلب فيما لو صادر السلطان جائز اً، سعيد).

Fatāwā Bazzāziyyah states:

ما يأخذه الأعونة من الأموال ظلماً ويخلطه بماله وبمال مظلوم آخر يصير ملكاً له وينقطع حق الأول فلا يكون أخذه عندنا حراماً محضاً نعم لا يباح الانتفاع به قبل أداء البدل في الصحيح من المذهب. (الفتاوى البزازية على هامش الهندية، قبيل كتاب الزكاة، ٤/۸۳).

(وكذا في فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۲۹۲، مطلب في التصدق من المال الحرام، سعيد).

A clear illustration of this question is that Islamic madrasahs generally appoint collectors to gather donations, and it happens very often that, having collected the donations, they are sometimes unable to reach the madrasah and hand the money over at once. The scholars have therefore permitted that if the collector cannot reach the madrasah and the madrasah has an immediate need, he may inform his household to pay the madrasah an amount equal to the donations out of his own money. The scholars say that once his household has paid the madrasah from his personal money, the collector is discharged of liability, and it will be entirely correct for him to use that zakat money himself.

It is also common practice to send zakat funds from one country to another, and here too the currency of the zakat payer's country is not given as zakat as it stands but is converted first. The zakat on rands, pounds, dollars and the like, for instance, is given to the poor in India and Pakistan in the form of rupees. See: (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā, vol. 3, p. 193).

In sum, it is not better to pay the interest out of one's own untainted money; the interest money should be withdrawn first and then given to some poor person. Nor is it correct to deposit money in a bank with the intention of obtaining interest.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
