### TITLE
Paying interest with interest money

### QUESTION
Khālid took a loan of 100,000 rand from a bank and bought a house with it, and the bank told him that he must pay back 110,000. Khālid now wants to sell that house to Zayd for 110,000 so that he can settle his debt. Zayd has said that he will pay 100,000 rand from lawful wealth and give 10,000 from interest money. Is it permissible to do this or not?

### ANSWER
Dealing in interest is a major sin, and painful warnings about it have come in the Qur'an and the hadiths. Khālid should therefore repent and resolve not to take an interest-bearing loan in future. As for interest money, the jurists have written that it should either be returned to its real owner, if the owner is known, or else given away in charity to the poor without any intention of earning reward. As for paying interest out of interest money, the ruling is that the interest money may be returned, under any heading whatever, to the same bank from which the interest was received; but taking it from one bank and paying it to another bank is not permissible.

Badhl al-Majhūd states:

وأما إذا كان عند رجل مال خبيث فأما إذا ملكه بعقد فاسد أو حصل له بغير عقد ولا يمكنه أن يرد إلى مالكه ويريد ان يدفع مظلمته فليس له حيلة إلا أن يدفع إلى الفقراء. (بذل المجهود: ۱/۳۷).

Al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

قالوا: وعلي هذا لو مات رجل وكسبه من ثمن الباذق والظلم أو أخذ الرشوة تعود الورثة ولا يأخذون منه شيئاً وهو الأولي لهم ويردونه على أربابه إن عرفوهم وإلا يتصدقوا به لأن سبيل الكسب الخبيث التصدق إذا تعذر الرد. (البحر الرائق: ۸/۲۰۱، فصل في البيع، كتاب الكراهية، كوئته).
وللاستزادة انظر: (تبيين الحقائق: ٦/۲۷، فصل في البيع، ملتان. والدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٦/۳۸۵، فصل في البيع، سعيد).

Niẓām al-Fatāwā states:

The ruling of the sharia for any unlawful wealth is that if a person can return it to the place he received it from, he should do so. Under this principle of the sharia, money received under the heading of interest should be conveyed back to the place it came from, and so this arrangement will be allowed by the sharia. If that is not possible, then the ruling of the sharia for interest money is that it should be given away as charity to the poor and needy with the intention of escaping its evil consequences. (Niẓām al-Fatāwā: 1/191).

Fatāwā ʿUthmānī states:

If, however, money was in the past mistakenly placed in an interest-bearing account and an interest-bearing loan was taken, the matter may be settled by taking from the one account and paying into the other, provided that the interest received is no more than the interest paid but equal to it. For the future, though, this practice should be stopped altogether. (Fatāwā ʿUthmānī: 3/280).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

If a person is compelled to take an interest-bearing loan and does take it, and he then has with him a sum of interest money received from a bank, there is no harm in his paying that very money as the interest. Paying interest is itself a sin, because in doing so a person spends the lawful wealth that Allah has given him on an unlawful purpose; so if a person pays out interest money for this purpose, he saves lawful wealth from being dishonoured, and it is hoped that he will not be called to account for it. Mawlānā Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) also inclined to this view. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil, vol. 1, p. 254).
