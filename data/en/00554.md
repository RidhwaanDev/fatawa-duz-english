### TITLE
Joining an imam who has stood up for a fifth rakʿah

### QUESTION
If the imam mistakenly stood up for a fifth rakʿah and a man came and joined him, is that valid or not? And what is the ruling if the imam then returned and sat down for the final sitting? And what if he did not return but went on to add a sixth rakʿah as well?

### ANSWER
A latecomer's joining of the imam in the fifth rakʿah is valid only if the imam returns before performing the prostration of that fifth rakʿah. If the imam went on to add a sixth rakʿah, the latecomer's joining is not valid, because in that case it would be a case of "اقتداء المفترض خلف المتنفل" — an obligatory prayer performed behind someone praying a voluntary one — and the jurists do not permit this. Fatāwā Qāḍī Khān states:

إذا صلى الإمام الظهر أربع ركعات وقعد على الرابعة وقام إلى الخامسة ساهياً وجاء إنسان واقتدي به في صلاة الظهر قال الشيخ الإمام أبو بكر بن الفضل رحمه الله: يصح اقتداء الرجل لأن الإمام ما لم يقيد الخامسة بالسجدة يكون في تحريمة تلك الصلاة. (فتاوى قاضي خان على هامش الهندية: ۱/۱۰۲، فصل في المسبوق).

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states:

If the imam sat in the fourth rakʿah long enough for the tashahhud and then stood up absent-mindedly and also performed the prostration of the fifth rakʿah, he should add a sixth rakʿah and perform the prostration of forgetfulness; his obligatory prayer is complete. If someone joined this imam in the fifth or sixth rakʿah, that man's prayer will not be valid, because those two rakʿahs of the imam are voluntary. (Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, Mudallal wa Mukammal: 4/410).
