### TITLE
The ruling on women adopting men's dress in a play

### QUESTION
At an annual gathering of a madrasat al-banāt, some girls presented a play in which the girls wore boys' clothing. The form of the play was this: an essay concerning death was read out which dealt with the rulings of preparing and shrouding the deceased; then several girls, wearing boys' clothing — a white kurtā, pyjamas and a white cap, for instance — wrapped a doll in a shroud, carried it upon their shoulders and buried it in a mock graveyard. Is this method permissible for the purpose of instruction, or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, it is neither permissible nor correct for women to wear men's clothing and to adopt a resemblance to them, even if it be for a short while and for the purpose of instruction; it should be avoided. As for the rest, there is room for a beneficial dialogue and the like while remaining within the bounds of the Sharīʿah. Consider what is in Bukhārī Sharīf:

عن عكرمة عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنه قال: لعن رسول الله ﷺ المتشبهين من الرجال بالنساء والمتشبهات من النساء بالرجال. (صحيح البخاري: ۲/٤۷۸، باب المتشبهين بالنساء).

For further detail see: (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā, vol. 7, pp. 640–642).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
