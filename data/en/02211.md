### TITLE
A condition requiring notice before vacating a shop

### QUESTION
A man rented a shop from Zayd at 2,000 rand a month. The owner of the shop wrote in the agreement that notice must be given three months before vacating the shop, and that otherwise a further three months' rent would have to be paid. Is it permissible to lay down such a condition or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, this arrangement is of the nature of a financial penalty. Those scholars who hold that imposing a financial penalty is permissible say that this arrangement is permissible, while those who do not regard a financial penalty as valid hold that it is not permissible. The detailed discussion of financial penalties has already appeared in Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā, volume four.

Similarly, if a condition is placed on an employee that he will give three months' notice before leaving his job, failing which he will pay the company three months' salary, the answer to that is the same as the one given above.

In the same way, if there is a law of the government in the employee's favour that the employer may not dismiss him from his job without three months' notice, failing which the employee may lodge a complaint with the government and have a further three months' salary recovered, the ruling on that is also the same.
