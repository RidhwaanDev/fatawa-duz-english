### TITLE
Wearing a stitched wrap while in the state of iḥrām

### QUESTION
Some people are not used to wearing an open sheet, and while in the state of iḥrām there is often a serious risk that their private parts will become exposed. Will such people be permitted to use a stitched wrap, or not? بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
The stitched clothing that may not be worn in the state of iḥrām means a garment made to fit the whole body or one of its limbs, which encircles the body or a limb by stitching, weaving or some other means, and which is worn in the ordinary way. A wrap is not like this, so using a wrap while in iḥrām is permissible. Since it does bear some resemblance to stitched clothing, however, using a wrap while in iḥrām falls under what is contrary to what is preferable.

قال العلامة الملا علي القاري في شرح اللباب: وعقد الإزار والرداء أي ربط طرف أحدهما بطرف الآخر، وأن يخله أي كل واحد منهما بخلال كنحو إبرة، وشدهما بحبل ونحوه من رباط ومنطقة. (شرح اللباب، ص۱۳۳، فصل في مكروهات الإحرام، ط: بيروت).

قال في الدر المختار: فإن زرره أو خلله أو عقده أساء ولا دم عليه. وقال في فتاوى الشامي: وكذا لو شده بحبل ونحوه لشبهه حينئذٍ بالمخيط. (الدر المختار مع فتاوى الشامي: ۲/٤۸۱، سعيد).

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

If there is a fear of the private parts being exposed, there is room to stitch the iḥrām sheet; stitching it without need is disliked. Ghunyat al-Nāsik states:

عقد الإزار والرداء بأن يربط طرف أحدهما بطرفه الآخر شرح وأن يخلله بخلال أو يشده بحبل ونحوه.
(غنية الناسك، ص ٤۷، فصل في مكروهات الإحرام ومحظوراته التي لا جزاء فيها سوى الكراهة).

Muʿallim al-Ḥujjāj states: Ruling: it is disliked to stitch the two ends of the lower wrap together at the front; but if someone stitches it to protect himself against exposure of the private parts, no sacrificial penalty becomes due. (Muʿallim al-Ḥujjāj, p. 128, the disliked acts of iḥrām). فقط واللہ اعلم بالصواب (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 4/206, Deoband).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh records:

If a person knots the sheet, or ties the lower wrap with a cord and keeps it tied for a whole day, this is disliked, because it then resembles a stitched garment. No penalty is due for it, however, since in this way the cloth does not come to encircle the body by means of stitching. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 4/494).

Elsewhere it is stated:

Al-Ḥalabī (may Allah have mercy on him) mentioned in his manual of the hajj rites that the principle here is that any garment made to fit a person's whole body or some part of it, in such a way that by stitching, or by joining one part to another, or in some other way (for instance by weaving), it covers the whole body or a part of it and stays on the body of its own accord — wearing such a garment in the state of iḥrām is forbidden. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 4/138).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
