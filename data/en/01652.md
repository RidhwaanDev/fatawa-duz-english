### TITLE
Providing a house for a son after his marriage

### QUESTION
Are the expenses of a son's wedding, and arranging a house for him after the wedding, binding on the father or not?

### ANSWER
The maintenance and similar expenses of an adult son are not binding on the father; if, however, he is unable to earn because of illness or the like, then his maintenance is binding on the father. Maintenance covers food, clothing and the like, but providing a separate house after the wedding is not binding on the father. Likewise the expenses of the wedding — the dower, the wife's maintenance and so on — are not obligatory on the father. Still, if the father has the means, arranging a house for his son is a good deed and a source of reward, even though it is neither obligatory nor binding.

Consider Fatāwā Qāḍī Khān, which states:

ولا يجب عليه نفقة الذكور الكبار إلا أن يكون الولد عاجزاً عن الكسب لزمانة أو مرض، فتكون نفقته على والده. (فتاوى قاضي خان على هامش الهندية: ۱/٤٤۵، فصل في نفقة الأولاد).

Fatḥ al-Qadīr states:

أما الكبار فعلى الظاهر وإن لم يكونوا عاجزين لا نفقة لهم. (فتح القدير: ٤/٤۱۰، دار الفكر).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

The maintenance of an adult son does not rest on the father; but if he is unable to earn because of illness or the like, or he is a student of the religious sciences and has no wealth of his own, then his maintenance rests on the father. The costs of the children's weddings do not rest on the father. In the case of a daughter's wedding there is in fact no expense at all; nothing becomes obligatory on her on account of the marriage, and indeed her own expenses become the husband's responsibility. A son's wedding, however, does involve expenses, among them the dower and the wife's maintenance, which are obligatory, and the wedding feast, which is a sunnah; but none of these costs rests on the father. (احسن الفتاویٰ: ۵/٤٦٤) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالیٰ اعلم
