### TITLE
Saying "If I keep you, I keep my own mother and sister"

### QUESTION
If a man says to his wife, "If I keep you, then I keep my own mother and sister," do these words bring about ẓihār or divorce or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, saying "If I keep you, then I keep my own mother and sister" without a word of comparison brings about neither ẓihār nor divorce; the words are simply of no effect. Saying such things is, however, disliked.

Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām writes:

A word of comparison — "like", "as", "just as" and so on — must be expressly stated. This is the chief element, and without it the words are of no effect. So if a man says, "You are my mother," or, "You are my mother's back," the words are of no effect. (مجموعہ قوانین اسلامی: ص ۱٦۲،) ۔

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Question: In a fit of violent rage, in the course of which he split his own head open in several places, a husband said to his wife, "If I have relations with you, may I have relations with my mother." Are these words an oath? Will he have to pay an expiation?

Answer:

لو قال: إن وطئتك وطئت أمي، فلا شيء عليه، كذا في غاية السروجي،

— Al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah. The passage quoted shows that no expiation is due from the husband for saying these words, that no divorce has fallen upon his wife, and that this statement of his is of no effect.

فلو قال: إن فعلت كذا فأنت أمي، وفعله، فهو باطل إن نوى التحريم، سكب الأنهر. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۳/۳۲٦، فاروقیہ) ۔

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

لو قال: أنت أمي، لا يكون مظاهراً، وينبغي أن يكون مكروهاً، ومثله أن يقول يا ابنتي ويا أختي. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۵۰٦).
