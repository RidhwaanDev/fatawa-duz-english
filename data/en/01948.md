### TITLE
Buying the offerings made at shrines from the shrine-keeper

### QUESTION
Some people place sheets and cloths on graves, and the keeper and custodian of the grave then sells them. Is it permissible to buy them or not?

### ANSWER
Placing sheets on graves and offering other things there is impermissible and unlawful, and the keeper does not become the owner of these things. Selling them is therefore not permissible, and buying them will not establish ownership for the buyer. If, however, the owner has repented and given up his wrong intention and then sells his own property, that is permissible and valid.

Allah Most High says:

قال الله تعالى: ﴿حرمت عليكم الميتة والدم ولحم الخنزير وما أهل لغير الله به﴾. (سورة المائدة: الآية، ۳).

Al-Taḥrīr wa'l-Tanwīr states:

قوله سبحانه وتعالى: ﴿وما أهل به لغير الله﴾ أي ما أعلن به أو نودي عليه بغير اسم الله تعالى وهو مأخوذ من أهل إذا رفع صوته بالكلام فأهل في الآية مبني للمجهول، ضمن أهل معني تقرب فعدي لمتعلقه بالباء وباللام مثل تقرب،

وفائدة هذا التضمين تحريم ما تقرب به لغير الله تعالى. (التحرير والتنوير للعلامة محمد طاهر بن عاشور، ۲/۱۱۹).

ʿIṭr-e-Hidāyah states: Khulāṣat al-Tafāsīr, volume one, Sūrat al-Māʾidah, says that wa-mā uhilla is general; and even if it were not general but confined to slaughtering, the ruling would still be general, because the underlying cause is shared. (ʿIṭr-e-Hidāyah, p. 474).

Al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

وأما النذر الذي ينذره أكثر العوام على ما هو مشاهد كأن يكون لا نسان غائب أو مريض أو له حاجة ضرورية فيأتي بعض الصلحاء فيجعل ستره على رأسه فيقول يا سيدي فلان إن رد غائبي أو عوفي مريضي أو قضيت حاجتي فلك من الذهب كذا أو من الفضة كذا أو من الطعام كذا أو من الشمع كذا أو من الزيت كذا فهذا النذر باطل بالإجماع لوجوه منها أنه نذر مخلوق والنذر للمخلوق لا يجوز لأنه عبادة والعبادة لا تكون للمخلوق

ومنها إن ظن أن الميت يتصرف في الأمور دون الله تعالى واعتقاده ذلك كفر

ولا تشتغل الذمة به ولأنه حرام بل سحت ولا يجوز لخادم الشيخ أخذه ولا أكله ولا التصرف فيه بوجه من الوجوه

فإذا علمت هذا فما يؤخذ من الدراهم والشمع والزيت وغيرها وينقل إلى ضرائح الأولياء تقرباً إليهم فحرام بإجماع المسلمين ما لم يقصدوا بصرفها للفقراء الأحياء قولاً واحداً. (البحر الرائق: ۲/۲۹۸، كوئته. وكذا في حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح، ص ٦۹۳. والدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۲/٤۳۹، سعيد).
وفي الدر المختار: الحرمة تتعدد مع العلم بها. (الدر المختار: ۵/۹۸، سعيد).
وفي رد المحتار: (تتمة) في الأحكام عن الحجة: تكره الستور على القبور. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۲۳۸، سعيد).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states: Offering flowers at shrines, lighting lamps and placing sheets there is impermissible. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 1/348). The same is in Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband by Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him): 2/166.

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states: A thing other than an animal that is vowed to other than Allah is also unlawful, because — through the cause of seeking nearness to other than Allah — it comes under mā uhilla bihī li-ghayr Allāh. That is to say, the unlawfulness of an animal is what the text indicates directly, whereas the unlawfulness of what is not an animal is indicated by the text by way of analogy. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 5/491. The same is in Imdād al-Fatāwā: 2/554; and Maʿārif al-Qurʾān by Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him): 1/424).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states: Question: Is it lawful or unlawful to eat a bull that has been let loose as an offering? Since there is disagreement about this between those who follow a school of law and those who do not, please write in detail; and please look at Tafsīr-e-Aḥmadī of Mullā Jīwan (may Allah have mercy on him). Also, what is the meaning of ما جعل الله من بحيرة ولا سائبة الخ?

Answer: There is detail in this. One case is that a person dedicated an animal in the name of other than Allah and slaughtered it with that same intention, even if he also said bismillāh at the time of slaughter. This is unlawful; its prohibition is established by an explicit text of the Qurʾān and is stated expressly in books of fiqh such as Al-Durr al-Mukhtār.

The second case is that the name of other than Allah is used merely by way of description and designation, and gaining their nearness or pleasure is not intended — as it is reported in the hadith that at the time of the ʿaqīqah one says: "هذا عقيقة فلان". This is undoubtedly lawful, and the author of Tafsīr-e-Aḥmadī declares this to be lawful; what he has ruled forbidden bears witness to it.

The third case is that a person let the animal loose with a corrupt intention and belief, and the ruler of the time seized it for some reason and auctioned it, and someone bought it and slaughtered it. This is lawful, because taking control establishes ownership; once that first man was no longer its owner, his corrupt intention is of no account.

The fourth case is that a person had let it loose with an evil intention and another man stole it and slaughtered it in secret. This is unlawful for two reasons: first, because of the owner's corrupt intention, since making an animal sāʾibah does not take it out of his ownership; and second, because of usurpation and theft.

The fifth case is that the owner repented of his corrupt intention and slaughtered the animal. This is lawful, because the cause of the prohibition has been removed. And the meaning of wa-mā jaʿala Allāh, etc., is the negation of the practice that the disbelievers claimed to be from Allah — that is, the prohibition of benefiting from such animals out of reverence and respect for them. And Allah knows best. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 4/99, Kitāb al-Ḥaẓr wa'l-Ibāḥah).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states: Through this dedication to other than Allah, an unlawfulness like that of carrion has come into it; so, just as it is not permissible to buy carrion, neither is it permissible to buy this. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 2/562; and Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 16/70, The sale of offerings, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
