### TITLE
Some forms of sharecropping and their rulings

### QUESTION
I have read in a booklet that the following forms of sharecropping are permissible and the rest are not:

(1) The land belongs to one party and everything else — the seed, the tractor, the labour — belongs to the other. This is permissible.

(2) The land and the seed belong to one party and the labour and the tractor to the other; or the land and the tractor belong to one party and the labour and the seed to the other. This too is permissible.

Nowadays, however, a great deal of expense goes into working land, and so the custom and practice is that the land belongs to the owner, the labour is the cultivator's, and the two share the seed, the tractor and the pesticides between them. Can this be allowed in the sharia or not? The jurists treat sharecropping as being like a muḍārabah partnership, and in a muḍārabah the capital comes from one party and the work from the other. So if in sharecropping too the land belongs to one party and the labour to the other, while both share the remaining expenses, will there be room for that or not? بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
As a rule, the Hanafi jurists (may Allah have mercy on them) have declared only four forms of sharecropping to be permissible. Ibn ʿĀbidīn al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) and others, however, have set out further permissible forms besides these, and Ibn ʿĀbidīn also stated that there is no single comprehensive rule in this matter within which all the permissible forms of sharecropping can be confined.

The upshot is that sharecropping turns essentially on custom and common practice. It was on the basis of common practice that Imam Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) declared some conditions permissible which, on the standard transmitted position, invalidate the contract.

On this basis there ought to be room for the arrangement described in the question as well, on the strength of custom and common practice — all the more so as Mawlānā Fatḥ Muḥammad Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him) has expressly declared it permissible.

The evidence is set out below:

وأما الشرائط المفسدة للمزارعة فأنواع

ومنها شرط الحصاد والرفع إلى البيدر والدياس والتذرية

وروي عن أبي يوسف رحمه الله أنه أجاز شرط الحصاد ورفع البيدر والدياس والتذرية على المزارع لتعامل الناس وبعض مشايخنا بما وراء النهر يفتون به أيضاً وهو اختيار نصير بن يحيى رحمه الله ومحمد بن سلمة رحمه الله من مشايخ خراسان. (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/۱۸۰، فصل في الشرائط المفسدة للمزارعة، سعيد).

وللمزيد راجع: (حاشية ابن عابدين: ٦/۲۸۲، سعيد، والموسوعة الفقهية الكويتية: ۳۷/٦۳، وزارة الاوقاف، والفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۲۳۷، والمبسوط للإمام السرخسي: ۲۳/۳٦، وتبيين الحقائق: ۵/۲۸۳، وتكملة البحر الرائق: ۸/۱٦۳، وشرح المجلة: ۴/۳۷۷).

Mullā ʿAlī al-Qārī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Sharḥ al-Niqāyah:

ونفقة الزرع عليهما بالحصص، كأجر الحصاد ونحوه، فإن شرط على العامل، صح عند أبي يوسف رحمه الله، وبه يفتي. وهذا اختيار مشايخ بلخ. قال شمس الأئمة: وهو الأصح في ديارنا، يعني لتعامل الناس بها، كذا في الهداية. وفسد في ظاهر الرواية

وعن نصير بن يحيى رحمه الله، ومحمد بن سلمة رحمه الله أن هذا كله يكون على العامل، شرط عليه أم لا، بحكم العرف. قال شمس الأئمة السرخسي رحمه الله: هذا هو الصحيح في ديارنا، كذا في فتاوى قاضيخان، والله أعلم. (فتح باب العناية: ۳/۴۳۹، كتاب المزارعة، ط: بيروت).

(وكذا في التصحيح والترجيح، ص: ۳۱۷).

The gist of the passage above is precisely this: what may and may not be laid down as the working partner's responsibility is referred back to custom and common practice, and Shams al-Aʾimmah al-Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him) declared this to be the correct position.

Permissibility is also to be gathered from the words of Mawlānā Fatḥ Muḥammad Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him), a student of Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him) and a mufti of careful and exacting scholarship. He says in ʿIṭr-e Hidāyah:

The second kind is crop-sharing, and it is this that should be called sharecropping: that is, of whatever is produced, one portion goes to the owner of the land and the other to the man who sows it. This is the very arrangement the Prophet ﷺ made with the people of Khaybar. In it the land is the subject matter; the oxen, the water-wheel and other implements, the seed, and whatever is spent on irrigation and the like are the expenses; and by "work" is meant the running and management of the land, while whatever a man does with his own hands or has done specially on his own account is also included in the work. It is therefore essential that the subject matter — that is, the land — be set against the work: if the land belongs to one man, the work must come from the other, while the expenses, according to what is stipulated, may either be shared or fall on one party alone. (ʿIṭr-e Hidāyah, p. 121, Kutub-khānah ʿAṣriyyah, Deoband).
