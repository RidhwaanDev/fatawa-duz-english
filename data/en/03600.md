### TITLE
The ruling on separation when the husband has become incapable of intercourse

### QUESTION
A woman's husband was injured in an accident, on account of which his private organ was severed, or he became incapable of intercourse. The two of them also have children, and the woman is young. She demanded divorce or khulʿ from her husband, but the husband refused. In this situation, is there any way in the Sharīʿah for the woman to obtain release from this man or not? Does this woman have the right to place her case before the Muslim community and have the marriage annulled or not? Furthermore, what procedure should the Muslim community adopt in this matter according to the Sharīʿah? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, the established position of the Ḥanafīs is that when, after the marriage, the husband has had marital relations once and thereafter becomes unable to do so for some reason, the woman is not permitted to demand annulment. The jurists in general have written to this effect in the books of fiqh, and moreover several reports of the Successors in Muṣannaf Ibn Abī Shaybah support it. Accordingly, in the case in question, since marital relations have taken place repeatedly and the two of them also have children, the woman should exercise patience and should devote her attention to the upbringing of the children, for in that there is also protection of the children from being ruined. The woman should likewise set the direction of her thoughts aright, and if desire overpowers her she should consult a doctor and, on his advice, use medicines that suppress carnal urges; and she should regard her children as the capital and treasure of her future. Allah Most High will grant her reward beyond reckoning. She should not demand annulment and separation.

Consider the statements of the scholars and jurists:

قال في البحر الرائق: وجدت زوجها مجبوباً فرق في الحال، وهو من استؤصل ذكره وخصيتاه

وأشار إلى أنه لو جب بعد الوصول إليها مرة لا خيار لها كما صار عنيناً بعده. (البحر الرائق: ٤/۱۳۳، ط: دار المعرفة). (وكذا في الدر المختار مع فتاوى الشامي: ۳/٤۹۵، سعيد، والفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۵۲۵، وتبيين الحقائق: ۳/۲۲، إمداديه).

Several reports are transmitted in Muṣannaf Ibn Abī Shaybah:

عن الحسن قال: إذا وصل إليها مرة لم يفرق بينهما. وعن الزهري: إذا قدر عليها مرة فهي امرأته أبداً. وعن عطاء قال: إذا أصابها مرة فلا كلام لها ولا خصومة. وعن عمرو بن دينار، قال: ما زلنا نسمع أنه إذا أصابها مرة فلا كلام لها ولا خصومة، وعن طاووس، قال: إذا أصابها مرة فلا كلام لها ولا خصومة. وعن قتادة وأبي هاشم قالا: إن تزوجها ثم وطئها مرة ثم لم يستطع أن يغشاها، فإنه لا خيار لها بعد تلك المرة. وعن إبراهيم قال: إذا وطئها مرة فليس لها خيار. (مصنف ابن أبي شيبة: ٤/۲۰۹، باب: إذا وصل مرة ثم حبس عنها).
وقال في" الفوائد العلية على الأحكام الشرعية في الأحوال الشخصية على مذهب الإمام أبي حنيفة النعمان ": مادة: ۱۵۱: يجب قضاءً على الزوج أن يواقع مرة واحدة في مدة الزوجية. أي يجب عليه في القضاء مرة واحدة، ويؤمر بها ديانة، أي فيما بينه وبين الله تعالى، وقال بعضهم: بل يجب عليه الزيادة قضاءً أيضاً، لدفع الضرر عنها ولتأتي بالأولاد التي هي أهم مقاصد الزواج، والله أعلم. (ص: ۸۷).

The passage from the work on personal status law also shows that by a single act of marital relations during the term of the marriage the woman's obligatory right lapses so far as judicial enforcement is concerned; yes, as a matter of religious obligation the right remains, and therefore, in a state of health and soundness, the husband should have regard for it from time to time. In a state of genuine incapacity, however, the religious obligation also lapses, as is recorded in the fatwas of Dār al-Iftāʾ al-Miṣriyyah (the passage follows below); and as for those jurists who have declared repetition to be obligatory judicially as well, that is for the sake of averting harm.

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

An important aspect of this question is that in the view of most jurists, if the man has had marital relations with the woman even once, the woman's conjugal right comes to an end, and thereafter, even if the man becomes impotent, the woman cannot demand annulment of the marriage. ʿAllāmah Ṣanʿānī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written that this is the position of Awzāʿī (may Allah have mercy on him), Sufyān Thawrī (may Allah have mercy on him), Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), Imām Mālik (may Allah have mercy on him), Imām Shāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) and Isḥāq b. Rāhawayh (may Allah have mercy on him). The jurists have set this down in a very clear and explicit manner:

ولو وطيها مرة ثم عجز عن الوطي في هذا النكاح لا يكون لها حق الخصومة. (شرح النقاية للبرجندي: ۲/۵٦).
(جدید فقہی مسائل: ۳/۱۸۸) ۔

In the fatwas of Dār al-Iftāʾ al-Miṣriyyah a fatwa of the year 1310 AH is transmitted in which, on the authority of Shāmī and al-Baḥr, it is written that judicially the woman's right is to a single occasion: if marital relations have taken place once after the marriage, then judicially the right has lapsed — that is, the woman does not have the right to demand annulment. As a matter of religious obligation, however, the right remains: if the husband, despite having the ability, refrains from marital relations in order to torment her, he will be sinful, and if there is a sound excuse he will not be sinful. Consider the Arabic fatwa:

ولو تزوج ووصل إليها ثم عجز عن الوطء بعد ذلك، وصار عنيناً، لم يكن لها حق الخصومة، وعلى أنه (الدر المختار شرح تنوير الابصار للعلائي وحاشية رد المحتار لا بن عابدين: ۲/۹۱۷، ۱۹۸. والبحر الرائق لا بن نجيم المصري الحنفي: ٤/۱۳۵) فلو جُن بعد وصوله إليها مرة أو صار عنيناً بعد الوصول إليها لا يفرق بينهما لحصول حقها بالوطء مرة، وما زاد عليها فهو مستحق ديانة لا قضاء، ويأثم إذا ترك الديانة متعنتاً مع القدرة على الوطء. (فتاوى دار الإفتاء المصرية: ۱/۳۲۷، ط: وزارة الأوقاف المصرية).

Among the scholars of the present age, however, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh Ṣāḥib holds the view that impotence arising later should also be treated as a ground for annulment. Thus, after first explaining the well-known position, he writes:

We ought to reflect upon this question in the light of the Qurʾān, the hadith and analogy. The real pivot of this question is whether the woman has a right after one act of intercourse or not. If it is established that the woman's right remains even after that, then it is obvious that impotence occurring even after one act of intimacy will be a cause for annulment of the marriage; and if the woman's right to conjugal relations lapses through a single act of intercourse, then it is obvious that there will now be no room for her to demand annulment of the marriage.

Ibn Ḥazm holds the view that it is obligatory upon the man to have relations with the woman at least once in every period of purity, because Allah Most High has said:

فإذا تطهرن فأتوهن من حيث أمركم الله. [البقرة: ۲۲۲]، اور

Shaykh Sābiq has written that this is the view of the majority of the scholars:

وذهب جمهور العلماء إلى ما ذهب إليه ابن حزم من الوجوب على الرجل إذا لم يكن له عذر.

Imām Aḥmad (may Allah have mercy on him) holds the view that intimacy at least once in four months is obligatory, because the term of īlāʾ is four months; and if the husband is away on a journey, even then he must certainly come once within six months and have relations with her — so much so that if he refuses to do this and the woman seeks separation, the judge will effect a separation between the two. Imām Aḥmad's evidence is the incident which Zayd b. Aslam has related from Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him): that once he went out to inspect Madīnah and passed by the house of a woman who was reciting these verses:

تطاول هذا الليل وأسود جانبه
ظ
وطال علي أن لا خليل ألاعبه
والله لولا خشية الله وحده
ظ
لحرك من هذا السرير جوانبه
ولكن ربي والحياء يكفيني
ظ
وأكرم بعلي أن توطأ راكبه

Translation: This night has drawn out long and its horizon has turned black, and it weighs heavily upon me that I have no companion to keep me company. By God, were it not for the fear of the One God, the sides of this bed would be shaking. But my Lord and my sense of modesty suffice me, and I hold my husband in too great honour for his mount to be trodden by another.

Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) enquired about this woman and learnt that her husband had gone out on jihad. He asked Ḥaḍrat Ḥafṣah (may Allah be pleased with her) how many days a woman can endure without her husband. Ḥaḍrat Ḥafṣah (may Allah be pleased with her) was at first shy, but then, upon his insistence, told him: five or six months. Accordingly he laid down a rule for the fighters that they should spend six months on jihad and four months at home, one month in the outward journey and one month in the return journey.

All these testimonies are sufficient to establish that just as marital relations are the right of the man, so too they are an independent right of the woman, and that this right does not lapse through a single act of intercourse. The clearest proof of this is īlāʾ: had the woman's right to marital relations lapsed after one act of intimacy, it would have been necessary for the Glorious Qurʾān to make intercourse or separation obligatory in the case of īlāʾ only where the occasion for intimacy had never previously arisen — whereas that is not the case.

Accordingly Abū Thawr is of the opinion that even if the husband becomes impotent after having had relations once, his marriage will be annulled. In our age, which is an age of trial, and when indecency, obscenity and fornication are (it may be said) being actively propagated, and when in the eyes of the government and the law fornication is not even a crime unless there is coercion, it is necessary that this opinion be acted upon and that impotence arising later also be treated as a ground for annulment. (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۳/۱۸۸ ۔ ۱۹٤) ۔
