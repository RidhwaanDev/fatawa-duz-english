### TITLE
Eating the fruit of a fruit tree standing in a graveyard or a mosque

### QUESTION
There is a fruit tree growing in a graveyard or in a mosque. Is it permissible to eat its fruit or not?

### ANSWER
If that mosque or graveyard is endowed, then no one is permitted to pick the fruit and put it to his own use — neither the fruit itself nor its price. It is necessary that it be spent on the purposes of the endowment. That said, it appears from a fatwa of Ḥaḍrat Gangōhī (may Allah have mercy on him) that if the fruit trees of the mosque or graveyard were planted for the worshippers or for people at large, then taking from them is permissible.

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

"وأما حكم الوقف الجائز فحكمه انه يزول الموقوف عن ملك الواقف ولا يدخل في ملك الموقوف عليه لكنه ينتفع بغلته بالتصدق عليه لأن الوقف حبس الأصل وتصدق بالفرع والحبس لا يوجب ملك المحبوس والواجب أن يبدأ بصرف الفرع إلى مصالح الوقف من عمارته وإصلاح ماله من بنائه وسائر مؤناته للتي لا بدّ منها سواء شرط ذلك الواقف أو لم يشترط لأن الوقف صدقة جارية في سبيل الله تعالى ولا تجري إلا بهذا الطريق." (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/۲۲۰، ۲۲۱)

If only the land of the graveyard or mosque was endowed and not the trees, even then it will not be correct to use anything from them without the owner's permission. Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

If the endower endowed only the land and did not endow the trees and their fruit, then these remain his property, and it is not permissible to use anything from them without his permission. He will, however, be compelled to uproot those trees (and fruit) and clear the graveyard land. If he endowed the trees and the like along with the land, then whatever the endowment is to be spent on is also what these trees are to be spent on. (احسن الفتاوی: ٦/٤۱۸)

It states:

رجل جعل أرضا مقبرة وفيها أشجار قال الفقيه أبو جعفر رحمه الله تعالى وقف الأشجار لا يصح فتكون الأشجار للواقف ولو رثته إن مات." (فتاوى قاضيخان: ۳/۳۱۳)

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

If the graveyard is endowed, then the fruit should be sold and the price spent on the needs of the graveyard. One should not use it oneself — neither the fruit nor its price. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۲/۳۸۹)

Concerning the mosque, Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

If the endowed land of a mosque can be put under cultivation or let out, then it should be cultivated or let out and the income spent on the needs of the mosque. Otherwise trees should be planted on it and the fruit sold and the proceeds spent on the mosque. واللہ اعلم (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۵/۱۷٦)

Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah states, under the question of fruit trees belonging to a mosque:

Question: If there is a guava tree in the mosque, may the worshippers make use of it or not?

Answer: It is correct to eat from a tree that someone has planted for the worshippers to eat from. (فتاوی رشیدیہ ۵۷۸) ۔
