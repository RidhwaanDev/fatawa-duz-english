### TITLE
Having the sacrifice performed in another country

### QUESTION
Some people in South Africa have their sacrifices performed outside the country. Those who supervise this work, or run it as a business, deal in two ways:

(1) Some of them collect 500 rand per sacrifice from people and keep the animals over there in India. They sell one animal to one person for 500 and inform their agent to put a mark on an animal for so-and-so. What is the ruling on this? In this case the buyer has not taken possession, so if the animal dies the loss ought to fall on the seller. Moreover, neither the buyer nor his agent has even seen the animal, so this is also a sale of something unknown.

(2) The second method is that they tell the buyers: we will buy a sacrificial animal for you for four hundred rand, and one hundred rand is our fee for the work. The apparent problem with this is that if the animal is bought for less than four hundred, the balance ought to be returned, and that does not happen.

### ANSWER
In the case described, in the first form, if the buyer who is having the sacrifice performed has given the agent full authority — that is, "hand over to me whichever animal you wish" — and to all appearances this is what happens, then the man who looks after the animals in India has become, on the buyer's behalf, his agent for taking possession. Therefore, if the animal dies, the loss falls on the buyer and not on the seller; and the agent's seeing the animal counts as the principal's seeing it. On this basis the transaction is valid.

(2) In the second form, it is permissible for the purchasing agent to take a hundred rand as his fee for the work. If he buys the animal for less than four hundred, then there are two possibilities regarding the balance: (a) the first is that the principal has said that he may keep the extra amount as well, in which case he may keep it for himself; (b) if he has not said this, then it must be returned.

A question of this kind was put to Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him). The gist of the question and the answer is as follows:

Question: If a doctor makes up medicine for someone and charges the patient more than it cost, is it permissible to take the extra amount or not?

In reply Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote: If he used it for himself with the patient's permission, it is permissible; otherwise it must be returned. And if those people are not known, he should give it away in charity on their behalf. (امداد الفتاوی: ۳/۱۱۵) ۔
