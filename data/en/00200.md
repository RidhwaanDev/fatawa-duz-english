### TITLE
The ruling on audible remembrance, saying "Allah, Allah"

### QUESTION
Some people regard audible remembrance — the Sufis' practice of saying "Allah, Allah" — as an innovation, or as something without evidence and without reward. Is this correct?

### ANSWER
Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

Audible remembrance is permissible. It is the settled and inherited practice of the Sufi masters, and it is established by many hadiths. Where the sacred law has itself prescribed audible remembrance, no objection can be raised at all — the call to prayer, the takbīr, the talbiyah of the Hajj, the takbīr of tashrīq, and so on: all of these are forms of remembrance, and they are established as being said aloud. As for occasions for which there is no such express warrant, the ruling in principle remains the same so long as no incidental reason stands in the way — for instance that a sleeping person will be disturbed, or that someone's prayer will be interrupted, or that the one making remembrance regards saying it aloud as necessary or obligatory, and the like. Where none of these obstacles is present, audible remembrance is permissible, though silent remembrance is preferable. (كفاية المفتي: ۲/۷۷).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

In itself the remembrance of Allah is a most blessed thing, and the Noble Qur'an and the hadith urge it again and again. Great merit has been reported for the words mentioned in the question (سبحان اللہ، الحمد للہ، لا الہ الا اللہ). Saying them quietly or aloud is correct either way, but the more fitting course is to say them quietly. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 15/102)

It also states:

أما الذكر في قوله تعالى: ﴿فإذا قضيتم الصلاة فاذكروا الله قياماً وقعوداً وعلى جنوبكم﴾ هو الصلاة، ولكنه على أحد الوجهين أما الذكر بالقلب وهو الفكر في عظمة الله تعالى وجلاله وقدرته في خلقه وصنعه من الدلائل عليه وحكمه وجميل صنعه والذكر الثاني: الذكر باللسان بالتعظيم والتسبيح والتقديس وروي عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما قال أحد في ترك الذكر ألا تعلو بأعلى عقله. (أحكام القرآن ۲/۳۲۳) قال أبو سعود في قوله تعالى: ﴿فاذكروا الله قياماً وقعوداً وعلى جنوبكم﴾ أي فداوموا على ذكر الله تعالى وحافظوا على مراقبته ومناجاته ودعائه في جميع الأحوال حتى في حال المسابقة والقتال كما في قوله تعالى: ﴿إذا لقيتم فئة فاثبتوا واذكروا الله كثيراً لعلكم تفلحون﴾ (تفسير أبي السعود: ۳/۹). (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 6/39)

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

A study of the remembrance "الا اللہ":

Question: What do the scholars of religion and the muftis of the firm sacred law say about this matter — is it permissible to make remembrance aloud with the words "الا اللہ" alone, that is, to recite them, or not? I hope that after a full explanation you will write out a reasoned and verified answer, supported by verses of the Glorious Qur'an or by hadith, and send it; it will bring a great reward. Let me repeat that raising the voice is not what is specifically at issue; what is asked is simply whether this remembrance is permissible or not.

Answer: It is permissible, because at the very most it involves the omission of the term from which the exception is made and of the governing word, and where there is a contextual indication such omission occurs in the speech of the most eloquent of the Arabs and non-Arabs ﷺ, just as the omission of the excepted term occurs.

أما حذف المستثنى فما اخرج ابن ماجة عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ كذلك لا يجتني من قربهم إلا قال محمد بن الصباح كانه يعني الخطايا كذا في المشكاة وقع كلامه ﷺ بلا ذكر المستثنى لكمال ظهوره فالحقه محمد كذا

في المرقاة أما حذف المستثنى منه فما أخرج الشيخان عن ابن عباس فقال العباس يا رسول الله إلا الإذخر فانه لقينهم ولبيوتهم فقال إلا إلا

ذخر الحديث

And in the case under discussion the contextual indication is plain. Sometimes it is in the words themselves, when the person has recited لا الہ الا اللہ beforehand; and sometimes it lies in his state, since the very condition of a Muslim indicates his belief that divinity belongs to none other than Allah. واللہ تعالی اعلم (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 5/223, 224)

Maʿārif al-Qur'ān states:

﴿واذكر اسم ربك﴾

In this noble verse the command to remember Allah is tied to the word "name": He said واذكر اسم ربك and not واذكر ربك. This points to the fact that repeating the name of the Lord — that is, saying "Allah, Allah" — is also desired and commanded. (Maẓharī) Some scholars have called the mere repetition of the divine name "Allah, Allah" an innovation; from this it is clear that calling it an innovation is not correct. (Maʿārif al-Qur'ān: 8/594)
