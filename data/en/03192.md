### TITLE
Is the ink of the scholars' pens equal to the blood of the martyrs?

### QUESTION
Has it come in any hadith that the ink of the scholars' pens is equal to the blood of the martyrs? If so, what is the grade of that hadith? And have our elders quoted it anywhere or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, a great many narrations have been transmitted concerning the superiority of the scholars over the martyrs, or of the superiority of the scholars' ink over the blood of the martyrs; but most of them are either severely weak or fabricated, and in the estimation of the hadith scholars they do not reach the level of being established. On this basis these narrations carry no weight. Among our elders, Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) quoted this narration in Maʿārif al-Qurʾān (3/523), but the narration is unreliable.

This hadith is transmitted from various Companions through various chains. Consider:

1. Ḥaḍrat Abu'l-Dardāʾ (may Allah be pleased with him): Ḥāfiẓ Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr (may Allah have mercy on him) related this narration in Jāmiʿ Bayān al-ʿIlm wa Faḍlih (1/138); but there is criticism of Ismāʿīl ibn Abī Ziyād in its chain — Ibn Ḥibbān called him a dajjāl (an arrant liar) — and Ḥāfiẓ ʿIrāqī too declared this chain weak. (Takhrīj al-Iḥyāʾ 1/13.)

2. Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ (may Allah be pleased with him): Abū Nuʿaym al-Aṣbahānī related this narration in Akhbār Aṣbahān (no. 1718) and Daylamī in Musnad al-Firdaws (2/96); but in its chain too there is Ismāʿīl ibn Abī Ziyād. Ibn al-Jawzī (may Allah have mercy on him), after relating this narration through another route in al-ʿIlal al-Mutanāhiyah (1/81), said that it is not sound.

3. Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him): Daylamī related it in Musnad al-Firdaws; but in its chain Isḥāq ibn Qāsim and his father Qāsim are both unknown. Khaṭīb Baghdādī (may Allah have mercy on him) also related it in Tārīkh Baghdād (2/193); and Ibn al-Jawzī (may Allah have mercy on him), after relating it in al-ʿIlal al-Mutanāhiyah (1/80) by way of Khaṭīb Baghdādī (may Allah have mercy on him), said: this hadith is not sound. Khaṭīb Baghdādī (may Allah have mercy on him) too criticised it.

4. The narration of Ḥaḍrat ʿImrān ibn Ḥuṣayn (may Allah be pleased with him): Marhabī related it in Faḍl al-ʿIlm, and ʿAllāmah Suyūṭī quoted it from him in al-Durr al-Manthūr (3/423); but in its chain Aḥmad ibn Muḥammad ibn Qāsim, the muezzin of Ṭarsūs, is an unknown narrator.

5. The narration of Ḥaḍrat Anas (may Allah be pleased with him): Ibn Ḥajar (may Allah have mercy on him) mentioned it in Lisān al-Mīzān (5/226, Beirut), and in its chain there is Jarrāb, a liar; so this too carries no weight.

ʿAllāmah Sakhāwī (may Allah have mercy on him), in Mukhtaṣar al-Maqāṣid al-Ḥasanah, held this narration to be a saying of Ḥasan al-Baṣrī (may Allah have mercy on him) — that is, it is not raised to the Prophet ﷺ. In his notes upon it Dr. Muḥammad al-Ṣabbāgh writes:

ومتنه منكر تأباه الآيات القرآنية والأحاديث الصحيحة وروح الشريعة وما أحسب أن الحسن البصري على جلال قدره يقول مثل هذا القول والله أعلم. (مختصر المقاصد الحسنة، ص ۲۰۲).

That is, there is objectionable matter in the text of this hadith; it is contrary to the Qur'an and the sound hadiths, and it does not accord with the temper of the sacred law either.

The upshot is that this hadith is fabricated.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
