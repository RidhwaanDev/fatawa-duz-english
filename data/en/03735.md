### TITLE
The ruling on paying the fee for transferring a property into one's name from interest money

### QUESTION
When a person buys a property, the fee for transferring the property into his name has to be paid to the "South African Revenue Service". May the fee for transferring the property be paid from interest money? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
The principle concerning interest money is that it should be returned to its real owner when the owner is known; and if the owner is not known and ascertaining him is also difficult or impossible, then it should be given away in charity on his behalf to the poor and the needy. Accordingly, in the case in question it is not permissible to pay interest money as the fee, on account of the following two reasons:

(1) Generally the banks of South Africa are not governmental and state-owned but are private and privately held, so one cannot take money from one bank and give it to another bank.

(2) It is not permissible to derive any kind of benefit from interest money, and in the case in question the benefit of having the property put into one's name is being obtained; therefore this is impermissible.

Consider what is in Fatāwā Shāmī:

إذا علم المالك بعينه فلا شك في حرمته ووجود رده عليه. (باب البيع الفاسد: سعيد).

It is in Maʿārif al-Sunan:

قال شيخنا: ويستفاد من كتب فقهائنا كالهداية وغيرها أن من ملك بملك خبيث ولم يمكنه الرد إلى المالك فسبيله التصدق على الفقراء. قال: ومثله يقول ابن القيم في" بدائع الفوائد "

قال: والظاهر أن المتصدق بمثله ينبغي أن ينوي به فراغ ذمته، ولا يرجو به المثوبة. (معارف السنن: ۱/۳٤، ۳۵، سعيد).

It is in Badhl al-Majhūd:

وأما إذا كان عند رجل مال خبيث، فأما إذا ملكه بعقد فاسد أو حصل له بغير عقد ولا يمكنه أن يرد إلى مالكه ويريد أن يدفع مظلمته فليس له حيلة إلا أن يدفع إلى الفقراء. (بذل المجهود: ۱/۳۷).

For more, see: (Niẓām al-Fatāwā: 1/185; Kitāb al-Fatāwā: 5/318, 319; Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām: 1/163; Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/252; Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 5/410; and Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 5/412).
