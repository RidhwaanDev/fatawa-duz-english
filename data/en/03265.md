### TITLE
Claiming blood money from the government for a killing carried out by the government

### QUESTION
In 1971 a man was unlawfully killed. This year the court held an inquiry, and it came to light that the South African police — that is, the government — had killed him. Can his heirs now demand blood money from the government? If the government pays the blood money, how is it to be divided among the heirs? And if the government pays more than the amount of the blood money, will it be permissible to take the extra or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
An unlawful killing by the government comes under deliberate homicide, and the ruling for that is that retaliation is due from the killer. In the case in question, however, it is difficult to demand retaliation from the government, and the killer himself is not known and identified either; so it is correct to demand blood money. When the government pays the blood money, it will be divided among all the sharia heirs of the victim — those who were alive at the time he was killed — according to the shares fixed by the sharia. If the government pays something more, it is likewise permissible and correct to take it. See what a hadith states:

عن عمرو بن شعيب عن أبيه عن جده قال: كان رسول الله ﷺ يقوم دية الخطأ على أهل القري

وقال رسول الله ﷺ: إن العقل ميراث بين ورثة القتيل على قرابتهم. (
سنن أبی داود، باب دیات الأعضاء: ۲/۲۲۷/٤۵٦٤)۔

Al-Sharīfiyyah Sharḥ al-Sirājiyyah states:

واعلم أن دية المقتول خطأ كسائر أمواله حتى يقضي منها ديونه وتنفذ وصاياه ويرثها كل من يرث سائر أمواله

ولنا أنه ﷺ أمر بتوريث امرأة أشيم الضبابي من عقد زوجها، وقال الزهري: كان قتل أشيم خطأ، وكذا يثبت عندنا حق الزوجين في القصاص لقوله ﷺ: من ترك مالاً أو حقاً فلورثته، ولا ننكر أن القصاص حقه؛ لأنه بدل نفسه فيستحق جميع الورثة بحسب إرثهم كالدية

هكذا ذكره الإمام السرخسي في شرح كتاب الديات. (الشريفية شرح السراجية، ص ٤۹، ط: الرياض).

Fatāwā al-Shāmī states:
لأن الإرث يجري في الأعيان المالية. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۷٦۲، سعيد).
وللاستزادة انظر: (حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ٤/۳٦۵، ومجمع الأنهر: ۲/٤۰۱، وفتاوى الشامي: ٤/۵۱۷، سعيد، والفتاوى التاتارخانية: ۲۰/۲۱۳، والفتاوى الهندية: ٦/۷).

Qāmūs al-Fiqh records:

Blood money is the compensation for the loss of a life, or for an injury that the sharia has placed under the same ruling as killing. If a deliberate homicide occurs and the heirs of the victim agree to take blood money and the killer agrees to pay it, then blood money becomes due in that case. According to the Hanafis there are two conditions for blood money to become due: first, that the person killed or injured was protected, that is, that from the point of view of the sharia he did not deserve to be killed; and second, that the blood of the person killed or injured also has value in the eyes of the sharia. (قاموس الفقہ: ۳/٤۳۷)۔

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

Deliberate homicide is to kill someone intentionally with an iron weapon, or with something by which flesh and skin can be cut so that blood flows. Retaliation — that is, taking a life for a life — belongs exclusively to this class of homicide. In a killing that entails blood money, the blood money will be divided among the heirs in proportion to their shares. (جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام: ۳/۱۹۲)۔

Fatāwā ʿUthmānī states:

The compensation was in principle the liability of the truck driver, and there was a right to claim from him up to the amount of the blood money; there is no right to claim more than that. If, however, the man who killed by mistake gives more of his own accord, it is permissible to take it. (فتاوی عثمانی: ۳/۵٤٤)۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
