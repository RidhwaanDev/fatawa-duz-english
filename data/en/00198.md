### TITLE
Pledging allegiance to a second spiritual guide

### QUESTION
If a person has pledged allegiance to one shaykh and then, for some reason, pledges allegiance to another shaykh, is this correct or not?

### ANSWER
The pledge is not binding either with the first shaykh or with the second, so he may turn to another. There can be several reasons for turning to a second shaykh: for example, if he finds that the first shaykh does not follow the sacred law, or if the first shaykh dies, or if the shaykh is accomplished but for some reason the disciple is not benefiting from him. In such cases it is correct to turn to another. But turning to another for no reason is not good.

Tanqīḥ al-Fatāwā al-Ḥāmidiyyah states:

سوال: رجل من الصوفية أخذ العهد على رجل ثم اختار الرجل شيخاً آخر وأخذ عليه العهد فهل العهد الاول لا زم أم الثاني؟
جواب: لا يلزمه العهد الاول ولا الثاني ولا أصل لذلك. (تنقيح الفتاوى الحامدية: ۲/۳٦۹).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

It is not proper to pledge allegiance to a second person when one is already pledged to a shaykh and is benefiting from him. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 2/103)

Fatāwā Farīdiyyah states:

In the view of the jurists and the Sufis, giving a pledge elsewhere is not forbidden.

قال الشيخ محمد بن عبد الله النقشبندي: وجوزوا التعدد، بل في حياة الشيخ الأول إذا رأى الطالب رشده في موضع آخر يجوز له من غير إنكار لشيخه الأول أن يذهب إليه ويأخذ عليه ويتخذه شيخاً ثانياً فيجوز استفادة التعليم والصحبة مع مشايخ متعددة وينبغي أن يعلم أن الشيخ هو الذي يدل المريد على الحق تعالى وأكثر ما يلاحظ هذا المعنى وأوضح في تعليم الطريقة وشيخ التعليم أستاذ الشريعة ودليل الطريقة الخ. (Fatāwā Farīdiyyah: 1/374, together with the marginal notes)

In Sharīʿat wa Ṭarīqat, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

If a person remains in the service of a shaykh with sincere confidence in him for a considerable time but finds no effect from his company, he should seek what he is after somewhere else, because the goal is Allah, not the shaykh. He should not, however, form a poor opinion of the first shaykh; it may well be that the shaykh is complete and able to complete others, but that this man's portion was not there. In the same way, if the shaykh dies before the goal is reached, or there is no hope of meeting him, he should again look elsewhere. Nor should he imagine that drawing benefit from the grave is enough and that there is no need of a second shaykh, for teaching cannot be received from a grave. A man who already has the spiritual connection does advance in his states in that way, but this person is still in need of instruction; otherwise nobody at all would need to pledge allegiance, since there are hundreds of thousands of graves of accomplished men, and of prophets too. To pledge allegiance in one place after another with no need, out of sheer appetite, is very bad. It destroys the blessing of the pledge, it troubles the shaykh's heart, there is a danger that the spiritual connection will be cut off, and the man becomes known as someone who belongs everywhere and nowhere. (Sharīʿat wa Ṭarīqat, p. 490)
