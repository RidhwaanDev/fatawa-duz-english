### TITLE
The ruling on giving a particular place the name Bayt Allāh

### QUESTION
What do the noble scholars say regarding this matter: a gentleman gave a particular part of his building the name "Bayt Allāh," and objection was raised to this from certain religious circles. Can a particular place be given the name Bayt Allāh? Explain and be rewarded.

### ANSWER
Before the answer, understand by way of introduction that the term Bayt Allāh is regarded as being reserved for the Sacred Mosque, and in the Noble Qur'an the Sacred Mosque is called the House of Allah Almighty: "
ربنا إني أسكنت من ذريتي بواد غير ذي زرع عند بيتك المحرم، ربنا ليقيموا الصلاة" (إبراهيم: ۳۷)
Ḥaḍrat Ibrāhīm (peace be upon him) supplicates: "Our Lord, I have settled some of my offspring near Your Sacred House; our Lord, so that they may establish the prayer." From this noble verse it becomes clear that the Sacred Mosque is the House of Allah. It should be borne in mind that "the House of Allah" does not mean that Allah Almighty lives or sits in the Kaʿbah (we seek refuge with Allah), for Allah Almighty is neither a body nor bound by time and place. Rather, it means that a special manifestation and mercy of Allah Almighty is connected with the Kaʿbah and the Sacred Mosque, which is not the case with other places. Yes, when worshippers inside mosques turn their faces towards the Kaʿbah, they too absorb something of that light and mercy into their breasts.

Now hear the answer to the question. (1) In the Sharīʿah, a term that is reserved for a particular place or a particular thing is not applied to another place or another thing, especially when a particular act of worship is also bound up with that term. For example, the plain of ʿArafāt, Minā and Muzdalifah cannot be the names of any other place; otherwise there would remain the danger that some foolish person would begin performing the rites of Ḥajj there, or would at the very least come to hold that those places are to be venerated. For this reason the jurists have written that the names peculiar to Allah Almighty are not to be used for anyone else, such as Allāh and Raḥmān; whereas names that are shared, such as Karīm and Raḥīm, may be given to others.

(2) Some places or acts are among the symbols of Allah. The meaning of "symbols of Allah" is that they are emblems and reminders of the religion: seeing them brings Islam to mind and one senses the fragrance of Islam — for example, the mosque, the Bayt Allāh, the call to prayer, and so on. The symbols of Allah are commanded to be venerated, and their veneration lies in not using their names for other places. Otherwise, if a rubbish heap, a den of indecency or some marketplace were called a mosque, what veneration of the mosque would remain? Bear in mind that the relation of an ordinary place to the noble Bayt Allāh is like the relation of filth to a pure place.

(3) A place that has been given the name Bayt Allāh will sometimes have wrong things attributed to it, or will be abused. For instance, it may be said, "What a bad and useless place Bayt Allāh is — no sound can be heard here." In this, a defect will be attributed to Bayt Allāh, and the mind will shift to the Kaʿbah, and this term will be dishonoured. For this reason the jurists have written that in this age the name ʿAbd al-Raḥmān should not be given "among ignorant people," because they shorten the name and say "Raḥmān," and then, mentioning the word Raḥmān, they abuse the bearer of the name and so forth. On account of these considerations, this name should be changed and another name given in its place. Yes, if the name is protected, then it is a fine name.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
