### TITLE
The ruling on taking non-Muslim schoolchildren around a mosque

### QUESTION
For the purpose of daʿwah and tablīgh, is it permissible to take non-Muslim schoolchildren around a mosque or not? And is it permissible to bring men and women, among whom there may also be non-Muslim women who are menstruating? بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
In the case in question, the better and preferable course is to make the arrangements in the mosque courtyard, outside the boundaries of the mosque proper, and to present the invitation to Islam there. But if it is necessary to bring them into the mosque, then non-Muslims may enter it so long as no outward impurity is known of them, and so long as there is no fear of any other harm, mischief or trial. A screen should then be arranged between the men and the women. In the same way, a non-Muslim man in a state of major ritual impurity and a non-Muslim woman who is menstruating may all enter the mosque so long as their bodies are not soiled with any visible impurity. Consider what Al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

ولا بأس أن يدخل الكافر وأهل الذمة المسجد الحرام وبيت المقدس وسائر المساجد لمصالح وغيرها من المهمات. (البحر الرائق: ۵/۲۷۱، ط: دار الكتاب الإسلامي). (وكذا في الدر المختار: ٤/۳۷۸، سعيد).

Fatāwā al-Shāmī states:

قوله وجاز دخول الذمي مسجداً، ولو جنباً كما في الأشباه. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۳۸۷، سعيد).

Kifāyat al-Muftī records:

There is no objection to it. The entry of Hindus, Christians and other non-Muslims into a mosque is not forbidden. Non-Muslims enter the Jāmiʿ Masjid every day simply to look around and enjoy themselves, so what objection can there be to their entering for a sound purpose such as fostering unity and establishing peace? (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 10/376, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah)

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
