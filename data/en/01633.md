### TITLE
The lineage of a test-tube baby

### QUESTION
We have been married for the past six years but have no children. One of today's methods of treatment is artificial conception and reproduction, which takes many forms. I need a fatwa on three of these forms: is it permissible to undergo treatment by these three methods and to obtain children in this way? And will the children's lineage be established? The forms are as follows:

(1) (Artificial Insemination) The husband's semen is placed in his wife's womb by injection. This is done because some men, through weakness or illness, are unable to convey their semen to that place.

(2) (In Vitro Fertilisation [IVF]) The reproductive material of both husband and wife is obtained, nurtured for a set period in a tube outside the womb, and then transferred into the wife's womb. This method is used for a woman whose passage for the sperm to reach the womb has become blocked, so that pregnancy does not occur. This is what is called a test-tube baby (Test Tube Baby).

(3) (Gamete Intrafallopian Transfer [GIFT]) The reproductive material of husband and wife is mixed together and, instead of being kept outside in a tube, is introduced directly into the wife's womb. This method can be used only with a woman at least one of whose two fallopian tubes (Fallopian Tube — the tubes whose function is to carry the ovum to the womb) is sound and intact.

### ANSWER
In all three of the forms described above, the reproductive material combined is that of the husband and wife and not of any outsider. Obtaining children in this way, and undergoing such treatment, is therefore permissible and correct where there is a need. One should, however, avoid resorting to it without need. And even where there is a need, there is room only for combining the material of husband and wife; taking an outsider's material and introducing it into the womb is never permitted. If the procedure is completed and a child is born, the child's lineage will be established from the husband and wife.

Mawlānā Khālid Sayfullāh Raḥmānī writes as follows in Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil.

Reproduction brought about by combining the germinal material of husband and wife may take three forms:

(1) The husband's material is conveyed into the woman's womb by injection or the like.

(2) The material of husband and wife is obtained and nurtured in a tube for a set period, and then transferred into the womb of that same woman.

(3) The material of the couple is obtained and the mixture is transferred into the womb of another wife of the same husband, because the first wife cannot endure childbirth, or is unfit to bear a child for medical reasons.

First one must consider whether doing this is sound or not.

The jurists who have held it to be unsound have three points in view:

(1) The man will have to extract his semen by masturbation, and masturbation is not permitted.

(2) It will involve uncovering the private parts of the man and the woman, or at least of the woman; and uncovering them without severe compulsion is not permitted, even before doctors.

(3) This method is contrary to nature, and the general temper of the sharia is to forbid what is contrary to nature.

The answers to these objections are as follows.

(1) In ordinary circumstances the jurists have forbidden masturbation, but where necessity presses they have permitted it. ʿAbd al-Rashīd Ṭāhir al-Bukhārī writes:

"إذا عالج ذكره حتى أمني

ولا يحل هذا الفعل خارج رمضان إن قصد قضاء الشهوة وإن قصد تسكين شهوة أرجو أن لا يكون عليه وبال". (خلاصة الفتاوى: ۱/۲٦۰، الفصل الثالث فيما يفسد الصوم، المكتبة الرشيدية).

The real reason for the prohibition of masturbation is this: in masturbation the germinal material is wasted, whereas when the act is done for the purpose of artificial reproduction the aim is to make that germinal substance effective and fruitful. This case therefore does not come under the forbidden forms of masturbation.

(2) As for its involving the uncovering of the body without severe compulsion, the answer is that the jurists have tolerated such uncovering even in cases which are not themselves grave illnesses but which may possibly lead to grave illnesses — just as childlessness can become the cause of serious illness. Consider the words of ʿAllāmah al-Sarakhsī (may Allah have mercy on him):

"وقد روي عن أبي يوسف رحمه الله أنه إذا كان به هزال فاحش وقيل له إن الحقنة تزيل ما بك من الهزال فلا بأس بأن يبدئ ذلك الموضع للمحتقن وهذا صحيح فإن الهزال الفاحش نوع مرض تكون آخره الدق والسل". (المبسوط للإمام السرخسي رحمه الله: ۱۰/۱۵٦، كتاب الاستحسان، النظر إلى العورة).

Quite apart from necessity, the sharia has at times allowed such uncovering — which is otherwise unlawful — even for the performance of a sunnah or of a merely permissible act. Circumcision, for instance, is only a sunnah for a man and merely permissible for a woman, yet the jurists have counted it within the scope of necessity and excuse and have allowed the uncovering.

Consider the words of ʿAllāmah ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn al-Samarqandī:

"ولا يباح النظر والمس إلى ما بين السرة والركبة إلا في حالة الضرورة بأن كانت المرأة ختانة تختن النساء". (تحفة الفقهاء: ۳/۳۳٤، كتاب الاستحسان).

Again, putting on weight is neither a need nor a necessity, yet even here the jurists have allowed an enema to be used.

Consider the following from Khulāṣat al-Fatāwā:

"لا بأس بالحقنة لا جل السمن هكذا روي عن أبي يوسف رحمه الله". (خلاصة الفتاوى: ٤/۳٦۳، كتاب الكراهية، الفصل الخامس في الأكل، ط: المكتبة الرشيدية).

For a childless couple, recourse to the test tube is in reality a means of treatment. There is no doubt that the jurists have divided human affairs into three categories — necessity, need and refinement — and have allowed what is forbidden only where necessity or need calls for permission. Yet an examination of the detailed rulings of fiqh shows that in the field of medical treatment the jurists have allowed a somewhat wider latitude. The wish of a childless husband and wife for a child is a natural feeling and an instinctive urge, and for its sake the husband's uncovering before a male doctor and the wife's before a female doctor may be tolerated.

(3) As for its being a method contrary to nature, the answer is that in order to fulfil a natural need and urge — the obtaining of a child — it is permissible to adopt an unnatural means so long as there is no explicit text forbidding it. The natural route for the birth of a child, for instance, is the woman's private part, yet where there is a need an operation is permitted. So here too, in a situation of compulsion, this unnatural procedure should be permitted.

The ruling on the establishment of lineage:

For lineage to be established, it is enough that the wife becomes pregnant from the husband's sperm; it is not required that he have physical intercourse with her. So even without intercourse, once the pregnancy is established the child's lineage will be established from the husband and wife. A parallel case is the following:

"رجل وطي جارية في ما دون الفرج فأنزل فأخذت الجارية ماء ه في شيء فاستدخلته في فرجها فعلقت، عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله أن الولد ولده وتصير الجارية أم ولده كذا في فتاوى قاضيخان". (الفتاوى الهندية: ٤/۱۱٤، الباب الرابع عشر في دعوي النسب).

The ruling on the third form:

If the seminal material of a husband and wife is introduced into the womb of the husband's other wife — the co-wife — who will be the child's real mother? Scholars hold differing views on this. The reasonable position is that, for matters such as the prohibitions of marriage, both women should be treated as the child's real mother, while for rulings such as maintenance and inheritance the status of mother should be given to the woman who bore the hardship of the pregnancy and gave birth to the child, as Allah says:

﴿إن أمهاتهم إلا اللائي ولدنهم﴾. (سورة المجادلة: الآية: ۲).

There are also precedents among the jurists in which the lineage of one and the same child has been established from two men.

Consider the following from the commentary on al-Qudūrī:

"وإذا كانت جارية بين اثنين جاء ت بولد فادعياه حتى ثبت النسب منهما". (الجوهرة النيرة: ۲/۸٤، كتاب النكاح، ط: إمدادية، ملتان. وكذا في البحر الرائق: ۳/۱۱۹، كوئته).

Lineage is established from the first woman because the ovum was taken from her and the child has the status of a part of her; and lineage, together with the prohibitions arising from it, rests in essence on this relation of being a part of someone. Discussing the establishment of the prohibition of affinity through fornication, the author of al-Hidāyah writes:

"إن الوطي سبب الجزئية بواسطة الولد حتى يضاف إلى كل واحد منهما كملا". (الهداية: ۲/۳۰۹، فصل في المحرمات).

As for the husband's other wife, in whose womb the child was nurtured, the reason given for establishing lineage from her is that the greatest testimony in favour of the woman who bears the pains of childbirth and in whose womb the child is nurtured is the Qur'an's own way of speaking, which describes the mother by the word wālidah, "the one who gives birth"; and further, while in the womb the child is regarded as a part of that woman.
