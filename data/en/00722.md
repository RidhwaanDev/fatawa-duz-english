### TITLE
Attending congregational prayer in the mosque while wearing a colostomy bag

### QUESTION
A man is ill. A bag has been fitted to his abdomen into which waste — the kind that normally comes out through the back passage — passes through a tube. What is the ruling on this man coming to the mosque and praying with the congregation? Does this fall under the ruling of bringing filth into the mosque or not? And does the presence or absence of a bad smell make a difference to the ruling?

### ANSWER
In ordinary circumstances it is strictly disliked to bring foul-smelling things or filth into the mosque, especially where there is a risk of soiling the mosque. But for a man with a chronic excuse, as in the case described, this bag takes the ruling of his stomach and belly. So if there is no bad smell, and it is covered, and it does not cause people revulsion, then it is permissible and correct for him to go to the mosque. Yes, filth passing into the bag does break his ablution; but since this man has a chronic excuse and filth keeps coming out of his stomach continuously, his ablution will only break with the expiry of the prayer time, unless some other thing that breaks ablution occurs.

It is established that in the time of the Prophet ﷺ a woman suffering from prolonged bleeding kept spiritual retreat in the mosque. Filth was with her, but it was covered and there was no smell either, and so he ﷺ did not object.

Consider what Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī states:

عن عائشة رضي الله تعالى عنها قالت: اعتكفت مع رسول الله ﷺ امرأة من أزواجه مستحاضة فكانت تري الحمرة والصفرة فربما وضعنا الطست تحتها وهي تصلي. (رواه البخاري: ۱/۲۷۳/۱۹۹۱، باب اعتكاف المستحاضة، فيصل)

Commenting on this hadith, ʿAllāmah ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) says that as long as the clothing or the mosque is not soiled, it is fine; and in the same way, those who fall under the same category as a woman with prolonged bleeding — people with a chronic excuse and the like — are also permitted to enter the mosque and keep spiritual retreat there.

Consider what ʿUmdat al-Qārī states:

ومما يستنبط منه: جواز اعتكاف المستحاضة، وجواز صلاتها لأن حالها حال الطاهرات وإنها تضع الطست لئلا يصيب ثوبها أو المسجد وأن دم الاستحاضة رقيق ليس كدم الحيض، ويلحق بالمستحاضة ما في معناها كمن به سلس البول والمذي والودي ومن به جرح يسيل في جواز الاعتكاف. (عمدة القاري: ۳/۱۳۰، كتاب الحيض، باب الاعتكاف للمستحاضة، دار الحديث ملتان).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
