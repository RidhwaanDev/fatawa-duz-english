### TITLE
An examination of the beliefs of the Ismāʿīlī sect

### QUESTION
(a) Are the beliefs of the Ismāʿīlī sect correct or not? If they hold beliefs amounting to disbelief, please present the evidence, because I have heard that they recite the same kalimah as we do.

(b) May we attend their funerals? May our scholars lead their funeral prayer? And may they be buried in our graveyard?

(c) May any one of us marry an Ismāʿīlī, and may our scholars conduct such a marriage or not?

### ANSWER
(a) The beliefs of the Ismāʿīlī sect are as follows:

(1) They hold Allah, glorified and exalted, to be devoid of attributes.

(2) They hold the doctrine of the "first intellect": that Allah created a single created being, and that from it the whole of creation then came into being; and that this first intellect carries the attributes of Allah.

(3) They regard miracles as false.

(4) They deny the finality of prophethood and hold Muḥammad ibn Ismāʿīl to be the last prophet.

(5) In their view, obedience to the saints is obligatory:

"فطاعة الله مقترنة بطاعتهم"

(6) Their imams are made from the light of Allah, and their bodies are not like the bodies of ordinary human beings.

(7) They harbour hatred for the noble Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), and especially for the two Shaykhs, Abū Bakr and ʿUmar.

(8) In their view the delights of Paradise and the punishment of Hell are figurative, not physical.

(9) They resort to reinterpretation a great deal, so much so that they say every verse of the Noble Qur'an has an inner meaning, even when the verse is perfectly plain.

(10) They deny the Day of Judgement and hold the doctrine of the transmigration of souls.

(الملخص من الحركات الباطنية في العالم الإسلامي عقائدها وحكم الإسلام فيها، للدكتور محمد احمد الخطيب، فصل ثاني: الجانب الباطني في عقائد الاسماعيلين، ص: ۸۵. ۱٤۳).

A sect that holds the beliefs listed above cannot be called Muslim, because these beliefs put a person outside the fold of Islam.

For further detail see:

(الحركات الباطنية، ص: ۸۵. ۱٤۳، وإمداد الفتاوى: ٦/۱۰٦. ۱۰۸), Madhāhib-e ʿĀlam kā Encyclopedia, (ومكالمه بين المذاهب).

For the evidence see:

(الفقه الأكبر: ۳/ ۱۲، وشرح الفقه الأكبر: ۲۷، وعقيدة الطحاوي: ٦/۹).

(b) Attending their funerals is forbidden, leading their funeral prayer is likewise impermissible, and it is not permissible to give them a place in the graveyard of the Muslims either, because these rulings belong specifically to Muslims and these people are outside the fold of Islam. In non-Muslim countries, however, where graveyards are set aside for Muslims, we have no right under the law of the state to stop anyone who outwardly professes the kalimah; in that respect we are helpless.

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

﴿ولا تصل على أحد منهم مات أبداً ولا تقم على قبره﴾ ﴿ما كان للنبي والذين آمنوا أن يستغفروا للمشركين﴾

The disbelief of the Shia is likewise evident, and the verses quoted plainly forbid performing the funeral prayer over disbelievers, visiting their graves, and seeking forgiveness for them. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/220).

(c) It is clear from their beliefs that these people are disbelievers. So it is neither correct to enter into a marriage bond with them, nor correct to conduct their marriage. Consider the following:

قال الله تعالى: ﴿ولا تنكحوا المشركات حتى يؤمن﴾.

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

لا يجوز نكاح المجوسيات ويدخل في عبدة الأوثان عبدة الشمس والنجوم والصور التي استحسنوها والمعطلة والزنادقة والباطنية والإباحية وكل مذهب يكفر به معتقده كذا في فتح القدير. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۲۸۱، وكذا في الشامي: ۳/ ٤۹).
