### TITLE
The lineage of a child carried in the womb of an unrelated woman

### QUESTION
In modern methods of reproduction it sometimes happens that the seminal material of a husband and wife is combined and then placed by tube in the womb of an unrelated woman (whether married or unmarried); the material develops in her womb and is born as a child. The question is: from whom will this child's lineage be established? And what will be the status of that unrelated woman?

### ANSWER
In the case described, this procedure is unlawful and impermissible. However, once it has been carried through, the child's real mother is the woman who bore the hardship of the pregnancy and gave birth to the child. Further detail on the establishment of lineage is set out below.

Consider the following from ʿAṣr-e Ḥāḍir ke Fiqhī Masāʾil.

In this connection one should keep in mind that in law the child's mother will be the woman who gave birth to it, even if the ovum used was that of some other woman. Allah's declaration is quite explicit:

﴿إن أمهاتهم إلا اللائي ولدنهم﴾. (سورة المجادلة: الآية: ۲).

Translation: Their mothers are none but those who gave birth to them.

If the sperm of another man has been used, and the child is born from the womb of a married woman, then in that case the child's lineage will be established from that woman's actual husband, and the man whose material was used will be treated as a fornicator. The Prophet ﷺ said:

"الولد للفراش وللعاهر الحجر". (رواه البخاري: ۲/۹۹۹، باب الولد للفراش وللعاهر الحجر).

That is: the child belongs to the owner of the bed — the lawful husband — and for the fornicator there is the stone, that is, the punishment of stoning.

If a child is produced in this way from the womb of an unmarried woman, then even if the man whose material was used is known, lineage will not be established from him. The child will be ascribed to its mother, since in law a person does not acquire the honour of lineage through immorality.

The footnote states:

A remarkable view in this connection is the one expressed by Dr Muṣṭafā Aḥmad al-Zarqā, namely that the child's real mother will be the woman whose ovum was used with the man's sperm, while the woman who gives birth will have a status resembling that of a foster mother, and inheritance and similar ties will be with the first woman. It is plain that, given the emphasis and exclusivity with which the Qur'anic verse states that motherhood is bound up with giving birth, no room remains for such a view.

It is on Dr Muṣṭafā's research that al-Majmaʿ al-Fiqhī of Saudi Arabia based its fatwa; and there is no doubt that Dr Muṣṭafā al-Zarqā is a distinguished scholar of our time and a jurist of mature judgement. Yet this conclusion of his does not appear to be correct. The criticism made by his own compatriot and fellow student, the well-known writer Shaykh ʿAlī al-Ṭanṭāwī, who differed with his view vigorously, appears to be sound, and in our view it is indeed the correct position.
