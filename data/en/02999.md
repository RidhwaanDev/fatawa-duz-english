### TITLE
A judge's letter to another judge

### QUESTION
Is a judge's letter to another judge valid according to the sharīʿah or not? For example, in Johannesburg a man brings a claim against another man, but this second man is in Durban and it is not possible, or is difficult, to bring the two together. The plaintiff takes the witnesses to the judge and has them give their testimony, whereupon the judge accepts their testimony and writes in a letter: after the testimony of the witnesses I have given this decision against such and such a person, kindly enforce it; and he despatches this letter to the judge in Durban. Can the judge in Durban give a decision on the basis of it or not? Will this letter be given consideration or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, according to the ẓāhir al-riwāyah of the Ḥanafī school such a letter will not be given consideration in the case of movable property; but in our times the position on which the fatwa is given is that it is given consideration in movable property as well, and a decision may be given in accordance with it.

Fatḥ al-Qadīr records:

ولا يقبل في الأعيان المنقولة كالحمار والثوب والعبد للحاجة إلى الإشارة فيها

وعن محمد رحمه الله أنه يقبل في جميع ما ينقل من الدواب والثياب والإماء وعليه المتأخرون ونص الاسبيجابي رحمه الله على أن الفتوى عليه وبه قال مالك رحمه الله وأحمد رحمه الله والشافعي رحمه الله في قول. (فتح القدير: ۷/ ۲۸۸، دار الفكر).

Al-Bināyah fī Sharḥ al-Hidāyah states:

وقال في شرح الطحاوي: وقال ابن أبي ليلي يقبل في جميع ذلك أي يقبل كتاب القاضي إلى القاضي في المنقول وغيره ثم قال فيه والفتوى على هذا لتعامل الناس. (البناية في شرح الهداية: ۳/۲۸۳).

Durr al-Mukhtār states:

القاضي يكتب إلى القاضي في كل حق

وفي الشامي: قوله كل حق، من نكاح وطلاق وقتل موجبة مال وأعيان ولو منقولة وهو المروي عن محمد وعليه المتأخرون وبه يفتي للضرورة. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۵/٤۳۲، سعيد).
(وكذا في حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ۳/۲۰۹، والفتاوى الهندية: ۳/۳۸۱، ومجمع الأنهر شرح ملتقي الأبحر: ۲/۱٦۵، والبحر الرائق: ۷/۲، وتبيين الحقائق: ٤/۱۸٤).

Islāmī ʿAdālat records:

During the hearing of a case and the giving of a decision, occasion arises for correspondence between the judges of two districts and for the sending of case files, whether for the completion of the proceedings, for the decision, or for the execution of the decision. On what conditions may the second judge accept these files and the writings sent by the judge?

In all matters other than the prescribed punishments and retaliation, a writing and a case file sent by one judge will be valid in the view of another judge and may become the basis of a ruling. It is necessary that whatever papers a judge sends to another judge, whether for the execution of his decision or for a decision to be given, he should send by the most careful and trustworthy means.

For the acceptance of a judge's letter it is sufficient that the judge to whom it is addressed recognises the signature and the seal of the judge who wrote it, and that he is satisfied in his heart that this is a writing sent by the judge who wrote it and that there is no forgery in it. If the judge addressed has the slightest doubt, he should either summon the writing judge himself or else call for two reliable testimonies.

In the present age the conveyance of papers by post reduces the possibility of any forgery to a comparative minimum, and there is convenience in it as well; otherwise the sending of two witnesses with every case file would be a cause of great expense. Therefore sending them by registered or insured post, with a formal signature and seal — which should be on the papers inside as well as on the envelope — should be regarded as valid. However, if the judge addressed has any doubt in the matter, he may have his doubt removed by the first judge. (اسلامی عدالت مع الحاشیہ، جلدِ اول، ص ٤۲۵، ٤۲۸)۔
