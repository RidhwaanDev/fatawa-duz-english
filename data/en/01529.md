### TITLE
Saying "Go to your mother's house until your senses return"

### QUESTION
In a fit of anger a man said to his wife, "Go off to your mother's house until your senses return." There was no talk of divorce here, nor did the husband have any intention of divorce; rather, a quarrel arose over the children and the husband said this sentence. Does a divorce take effect by it or not, and why? Kindly answer and oblige.

### ANSWER
Assuming the question to be accurate, no divorce has taken effect in the case described. The reason is that the words "Go off to your mother's house" are among the allusive expressions, and the ruling for an allusive expression is that a divorce takes effect if there is an intention of divorce. In the case described the words that follow are themselves against divorce, namely, "until your senses return"; from this it is clear that the purpose was not to give a divorce but to keep her at her mother's house for a time.

Consider what al-Hidāyah states:

وبقية الكنايات إذا نوى بها الطلاق كانت واحدة بائنة، وإن نوى ثلاثاً كان ثلاثاً

وهذا مثل قوله أنت بائن، وألحقي بأهلك. (الهداية: ۲/۳۷۴. وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۳۷۵).

Al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

الفصل الخامس في الكنايات: لا يقع بها الطلاق إلا بالنية أو بدلالة الحال كذا في الجوهرة النيرة. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۳۷۴).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
