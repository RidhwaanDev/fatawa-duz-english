### TITLE
Further doubts raised by some of the noble muftīs, and their answers

### QUESTION
Further doubts raised by some of the noble muftīs, and their answers.

### ANSWER
Doubt (1): The wording of ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī:

ولا يخفي أن الجرح بالرصاص إنما هو بالإحراق والثقل بواسطة اندفاعه العنيف إذ ليس له حد. (فتاوى الشامي: ٤۷۱/٦، سعيد).

أقول:.

That there is no burning is an accepted fact, as Quṭb al-Aqṭāb Ḥaḍrat Shaykh Gangohī (raḥimahullāh) himself established by experiment. (تذکرہ الرشید: ۱۳۹/۱). Shaykh al-Ḥamzāwī, the Muftī of Damascus, has likewise established it by research.

As for the matter of weight, the question is: does the bullet penetrate by reason of its weight? Weight makes a thing fall, does it not?

The second point is that attributing weight to the bullet is contrary to what is self-evident; on the contrary, it does its work by sheer swiftness. The point of the bullet and the force of the gunpowder together generate this swiftness in it.

His words:
إذ ليس له حد فلا يحل
— ḥadd is used in the sense of ṭaraf, and ṭaraf is used for the edge, the extremity and the point, all alike.

ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (raḥimahullāh) in fact spoke with the gun of his own age in view when he said that it is not a sharp instrument and that on that account the flowing out of the blood is not achieved by it — for it is possible that at that time bullets made specially for hunting did not exist. But had ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (raḥimahullāh) observed the bullets of the present-day gun — "which are manufactured specially for hunting, which are sharp-pointed, and by which wounding and the flowing out of the blood are indeed achieved" — he would never have written that it is impermissible. Indeed, he himself stated explicitly that if the instrument is sharp and the blood flows out, the game is lawful.

Consider the following, which he quotes from Qāḍīkhān (raḥimahullāh):

قال قاضيخان: لا يحل صيد البندقة والحجر والمعراض

لأنه لا يخزق إلا أن يكون شيء من ذلك قد حدده وطوله كالسهم وأمكن أن يرمي به؛ فإن كان كذلك وخزقه بحده حل أكله، وأما الجرح الذي يدق في الباطن ولا يخزق في الظاهر لا يحل لأنه لا يحصل به إنهار الدم

الخ. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/٤۷۱، سعيد).

The gist of this passage is that if the wound is produced by khazq — that is, by penetration — and the blood flows out, the game is lawful; but if the wound is to be ascribed to weight, with no penetration in it, then that is daqq, that is, pounding, and no blood flows from it, so the game will not be lawful.

On observing the bullets of the present age it becomes clear that the wound is not to be ascribed to pounding; rather the wound is brought about by penetration, and the blood flows out as well. Hence, even according to the research of ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (raḥimahullāh), the game will be lawful.

Keeping in view the explanation given above and the nature of hunting bullets, in the light of the words of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Taqī ʿUthmānī Ṣāḥib (may his eminence endure) hunting with a gun becomes lawful by common agreement. Consider his words:

The difference of opinion set out above holds good where the bullet is not pointed; but if the bullet has been made such that it is pointed, then in that case the animal will become lawful by common agreement. (تقریر ترمذی: ۲/۱۳۳، بندوق سے شکار کیے ہوے جانور کا حکم).

From the photographs it is as clear as broad daylight that the special hunting bullets are pointed.

Experiment and observation:

Some hunters carried out an experiment in this way: they placed a Coca-Cola can on the ground and took aim at it; the bullet passed clean through, yet the can did not fall over. Had there been weight in the bullet, the can would have fallen — but it is not so.

The upshot is that there is no weight in it, just as ʿAllāmah Abū al-Suʿūd al-Miṣrī al-Ḥanafī has stated in Fatḥ al-Muʿīn that if the killing is by a wound on account of lightness, it is lawful. Consider the following:

وأما إذا كان القتل بالجرح للخفة بأن كان لها حدة فيحل. (فتح المعين: ۲/۴۳۱، كتاب الصيد، ط: سعيد).

Furthermore, ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (raḥimahullāh), in Kitāb al-Jināyāt, while ruling that retaliation is obligatory, says:

قلت: وعلى كل فالقتل بالبندقة الرصاص عمد لا نها من جنس الحديد وتجرح فيقتص به. (فتاوى الشامي: ۶/۵۲۸، كتاب الجنايات، سعيد).
وفي حاشية الطحطاوي على الرد المختار: قلت فعلى ظاهر الرواية لا شك في وجوب القصاص بالقتل بالبندقة لا نها من جنس الحديد وعلي الأصح يقتص أيضاً لجرحها. (حاشية الطحطاوي: ۲۵۲/۴).
وفي شرح منظومة ابن وهبان: والحاصل: إن كل ما تتعلق به الذكاة في البهائم يتعلق به وجوب القصاص وما لا، فلا، كذا ذكره الناطفي رحمه الله في الأجناس. (شرح منظومة ابن وهبان: ۲۰۰/ ۲، وهكذا في الفتاوى البزازية: ۳۸۱ /۶، على هامش الفتاوى الهندية).

(The well-known position of the Ḥanafīs is that retaliation is not obligatory for a killing with a blunt instrument.)

Here a question is put to the noble muftīs: if a man kills someone by firing a bullet, do you hold retaliation to be obligatory or not? Is this a killing with a heavy instrument?

And what is commonly said — that an arrow is light and a bullet heavy — is likewise contrary to what is self-evident and observed. The arrow is certainly the heavier of the two. (No analogy should be drawn with the arrows of today; indeed, even today's arrow is heavier than a bullet.)

Doubt (2): An arrow can also be used on its own, without a bow, as a weapon, whereas a bullet has no such capacity.

The answer to this is that a clipped nail or a broken tooth are likewise not weapons and cannot customarily cut the vessels; yet if they do cut them, it is lawful. In the same way, if a pointed bullet makes the blood flow — and it makes it flow amply — it ought to be lawful. For the flowing out of the blood is common to both voluntary and necessitated slaughter: when it makes the blood flow and is a thing possessing an edge, that is sufficient. As for the condition that it be capable of use as a weapon on its own, that has nowhere come to our notice.

Doubt (3): There remains the doubt that perhaps it died on account of the weight.

أقول:

If the doubt of its having been killed by weight is absent, and the blood also flows out well — which is the essential element in necessitated slaughter — then why should it not be lawful?

Moreover, the answer to this misgiving has been given by the commentator of Tuḥfat al-Mulūk, Shaykh Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd al-Laṭīf Ibn Malak, in Sharḥ Tuḥfat al-Mulūk: namely, that if the game is killed with a pellet of clay, the game is unlawful and there is no need to entertain any doubt about it; but if it is killed, after Bismillāh has been recited, with a bullet or shot of tin, copper or the like, and it has the capacity to make the blood flow, then the game so taken is without doubt lawful. Consider the passage:

ولا يحل الصيد بالبندقة وهي: طينة مدورة يرمي بها؛ لا نها لا تجرح بل يدق ويكسر

قال: في الكنز وما قتله المعراض بعرضه أو البندقة حرم.
وقال في الينابيع: وإن رماه بمعراض فجرحه أكل كيف ما أصابه، وكذلك البندقة والحجر، والعودة، فيحمل كلام صاحب الكنز على البندقة من الطين، وكلام الينابيع على البندقة من الرصاص. انتهي. وقال الشيخ عبد المجيد الدرويش في تعليقات شرح التحفة: قوله البندقة من الرصاص، وهو محدد يجرح. (شرح تحفة الملوك مع التعليقات: ۲/۱۷۱۸).

Then, too, to declare a lawful thing unlawful is itself an excess.

﴿قل من حرم زينة الله التي أخرج لعباده والطيبات من الرزق﴾

How can it be right to declare wholesome and lawful sustenance to be unlawful? When the lawful and the unlawful are combined, the unlawful is given precedence — but the instance of that is where a Magian's dog and a Muslim's dog share in the hunt: there the doubt is strong and unlawfulness prevails. Here the case is not so, فتأمل.

Doubt (4): The majority of the Ḥanafī scholars incline to non-permissibility.

The answer to this is that the Muftī of Syria, who was a Ḥanafī, wrote a treatise on lawfulness which is well known under the title
"فتوى الخواص في حل ما صيد بالرصاص"
and is set out in
منية الصيادين
from p. 192 to p. 198. Likewise the author of
الفقه الحنفي وأدلته
also holds it permissible. ʿAllāmah Shaykh Muḥammad ʿĀbid al-Anṣārī al-Sindī (raḥimahullāh) has also written a treatise on the lawfulness of such game. Besides these, the wording of the Ḥanafī, Mālikī and Ḥanbalī jurists has been cited above, and from this too it becomes clear that the majority hold it lawful.

As for the muftīs of the present day who have written that it is impermissible, on reflecting upon their evidences it appears that these scholars fall into two groups:

(1) Some have made the wording of ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī their foundation; the answer to that, together with some brief discussion of it, has already been given.

(2) Others have drawn an analogy between the gun of the present age and the bundaqah of the age of the jurists; and what the difference between the two is has already been written in detail.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم

The bullets used in the gun of the present age are pointed, and wounding and the flowing out of the blood are amply achieved by them. Observe this in the accompanying photographs.

The reasons for writing a paper on the question of hunting:

In reality there are three reasons for undertaking a fuller investigation of this question.

(1) In South Africa the noble muftīs of most of the country's dār al-iftās have established an academy under the name "Jamʿiyyat al-Muftīn", in which current affairs and modern questions are discussed and debated. In this connection, on 7 May 2003, corresponding to 6 Rabīʿ al-Thānī 1424 AH, on a Saturday, at Azaadville, a discussion and debate was held on the question of hunting, and the noble muftīs presented their respective researches and papers. It was for that very gathering that one part of this paper was prepared and presented to the participants of the seminar. Later, further additions were made; and afterwards this humble servant also obtained the treatise of ʿAllāmah Muḥammad ʿĀbid al-Anṣārī al-Sindī from a bookshop in Islamabad. Shaykh Muḥammad ʿĀbid al-Sindī is the shaykh of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ghanī Mujaddidī. Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ghanī Mujaddidī, in his marginalia on Sunan Ibn Mājah entitled "Injāḥ al-Ḥājah", says in the question of tawassul:

ذكر شيخنا عابد السندي في رسالته، (ص ۹۸).

Then on p. 99 he writes:

وقد كتب شيخنا المذكور رسالة مستقلة فيها التفصيل. (ص ۹۹)

And Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ghanī (raḥimahullāh) is the shaykh of both Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muḥammad Qāsim Nānautwī and Ḥaḍrat Gangohī.

(2) The second important and fundamental reason is that in this country hunting is widely practised. People generally go out during the holidays and hunt, and the animals are quite costly — an animal may cost four or five thousand. Then, after the shot has struck, it is sometimes difficult to reach the game because of the great distance, so that it sometimes happens that after being struck the animal dies before the hunter reaches it, which causes considerable loss. On this account those who hunt particularly drew attention to the need for this question to be pondered and for correct guidance to be given in the light of the sharīʿah.

(3) Since implements keep changing in every age, the nature of the gun in the age of the jurists was different, on account of which most of the jurists and hadith scholars applied the word "gun" to a clay pellet; whereas in our age the nature of the gun is altogether separate and different. Hence there was need to make clear the difference between the two — for it is obvious that with changing implements the rulings change too, and the ruling that applies to a clay pellet is certainly not the ruling that applies to a pointed bullet.
