### TITLE
Trade goods are valued at their selling price

### QUESTION
A man wants to pay the zakat on the trade goods in his shop. Which value should he use — the purchase price or the selling price? And the price at which point in time?

### ANSWER
In the case described, it is the selling price that counts when paying zakat on trade goods.

Consider what al-Fiqh al-Islāmī wa Adillatuh states:

يقوم التاجر العروض أو البضائع التجارية في آخر كل عام بحسب سعرها في وقت إخراج الزكاة لا بحسب سعر شرائها ويخرج الزكاة المطلوبة. (الفقه الإسلامي وأدلته: ۲/۷۹۲، ۷٦۰، دار الفكر).

Al-Fiqh ʿalā al-Madhāhib al-Arbaʿah states:

وتعتبر قيمتها في البلد الذي فيه المال حتى لو أرسل تجارة إلى بلد آخر فحال عليه الحول اعتبرت قيمتها في تلك البلد فلو أرسلها إلى مفازة اعتبرت قيمتها في أقرب الأمصار إلى تلك المفازة. (الفقه على المذاهب الأربعة: ۱/٦۰۷).

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states:

Question: A trader has goods in hand, and now, after a year has passed, he wants to pay zakat. Is the purchase price of those goods to be taken into account, or the market rate?

Answer: Zakat is to be paid according to the market price of the trade goods at the time the zakat is paid, whether that is higher or lower than the purchase price — on the authority of Shāmī. (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband: 6/141).

Jawāhir al-Fatāwā states:

In all wealth, whether gold and silver or trade goods, it is the selling price that counts for the obligation of zakat, not the purchase price. (Jawāhir al-Fatāwā: 1/63; Imdād al-Fatāwā: 2/42).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

The current rate of silver and gold is what counts for paying zakat. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 4/301).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
