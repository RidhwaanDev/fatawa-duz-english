### TITLE
The ruling on the fast when the smells of food and spices in the kitchen reach a woman

### QUESTION
Women cook in the kitchen, and the fragrance and smoke of the spices and the various dishes reaches up into their heads. Does this have any effect on the fast or not?

### ANSWER
There are certain things that give neither the benefit of nourishment nor that of medicine, and which it is very hard — often impossible — to avoid: smoke, for example, and dust, and, in the case of women, the fragrant smoke of the various dishes and spices while cooking. The ruling of the sharia about these is that if such things occur without one's choosing them, they neither invalidate the fast nor make it disliked, and they have no effect whatever upon it.

See al-Durr al-Mukhtār:

دخل حلقه غبار أو ذباب أو دخان ولو ذاكراً استحساناً لعدم إمكان التحرز عنه ومفاده أنه لو أدخل حلقه الدخان أفطر أي دخان كان ولو عوداً أو عنبراً لو ذاكراً لا مكان التحرز عنه. (الدر المختار: ۲/۳۹۵، سعيد. وكذا في مراقي الفلاح: ۲۳۹، بيروت. وفتاوى قاضيخان علي هامش الهندية: ۱/۲۰۸).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states:

If dust from grinding or sifting enters someone's throat, or the taste of a medicine (that is, a person pounded some medicine and felt its taste in his throat), or dust from pounding and the like, or smoke, or the dust of the earth that is raised by the wind or by the hooves of animals, or anything else of this kind, the fast will not be invalidated by it, whether he was remembering his fast or not, because avoiding these things is not possible. This ruling holds where the thing enters of itself, without any act of the fasting person. If, while remembering his fast, he brought any of these things in by an act of his own, his fast will be invalidated, since he could have avoided it by closing his mouth — and people are heedless of this. (عمدۃ الفقہ: کتاب الصوم حصۂ سوم: ۲۶۴، مجددیہ) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
