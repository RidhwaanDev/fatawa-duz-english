### TITLE
The ruling on selling goods before taking possession of them

### QUESTION
Some traders order goods from abroad. Once the deal has been settled with the suppliers abroad, these traders go round to the shopkeepers even before the goods arrive, take orders from them and collect the money as well, even though the goods have not yet come into their possession. Sometimes they only take the orders and do not collect the money. Is doing this permissible or not?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, this transaction falls under the heading of selling before taking possession, which the noble sharia does not allow. In selling before taking possession there is the possibility of uncertainty (gharar), of profiting from something one has not guaranteed, and of ribā al-nasīʾah (interest arising from deferment). For these basic reasons it is binding and necessary to keep away from transactions of this kind.

Mufti Rashīd Aḥmad Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) has, however, set out in Aḥsan al-Fatāwā a few ways in which a transaction of this kind may be made permissible.

See what Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

Selling goods before taking possession of them is not permissible, and so the profit made on such a sale is not lawful either. There are two ways of putting it right:

(1) He should appoint someone at the place where the goods were bought, or the freight company, as his agent to take possession; once that agent has taken possession, the sale is permissible.

(2) He should not make an actual sale before the goods arrive, but should only promise to sell, and should make the sale after the goods arrive. In this case, if either of the two parties then goes back on it, there will only be the sin of breaking a promise; he cannot be compelled to go through with the sale. (And if the goods are destroyed, it is not the buyer's property that is destroyed but the seller's.)

If the buyer pays the freight for delivering the goods, then the seller's handing the goods over, with the buyer's permission, into the custody of any freight company will count as the buyer taking possession, even if the buyer did not specify a particular company. Once the goods have come into the company's custody, the sale is permissible. (And if they are then destroyed, it is the buyer's property that is destroyed.) (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 6/525).

See what the noble hadith states:

عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنه أن رسول الله ﷺ قال: من ابتاع طعاماً فلا يبعه حتى يستوفيه قال ابن عباس رضي الله عنه وأحسب كل شيء مثله. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۵، باب بطلان بيع المبيع قبل القبض).

Another narration states:

عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه قال: كنا في زمان رسول الله ﷺ نبتاع الطعام فيبعث علينا من يأمرنا بانتقاله من المكان الذي ابتعناه فيه إلى مكان سواه قبل أن نبيعه. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۵، باب بطلان بيع المبيع قبل القبض).

In the view of the Ḥanafīs, the reason for the prohibition in these hadiths is the uncertainty of the contract being annulled. ʿAllāmah Ibn al-Humām (may Allah have mercy on him) (d. 861) says in Fatḥ al-Qadīr:

ومن اشتري شيئاً مما ينقل ويحول لم يجز له بيعه حتى يقبضه، لأنه عليه السلام نهي عن بيع مالم يقبض ولأن فيه غرر انفساخ العقد على اعتبار الهلاك

ثم علل الحديث (لأن فيه غرر انفساخ العقد) على اعتبار هلاك المبيع قبل القبض فيتبين حينئذٍ أنه باع ملك الغير بغير إذنه وذلك مفسد للعقد، وفي الصحاح أنه ﷺ نهي عن بيع الغرر. (فتح القدير: ٦/۵۱۰، ۵۱۲، دار الفكر).

Takmilah Fatḥ al-Mulhim states:

إن العلة في النهي عن بيع المبيع قبل القبض هي أنه يستلزم ربح مالم يضمن وإنما يضمن الإنسان ما يخاف فيه الهلاك وأما العقار فلا يخشى فيه ذلك إلا نادراً، حتى لو كان العقار على شط البحر أو كان المبيع علواً لا يجوز بيعه قبل القبض. (تكملة فتح الملهم: ۱/۳۵۳).

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states:

The prohibition of selling before taking possession has come in a number of hadiths. The mujtahid imams have held these hadiths to be based on an underlying legal cause: if that cause is present, the prohibition remains in force; otherwise it does not. Reflection on the statements of the mujtahid imams shows that there are three causes for the prohibition of selling before taking possession: (1) the possibility of uncertainty; (2) profiting from what one has not guaranteed; (3) the possibility of ribā al-nasīʾah. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 15/141).

Aham Fiqhī Fayṣale records:

In the present age many forms of buying and selling are in vogue in which the buyer sells the goods on to others without having taken possession of them. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade selling before taking possession. Against this background, the following points concerning such current transactions were settled at the ninth seminar of the Islamic Fiqh Academy, held at Jāmiʿat al-Hidāyah, Jaipur:

As a matter of principle it is not permissible to sell anything before taking possession of it. However, if a sale is made before taking possession, that sale will be defective (fāsid), not void (bāṭil), and after possession is taken it will convey ownership. (Aham Fiqhī Fayṣale, p. 100).

Naye Masāʾil awr ʿUlamā-e Hind ke Fayṣale records:

The prohibition of selling before taking possession rests on the cause of "the uncertainty of annulment" — that is, so long as the item sold has not come into the hands of the first buyer, there remains the risk that it may never come into his possession at all and that he may be unable to deliver it to the second buyer. (Naye Masāʾil awr ʿUlamā-e Hind ke Fayṣale, p. 106).

Fiqhī Maqālāt states:

In terms of the rulings of the sharia it is necessary that the thing you are selling should already exist, that it should be in your ownership, and that it should also be in your possession — whether that possession is actual or is constructive and customary. Now the question is this: if a thing is not with us and an order for that thing comes to us, then in that case we shall not conclude a sale with the person placing the order, but shall only make a promise of sale. (Fiqhī Maqālāt: 3/76).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

Regarding a defective sale, this too should be borne in mind: if the defect in the sale arose because of a right of the sharia itself and involves some sinful matter, then in every case it is a cause of sin. But if it arose out of the fear of a dispute, and no dispute actually arises, then although the sale will be defective in terms of judicial ruling, it will be correct and valid in terms of a person's conscience before Allah. On this point the following statement of Mawlānā Anwar Shāh Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) is eye-opening:

إن من البيوع الفاسدة ما لو أتي بها أحد جازت ديانة وإن كانت فاسدة قضاءً وذلك لأن الفساد قد يكون لحق الشرع بأن اشتمل العقد على مآثم فلا يجوز بحال وقد يكون الفساد لمخالفة التنازع ولا يكون فيه شيء آخر يوجب الإثم فذلك إن لم يقع فيه التنازع جاز عندي ديانة وإن بقي فاسداً قضاءً لارتفاع علة الفساد وهي المنازعة ويدل عليه مسائلهم في باب المضاربة والشركة فإنها ربما تكون فاسدة مع أن الربح يكون طيباً وراجع "الهداية" ونبه الحافظ ابن تيمية رحمه الله (۷۲۸م) في رسالته على أن من البيوع ما لا يقع فيه النزاع فتكون تلك جائزة فإذا أدخلتها في الفقه وجدتها محظورة لأن أكثر أحكام الفقه تكون من باب القضاء والديانات فيها قليلة وإنما يصار إلى القضاء بعد النزاع فإذا لم يقع النزاع ولم يرفع الأمر الي القاضي نزل حكم الديانة لا محالة فيبقي الجواز. (فيض الباري: ۳/۲۵۸).

For this reason it is the view of the present writer that the prohibition of "selling before taking possession" rests on a right of the servant, not on a right of the sharia, and that its defectiveness is in terms of judicial ruling, not in terms of conscience before Allah. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/213–214).
