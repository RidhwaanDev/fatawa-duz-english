### TITLE
The sharʿī status of democracy and of the vote

### QUESTION
Present-day democracy, in which a president is elected or a parliament is formed by the votes of the people — is it in accordance with the principles of the sharīʿah or contrary to them? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In Islamic government there are four methods for the selection of a leader:

1. The pledge of allegiance of the people of binding and loosing, as in the establishment of the caliphate of Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him).

لما قال الماوردي رحمه الله: أن بيعة أبي بكر رضي الله عنه انعقدت بخمسة اجتمعوا عليها ثم تابعهم ناس فيها وهم الخطاب بن الجراح وأسيد بن حضير وبشر بن سعد وسالم مولى أبي حذيفة ث. (الأحكام السلطانية، ص ۷، الباب الاول في عقد الإمامة).

2. The appointment of a successor, as Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) appointed Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) as caliph and as his own successor.

3. Consultation (shūrā), as Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) formed a shūrā of six men, and the members of the shūrā made Ḥaḍrat ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān (may Allah be pleased with him) caliph.

لما قال الإمام أبو الحسن الماوردي: والثاني: أن عمر رضي الله عنه جعل الشوري في ستة ليعقد لاحدهم برضاء الخمسة. (الأحكام السلطانية، ص ۷، الباب الاول في عقد الإمامة).

4. Seizure of power, as continued to occur in later times. (ملخص از فتاوی حقانیہ: ۲/۲۹۳۔۲۹٤)۔

Thus present-day democracy bears a resemblance to the first kind, the general pledge of allegiance, and to the third kind, consultation. That is to say, taking votes from the people is like consultation, and therefore it will not be called contrary to the sharīʿah; and this method is current in most countries of the world.

If someone raises the objection that this is the method of Europe, the answer is that since the method of consultation, of the pledge of allegiance and of the appointment of a successor is difficult in most Muslim countries, there is no harm in adopting this method. Yes, if any clause of this European method is explicitly contrary to Islam, then adopting it is not permissible.

A precedent for this procedure is found in the story of Ḥaḍrat Yūsuf (peace be upon him). Ḥaḍrat Yūsuf (peace be upon him) was bound by the law of Egypt, for he wished to keep his brother with him, but under the law of Egypt this was not possible, and so he resorted to a stratagem. The verifying scholar Ibn Kathīr (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

وقوله ﴿ما كان ليأخذ أخاه في دين الملك﴾ أي: لم يكن له أخذه في حكم ملك مصر، قاله الضحاك وغيره.

(تفسیر ابن کثیر: ٤/٤۰۱، ط: دار طیبۃ)۔

Qāḍī al-Bayḍāwī (may Allah have mercy on him) explains the law of Egypt:

لأن دينه الضرب وتغريم ضعف ما أخذه دون الاسترقاق.

(تفسیر البیضاوی: ۱/۳۰۱)۔

Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah states: In the present age the vote has several aspects:

1. It has the status of testimony and witness: the voter, when he gives his vote to a member, is testifying that he considers him to be of benefit to the country and the nation and a well-wisher of them.

2. It has the status of counsel: the voter expresses his opinion regarding the government and its administration as to who is better, more faithful and more honest in political affairs.

3. It has the status of a recommendation: the voter recommends this candidate for taking charge of an important office.

4. It has the status of appointing an agent: the voter nominates an agent for himself in the house of government, that this person (the candidate) will have his problems solved by the government.

5. The vote has the status of a political pledge of allegiance: by means of his vote the voter pledges allegiance to the head of state through the local candidate. (فتاوی حقانیہ: ۳۰۲)۔
