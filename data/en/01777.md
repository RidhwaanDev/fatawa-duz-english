### TITLE
Who is liable for blood money in the present age

### QUESTION
Who is liable for blood money, given that in the present age there is no system of the ʿāqilah, and in most places the tribal system too has disappeared?

### ANSWER
Blood money falls upon the ʿāqilah (the kinsmen who share the liability). If, however, the offender admits that he committed the act and his ʿāqilah do not confirm his admission, then it is payable out of the killer's own wealth. Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ولا ما لزم بصلح أو اعتراف إلى قوله إلا أن يصدقوه في إقراره أو تقوم حجة وإنما قبلت البينة هنا مع الإقرار مع أنها لا تعتبر معه لا نها تثبت ما ليس بثابت بإقرار المدعي عليه. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ٦/٦۴۳، كتاب المعاقل، سعيد).

Whether a group counts as a man's ʿāqilah depends upon mutual support and assistance. Those among whom there is mutual support and assistance are his ʿāqilah. So where there is a tribe, and the tribes are organised, and every man knows which tribe he belongs to, that tribe is his ʿāqilah and it should pay his blood money. If there is no tribe but there is an organised kinship group, then it should pay the blood money. If there is no kinship group either, then a body such as the trade unions of today, among whose members there is mutual support and assistance, can be his ʿāqilah. But if even this is lacking, then in any case the blood money should be payable out of the killer's own wealth. Consider what ʿAllāmah Shāmī says:

قلت: وفي زماننا بخوارزم لا يكون إلا في مال الجاني إلا إذا كان من أهل قرية أو محلة يتناصرون لأن العشائر فيها قد وهت ورحمة التناصر من بينهم قد رفعت وبيت المال قد انهدم. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/٦۴۵، سعيد).
