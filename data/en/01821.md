### TITLE
Building a mosque attached to a religious school

### QUESTION
Is it permitted to build a mosque attached to a religious school or not? Some people call such a mosque a masjid ḍirār, a mosque built to cause harm. What is the ruling on this?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, building a mosque attached to a religious school is a good thing and a good work; and when there is no other mosque nearby, the mosque becomes all the more important. A religious school has its own character and its own routine, and once that routine is kept in view, the case for building a mosque alongside the school cannot be denied. Because of study and meals after sunset, for instance, the night prayer is held in a school's mosque a good deal later than elsewhere; and the restrictions on the students' timetable and on their coming and going also make a mosque necessary. Building a mosque attached to this school is therefore not open to objection; it is rather something highly praiseworthy and a cause for gratitude. Those who call this mosque a masjid ḍirār are gravely mistaken and ought to repent.

Muftī ʿAzīz al-Raḥmān (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote in one of his fatawa that we are commanded to think well of others, and so a mosque built by a Muslim cannot be given the ruling of a masjid ḍirār; for one cannot be certain that its founder built the mosque in order to harm another mosque, or out of show and desire for a good name. The mosque of harm mentioned in the Qur'an was different: the corruptness of its foundation and of its builders' intention had been made known by decisive revelation. (ʿAzīz al-Fatāwā: 1/609).

In the case in question there is no corrupt intention at all; the second mosque is being built in view of a genuine need. Moreover, the very idea of demolishing this mosque is a very evil one.
