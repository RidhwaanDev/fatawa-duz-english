### TITLE
Divorce by calling one's wife a divorcée

### QUESTION
A man was in the middle of a quarrel with his wife, and in the course of the exchange he called her a divorcée. Has a divorce taken effect upon his wife or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, by calling her a divorcée one revocable divorce has taken effect upon his wife. If, however, he said it while abusing and insulting her, his word will be accepted as a matter of conscience and no divorce takes effect.

Consider what Imdād al-Aḥkām states:

Where he calls her "divorcée" or "divorced woman", however, one revocable divorce has already taken effect upon the wife (امداد الاحکام: ۲/۴۱۸) ۔

وفي الشامية: يا طالق أو يا مطلقة بالتشديد ولو قال أردت الشتم لم يصدق قضاء ودين، خلاصة، ولو كان لها زوج طلقها قبل فقال أردت ذلك الطلاق صدق ديانة باتفاق الروايات، وقضاء في رواية أبي سليمان وهو حسن كما في الفتح وهو الصحيح كما في الخانية ولو لم يكن لها زوج لا يصدق، وكذا لو كان لها زوج قد مات. (فتاوى الشامي: ۳/۲۵۱، وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۲۵۵، وخلاصة الفتاوى: ۲/۸۰).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

If she had already been divorced by a former husband, and that is why the man called her a divorcée, his word will be accepted in the Sharīʿah. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۲/۳۷۰) ۔

In sum, if he said it intending abuse and insult, and the wife raises no objection, the muftī may give a ruling that no divorce has taken place, since in common usage the word is used for abuse and insult.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
