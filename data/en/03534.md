### TITLE
Verifying the meaning of "wa anā ajzī bihi" in the ḥadīth on the merit of fasting

### QUESTION
Concerning the merit of fasting, a noble ḥadīth says:

الصوم لي وأناْ أجزي به

Some scholars say that this word ajzī is also correct in the passive form, and that its meaning is: he will be given the sight of Allah Most High and His pleasure. Is this meaning correct by the rules of Arabic grammar?

### ANSWER
In most of the books of ḥadīth ajzī has come in the active form, and this is what is well known in the view of most of the ḥadīth scholars and the commentators on ḥadīth; therefore this is what is correct. The explanations of a few of the scholars are presented below:

قال العلامة العيني: قوله أناْ أجزي به بيان لكثرة ثوابه لأن الكريم إذا أخبر بأنه يتولى بنفسه الجزاء اقتضى عظمته وسعته

الظاهر من السياق الأول أي أنا أجازيه لا غيري بخلاف سائر العبادات فإن جزاءها قد يفوض إلى الملائكة. (عمدة القاري: ۸/۱۲، باب فضل الصوم، ط: دار الحديث ملتان).

وفي غريب الحديث، قال: إنما خص الصوم بأن يكون هو الذي يتولى جزاءه. (۱/۳۲۵، ط: بيروت).

وفي فتح الباري: وأناْ أجزي به أي أجازي عليه جزاء كثيراً من غير تعيين لمقداره. (فتح الباري: ٤/۱۰۸، ط: دار المعرفة بيروت).

قال في فيض القدير: وأناْ أجزي به، صاحبه جزاءاً كثيراً وأتولى الجزاء عليه بنفسي فلا أكله إلى ملك مقرب ولا غيره لأنه سر بيني وبين عبدي لأنه لما كف نفسه عن شهواتها جوزي بتولي الله سبحانه إحسانه. (فيض القدير: ٤/۳۲۹/۵۱۹۸).

قال في المرقات: وأناْ أجزي به أي وأنا العالم بجزائه. (مرقاة: ٤/۲۳۲، كتاب الصوم، إمداديه).

وقال النووي: وقيل: معناه أناْ المنفرد بعلم مقدار ثوابه. (شرح صحيح مسلم: ۸/۲۹، فضل الصيام، ط: دار إحياء التراث العربي).

Muftī Muḥammad Taqī ʿUthmānī Ṣāḥib writes, while translating this ḥadīth:

Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah Most High be pleased with him) reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: Fasting is a shield, so a person should neither speak evil nor speak the speech of ignorance. If someone quarrels with him or abuses him, he should say, "I am fasting" — he should say it twice. By the One in whose hand is my life, the smell of the fasting person's mouth is better in the sight of Allah Most High than the fragrance of musk. He gives up food, drink and the things he loves for the sake of the fast. Fasting is for Me and I give the reward for it, and a good deed is rewarded tenfold. (Inʿām al-Bārī: 5/486, bāb faḍl al-ṣiyām, Maktabat al-Ḥirā, Karachi).

The scholars of the Arabic language too mention it only with the active verb:

قال في القاموس الفقهي: جزى الشئء جزاء، كفى وأغنى، وقوله تعالى: واتقوا يوماً لا تجزي نفس عن نفس شيئاً. (البقرة: ٤۸). (القاموس الفقهي: ۱/٦۲، دار الفكر دمشق).

وفي تاج العروس: جزاه كذا وبه وعليه جزاء، ومنه قوله تعالى:

وجزاهم بما صبروا جنة وحريراً. أولئك يجزون الغرفة بما صبروا. ولا تجزون إلا ما كنتم تعملون. (تاج العروس: ۳۷/۳۵۱، ط: دار الهداية).

Some scholars, however, have mentioned both the active and the passive forms of the ḥadīth and given the meanings of both. Consider the following:

It is recorded in the footnotes of Fayḍ al-Bārī:

وقد قرأه بعضهم: وأناْ أجزى به، مجهولاً، وحينئذٍ يكون كناية عن رؤيته تعالى، وقد نظمه الشيخ يعقوب الكشميري تلميذ التلميذ للعارف الجامي، وتلميذ الحافظ ابن حجر رحمه الله المكي الشافعي، في الحديث:

(جو در روزہ امساک از ما سوا است، جزائش آ کر حق بود خودسرا است) — thus ends the Arabic rendering of it. (Ḥāshiyat Fayḍ al-Bārī: 3/149, the Book of Fasting).

Sirāj al-Qārī records, on the authority of Awjaz al-Masālik:

وأناْ أجزي به has been read in two ways. One is "أناْ أجزِي به" (the active verb), that is, I Myself shall give the reward for it, which will be in keeping with My majesty. The other is "أناْ أجزى به" (the passive verb), that is, I Myself shall be given in this reward and prize; the sense being that I shall grant him My pleasure and good favour. (Sirāj al-Qārī: 6/237).

See the words of Ḥaḍrat Shaykh (may Allah have mercy on him): (Awjaz al-Masālik, Jāmiʿ al-Ṣiyām: 5/336/618).

Muftī Saʿīd Aḥmad Pālanpūrī Ṣāḥib has written in his commentary on al-Bukhārī:

His words "ajzī" have been read both in the passive and in the active. The well-known reading is the active, and the ḥadīth scholars read only the active; it is the Ṣūfīs who read the passive. The meaning of the passive is: I am given in return for the fast — that is, the fasting person is granted union (nearness) with Allah Most High. The meaning of the active is the well-known one, that is: I shall give him his reward on the Day of Judgement. (Tuḥfat al-Qārī: 4/571, Maktabah Ḥijāz, Deoband).

The translation mentioned above, however, does not fit in the light of the Qurʾān, the ḥadīth and the language, because the translation of the passive by the rules of Arabic is this: anā ujzā bihi, "I shall be given the reward" — and there is no sense in Allah Most High being given a reward.

Moreover, it is learnt from many verses of the Noble Qurʾān that the substitute subject of the passive ujzā is the one to whom something is given; so on this reading Allah Most High would be the one who receives the recompense and the reward, and this translation is not correct. It is a true statement that the fasting person will be granted the sight of Allah Most High on account of his fasting; the noble ḥadīth says:

للصائم فرحتان؛ فرحة عند فطره وفرحة عند لقاء ربه ". (رواه مسلم، رقم: ۱۱۵۱)،

That is, the fasting person has two joys: 1. the joy at the time of breaking the fast, and 2. the joy of meeting Allah Most High and seeing Him. But to draw the vision of Allah Most High out of the passive ujzā is not correct. Consider the translation of the passive in the light of the verses of the Noble Qurʾān:

1.
﴿فاليوم تجزون عذاب الهون بما كنتم تستكبرون في الأرض بغير الحق وبما كنتم تفسقون﴾ [الأحقاف: ۲۰]،
Translation: So today you will be given the punishment of disgrace, because you used to behave arrogantly in the world without any right, and because you used to disobey. (Mukammal Bayān al-Qurʾān: volume 11, p. 7).

2.
﴿لتجزى كل نفس بما تسعى﴾ [طه: ۱۵]،
Translation: So that every person may receive the recompense of what he did. (Bayān al-Qurʾān: 7/20, Taj Publishers, Delhi).

3.
﴿اليوم تجزى كل نفس بما كسبت لا ظلم اليوم﴾ [غافر: ۱۷]،
Today every person will be given the recompense of what he did. (Bayān al-Qurʾān: 10/36, Taj).

4.
﴿اليوم تجزون عذاب الهون بما كنتم تقولون على الله غير الحق﴾ [الانعام: ۹۳]،
Translation: Today you will be given the punishment of disgrace, because you used to utter false things against Allah Most High. (Bayān al-Qurʾān: 3/113, Taj).

5.
﴿هل تجزون إلا بما كنتم تكسبون﴾ [يونس: ۵۲]،
Translation: You have only been given the recompense of what you yourselves did. (Bayān al-Qurʾān: 5/17, Taj).

6.
﴿اليوم تجزون بما كنتم تعملون﴾ [الجاثية: ۲۸]،
Translation: Today you will receive the recompense of what you did. (Bayān al-Qurʾān: 10/113, Taj).

7.
﴿سيجزون بما كانوا يقترفون﴾ [الأنعام: ۱۲۰]،
Translation: They will soon be given the punishment of what they did. (Bayān al-Qurʾān: 3/124, Taj).

8.
﴿أولئك يجزون الغرفة بما صبروا﴾ [الفرقان: ۷۵]،
Translation: Such people will be given lofty chambers on account of their steadfastness. (Bayān al-Qurʾān: 8/57, Taj edition).

9.
﴿من يعمل سوءاً يجز به﴾ [النساء: ۱۲۳]،
Translation: Whoever does an evil deed will be given punishment in return for it. (Bayān al-Qurʾān: 2/159, Taj).

Had the translation intended been the one our elders (may Allah have mercy on them) gave, then the wording would have been like this:

[الصوم لي وأنت تجزى بي،

or

الصائم يجزى بي أي برؤيتي)، أنا أجزي به،

and it would not have been as it is.

Moreover, the translation based on the passive has been rejected in Hidāyat al-Muḥtār as well. Consider:

ملحوظة: قوله (وأنا أجزي به) يقرؤه بعض الناس مبنياً للمفعول، ويعنون به أن الله هو الجزاء للصائم، وهو خطأ؛ لأن نائب الفاعل يكون من ينال الجزاء، وأن الجزاء يكون مجرور الباء هنا، ولو كان الأمر كما ظنوا لكان حق العبارة أن يقال: (الصوم لي والصائم يجزى بي). (هداية المحتار في دراية الآثار، لمولانا محمد هداية الله القاسمي، ص ۱۲۹، ط: ديوبند).

Among our elders (may Allah have mercy on them), Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) too refuted the translation based on the passive, and Ḥaḍrat Shaykh Yūnus (may Allah have mercy on him) endorsed his refutation. Mawlānā Zayd Nadwī Maẓāhirī has related this. Consider:

The book Faḍāʾil-e-Ramaḍān of Shaykh al-Ḥadīth Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muḥammad Zakariyyā Ṣāḥib used to be read regularly after ʿAṣr in the blessed month of Ramaḍān at the place of Ḥaḍrat Shaykh Mawlānā Muḥammad Yūnus Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him). In it, in connection with the merits of fasting, the noble ḥadīth was mentioned in which the Truth Most High says: "وأناْ أجزي به" — that is, I Myself shall bestow the recompense and reward for the fasting person's fast. But Shaykh al-Ḥadīth Mawlānā Muḥammad Zakariyyā Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Faḍāʾil-e-Ramaḍān under this same ḥadīth number 2: "It is related from some of the mashāʾikh that this word is "وأناْ أجزي به", that is, that in return for it I give My own self — and what higher recompense can there be than meeting the Beloved?" (Faḍāʾil-e-Ramaḍān, first section, ḥadīth 2).

This passage of Faḍāʾil-e-Ramaḍān was read in the gathering of Shaykh al-Ḥadīth Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muḥammad Yūnus Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him). Afterwards this lowly one submitted to Ḥaḍrat Aqdas (may Allah have mercy on him) that Ḥakīm al-Ummah Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) had refuted this and had said that this copy is mistaken and that the meaning of the ḥadīth can never be this. The wording of Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) is given below. He said:

The noble ḥadīth has "وأناْ أجزِي به", that is, I shall give him the recompense. Another copy, with the passive form, "وأناْ اُجزى به", is also well known, that is, the recompense for it is that I shall meet him. Although this subject matter is in itself correct — the Truth Most High will be met in return for it — the mistake is that this subject matter is drawn out of this ḥadīth. Anyone who has even a slight acquaintance with Arabic will never understand this meaning from it, because by the reckoning of Arabic its translation will be "I shall be given the recompense" — that is, God forbid, someone will give Me a reward — and not "I shall be met in the recompense". That is not its translation. So this copy is mistaken, and the correct one is only "وأناْ أجزِي به", that is, I shall give the recompense for it. And is it any small merit of fasting that He says, "I shall give the recompense"? For the rest, the subject matter is in itself correct, that the Truth Most High will be met in return for it. (Waʿẓ al-Ṣawm, p. 120, and Aḥkām-e-Ramaḍān al-Mubārak, p. 73).

This lowly one submitted this matter to Ḥaḍrat Shaykh (may Allah have mercy on him) on the authority of Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him), and asked: Ḥaḍrat, which of the two statements is the more correct? Ḥaḍrat said that what Ḥakīm al-Ummah Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) has said is entirely correct by the reckoning of transmission, of understanding and of the chain. He said that Allah had granted Ḥakīm al-Ummah Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) such a faculty of research that the point he would state beforehand out of his insight, his understanding and his strength of comprehension is the very conclusion we reach only after turning over hundreds of pages — a point which Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) had already stated out of his understanding and comprehension. Truly, the narration " وانااجزی بہ" in the noble ḥadīth is not sound; Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) said exactly the right thing. (ʿIlmī o Iṣlāḥī Irshādāt maʿ Mukhtaṣar Sawāniḥ Shaykh Muḥammad Yūnus Ṣāḥib raḥimahullāh, by Mawlānā Zayd Maẓāhirī Nadwī, pp. 157, 158).

A point of benefit: الصوم لي can bear six meanings:

1. ـ الصوم لي لا للأصنام، the polytheists did not keep fasts for anyone other than Allah.

2. ـ الصوم أحب إلي.

3. ـ الصوم صفة لي (فإن الله لا يأكل ولا يشرب).

4. ـ الصوم، لا رياء فيه لأنه ترك لا فعل.

5. ـ الصوم المفروض لا يعطى في المظالم.

6. ـ لم يخبر الله بثوابه الملائكة بل أخفى ثوابه الكثير عنده.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
