### TITLE
Selling property endowed to a mosque

### QUESTION
A mosque owns several endowed properties. One of the mosque's patrons sold off part of them without the permission of the others. What is the ruling on this in the eyes of the sharia? Please explain.

### ANSWER
No one has the authority to sell land endowed to a mosque. By selling it, the patron in question did something contrary to the sharia which was not permissible. See the following:

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī states:

"عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه أن عمر رضي الله عنه تصدق بمال له على عهد رسول الله ﷺ وكان يقال له ثمغ وكان نخلا فقال عمر رضي الله عنه يا رسول الله اني استفدت مالاً وهو عندي نفيس فاردت أن أتصدق به فقال النبي ﷺ تصدق بأصله لا يباع ولا يوهب ولا يورث، ولكن ينفق ثمره الخ. (رواه البخاري: ۱/۳۸۷/۲٦۸۳).

وفي الهداية: قال وإذا صح الوقف لم يجز بيعه ولا تمليكه الخ. (الهداية ۲/٦٤۰)

وفي الشامي: فإذا تم ولزم لا يملك، ولا يملك ولا يعار ولا يرهن قوله لا يملك أي لا يكون مملوكا ً لصاحبه ولا يملك أي لا يقبل التمليك لغيره بالبيع ونحوه لاستحالة تمليك الخارج عن ملكه. (فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۳۵۱).

وفي الفتاوى الهندية: وفي الفتاوى النسفية سئل عن أهل المحلة باعوا وقف المسجد لا جل عمارة المسجد قال لا يجوز بأمر القاضي وغيره كذا في الذخيرة. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۲/٤٦۳).

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

So long as any benefit at all can be derived from endowed land, the sharia gives no permission for it to be sold — on the authority of Shāmī. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ٦/۷۳) ۔

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

If the item is immovable property that has been endowed for the mosque, then selling it is in no way permissible — on the authority of ʿĀlamgīrī. (امداد الفتاوی: ۲/۷۱۷) ۔

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Where a house has been endowed for a mosque, the permission of the Sunni Central Waqf Board is not enough for it to be sold. There is no right to sell an endowed house. The trustee should be called to account for why he sold it, for it is not fit to be sold. The sale should be cancelled and the house restored to its former standing as an endowment.

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah says in another place:

The purpose of endowing land is that the land itself should remain and its income be spent in the way of Allah. Such land is not handed over for trading. It is therefore not permissible to sell it and acquire other land with a larger income — unless no benefit whatever can any longer be derived from the endowed land, in which case the ruling is different: it is then correct to buy other land in its place with the proceeds and endow that instead. (فتاوی محمودیہ ۱۵/۱۷۵) ۔

These passages of the jurists make it clear that selling property endowed to a mosque is in no way correct in the sharia, whether everyone concerned gives permission or no one does. In the case at hand, even though the land has been sold, the buyer has not become its owner. It should be taken back and endowed once more for the mosque. If recovering it is legally impossible, then another site should be bought for the mosque with that money and used for the interests of the mosque.
