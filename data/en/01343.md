### TITLE
Using contraceptive methods other than ʿazl

### QUESTION
Apart from the method of ʿazl, are the other contraceptive methods that resemble it — barrier methods and the like — also permissible?

### ANSWER
The other methods of preventing pregnancy besides ʿazl, whether they take the form of injections or of pills, will all be permissible if they are resorted to on account of an excuse recognised by the sharīʿah. And if the use of some medicine also has negative effects on bodily health — what are called side effects — then it should not be used without the advice of an expert physician, because in the light of the Qur'ān and the Sunnah the protection of human health is necessary.

If, however, the measure is taken some weeks after the seminal fluid has reached the womb, that is not called ʿazl. The ruling on that is that it should not be done without some severe necessity and the advice of an expert physician.

Consider what Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

The second form of birth control is that the seminal fluid does reach the womb, but such medicines are used that the pregnancy cannot become established. From the parallels in fiqh it appears that this form too is impermissible in ordinary circumstances. It is correct that the substance is as yet devoid of soul and life, so that destroying it will not fall within the category of killing in the technical sense; but if it were left in its own state, then after some time had passed it would take on the form of a living soul, and so, keeping the outcome in view, it will be regarded as tantamount to taking a life.

The jurists have offered this parallel: if a person in the state of iḥrām breaks a bird's egg, then just as killing the bird entails a compensatory sacrifice and expiation, in the same way merely breaking the egg will also entail a compensatory sacrifice…

Therefore, without some extraordinary excuse, the use of such means merely to avoid having children is not permitted. Yes, if there is a fear of some greater harm, then in such a case this lesser harm will be tolerated in order to avoid the other, greater harm. For example, if according to a reliable medical assessment there is a fear of the mother's death in the event of the child's birth, or there is a danger of the child in the womb itself being afflicted with a serious hereditary disease, or the pregnancy is the result of fornication, then in such cases the use of contraceptive means will be permitted. (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۵/۱۲۷ ۔ ۱۳۰، مانع حمل دوائیں)۔
