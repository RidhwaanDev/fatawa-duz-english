### TITLE

Finding a bag of charity at the door on the night of Eid

### QUESTION

On the night of Eid someone anonymously left a bag of rice, given as the charity of Eid al-Fitr, at a man's door and went away. Now this man is himself well off, so may he give its value in charity and use the rice himself, or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER

Apparently the owner left the bag of rice thinking that the householder was entitled to receive it. But since the householder is not entitled to it, he should return it to the owner; and if reaching the owner is difficult or impossible, he should give it in charity on the owner's behalf. It is not correct for a man of means to use it himself. Moreover, this rice has the status of a trust, so he is not permitted to alter it either.

Consider what is transmitted in Fatāwā Shāmī:

قال في شرح الوهبانية وفي البزازية قال الإمام الحلواني إذا كان عنده وديعة فمات المودع بلا وارث له أن يصرف الوديعة إلى نفسه في زماننا هذا، لأنه لو أعطاها لبيت المال لضاع؛ لأنهم لا يصرفون مصارفه فإذا كان من أهله صرفه إلى نفسه وإن لم يكن من المصارف صرفه إلى المصرف. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۳۳٦، سعيد، وكذا ٤/۱۵۹، و ٦/۷۳٤، سعيد).

Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah states:

If the trustee does not know the whereabouts of the person who left the trust with him, he should himself give the property in charity on that person's behalf, or make a bequest that it be given in charity. If, however, the trustee is poor, he may also consume it himself. الخ. قال العلامة الحصكفي رحمه الله: فينتفع الرافع بها لو فقيراً وإلا تصدق بها على فقير ولو على أصله وفرعه وعرسه

(الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٤/۲۷۹، ط: سعيد)،

(Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah: 6/403).

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā states:

Question: A man is holding something in trust. Even after a long time there is no trace of the owner. What is the ruling on this trust now? For how much longer should it be kept? And if the owner does not come, what should be done with it?

Answer: In the case in question, if it is most likely that the owner has died or will not come, and no heir of his is known either, then its ruling is like that of found property: if he is well off he should give it in charity, and if he is himself in need he is permitted to use it. (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 6/97.)
