### TITLE
Ruling on a written ṭalāq obtained by force and coercion

### QUESTION
A woman had a divorce document drawn up, took a policeman along with her and went to her husband. The policeman threatened the husband that he must sign the paper or he would shoot him. The husband asked what was written in it, and the policeman said, "One ṭalāq." Under force and coercion, and against his will, the husband signed the divorce document. He was told afterwards that three ṭalāqs were written in it. What is now to be determined is how many ṭalāqs have taken effect — one or three?

### ANSWER
In the matter of a written ṭalāq, the ṭalāq takes effect in the Sharia through the husband's own consent: that is, he himself had the document drawn up of his own accord and signed it willingly. But a ṭalāq does not take effect where the document is drawn up under force and coercion, or where the husband's signature is obtained on it by force. So in the case in question, if the husband said nothing with his tongue, no ṭalāq has taken effect.

Radd al-Muḥtār states:

وفي البحر:
فلو أكره على أن يكتب طلاق امرأته فكتب لا تطلق. (فتاوى الشامي: ۳/۲۳٦، مطلب في الإكراه على التوكيل، سعيد).

Fatāwā Qāḍīkhān states:

رجل أكره بالضرب والحبس على أن يكتب طلاق امرأته فلانة بنت فلان بن فلان فكتب امرأته فلانة بنت فلان بن فلان طالق لا تطلق امرأته. (فتاوى قاضيخان على هامش الهندية: ۱/٤۷۳، الطلاق بالكتابة).

Al-Fatāwā al-Tātārkhāniyyah states:

وفي الظهيرية: رجل أكره بالضرب والحبس على أن يكتب طلاق امرأته فكتب فلانة بنت فلانة امرأته طالق، وفي الحاوي: ولم يعبر بلسانه لا تطلق. (الفتاوى التاتارخانية: ۳/۳۸۰، إيقاع الطلاق بالكتابة).

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states: Where a signature on a divorce document is obtained by force, and the husband neither pronounced the ṭalāq with his tongue nor wrote it himself, no ṭalāq has taken effect. (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: ۹ /۱۵٤، مدلل مکمل) ۔

Kitāb al-Fatāwā states: A threat from the police also falls under coercion. (کتاب الفتاویٰ: ۵/۹۰) ۔
