### TITLE
Calling out to the Prophet ﷺ after his passing

### QUESTION
The narration about seeking a means of approach (tawassul) contains the call "يا محمد" addressed to the Prophet ﷺ, and it is said after his passing and when one is not standing before his grave. What is the ruling on this?

Al-Muʿjam al-Ṣaghīr of al-Ṭabarānī states:

"حدثنا طاهر بن عيسى قيرس المقري المصري التميمي حدثنا أصبغ بن الفرج حدثنا عبد الله بن وهب عن شبيب بن سعيد المكي عن روح بن القاسم عن أبي جعفر الخطمي المدني عن أبي أمامة بن سهل بن حنيف عن عمه عثمان بن حنيف أن رجلاً كان يختلف إلى عثمان بن عفان رضي الله عنه في حاجة له فكان عثمان رضي الله عنه لا يلتفت إليه ولا ينظر في حاجته فلقي عثمان بن حنيف فشكا ذلك إليه، فقال له عثمان بن حنيف: ائت الميضأة فتوضأ ثم ائت المسجد فصل فيه ركعتين ثم قل: اللهم إني أسألك وأتوجه إليك بنبينا محمد ﷺ نبي الرحمة، يا محمد إني أتوجه بك إلى ربك [ربي] عزوجل فيقضي لي حاجتي الخ. (رواه الطبراني في المعجم الصغير: ۱/۱۸۳، دار الفكر).

The gist of the incident is as follows.

Abū Umāmah ibn Sahl ibn Ḥunayf (may Allah be pleased with him) narrates from his uncle ʿUthmān ibn Ḥunayf (may Allah be pleased with him) that a man used to come to ʿUthmān (may Allah be pleased with him) about a need of his, but ʿUthmān (may Allah be pleased with him), being occupied, paid him no attention and did not see to his need. So the man met ʿUthmān ibn Ḥunayf (may Allah be pleased with him) and complained to him about this. ʿUthmān ibn Ḥunayf (may Allah be pleased with him) told him: fetch water for ablution, perform ablution, go to the mosque, pray two units, and make this supplication:

"اللهم إني أسئلك وأتوجه إليك بنبينا محمد ﷺ نبي الرحمة يا محمد إني أتوجه بك إلى ربك عزوجل فيقضي لي حاجتي"

Then mention your need. The man did exactly that, and when he went to ʿUthmān (may Allah be pleased with him), he received a warm welcome and his need was met. Afterwards the man met ʿUthmān ibn Ḥunayf (may Allah be pleased with him) and thanked him, and ʿUthmān ibn Ḥunayf (may Allah be pleased with him) said: I was once present with the Prophet ﷺ when a blind man came to him and complained about his blindness. The Prophet ﷺ told him to be patient. The man said that he had nobody to lead him about and that he was in great difficulty. So the Prophet ﷺ said: fetch water for ablution, perform ablution, then pray two units and make this supplication — the one just mentioned. ʿUthmān ibn Ḥunayf (may Allah be pleased with him) says: by Allah, we had not yet left that gathering, and the conversation had grown somewhat long, when that same man came back to us so completely recovered that it was as if there had never been anything wrong with his eyes.

### ANSWER
We are bound to keep to the words of supplication exactly as the Prophet ﷺ taught them. The words "يا محمد" occur in the hadith about seeking a means of approach, and these too were taught by the Prophet ﷺ; in saying them we are simply repeating his words. It is therefore correct to recite this supplication, even when it is not in his lifetime and not before his blessed grave, because the aim is not to make him hear, nor to ask him for one's need, but only to repeat the words he taught. The One who hears and who fulfils needs is Allah, the Lord of Glory. If a person recites this supplication with the belief that the Prophet (peace be upon him) hears him from afar and that it is he who will fulfil the need and grant the cure, that is forbidden, and to supplicate in that way is not correct.

ʿUmdat al-Qārī states:

فإن قلت: ما الحكمة في العدول عن الغيبة إلى الخطاب في قوله: "عليك أيها النبي" مع أن لفظ الغيبة هو الذي يقتضيه السياق كان يقول: السلام على النبي فينتقل من تحية الله إلى تحية النبي ثم تحية النفس ثم إلى تحية الصالحين.
قلت: أجاب الطيبي: بما محصله، نحن نتبع لفظ الرسول بعينه الذي علمه للصحابة. (عمدة القاري: ٤/۵۸٤).

The commentary on al-Adab al-Mufrad states:

"السلام عليك" هذا هو المشروع أن يقوله المصلي سراً، كما كان الصحابة يقولونه سراً في عهد النبي ﷺ وبعد وفاته ﷺ، سواء كانوا قريباً من النبي ﷺ أو بعيداً منه، في مسجده أوفي مسجد آخر، في المدينة أو في البيت، أو الصحراء أوفي بلاد بعيدة، وإن كان السياق يقتضي أن يؤتى بلفظ الغيبة؛ لأن المصلي ينتقل من تحية الله إلى تحية النبي ثم إلى تحية النفس ثم إلى الصالحين. قال الطيبي: ويحتمل أن يقال على طريق أهل العرفان: إن المصلين استفتحوا باب الملكوت بالتحيات، فأذن لهم بالدخول في حريم الحيي الذي لا يموت، فقرت أعينهم بالمناجاة، فنبهوا على أن ذلك بواسطة نبي الرحمة وبركة متابعته، فالتفتوا فإذا الحبيب في حرم الحبيب حاضر، فأقبلوا عليه قائلين: "السلام عليك
والخطاب ليس على إرادة الاستماع، وإن كان الأصل في الخطاب يؤتى به لإسماع المخاطب، فكثيراً ما يؤتي به لغير ذلك، كما هو كثير فاش في كلام العرب والسنة وكلام الصحابة وفي كلام الناس، كما في الندبة وذكر المرء حبيبه في غيبته وأمثال ذلك، فكانت هذه الأمور تجري بمرأي ومسمع من الصحابة، فلم يكن يخطر ببال أحدهم أن النبي ﷺ يسمع ما يتكلم به الناس في بيوتهم، وإن أمكن أن تخرق له العادة في بعض ذلك، إلا أن الذي جرى عليه خرق العادة أن يخبر الله تعالى نبيه بما شاء لا أن يسمعه كلام من بعد عنه، وإن كان ذلك مما يجوز عقلاً. فالصحابة لم يكونوا يتوهمون أن الخطاب على إرادة الإسماع الخ. (فضل الله الصمد في توضيح الأدب المفرد: ۲/۲۹۸. ۲۹۹، باب السلام اسم من اسماء الله عزوجل، ط: بيروت).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Question: What is the position on saying "Yā Rasūlallāh"?

Answer: If it is said in the belief that he is present and watching everywhere, it is polytheism. Saying "Yā Rasūlallāh" while standing at his blessed tomb, however, is correct. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 9/408, Kutub Khānah Maẓharī).

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

Question: Is it permissible to say "Yā Rasūlallāh" or not?

Answer: There is a good deal of detail in the matter of saying "Yā Rasūlallāh": in one way it is permissible, in another it is not — and so on to his words: the point is that whether one is near or far, if "Yā Rasūlallāh" is said while sending blessings and greetings upon him, with correct belief, then it is permissible. But one must hold the belief that blessings and greetings recited from a distance are conveyed to him by an angel; one must not hold the belief that he hears them in person, as Allah does. In the same way, greetings are conveyed in the tashahhud by saying "السلام عليك أيها النبي", and there is no doubt or difficulty in that. Again, while reciting the Noble Qur'an one reads ﴿يا أيها المزمل﴾ as part of the text, and there is no harm in that either; to turn it into a proof that he is present and watching is sheer ignorance. Likewise, if "يا رسول الله" is said purely out of a surge of love, without the belief that he is present and watching, this too is permissible. Sometimes, in a state of overwhelming love or deep grief, people use a word of calling for someone who is absent, without any notion of his being present and watching; this too is permissible. Sometimes an address is made simply as an imaginative device, in the manner of poets and lovers, and there is no harm in this either — poets address walls and ruins; it is only a figure of speech, and no belief about being present and watching is involved. But to go about saying "يا رسول الله" "يا علي" "يا غوث" and the like at all times, without sending blessings and greetings, believing him to be present and watching, and in order to have one's needs fulfilled, is without question impermissible and forbidden. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 2/62–63, Maktabat al-Iḥsān, Deoband).

In Taskīn al-Ṣudūr, Mawlānā Sarfarāz Khān Ṣafdar (may Allah have mercy on him) (d. 1432 AH) says:

Mawlānā Khalīl Aḥmad Sahāranpūrī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes about this hadith: I say: in this incident the Pride of the World ﷺ was himself alive in this world, the act was done at his own instruction, and the man was present before him — so at that time no answer or explanation was called for at all. And if the practice is continued after him, it is understood in this way: that the words are conveyed to him, the angels carrying them. There is no independent knowledge involved here (that is, knowledge without the angels conveying it — Ṣafdar), nor is it correct to recite it with such a belief; in that case this too would become polytheism. (Taskīn al-Ṣudūr, p. 433, quoting al-Barāhīn al-Qāṭiʿah, 219).
