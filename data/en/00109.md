### TITLE
Did the Prophet ﷺ know the meaning of the ambiguous letters?

### QUESTION
Did Rasūlullāh ﷺ know the meanings of the ambiguous letters such as حٰمٓ, or not? And what benefits are there in their revelation?

### ANSWER
وفي الجامع الأحكام للقرطبي:

اختلف أهل التأويل في الحروف التي في أوائل السور فقال عامر الشعبي وسفيان الثوري وجماعة من المحدثين: هي سر الله في القرآن، ولله في كل كتاب من كتبه سر، فهي من المتشابه الذي انفرد الله تعالى بعلمه. (الجامع الأحكام ۱ /۱۰۸)

وأيضا قال القرطبي في تفسيره:

وروي عن محمد بن علي الترمذي رحمه الله أنه قال: إن الله تعالى أودع جميع ما في تلك السورة من الأحكام والقصص في الحروف التي ذكرها في أول السورة، ولا يعرف ذلك إلا نبي أو ولي. ثم بين ذلك في جميع السورة ليفقه الناس. (القرطبي ۱/۱۱۰)

Rūḥ al-Maʿānī states:

فلا يعرفه بعد رسول الله ﷺ إلا الأولياء الورثة فهم يعرفونه من تلك الحضرة وقد تنطق لهم الحروف عما فيها كما كانت تنطق لمن سبح بكفه الحصى وكلمه الضب والظبي ﷺ كما صح ذلك من رواية أجدادنا أهل البيت رضي الله عنه، بل متى جنى العبد ثمرة شجرة قرب النوافل علمها وغيرها بعلم الله تعالى الذي لا يعزب عن علمه مثقال ذرة في الأرض ولا في السماء. (روح المعاني ۱/۱۰۰)

Muftī Muhammad Shafīʿ writes:

In the view of the majority of the Companions, the Successors and the scholars of the ummah, the stronger position is that these letters are symbols and secrets whose knowledge has been given to no one but Allah. It is possible that Rasūlullāh ﷺ was given knowledge of them as a secret, and that he was prevented from conveying it to the ummah. That is why nothing is reported from the Prophet ﷺ by way of explaining or interpreting these letters. (معارف القرآن ۱/۱۰۷).

Mawlānā Idrīs (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

In the view of the Rightly Guided Caliphs, the majority of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) and the Successors (may Allah have mercy on them), these letters are among the ambiguous texts, and no one but Allah knows what is meant by them.

كما قال الله تعالى وما يعلم تأويله إلا الله

(معارف القرآن: ۱/۳۸).

Mawlānā Idrīs (may Allah have mercy on him) goes on to write:

In terms of the outward sharia these are ambiguous texts and hidden secrets of the Majestic Lord. People in general have not been informed of their meanings, nor do they have the capacity for that. This is the position of the commentators and of the muḥaddithīn (with a kasrah on the dāl, that is, the scholars of hadith). As for the muḥaddathūn (with a fatḥah on the dāl) — meaning that Allah acquaints certain chosen servants with the meanings and secrets of the disjointed letters by way of inspiration — the disagreement here is not a real one but merely verbal. When the scholars of hadith deny that these letters are known and grasped, they mean this with respect to ordinary people, and what they deny is certain knowledge, not conjectural or intuitive knowledge. Those who affirm knowledge and grasp of the meanings of the disjointed letters for the muḥaddathūn (with a fatḥah on the dāl) affirm it for the elect, not for ordinary people; and even the knowledge the elect have is conjectural and intuitive, not decisive and certain. Nor would it be surprising if the disjointed letters have many aspects in the unseen realm, one meaning being disclosed to one person and another to another. (معارف القرآن: ۱/٤۰).

The inclination of the commentators quoted above shows that the Prophet ﷺ may well have known the meanings of the ambiguous letters such as حٰمٓ, but only at the level of conjecture.

The benefits of revealing the ambiguous letters:

(1) That people should believe in them and be certain that they are from Allah.

(2) The testing of minds: the horse of the intellect, which wants to gallop everywhere, is brought to a halt here.

(3) To make clear the difference between the knowledge of the Creator and the knowledge of the creation.

(4) To make clear man's helplessness. (Summarised from ʿUlūm al-Qurʾān by Mawlānā Shams al-Ḥaqq Afghānī.)

Mawlānā Idrīs (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

The purpose of revealing the disjointed letters is that people should believe in them and be certain that they are from Allah, so that the servants' perfect submission may become plain. (معارف القرآن: ۱/٤۰).

وقال القرطبي في تفسيره:

قال أبو بكر: فهذا يوضح أن حروفا من القرآن سترت معانيها عن جميع العالم اختباراً من الله عزوجل وامتحاناً، فمن آمن بها أثيب وسعد ومن كفر وشك أثم وبعد. (تفسير القرطبي: ۱/ ۱۰۹).

In short, no text establishes that Rasūlullāh ﷺ knew the meanings of these letters. Certain knowledge of them therefore belongs to Allah alone.
