### TITLE
Trading in greetings cards

### QUESTION
Just as there are Eid cards, Christians use cards for birthdays, Mother's Day and the like, which they call greetings cards. May Muslims trade in them or not, given that these cards also carry small pictures? بینوا توجروا

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, trading in cards of this kind is permissible and correct, because the picture is not what is intended in them; it is merely incidental, just as in the present age pictures appear on the labels of most goods. The jurists have ruled that trade of this kind is permissible without any dislike. Care should be taken, however, to keep items bearing pictures covered in the shop or the house, so as to be free of any breach of the apparent import of the hadith.

قال في رد المختار: لكن في الخزانة إن كانت الصورة مقدار طير يكره وإن كانت أصغر فلا. (رد المحتار: ۱/٦٤۸، سعيد).

Among the jurists there is a well-known principle:

"الأمور بمقاصدها". (الأشباه والنظائر: ۱/۱۰۲).

In the light of this principle, since it is the trade in the cards that is intended and not the pictures, it is not impermissible.

In the same way, the jurists have said concerning the sale of grape juice:

وذكر قاضيخان في "فتاواه" أن بيع العصير ممن يتخذ خمراً إن قصد به التجارة فلا يحرم وإن قصد به لا جل التخمير حرم. (الأشباه والنظائر: ۱/۱۰۲).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

Buying and selling pictures is impermissible, whether they are small or large, and whether they are for children to play with or for some other purpose. But things in which the buying and selling of the picture is not what is intended — for example, matchboxes, which have a picture on them although the buying and selling of the picture is not intended — the buying and selling of such things may be allowed. (کفایت المفتی: ۹/۲۳۵).

Elsewhere it is recorded:

Question: A bolt of cloth on which the factory's registered trademark label is affixed bearing the picture of a living creature, or a box which carries the picture of a living creature and in which goods for sale are packed — will keeping such things in a shop come under the ruling of keeping pictures or not?

Answer: Since the buying and selling of the picture is not what is intended here, there is scope for it out of necessity. (کفایت المفتی: ۹/ ۲٤۱).

ʿIṭr-e Hidāyah states:

If such pictures are on a book or a utensil and the like, and the price or the desirability of the item is increased because of them, then it is disliked. Even if they do not increase its desirability, it is still not free of dislike. Where avoiding them is difficult, however — as with money, paper, stamps, cards and so on, which have pictures printed on them — those who print them will be sinful, but ordinary people will not be sinning. (عطر ہدایہ ص۱۵۳، ۱۵٤، تصاویر کے احکام).

Āp ke Masāʾil states:

Question: How should newspapers containing pictures be brought into the home?

Answer: The practice of some of the senior scholars was to erase the pictures before reading the newspaper, and some of them would place a hand over the pictures. For people like us it is something at least that, after reading the newspaper, we fold it away so that the pictures are covered. (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ۷/٦۸).

Kitāb al-Fatāwā states:

It is unfortunate that nowadays labels with pictures are put even on things with which a picture has no connection at all, and the expense laid out on the label makes the soundness of the goods doubtful. In selling such things the picture is not what is intended; rather, it is the article itself that is intended. Under present conditions, therefore, the buying and selling of these goods will be valid. It is, however, the duty of Muslim manufacturers not to make their products attractive by such cheap means, but rather to draw people's attention through quality and workmanship. (کتاب الفتاوی: ۵/۲۰۹).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
