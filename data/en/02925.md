### TITLE
Writing sacred words beneath the name of a town

### QUESTION
Before one enters any district or town, a board bearing the name of that town is set up, with the name of the town written on it in bold letters. There is permission from the government to use the space beneath it, so the Muslims have begun to write sacred words beneath it — words such as "سبحان الله وبحمده، سبحان الله العظم، الله أكبر" and the like. Some people have objected to this, saying that it involves disrespect to blessed words. The question now is whether there is disrespect in writing them in this manner or not. Kindly inform me of the ruling of the Sacred Law and earn a great reward.

### ANSWER
In the case in question, writing the name of the locality above and the sacred words below is in our view permissible, and it does not come under disrespect, because the name of the town is what is intended on the board and is therefore written above, while other things are written below. The proof of this is that in the letters which came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, the name of the writer and sender was written first and the name of the addressee, Muḥammad ﷺ, came afterwards, so that it would be known from the beginning who had written the letter. Consider what al-Sunan al-Kubrā of Imām al-Bayhaqī states:

عن عن زاذان عن سلمان رضي الله عنه قال: لم يكن أحد أعظم حرمة من رسول الله ﷺ كان أصحاب رسول الله ﷺ إذا كتبوا إليه يكتبون من فلان إلى محمد رسول الله ﷺ. (السنن الكبري: ۱۰/۱۳۰/۲۰۹۲۸).

وأخرجه الطبراني في الكبير (٦/۲٤۱/٦۱۰۸).

قال الهيثمي: وفيه: قيس بن الربيع وثقه الثوري وشعبة وضعفه غيرهما وبقية رجاله ثقات. (مجمع الزوائد: ۸/۱۸٤/۱۳۱۷۱، باب كتابة الكتب وختمها).

Moreover, whatever serves for identification is written above in bold letters, so that a traveller coming from afar may recognise it and not be put to difficulty and distress.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
