### TITLE
Dealing with a person who holds money obtained from interest

### QUESTION
If a person has money obtained from interest actually in his hands, may we enter into any kind of transaction with him or not? And if he wishes to pay it towards a debt he owes, may we accept it in settlement of our debt or not? Is there any difference between a Muslim and an unbeliever?
بينوا بالتفصيل توجروا بأجر جزيل.

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, if it is known for certain that a particular sum of a Muslim's money is unlawful, then it is not permissible to sell him anything in exchange for it and to take that money; where it is not known for certain, there is room for it. But if an unbeliever holds money obtained from interest, it is permitted to accept that money in settlement of a debt, so long as he has not committed something that reason itself holds forbidden and repugnant, such as deception, theft or robbery.

Imdād al-Aḥkām states:

Unbelievers are indeed addressed by the subsidiary rules in matters of penalties and transactions; but for a ruling of unlawfulness and invalidity a general address is not sufficient — commitment to the law is a further condition. The people of the abode of war have made no commitment whatever to the rulings of Islam, neither in what agrees with their own beliefs nor in what opposes them. So however they earn their money — whether through interest, through usurpation, or through void and defective sales, and whether by a means that agrees with their own religion or one that opposes it — in every case that money and the like enters into their ownership, and it is permissible for a Muslim to receive it as wages. (Imdād al-Aḥkām: 4/390, dealings of Muslims with the People of the Book and the polytheists).

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

ولوكان لمسلم على نصراني دين، فباع النصراني خمراً أخذ بثمنها وقضاه المسلم من دينه، جاز له أخذه، لأن بيعه له مباح، ولوكان الدين لمسلم على مسلم فباع المسلم خمراً وأخذ ثمنها وقضاه صاحب الدين، كره له أن يقبض ذلك من دينه، كذا في السراج الوهاج. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳٦۷، كتاب الكراهية، باب في القرض والدين).

Al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

وكره لرب الدين أخذ ثمن خمر باعها مسلم لا كافر، يعني إذا كان لشخص مسلم دين على مسلم فباع الذي عليه الدين خمراً وأخذ ثمنها وقضي الدين لا يحل للمدين أن يأخذ ذلك بدينه وإن كان البائع كافراً جاز له أن يأخذ والفرق أن البيع في الوجه الأول باطل فلم يملك البائع الثمن وهو باقٍ على ملك المشتري فلا يحل له أن يأخذ مال الغير بغير رضاه والبيع في الوجه الثاني صحيح فملك البائع الثمن لأن الخمر مال متقوم في حق الكافر فجاز له الأخذ بخلاف المسلم. (تكملة البحر الرائق: ۸/۲۰۱، كوئته).
وللاستزادة انظر: (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۳۸۵، فصل في البيع، سعيد. وتبيين الحقائق: ۷/٤٦۸، كتاب الكراهية).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

It is not correct to sell anything in exchange for money that is known with certainty to be unlawful, or to accept that money; where there is no such knowledge, there is room for it. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 11/273, Kutub Khānah Maẓharī; and Jawāhir al-Fatāwā: 3/294).

For further detail, see: Imdād al-Aḥkām: 4/386–390; and Muntakhabāt-e Niẓām al-Fatāwā, p. 467, "The sharia standing of the provident fund".
