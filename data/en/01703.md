### TITLE
Swearing "By Allah, I will never listen to that scholar's talk"

### QUESTION
Someone said to Zayd: do not listen to such-and-such Mawlānā Ṣāḥib's talks; his talk is full of poison. Zayd said, "By Allah, I will never listen to his talk." Some days later Zayd listened to a cassette of that Mawlānā Ṣāḥib's talk on a tape recorder. Has his oath been broken or not? And is expiation binding on him or not?

### ANSWER
In common usage, "listening to a talk" covers both listening directly and listening indirectly. So whether Zayd listened on a tape recorder or listened to the speaker in person, in either case he breaks his oath, and the expiation for an oath becomes binding.

Consider what al-Ashbāh wa al-Naẓāʾir states:

صرح الزيلعي رحمه الله وغيره بأن الأيمان مبنية على العرف، لا على الحقائق اللغوية، وعليها فروع: منها: ـ لو حلف لا يأكل الخبز حنث بما يعتاده أهل بلده. (الأشباه والنظائر: ۱/۲۷٦).

See further: (الفتاوی الہندیۃ: ۲/۸۳ ۔ و تبیین الحقائق: ۳/۱۱٦) ۔

Besides this, when Zayd swore the oath his purpose was to keep himself away from the poison of that talk; and by listening to it on a tape recorder that purpose was lost. On this ground too Zayd has broken his oath, and the expiation for an oath has become binding on him.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
