### TITLE
An objection and its answer

### QUESTION
Objection: An objection arises against the ruling above. The fatwa collections generally state that shaking hands and embracing after the Eid prayers, or after the five daily prayers, is an innovation, and our scholars have stressed this point. What is the reason for it?

### ANSWER
If a person shakes hands and embraces on the day of Eid because of the Eid prayer itself, then shaking hands and embracing after that prayer is an innovation. But if it is done out of the joy of Eid — as is the common practice, people doing it for a day or two after the prayer — then, so long as he does not regard it as sunnah, there is room for it as an expression of joy. People have, however, turned it into a fixed custom, so it is better to give it up. Consider what Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:
The sharia has fixed the moment of first meeting as the time for shaking hands. To fix a time for it after a particular prayer has no basis in the sharia and is wrong; it is a disliked innovation and the practice of the Rāfiḍīs. ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) has quoted exactly this in Radd al-Muḥtār from the Ḥanafīs, Mālikīs, Shāfiʿīs and others:

ونقل في تبيين المحارم عن الملتقط أنه تكره المصافحة بعد أداء الصلاة بكل حال لأن الصحابة ث ما صافحوا بعد أداء الصلاة، ولأنها من سنن الروافض ثم نقل عن ابن حجر رحمه الله عن الشافعية أنها بدعة مكروهة لا أصل لها في الشرع وأنه ينبه فاعلها أولاً ويعذر ثانياً ثم قال: وقال ابن الحاج من المالكية في المدخل إنها من البدع، وموضع المصافحة في الشرع إنما هو عند لقاء المسلم لأخيه لا في أدبار الصلوات فحيث وضعها الشرع يعضها فينهي عن ذلك ويزجر فاعله لما أتي به من خلاف السنة ثم أطال في ذلك فراجعه. "الشامي: ٦/۳۸۱"

(Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 3/143, classified and arranged edition)

Fatāwā Farīdiyyah states:
Embracing is in itself sunnah. Restricting it to a particular time, however — such as after the Eid prayer — is a disputed matter: some call it sunnah, some permissible and some disliked. Caution therefore lies in avoiding it, though one should not condemn those who do it too harshly. (Fatāwā Farīdiyyah: 1/302)

Fatāwā Muftī Maḥmūd states: These embraces, handshakes and congratulations after the prayer on the day of Eid did not exist in the time of the pious predecessors, so it is better to give them up. (Fatāwā Muftī Maḥmūd: 2/523)

For further reference, see: Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah: p. 147; Imdād al-Aḥkām: 1/188; Imdād al-Fatāwā: 1/481; Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 1/354; Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 1/280.
