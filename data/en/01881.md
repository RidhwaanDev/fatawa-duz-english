### TITLE
The seller rescinding the sale unilaterally when the price is not paid

### QUESTION
A man sold his valuable land to a buyer for an agreed price. The buyer promised to pay the money after six months and paid part of the price as a deposit. After six months the price was demanded from him several times, but he is not paying it, and he is also not willing to cancel the sale by mutual agreement. After a considerable time the seller rescinded the contract and sold the land to someone else. Is this transaction permissible or not?

### ANSWER
Our Ḥaḍrat Mufti Walī Ḥasan (may Allah have mercy on him) and Mufti Rashīd Aḥmad Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) used to give the fatwa, in cases of this kind, that the sale may be rescinded unilaterally: when the buyer neither agrees to cancel the sale nor pays the price, the seller may rescind the sale unilaterally. On one occasion a request for a fatwa came to the Dār al-Iftāʾ of Binnorī Town through Ḥaḍrat Ḥakīm Akhtar Ṣāḥib (may his glory endure) in which the situation was very much like the one in the question above, and the name of the questioner was written in veiled terms. Ḥaḍrat Mufti Walī Ḥasan (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote a brief answer, without any reference, that the seller may rescind unilaterally. A day or two later the question came again, saying that since Zayd was himself a mufti he needed a reference. Ḥaḍrat Mufti Walī Ḥasan (may Allah have mercy on him) then wrote out the following passage from the third volume of al-Hidāyah:

ولأنه لما تعذر استيفاء الثمن من المشتري فات رضا البائع فيستبد بفسخه. (الهداية: ۳/۱٤۷، كتاب أدب القاضي، مسائل شتى من كتاب القضاء).

After that no further question came. This question had been sent by Ḥaḍrat Mufti Rashīd (may Allah have mercy on him), and he agreed with this answer and recorded the same ruling in احسن الفتاوی: ٦/۵۰٦.

Although the author of al-Hidāyah cited this passage under one particular case, it has the standing of a general principle.

Objection and answer:

Objection: An objection arises here. In the case in which unilateral rescission is mentioned, the buyer denies the sale, and that is why the seller may rescind unilaterally, since from the buyer's side the sale has already come to an end. Yet the author of al-Hidāyah wrote on the very same page:

لأن أحد المتعاقدين لا يتفرد بالفسخ. (الهداية: ۳/۱٤۷، مسائل شتى من كتاب القضاء).

Similarly, in Sharḥ al-Majallah of al-Atāsī, ۲/۲۵۸, it is written that where the other party acknowledges the contract but does not pay the price, the first party cannot rescind it.

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ likewise records:

ونوع لا يرتفع إلا بالإقالة وهو حكم كل بيع لا زم وهو البيع الصحيح الخالي عن الخيار. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/۳۰٦، سعيد).

The respected Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Mufti Muhammad Taqī has also written to the same effect, that is, that unilateral rescission is not possible. (فتاوی عثمانی: ۳/٦۲۱).

Answer: If, however, the passage of al-Hidāyah is examined carefully and read in the light of what the commentators have said, it becomes as clear as daylight that the real basis of the matter is the recovery of the price: when recovery of the price is not possible, or is extremely difficult, unilateral rescission will be allowed.

Al-ʿInāyah states:

لما تعذر استيفاء الثمن يستبد وههنا لما أقر المشتري في مكانه بالشراء لم يتعذر الاستيفاء فلا يستبد بالفسخ. (العناية على هامش فتح القدير: ۷/۳۳٤، دار الفكر).

That is, when the buyer acknowledges the price, recovery of the price is possible, and for that reason the sale cannot be rescinded unilaterally. In former times the Islamic judicial system was in place and redress for the wronged could be obtained easily, so the price could indeed be recovered. In the present age, however, because of stalling and evasion, recovering the price is next to impossible, and going to court brings nothing but loss and amounts to putting oneself through years of distress.

Furthermore, the passage of Badāʾiʿ, which states that in a binding sale the only means of rescission is mutual cancellation, holds good in ordinary circumstances; but where recovery of the price or of the goods sold is not possible, unilateral rescission may take place. If, in a sale, a slave runs away before the buyer has taken possession of him, the judge may rescind the contract even though the contract was complete. کما فی الدر مع الرد: (٤/۲۹۲، سعید).

Ḥaḍrat Mufti Muhammad Taqī (may his excellence endure) did not regard unilateral rescission as correct in the question mentioned above, but scope for it appears in another of his books.

See what is stated in Takmilat Fatḥ al-Mulhim:

فإن الخادع يجب عليه ديانة أن يفسخ البيع أو يقيل المخدوع إذا طلب منه الإقالة وقد صرح به في الدر المختار والشامي. (تكملة فتح الملهم: ۱/۳۳۳).

In Taqrīr-e Tirmidhī he has said: the later Ḥanafī scholars, however, give the fatwa according to the view of the Mālikīs, because in our day deception and fraud are widespread. So if a person buys something at a low price or sells it at a high price by deceiving the other party, the one who has been deceived should be given the option of rescission, as is the position of Imām Mālik (may Allah have mercy on him); and today the fatwa is upon this. The one who has been cheated will therefore have the option of the defrauded party. (تقریر ترمذی: ۱/۱٦۹).

In Fayḍ al-Bārī, ʿAllāmah Anwar Shāh Kashmīrī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

إن الغرر إما قولي أو فعلي فإن كان الغرر قولياً فالإقالة واجبة بحكم القاضي وإن كان الثاني تجب عليه الإقالة ديانة،

وهكذا أقول فيما إذا اشتري سلعة، فلم يؤد ثمنها حتى أفلس، أنه يكون فيه أسوة للغرماء عندنا قضاء، ويجب عليه أن يرد المبيع إلى البائع خفية ديانة، فإنه أحق به، لكنه حكم الديانة دون القضاء. (فيض الباري: ۳/۲۳۲).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
