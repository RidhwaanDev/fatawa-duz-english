### TITLE
The ruling on sacrificing a stolen goat

### QUESTION
If someone stole an animal and sacrificed it, and afterwards regretted it and conveyed its price to the owner, has his sacrifice now been valid or not? Some scholars say that in the books of fiqh one finds the case of usurpation — that if a person pays compensation after sacrificing a usurped animal, the sacrifice is valid — but that the case of theft is not found. Could you please provide a reference for the case of theft?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, the thief who slaughtered the animal paid the price of the stolen goat, and for that reason his sacrifice has been fulfilled. Yes — because he began a good deed with an act of disobedience, repentance and seeking forgiveness are binding upon him. The explicit reference for theft is given below.

ʿAllāmah Ibn al-Shiḥnah al-Ḥalabī has written that the sacrifice is permissible:

وفي نظم الزندوستي: خمسة أشياء إذا أخذها من ملك الغير تجوز بها الأضحية وضمن قيمتها، أولها: غصب شاة وضحي بها، والثاني: لو سرق شاة وضحي بها

الخ. (لسان الحكام في معرفة الأحكام، ص ۳۸۷، نوع فيما يجوز من الأضحية ومالا يجوز).
(وكذا في خلاصة الفتاوى: ٤/۳۱۷، الفصل الرابع، المكتبة الرشيدية).

In Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ:

لكنه يأثم لأن ابتداء فعله وقع محظوراً فيلزمه التوبة والاستغفار. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/۷٦، سعيد، ورد المحتار: ٦/۳۳۱، سعيد).

Note: In Aḥsan al-Fatāwā (۷/۵۰۵) impermissibility is recorded, and the reference given there is to usurpation; but we have written that it is permissible, with references.

An objection and its answer:

Objection: Mā shāʾ Allāh, the reference for this question has been found — that if, after sacrificing a stolen animal, one pays its price to the owner, the sacrifice is valid.

لو سرق شاة وضحي بها

الخ. (لسان الأحكام، ص: ۳۸۷، وغيره من الكتب الفقه).

But one point sticks in the mind, and it is hoped that you will give its solution. It is that the jurists write that permission does not operate upon something that no longer exists; for example, if an unauthorised person sells someone's goat and it dies in the buyer's possession, and then the owner grants permission, the permission will not take effect, because permission does not operate upon what no longer exists,

كذا في عامة كتب الفقه.

Moreover, it is stated in Al-Hidāyah:

ولا نفاذ بدون المحل.
That is, permission does not operate upon what has perished. In the footnote it is stated:
يعني أن المعقود عليه بالهلاك صار إلى حالة لا يجوز ابتداء العقد عليه فلا يلحقها الإجازة. (الهداية: ۳/۳۰، رقم الحاشية، ص ۱۲).
Now, after the sacrificial animal has been slaughtered and eaten, how will the permission take effect?

Answer: The answer to this objection is that by paying the compensation the thief became the owner of that goat, and the cause of the obligation to compensate is the theft; so the payment of compensation is attributed back to the time of the theft — that is, the thief in reality became owner at the time of the theft, and the slaughter took place after the theft; therefore the sacrifice was valid.

See what the jurists say under the sacrifice of a usurped goat:

وإن ضمن قيمتها حية تجزي عن الذابح لأنه ملكها بالضمان من وقت الغصب بطريق الاستناد فصار ذابحاً شاة هي ملكه فتجزيه. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۳۳۱).

Tabyīn al-Ḥaqāʾiq states:

لأن المضمونات تملك عند أداء الضمان مستنداً إلى وقت وجود سبب الضمان. (وهو السرقة في مسئلتنا فتستند إلى وقت السرقة فكأنه صار مالكاً حين السرقة). (تبيين الحقائق: ۳/۱۸٦، إمدادية، ملتان).

For further detail see: (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/ ۷۷، سعيد، ورد المحتار: ٦/۳۳۱، سعيد، وتملكة البحر الرائق: ۸/۱۷۹، كوئته).

This passage is also found in Al-Hidāyah:

وكذلك الملك في المضمون لان المضمونات تملك بأداء الضمان مستنداً إلى وقت الغصب عندنا. (الهداية: ۳/۳۷۵).

However, since this attributed ownership is not strong — being ownership in one respect and not ownership in another respect — it is not sufficient for the transfer of the corporeal item itself. For this reason, in the sale by an unauthorised person, if the item perishes in the buyer's possession, this ownership will not be sufficient for the transfer of the item itself and the seller's permission will be of no effect. Yes, this weak ownership, or ownership in one respect, will be effective for the carrying out of an act of devotion — and this is a sacrifice; so the sacrifice will be fulfilled, and it will be accepted that by paying the price he became owner, that ownership was established before the slaughter, and that the sacrifice was valid.

But in the sale by an unauthorised person this is not accepted; otherwise the door to the wasting of people's wealth would be thrown open. See the detail of this in the following passage of Al-Muḥīṭ al-Burhānī:

والفرق: أن عند أداء الضمان يثبت للغاصب الملك مستنداً إلى وقت الغصب السابق والمستند فاتت للحال من وجه، ومن ذلك الوقت من وجه، وكان الملك فيما بين الغصب وأداء الضمان ثابتاً من وجه، ومثل هذا الملك لا يكفي لنفاذ العين، ويكفي لنفاذ القرب. (المحيط البرهاني: ٦/٤۸۳).
