### TITLE
Conditions laid down by the seller in a sale

### QUESTION
Is it permissible for a seller to lay down the following conditions or not?

(1) Once the cloth has been cut, no claim will be entertained.

(2) If goods are to be returned, the invoice number must be quoted.

(3) After cloth has been bought, there will be a right of return for seven days only.

(4) The goods will remain the property of the seller until the buyer has paid the full price.

### ANSWER
(1) The first condition is valid. It means that once the cloth has been cut the seller is free of liability for every defect; and it is valid and correct to stipulate freedom from all defects.

Consider the following. Al-Hidāyah states:

ومن باع عبداً وشرط البراءة من كل عيب فليس له أن يرده بعيب وإن لم يسم العيوب بعددها. (الهداية، كتاب البيوع، باب خيار العيب، ۳/٤۸، وكذا في المبسوط للإمام السرخسي رحمه الله: ۱۲/۹۱).

Sharḥ al-Majallah states:

إذا باع ماله بشرط براءة ذمته من دعوي كل عيب فلا يكون للمشتري خيار عيب. (شرح المجلة لمحمد خالد الأتاسي، البيوع، الفصل السادس في خيار العيب، ۲/۳۰۵، رشيدية).

(2) Nowadays the world is moving ahead: every article is given its own particular number, priced, and stored in a computer — this is called a barcode. When the buyer makes a purchase, he is handed a receipt (a bill or invoice) bearing that particular number at the time he pays the price, and he must keep it safe, because that receipt is needed in order to return the goods. This is the established custom, and it is done for the sake of convenience. The condition is therefore valid and correct; there is no reason to call it impermissible.

Consider the following. ʿAllāmah Ibn Nujaym al-Miṣrī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Al-Ashbāh:

العادة المطردة هل تنزل منزلة الشرط قال في إجارة الظهيرية: والمعروف عرفاً كالمشروط شرطاً. انتهى. (الأشباه والنظائر: القاعدة السادسة، العادة محكمة، ۱/۲۷۸).

(3) The third condition is exactly like the first, namely that after seven days the seller is free of liability for every kind of defect. This too is valid and correct, as has just been explained.

(4) Once the contract is complete the buyer becomes the owner of the goods sold. A condition that they remain the seller's property is therefore not valid in a sale by instalments. In a cash sale, however, the parties may stipulate that the goods will remain in the seller's possession until the buyer has paid the full price.

Muḥammad Khālid al-Atāsī writes in Sharḥ al-Majallah:

لو باع بشرط أن يحبس المبيع إلى أن يقبض الثمن فهذا الشرط لا يضر بالبيع، بل هو بيان لمقتضي البيع، فإن للبائع حبس المبيع إلى أن يقبض الثمن ولو لم يشترط ذلك في العقد. (شرح المجلة للأتاسي، البيوع، الباب الاول في بيان المسائل المتعلقة بعقد البيع، الفصل الرابع في حق البيع بشرط: ۲/٦۱، رشيدية).
