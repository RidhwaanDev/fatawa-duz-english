### TITLE
Reciting the basmalah aloud in tarāwīḥ

### QUESTION
Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah states: in tarāwīḥ it is correct to recite either quietly or aloud, and if someone recites aloud it is improper to object to him. (ص۳۳۷)

After this, Ḥamīdullāh, resident at Madrasah Maṭlaʿ al-ʿUlūm, Meerut, writes: there is this much to be said, that reciting the basmalah aloud is falling into disuse, so it now has the status of a dead sunnah. There is therefore hope that whoever brings it back into practice will receive the reward of a hundred martyrs. It is better, then, to recite the basmalah aloud in prayer most of the time — whether these are the obligatory prayers, or the prayers in which the recitation is made aloud such as Fajr, ʿIshāʾ and Maghrib, or tarāwīḥ.

My question is this: if the basmalah is recited aloud in tarāwīḥ with the intention of reviving a sunnah, will one deserve the reward of 100 martyrs? And does the same ruling apply to the obligatory prayers? بينوا توجروا !

### ANSWER
According to the Ḥanafī school, reciting the basmalah aloud is contrary to what is preferable, whether in tarāwīḥ or in the obligatory prayers. Over the course of the entire Qur'an, the basmalah should be recited aloud in tarāwīḥ only once, at the beginning of one of the sūrahs; apart from that it should be recited quietly.

Explicit statements to this effect are found in the texts of the jurists and in the books of fatwa. As for the hadith that reviving a sunnah brings the reward of a hundred martyrs, its examination has already been given in the chapters on hadith.

Its meaning is that if a person does away with an ugly innovation that has become current and puts the original sunnah back in its place, he will receive the reward of 100 martyrs. In other words, what stands opposite the sunnah there is an innovation. In the matter under discussion — reciting the basmalah aloud or quietly — there are hadiths on both sides, although the hadiths for reciting it quietly are more numerous and of a higher grade.

The meaning of the statement of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Rashīd Aḥmad Gangohī (نوّر الله مرقده) is that reciting it aloud is contrary to what is preferable, but that one should not create disagreement and quarrelling in the mosque over it. One should first explain the matter, and if the person does not accept it, one should not press the objection, since this practice too is established from hadith and is valid.

Ḥāshiyat al-Ṭaḥṭāwī states:

وتسن التسمية أول كل ركعة قبل الفاتحة لأنه ﷺ كان يفتتح صلاته ببسم الله الرحمن الرحيم

وهي آية واحدة من القرآن

وأنزلت للفصل بين السور

ويسن الإسرار بها للآثار الواردة بذلك. (حاشية الطحطاوي مع مراقي الفلاح: ص۲٦۰، ۲٦۲، قديمي)

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وسمي غير المؤتم بلفظ البسملة سرا في أول كل ركعة ولو جهرية

وفي الشامية: (قوله سراً في أول كل ركعة) كذا في بعض النسخ وسقط سراً من بعضها ولابد منه، قال في الكفاية عن المجتبى: والثالث أنه لا يجهر بها في الصلاة عندنا. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ۱/٤۹۰، سعيد)

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states:

In the Ḥanafī school it is agreed by all that the basmalah should be recited quietly inside the prayer; not one of the Ḥanafīs differs on this. The word "prayer" is used without qualification and so covers the obligatory prayers, the optional prayers, tarāwīḥ and the rest. It is also clear from this passage that following one of the imams of recitation applies outside the prayer, not within it; and this is how we found our teachers, the Ḥanafī scholars. (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: ٤/۲٦٤، ۲٦٥ مدلل و مکمل)
