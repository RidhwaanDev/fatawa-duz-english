### TITLE
Stipulating that there will be no intimacy before the bride moves in

### QUESTION
If a young man stipulated at the time of the marriage that he would not have intimacy with his wife before the rukhṣatī, and the rukhṣatī has still not taken place, but he now wants to have intimacy with her — is that permissible or not? And is the condition he made valid or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question it is necessary to keep this condition. Furthermore, even if no such condition had been made, it is the custom that people do not have intimacy before the rukhṣatī (the bride's formal departure to her husband's home), so out of regard for custom as well, intimacy should be avoided. ʿAllāmah Shāmī says:

"والعرف في الشرع له اعتبار
٭
لذا عليه الحكم قد يدار".
(شرح عقود رسم المفتي: ۳۸، دار الاشاعت).

Consider a few other examples of custom. If a person borrows loaves of bread and later returns them, going only by the number of loaves, this ought not to be valid in strict law, since loaves differ in size; yet it is permissible because of custom and settled practice. Likewise, going about bare-headed is regarded as unseemly by custom, so students and scholars should cover their heads. In the same way, going about without a shirt is regarded as unseemly by custom, so one should not go about like that. On this basis, both because of the condition and because of custom, intimacy before the rukhṣatī should be avoided.

قال الله تعالى: ﴿يا أيها الذين اٰمنوا أوفوا بالعقود﴾ (سورة المائدة: ۱).
وفي الحديث: "أحق الشروط أن توفوا به ما استحللتم به الفروج. (رواه البخاري: ۱/۳۷٦، باب الشروط في المهر).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states: The third category of conditions are those that benefit the woman and that the sharia has neither made necessary nor forbidden. In accepting such conditions the man gives up certain rights of his own which he is free to give up — for example, the woman's stipulation that he will not take a second wife while she is married to him, or that he will not take her away from her home town, and the like. It is agreed that a marriage contracted with such conditions is valid. There is, however, a difference of opinion as to whether these conditions are binding and must be fulfilled. The righteous predecessors and the leading jurists differ over this.

Those who uphold them, and their evidences:

Among those who hold these conditions to be binding are ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him), ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ (may Allah be pleased with him), Qāḍī Shurayḥ (may Allah have mercy on him), ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz (may Allah have mercy on him), Isḥāq ibn Rāhwayh (may Allah have mercy on him), al-Awzāʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) and others.

Their evidences are as follows. (1) Allah says:

﴿يا أيها الذين اٰمنوا أوفوا بالعقود﴾ (سورة المائدة: ۱).

Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ al-Rāzī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

وكذلك كل شرط شرطه إنسان على نفسه في شيء يعمله في المستقبل فهو عقد.
وأيضاً قال: وهو عموم في إيجاب الوفاء بجميع ما يشترط الإنسان على نفسه ما لم تقم دلالة تخصصه. (أحكام القرآن: ۳/۳۸٤، ۲۸۵).

(۲) "أحق ما أوفيتم من الشروط أن توفوا به ما استحللتم به الفروج. (رواه الجماعة).

(3) These are conditions that do not stand in the way of the purposes of marriage, and a lawful aim and benefit is bound up with them, so they ought to be binding. (Summarised from Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 35–39).
