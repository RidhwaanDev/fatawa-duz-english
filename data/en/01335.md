### TITLE
Decorating the bride's car

### QUESTION
Is it correct to decorate the bride's car or not?

### ANSWER
This is an unestablished custom that deserves to be abandoned. It is the practice of the Christians, and it is necessary to avoid it. Even if people do not regard it as necessary or as a sunnah, it still deserves to be given up because it is pointless and serves no need.

In a hadith the Prophet ﷺ forbade adopting the distinctive culture and customs of other communities:

"من تشبه بقوم فهو منهم". (رواه أبو داود في باب لبس الشهرة).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah records the following about the bride and bridegroom riding in a palanquin:

This is an unestablished custom. Adhering to it amounts in practice to binding oneself to what is not binding, and it is a mere custom, so it should be abandoned. And if the notion of gaining nearness to Allah is also attached to it, then beyond being a custom it becomes an innovation as well. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۱/۲۱۱، مبوب و مرتب)۔

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah records the following about wedding customs and conventions:

The jurist and ḥadīth scholar Qāḍī Thanāʾ Allāh Pānīpatī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes: for a Muslim, resembling the disbelievers and the open sinners is unlawful (Mā Lā Budda Minhu). (فتاوی رحیمیہ: /۱۸۹)۔

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

(The need to put an end to wedding customs): There is no doubt whatsoever that the greater part of the ruin and economic distress of the Muslims at present is due to these extravagant customs. These customs are eating away at the wealth, honour and self-respect of the Muslims from within, like a wood-worm. As for those customs which the Muslims have learnt from the disbelievers and adopted, there can be no hesitation at all about their being impermissible and obligatory to abandon. (کفایت المفتی: ۵/۱۵۵، کتاب النکاح، دار الاشاعت)۔

For further detail, see: Islāmī Fiqh: 2/85–88, "Wedding customs"; and Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal: 5/200, "Hindu customs at weddings are not permissible".
