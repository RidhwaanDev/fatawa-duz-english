### TITLE
The ruling on women going to offer condolences to the family of the deceased

### QUESTION
When a woman passes away in a town, the other women of the town go to offer condolences and recite something of the Qurʾān and the like. Is this a right of the deceased or not? Should one go or not? And will there be reward in reciting the Qurʾān and so forth and making a gift of it? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
To go and offer condolences to the household of the deceased, whether before the burial or after it, is a sunnah; it is established from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and from the women Companions — in particular from the mistress of the women of Paradise, the very embodiment of modesty and shyness, Ḥaḍrat Fāṭimah (may Allah Most High be pleased with her). Then, if some recitation and the like is done there and the reward is conveyed, its reward reaches the deceased. Ḥaḍrat Fāṭimah (may Allah Most High be pleased with her) went to the house of a deceased person to offer condolences.

Consider what is in Abū Dāwūd Sharīf:

عن عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص رضي الله عنه قال: قبرنا مع رسول الله ﷺ يعني ميتاً، فلما فرغنا انصرف رسول الله ﷺ وانصرفنا معه فلما حاذى بابه وقف فإذا نحن بامرأة مقبلة قال: أظنه عرفها فلما ذهبت إذا هي فاطمة رضي الله عنه، فقال لها رسول الله ﷺ: ما أخرجك يا فاطمة من بيتك؟ فقالت: أتيت يا رسول الله أهل هذا البيت فرحمت إليهم ميتهم أو عزيتهم به. (رواه أبو داود، رقم: ۳۱۲۵).

From the hadith mentioned it appears that it is permissible and correct for women to go to offer condolences while observing the veil, and great merits have been reported for it.

It is recorded in Kifāyat al-Muftī:

It is permissible to go to offer condolences for a deceased person; the condolence should be within three days, except for one who comes from a journey after three days. But to go there and recite the Fātiḥah as a fixed practice is without any established basis. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 4/141, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

It is mentioned in Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah:

Conveying reward is valid on any day and is a cause of reward; no date or time is fixed for it by the Sharīʿah. So if one does not regard some particular day as necessary but treats it like any other day for conveying reward, and no harm comes to the common people through such conveying of reward, then there is no objection to it; it is valid in the view of all. (Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah, p. 118).

For further detail concerning the merits of condolence and the conveying of reward, see: (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā, volumes two and eight).
