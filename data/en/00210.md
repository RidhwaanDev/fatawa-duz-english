### TITLE
An objection against the workers of tablīgh and its answer

### QUESTION
Some people raise this objection: in Allah's words ﴿كنتم خير أمة اخرجت للناس﴾ the word "للناس" is general and includes both Muslims and disbelievers, so why do the people of tablīgh go only to Muslims and not to the disbelievers?

### ANSWER
First of all, the workers of tablīgh have not confined this great work of tablīgh to Muslims alone. Through their travelling about, and through the blessing of this work, Allah has honoured countless non-Muslims with the great wealth of faith. And even supposing we were to accept that they do not go to the disbelievers, their going to Muslims and not going to disbelievers would still not be contrary to this noble verse. Indeed, there are many hadiths that support this practice. For example, the Prophet ﷺ sent Muʿādh ibn Jabal (may Allah be pleased with him) to Yemen. A group of Companions went to Kufa to call the Muslims. Similarly, ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) sent ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) to Kufa with a group of Companions during his caliphate; Maʿqil ibn Yasār, ʿAbdullāh ibn Mughaffal and ʿImrān ibn Ḥuṣayn went to Basra; and ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit and Abū al-Dardāʾ (may Allah be pleased with them) went to Syria — all of this was to call the people of Islam, the Muslims.

Moreover, the benefit of daʿwah and tablīgh among Muslims becomes apparent more quickly than among non-Muslims. In ﴿عبس وتولي أن جاءه الأعمى﴾ there is an indication of this: that giving attention to Ibn Umm Maktūm was better and more beneficial than giving it to the proud chiefs of Quraysh.

Even so, if anyone still finds this difficult, these workers have not stopped anybody. Anyone may go to the disbelievers on his own and convey the message to them; the way is open and there is no obstruction. It is therefore not right to level accusations against these workers.

For details, see: (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 1/452; Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 6/381; Jamāʿat-e Tablīgh par Iʿtirāḍāt ke Jawābāt, p. 12; Dīnī Daʿwat awr Tablīgh ke Uṣūl-o-Aḥkām; and Muntakhab Aḥādīth).
