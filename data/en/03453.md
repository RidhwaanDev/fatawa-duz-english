### TITLE

A sick person praying while facing away from the qiblah

### QUESTION

A sick man is unable to face the qiblah. There is someone with him who could turn him towards the qiblah, but without telling him to do so the sick man prayed facing away from the qiblah. Was his prayer valid or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER

In the case above, according to Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) the sick man's prayer was valid. According to the two disciples (may Allah have mercy on them), however, it was necessary for the sick man to ask the other person, and since he prayed without asking, his prayer was not valid. Most scholars have given preference to the view of the two disciples. Consider what is recorded in al-Durr al-Mukhtār with Radd al-Muḥtār:

وقبلة العاجز عنها لمرض وإن وجد موجهاً عند الإمام أو خوف مال وكذا كل من سقط عنه الأركان، جهة قدرته. وفي رد المحتار: قوله عند الإمام؛ لأن القادر بقدرة الغير عاجز عنده لأن العبد يكلف بقدرة نفسهٖ لا بقدرة غيره خلافاً لهما فيلزمه عندهما التوجه إن وجد موجهاً وبقولهما جزم في المنية والمنح والدرر والفتح بلا حكاية خلاف. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۱/٤۳۲، سعيد).

Fatḥ al-Qadīr records:

وعلى هذا الخلاف إذا كان مريضاً لا يقدر على الاستقبال أو كان في فراشه نجاسة ولا يقدر على التحول عنه ووجد من يحوله ويوجهه لا يفترض عليه ذلك عنده

وعندهما تثبت القدرة بآلة الغير لأن آلته صارت كآلته بالإعانة وكان حسام الدين يختار قولهما. (فتح القدير: ۱/۱۲٤، دار الفكر).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh records: if a sick person is unable, because of some illness, to face the qiblah, and there is no one with him to turn his face towards the qiblah — or there is someone to turn him, but turning him would harm the patient — then he may pray in whatever direction he wishes. And if there is someone present to turn the patient's face towards the qiblah, and turning him would not harm him, then according to Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) he too is excused, because in his view every person is charged according to his own ability and not according to the ability of another; so being able only through another's ability is itself an excuse and an inability. According to the two disciples (may Allah have mercy on them), however, if he can be turned towards the qiblah with another's help and that other person is present, then he is not excused and not unable, and his prayer will not be valid if he prays, like one who is excused, in whatever direction he is able; rather, facing the qiblah will be necessary. It is this view that has been relied upon. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 2/63.)

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
