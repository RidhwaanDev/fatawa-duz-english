### TITLE
Issuing a fatwa on questions in which the jurists differ

### QUESTION
In questions on which the jurists differ, whose view should a fatwa be given on?

### ANSWER
Rasm al-Muftī states:

والحاصل أنه إذا اتفق أبو حنيفة وصاحباه على جواب لم يجز العدول عنه إلا للضرورة، وكذا إذا وافقه أحدهما

(1) If Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) and his two companions (may Allah have mercy on them) agree on a point in a given question, and that ruling does not run against a genuine need, then it is that ruling which is acted upon.

(2) If the three Hanafi imams agree on something but a genuine need calls for something else, then a fatwa may be given, in the light of the evidence, on a view other than theirs, in accordance with that need.

(3) If one of the two companions is on the same side as Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), then, in the light of Qāḍī Khān's statement, the Imam's view is taken, because the required conditions are found in the Imam (may Allah have mercy on him) in full and the proofs of its soundness are present.

(4) If the Imam (may Allah have mercy on him) is on one side and the two companions on the other, and neither of them agreed with the Imam, then ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak (may Allah have mercy on him) says that the Imam's view is to be taken absolutely, while some scholars say that the mufti has a choice and may take whichever view he wishes.

The verifying scholars have weighed these two statements in the following way: the statement of ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak (may Allah have mercy on him) concerns a mufti who is not a mujtahid, and the statement of the others concerns a mufti who is a mujtahid. That is, a mufti who is a mujtahid has a choice, and a mufti who is not a mujtahid does not.

All of the detail given above applies where the later scholars have not, on the basis of some evidence or need, issued a fatwa according to the two companions or some other position. If the later scholars have adopted the position of the two companions, then their preferences are acted upon — as in the question of muzāraʿah (sharecropping) and musāqāt (tending orchards for a share of the crop).

When is the Imam's view set aside in favour of the view of the two companions?

ʿAllāmah Ibn Nujaym (may Allah have mercy on him) has mentioned three cases:

(1) Weakness in the Imam's evidence.

(2) Need and established practice, as in the question of muzāraʿah and musāqāt.

(3) Where the disagreement of the two companions arises from a change of times. (ملخص من شرح عقود رسم المفتي ص ۱۹).

In sum, ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) gave preference to this: a mufti who is a mujtahid may issue a fatwa in the light of the strength of the evidence, while for one who is not a mujtahid the detail given above applies.
