### TITLE
The position of the two companions on the standard for the nisāb, and the fatwa of some muftis on their view

### QUESTION
What is the position of the two companions on combining gold and silver? And can a fatwa be given on it in present circumstances or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the view of the two companions, gold and silver are combined by their parts. For example, if a person owns two-thirds of the silver nisāb (the threshold at which zakat becomes due) and one-third of the gold nisāb, zakat is due from him; and if the gold is less than one-third of its nisāb, zakat is not due.

Consider what Shāmī states:

وقالا: بالأجزاء فإن كان من هذا ثلاثة أرباع نصاب ومن الآخر ربع ضم، أو النصف من كل أو الثلث من أحدهما والثلثان من الآخر، فيخرج من كل جزء بحسابه. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۳۰۳، باب زكاة المال، سعيد. وبدائع الصنائع: ۲/۲۰. والهداية: ۱/۱۹۵).

ʿAllāmah al-ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in al-Bināyah fī Sharḥ al-Hidāyah:

(وعندهما بالأجزاء) أي عند أبي يوسف رحمه الله ومحمد رحمه الله الضم بالأجزاء، وبه قال مالك رحمه الله وأحمد في رواية، ولا يري الشافعي رحمه الله بالضم، وبه قال أحمد في رواية وأبو ثور وأبو داود، وذهب آخرون إلى أن الضم إنما يكون إذا كمل النصاب من أحدهما، بيان ذلك إذا كان أحدهما ثلث النصاب فلا بد أن يكون الآخر ثلثي النصاب وكذلك النصف وغيره، ولو كانت عشرة دنانير ومائة درهم، وقيمة الدنانير مائة يجب الضم بالاتفاق على اختلاف الترجيح عنده باعتبار القيمة وعندهما باعتبار الأجزاء

وفي الاسبيجابي وغيره معني الضم بالأجزاء أن يكون من كل واحد منهما نصف نصاب من غير نظر إلى قيمتها أو من أحدهما نصف وربع ومن الآخر ربع، أو من أحدهما نصف وربع وثمن، ومن الآخر ثمن

(وهو رواية عنه) أي الضم بالأجزاء إنما هو من مذهب صاحبيه ورواية عن أبي حنيفة رحمه الله رواها هشام عنه، وفي المفيد: رواها الحسن عنه. (البناية في شرح الهداية: ۳/٤۵٤، مكتبة رشيدية).

وللاستزادة انظر: (فتح القدير: ۲/۲۲۲، دار الفكر، وتبيين الحقائق: ۱/۲۸۲، وبدائع الصنائع في ترتيب الشرائع: ۲/۱۹، وفتح باب العناية: ۲/۱۱۰، ودرر الحكام في شرح غرر الأحكام: ۱/۱۸۲، والمحيط البرهاني: ۲/۳۸٤، وغيره من الكتب الفقهية).

ʿAllāmah al-ʿAynī's words show that combining by parts is not the position of the two companions (may Allah have mercy on them) alone. It is also a narration from Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), transmitted from him by both Hishām and al-Ḥasan ibn Ziyād. It is the position of Imam Mālik (may Allah have mercy on him), and a narration to the same effect is reported from Imam Aḥmad (may Allah have mercy on him) as well.

In our own day gold stands at the height of its value while silver has sunk to the lowest of the low, and this creates difficulties in the nisāb of zakat. If we make gold the standard for the nisāb (as we have written in detail elsewhere), then people of ordinary means will generally be freed of zakat, charity, sacrifice and the like. If silver is the standard — as the well-known view is, and the one on which fatwa is given — then every salaried man will carry the weight of these obligations even while he is hard pressed by the needs of his family, and widows and grown daughters who own a little jewellery will also be put in difficulty. The middle path, then, is to fix the nisāb by combining the two, gold and silver, according to their parts, following the position of the two companions (may Allah have mercy on them), which is also a narration from Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him). This would be far easier for ordinary people. Tarjīḥ al-Rājiḥ has pointed in the same direction.

Consider the passage:

قال أستاذنا غلام قادر النعماني: أفتي الفقهاء على قول أبي حنيفة رحمه الله لأن قوله أحوط والفتوى على قوله موافق لا صول الإفتاء لأن الأصل في العبادات الفتوى على قول أبي حنيفة رحمه الله لكن قولهما أوفق للقياس لأن على قول أبي حنيفة رحمه الله يصير نصاب الذهب تابعاً لقيمة الفضة مع أن القيمة تابع للذهب والفضة في تعيين النصاب وأيضاً في صورة الذهب

وأما على قولهما فيكون القيمة تابعاً للذهب والفضة ولا يكون الموجود تابعاً للمعدوم وأن قولهما أرفق بالناس فينبغي أن يفتي بقولهما في هذا الزمان وخصوصاً في مسألة الأضحية ترفقاً بالنساء لأن أكثر النساء يوجد معهن شيء من الذهب وهو يساوي نصاب الفضة باعتبار القيمة وهن لا يستعدن لبيع الذهب ولشراء الأضحية وإذا كان الانضمام بالأجزاء فحينئذٍ يكون الحكم أسهل عليهن. وقول الإمام قول المتون. (القول الراجح: ۱/۱۷٤).

Mawlānā Khālid Sayfullāh says: an extraordinary gap has now opened up between the value of seven and a half tolas of gold and that of fifty-two and a half tolas of silver, so that with one nisāb of gold it has become possible to buy several nisābs of silver. If the ratio between the value of gold and of silver were the same as it was in the earliest period, then the question of "combining the nisābs" would be entirely just, as the Hanafi position holds, since it also benefits the poor. But in present circumstances, when a marked difference has arisen between the values of the two, one feels the need for this particular ruling to be reconsidered, and for the view of the majority to be adopted — or at least the view of the two companions (may Allah have mercy on them), that the nisāb of gold and the nisāb of silver should be combined by their parts and not by their value. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil 2/169–170)

In Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith a mufti who contributed a paper writes: it has been made clear in the preceding pages that in the Prophetic era the two nisābs laid down for the obligation of zakat on the two currencies and on trade goods — twenty mithqāls of gold and two hundred dirhams — were no different from each other in value. In my view, therefore, the more suitable and balanced approach is one in which both nisābs are taken into account together and in which the equivalence of value that existed between them in the Prophetic era is preserved as far as possible.

For valuing trade goods, the most fitting and even-handed method would be to add together the two nisābs of silver and gold — whose value was one and the same in the era of prophethood — work out their combined value in the currency of the day, and take half of that as the standard for the nisāb.

This method closely resembles the practice adopted with regard to the dirham in the time of ʿUmar al-Fārūq. If the two nisābs are brought together and half of the total is taken as the standard for the nisāb, this will, in present circumstances, be more suitable, and more even and balanced, than giving preference to one of the two nisābs over the other. It takes both nisābs into account in a general way, while also preserving as far as possible the equivalence of value that existed between the two nisābs in the Prophetic era; and it gives due weight, in a general way, to the interests of both the poor and the owners of wealth.

The same point may be put in other words: in the zakat on trade goods a mixed nisāb of half gold and half silver should be taken as the measure. If the total value of the zakatable wealth equals or exceeds the combined value of ten mithqāls of gold and one hundred dirhams of silver, zakat should be held to be due; otherwise not. Seen in this way, the method resembles combining gold and silver by their parts. (Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith 7/811–815)

An illustration of how far apart silver and gold now stand in value is this: today, 13 Muḥarram al-Ḥarām 1437 AH, corresponding to 27 October 2015, the nisāb of gold comes to approximately 40,678 rand at the price of gold, while the nisāb of silver comes to only 4,605 rand at the price of silver; whereas taking half of each of the two nisābs together, it comes to 22,641 rand. This is the middle path, free of both excess and neglect. In the Prophetic era, moreover, the two nisābs were far closer to each other. For this reason the muftis of the present age need to look again at the standard for the nisāb and give the matter careful thought.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
