### TITLE
The multiplication of reward in the path of Allah

### QUESTION
It is said of those who go out with the Tablīghī Jamāʿat that the reward for one prayer of theirs is 490 million. If there is evidence for this, please explain it. And if there is evidence, is the reward for the prayer of one who goes out for tablīgh greater even than the reward for prayer in the Masjid al-Ḥarām, given that the reward for one prayer in the Masjid al-Ḥarām is a hundred thousand?

### ANSWER
What the brothers of tablīgh say is not something particular to them; it is general. Whoever goes out in the path of Allah — whether for jihad, for seeking knowledge, for Hajj and Umrah, or for any other religious purpose — will be entitled to this reward. Nor is this merit drawn from a single narration; the brothers of tablīgh state it by combining two hadiths.

The first hadith:

"عن جماعة من الصحابة مرفوعاً: من أرسل بنفقة في سبيل الله ومن غزي بنفسه في سبيل الله وأنفق في وجه ذلك فله بكل درهم سبع مائة ألف درهم، ثم تلا هذه الآية والله يضاعف لمن يشاء". (رواه ابن ماجه باب فضل النفقة في سبيل الله، ص ۱۹۸).
قلت: إسناده ضعيف؛ فيه: الخليل بن عبد الله وهو مجهول.
وقال بشار عواد: إسناده ضعيف لأجل الخليل بن عبد الله الخ. (سنن ابن ماجه بتعليق بشار عواد: ٤/۵٦).
وبالجملة فالحديث ضعيف لكن يعمل به في فضائل الأعمال.

That is, whoever fights in the path of Allah in person and spends even a single dirham in the path of Allah will receive for every dirham the reward of seven hundred thousand dirhams. From this we learn that spending one dirham in the path of Allah is equal to spending seven hundred thousand dirhams.

A caution: there is, however, a difficulty here. The hadith uses the word "غزي", which is used in the specific sense of attacking the enemy on the field of battle. To apply it to other meanings — daʿwah and tablīgh, seeking knowledge and the like — therefore does not, in all likelihood, seem appropriate.

This difficulty was raised by Mawlānā Muftī Rashīd Aḥmad Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him).

Consider what Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muftī Rashīd Aḥmad (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

Even if these two hadiths are accepted as valid evidence, one of the two narrations states explicitly "من غزي بنفسه". For that reason there is no room on this occasion to take "in the path of Allah" in a general sense and to hold that this merit extends to the other branches of the religion as well. Rather, this merit will be particular to the one who fights — that is, to those engaged in battle against the disbelievers. (احسن الفتاوی: ۹/۱۸۳) ۔

Consider a few statements of the lexicographers:

Al-Qāmūs al-Muḥīṭ states:

غزاه وغزواً أراده وطلبه وقصده والعدو: سار إلى قتالهم وانتهابهم، غزواً وغزواناً وغزاوة. (القاموس المحيط: ۱/۱٦۹۸، وكذا في المعجم الوسيط، ص: ٦۵۲).
وفي المحيط الأعظم (٦/۳۸): الغزو: السير إلى قتال العدو وانتهابه.

But the hadith of Bukhārī —

"من جهز غازياً في سبيل الله فقد غزي ومن خلف غازياً في سبيل الله فقد غزي". (۱/۵۹۸)

— shows that ghazw is sometimes used for something other than actual fighting.

The second hadith:

عن سهل بن معاذ عن أبيه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: إن الصلاة والصيام والذكر يضاعف على النفقة في سبيل الله بسبع مائة ضعف".
رواه أبو داود: (۱/۳۳۸، باب في تضعيف الذكر في سبيل الله عز وجل، ط: سعيد)، والحاكم (رقم: ۲٤۱۵)، وقال: صحيح الإسناد، ووافقه الذهبي، وأخرجه البيهقي في "الكبرى" (۹/۱۷۲).
قلت: إسناده ضعيف؛ فيه: زبان بن فائد المصري، قال أحمد: أحاديثه مناكير. وقال ابن معين: شيخ ضعيف. وقال ابن حبان: منكر الحديث جداً يتفرد عن سهل بن معاذ بنسخة كأنها موضوعة لا يحتج به. وقال الساجي: عنده مناكير. (تهذيب التهذيب: ۳/۲۷٤/۲۰٦۷).
(۲) وسهل بن معاذ بن أنس الجهني، قال ابن معين: ضعيف. قلت (ابن حجر): لا يعتبر حديثه ما كان من رواية زبان بن فائد عنه وذكره في الضعفاء فقال: منكر الحديث جداً. (تهذيب التهذيب: ٤/۲۳٤/۲۷٦۰، وتحرير التقريب، ج ۲).
وقال المناوي: وهو حديث ضعيف لضعف زبان بن فائد. (التيسير بشرح الجامع الصغير للمناوي: ۱/۵۹۲/۲٤۹۸).
وبالجملة فالحديث ضعيف لكن يعمل به في فضائل الأعمال.

That is, the reward for prayer, fasting and remembrance in the path of Allah is seven hundred times greater than spending in the path of Allah. If that seven hundred thousand is multiplied by seven hundred, it comes to 490 million — that is, 49 crore. This is the truth behind the narration about 49 crore.

Further, no comparison is intended between this reward of 49 crore and the reward of the Masjid al-Ḥarām. It is like saying that a certain person is the best of all: this does not entail that he has become better and more excellent than the Companions, because no comparison between the two is intended. In the same way, no comparison is intended here between the reward that has been mentioned and the reward of the Masjid al-Ḥarām.

Even so, if a comparison is made, then according to one narration the reward of the Masjid al-Ḥarām comes to far more than a hundred thousand. Mullā ʿAlī al-Qārī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote in his commentary on Mishkāt, under one hadith:

"قال رسول الله ﷺ: صلاة الرجل في بيته بصلاة أي تحسب بصلاة واحدة وصلاته في مسجد القبائل بخمس وعشرين صلاة أي بالإضافة إلى صلاة في بيته لا مطلقا وصلاته في المسجد الذي يجمع فيه بخمس مائة صلاة أي بالنسبة إلى مسجد الحي وصلاته في المسجد الأقصى بخمسين ألف صلاة أي بالنسبة إلى ما قبله وصلاته في مسجدي بخمسين ألف صلاة أي بالإضافة إلى ما يليه وصلاته في المسجد الحرام بمائة ألف أي بالنسبة إلى مسجد المدينة على ما يدل عليه سياق الكلام فيحتاج إلى ضرب الأعداد في بعض فإنه ينتج مضاعفة كثيرة". (المرقاة: ۲/۲۲۸).

The Prophet ﷺ said that a man's prayer in his house is one prayer, that is, it is counted as a single prayer; his prayer in the mosque of his locality is 25 prayers — that is, in relation to the prayer in his house, not absolutely; his prayer in the congregational mosque is 500 prayers, that is, in relation to what precedes it; his prayer in my mosque is 50,000 prayers in relation to what precedes it; and his prayer in the Masjid al-Ḥarām is a hundred thousand prayers, that is, in comparison with the Prophet's Mosque. So the numbers must be multiplied one by another, and the result will emerge as a very great increase.

The multiplication and the reckoning are therefore as follows:

In the house: 1 (one).

The mosque of the locality: 25.

The congregational mosque: 25 × 500 = 12,500.

Masjid al-Aqṣā: 12,500 × 50,000 = 625,000,000.

The Prophet's Mosque: 625,000,000 × 50,000 = 31,250,000,000,000.

The Masjid al-Ḥarām: 31,250,000,000,000 × 100,000 = 3,125,000,000,000,000,000 (مسجدِ حرام: ۰۰۰، ۰۰۰، ۰۰۰، ۳۱۲۵۰×۰۰۰، ۱۰۰=۰۰، ۰۰۰، ۰۰۰، ۰۰۰، ۳۱۲۵۰).

From this hadith we learn that if a person prays in his house he receives the reward of one prayer; if he prays in the mosque of his locality he receives 25 times that reward; if he prays in the congregational mosque he receives (25 × 500 = 12,500) reward; if he prays in Masjid al-Aqṣā he receives (12,500 × 50,000 = 625,000,000) reward; if he prays in the Prophet's Mosque he receives (625,000,000 × 50,000 = 31,250,000,000,000) reward; and if he prays in the Masjid al-Ḥarām he receives (31,250,000,000,000 × 100,000 = 3,125,000,000,000,000,000) reward.

That is, he receives a reward of 31 sankh and 25 qadam. Bear in mind that a hundred thousand make one lakh, a hundred lakh make one crore, a hundred crore make one arab, a hundred arab make one kharab, a hundred kharab make one padam, a hundred padam make one qadam, and a hundred qadam make one sankh. Now judge for yourself how immense the reward of a single prayer in the Masjid al-Ḥarām is — an amount our minds cannot grasp. May Allah grant this reward to us all. Āmīn.
