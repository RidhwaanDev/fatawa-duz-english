### TITLE
A resident of Jeddah entering iḥrām for Hajj and ʿumrah from Jeddah

### QUESTION
A man is resident in Jeddah, where he runs a business and the like. He now wishes to go for Hajj and ʿumrah: from where will he enter iḥrām for Hajj and ʿumrah? Is it necessary for him to enter iḥrām from his home? Or is it also correct for him to come to Makkah Mukarramah and enter iḥrām there? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, it is better for such a person to enter iḥrām for Hajj and ʿumrah from his own home. If he did not enter it at home, then he must enter it before crossing into the precincts of the Ḥaram; otherwise he will be sinful, and it will be necessary for him to come back out into the ḥill (the area outside the sacred precincts) and enter iḥrām there. If he does not come back, but enters iḥrām within the Ḥaram and performs the ʿumrah or Hajj, one expiatory sacrifice (dam) will be necessary. Consider what Sharḥ al-Lubāb states:

فصل في الصنف الثاني، وهم الذين منازلهم في نفس الميقات، أو داخل الميقات إلى الحرم فوقتهم الحل أي فميقاتهم جميع المسافة من الميقات إلى انتهاء الحل للحج والعمرة وهم في سعة أي جواز ورخصة، وعدم لزوم كفارة ما لم يدخلوا أرض الحرم أي بلا إحرام، ومن دويرة أهلهم أفضل أي لهما، ولهم دخول مكة بغير إحرام إذا لم يريدوا نسكاً وإلا أي: وإن أرادوا نسكاً فإن النفي إثبات، فيجب أي الإحرام حينئذٍ. (شرح اللباب، ص۹۲، باب المواقيت).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states:

If a Muslim who is legally responsible — that is, sane and adult — and who lives in the ḥill or in the Ḥaram intends to perform Hajj and goes on past his mīqāt without iḥrām, he will be sinful, whether he enters iḥrām afterwards or not, and it is necessary for him, just as for a pilgrim from outside the mīqāt (āfāqī), to return to his mīqāt. If he does not go back to his mīqāt, an expiatory sacrifice will be due from him. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 4/108, the chapter on the mīqāt).

Note: some books record the ruling that if a person entered iḥrām inside the Ḥaram and did not return towards the mīqāt, but went out to the plain of ʿArafāt — which lies outside the Ḥaram — for the standing at ʿArafah, the sacrifice due for crossing the mīqāt lapses. (See: Irshād al-Sārī, pp. 92–93, and Fatāwā al-Shāmī: 2/478, Saʿīd, and others).

Other books (Sharḥ al-Lubāb, for instance), however, record that the return is obligatory before beginning the rites of Hajj, and that if he has already begun the rites the sacrifice does not lapse. Now, the rites of Hajj begin before the standing at ʿArafah, and going out to the plain of ʿArafāt is for the performance of an essential element of the Hajj, not to make good an omitted obligation. That means that leaving the Ḥaram in this instance is for the continuation of an iḥrām already in force, not for the commencement of one, and so the sacrifice ought not to lapse.
