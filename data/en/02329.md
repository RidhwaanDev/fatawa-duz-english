### TITLE
The correct way to slaughter a duck

### QUESTION
A duck seller told me that for a duck to be lawful the web between its feet must be cut before it is slaughtered, and that "Bismillāh, Allāhu Akbar" must be said while cutting the web as well. He also said that in the Ḥanafī school this same ruling applies to every web-footed animal and bird. Acting on his advice, we cut the web — blood came out too — and then we slaughtered the bird. Please explain the correct ruling in the light of the sacred law.

### ANSWER
Wounding an animal pointlessly before slaughter and causing it pain is disliked and forbidden in the sacred law. As for what lawfulness actually depends on, in ordinary slaughter of animals other than the camel the sacred law has made it depend on the cutting of the vessels: if a person says "Bismillāh" and cuts most of the animal's vessels, the animal becomes lawful. In this respect web-footed animals and animals without webbed feet are exactly the same; there is no difference between them.

Consider the following reasons why the practice is disliked:

(1) The sacred law has commanded that everything be done well. A hadith states:

عن شداد بن أوس رضي الله عنه قال: ثنتان حفظتهما عن رسول الله ﷺ، قال: إن الله تعالى كتب الإحسان على كل شيء فإذا قتلتم فأحسنوا القتلة، وإذا ذبحتم فأحسنوا الذبح، وليحد أحدكم شفرته وليرح ذبيحته. (رواه مسلم: ۲/ ۱۵۲، باب الامر بإحسان الذبح والقتل).

Ḥaḍrat Shaddād ibn Aws (may Allah be pleased with him) says: I memorised two things from the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ said: Allah has prescribed doing everything well, so when you kill (a disbeliever), kill him well, and when you slaughter (an animal), slaughter it well; let each of you sharpen his blade and spare his animal suffering.

The hadith shows that the animal must be spared distress, whereas wounding it is the very opposite of that.

(2) Causing an animal pain of any kind before slaughter is disliked and against the sacred law. Consider what the author of al-Hidāyah states:

ومن بلغ بالسكين النخاع أو قطع الرأس كره له ذلك

لما روي عن النبي ﷺ أنه نهي أن تنخع الشاة إذا ذبحت

وكل ذلك مكروه وهذا لان في جميع ذلك زيادة تعذيب الحيوان بلا فائدة وهو منهي عنه والحاصل أن ما فيه زيادة إيلام لا يحتاج إليه في الذكاة مكروه. (الهداية: ٤/ ٤۳۹).

(3) The jurists have stated explicitly that in ordinary slaughter, if the animal is wounded somewhere other than the place of slaughter and dies from that wound, it is unlawful. In the case in question, making the lawfulness of the duck depend on cutting the web is contrary to the transmitted evidence and to sound reasoning, and it also conflicts with the principles and rules of the sacred law. Indeed, if that very wound were to prove fatal, the bird would be unlawful. It is therefore binding and necessary to avoid this practice altogether.

Consider the manner of ordinary slaughter laid down by the sacred law:

قال الإمام القدوري: والذبح في الحلق واللبة والعروق التي تقطع في الذكاة أربعة: الحلقوم، والمري، والودجان، فإذا قطعها حل الأكل، وإن قطع أكثرها فكذلك عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله. (مختصر القدوري مع، ص ٤۹٤، مؤسسة الريان).
وفي التصحيح والترجيح: قال في زاد الفقهاء: الصحيح قول أبي حنيفة رحمه الله، واعتمده الإمام المحبوبي والنسفي وغيرهما. (التصحيح والترجيح على هامش مختصر القدوري مع، ص ٤۹۵، مؤسسة الريان).

The commentators have written that web-footed animals were made unlawful for the Jews only temporarily, because of their mischief and rebelliousness, and that this was later abrogated — just as suet and fat had been made unlawful for them.

Consider what Imam Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ states in Aḥkām al-Qurʾān:

وعلى الذين هادوا حرمنا كل ذي ظفر

بين النبي ﷺ أن حكم ذلك التحريم إنما كان موقتاً إلى هذا الوقت وإن مضي الوقت أعاده إلى ما كان عليه من حكم الإباحة فلا فرق بينه في هذا الوجه وبين ما لم يحظر قط

كل ذي ظفر قال ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير وقتادة والسدي ومجاهد هو كل ما ليس بمفتوح الأصابع كالإبل والنعام والإوز والبط. (أحكام القرآن: ۳/ ۲۲، ط: سهيل، تفسير ابن كثير: ۲/ ۲۰۷).

ʿAllāmah al-Ālūsī, the Muftī of Baghdad, states:

﴿وعلي الذين هادوا﴾ أي اليهود خاصة لا على من عداهم من الأولين والآخرين. (روح المعاني: ۸/ ٤۷).

Mawlānā Idrīs Kāndhlawī Ṣāḥib states in Maʿārif al-Qurʾān:

These things are lawful and wholesome in themselves, but because of the disobedience and mischief of the Jews We made them unlawful for the Jews in particular, so that their provision might be straitened. Otherwise these things did not in themselves call for prohibition; they were declared unlawful for the Jews only as a temporary measure. (Maʿārif al-Qurʾān: 3/43, published by Maktabat al-Maʿārif).
