### TITLE
Forward exchange contracts

### QUESTION
Is it correct to enter into a forward exchange transaction or not? A brief introduction is given below.

The gist is that the currencies of different countries are mutually exchanged at a rate fixed at the present moment, in which one currency — dollars, for example — will be delivered on an agreed future date. This transaction is called forward exchange (F.E. RATE).

The agreed future date is generally three months, and this deferred price of the currency differs from the spot price; that is, it carries a certain increase.

For example, a South African company buys goods worth one hundred thousand dollars from an American company. Both sides promise to conclude a contract whereby the goods will be handed over after three months and the price too — that is, one hundred thousand dollars — will be paid at that same time. Now, within these three months there is a strong possibility of a rise in the price of the dollar, on account of which the South African company fears a loss, in that it will have to spend more rand for the dollars. To guard against this feared loss the South African company enters into a forward exchange with a bank or the like, in which it buys dollars against rand at today's fixed rate. The rand will be paid at once; the dollars, however, will be received after three months, when the time comes to pay the American company in dollars. What is the ruling of the sharīʿah on this transaction?

### ANSWER
If a person sells one currency for another currency for a future date, there can be three forms of it.

(1) The first form is that he buys dollars against rand for the future and also hands over all the rand, so that after three months, on such-and-such a date, he receives the dollars. This transaction can be brought under salam; if the conditions of salam are present, then as a salam this transaction is sound and correct.

The conditions of salam have been gathered together in the phrase

"مصّ جنا مرأة"

or

"امرأة مصت جنا"

That is: (1) mīm stands for the quantity of the item contracted for, (2) ṣād: the description being known, (3) jīm: the genus being known, (4) nūn: the type being known, (5) alif: the term being known, (6) mīm and rāʾ: the amount of the capital sum, (7) tāʾ: an indication of naming the place of delivery.

In my view currencies come under the ruling of fulūs nāfiqah — that is, they are currency by convention and not a real medium of price, because the real media of price are gold and silver.

For salam, taking possession of the capital sum is necessary. For possession, clearing the way and constructive possession will also suffice, and a transfer into someone's account will likewise suffice.

If he does not conclude a contract of salam, then even so there is scope for giving one currency at once and taking the other currency afterwards.

Al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

ويجوز السلم في الفلوس عدداً في ظاهر الرواية كذا في الينابيع وهو الصحيح هكذا في النهاية. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۳/۱۸۳، فصل في بيان ما يجوز السلم).

Fatḥ al-Qadīr states:

وكذا في الفلوس عدداً أي يجوز السلم في الفلوس عدداً هكذا ذكره محمد رحمه الله في الجامع الصغير من غير ذكر خلاف فكان هذا ظاهر الرواية عنه. (فتح القدير: ۷/۷۵، دار الفكر).

(2) The second form is that the one paying the salam capital gives nothing. This form is impermissible; it is a sale of debt for debt, and moreover in salam the handing over of the capital sum is obligatory.

قال في الهداية: ولا يصح السلم حتى يقبض رأس المال قبل أن يفارقه فيه أما إذا كان من النقود فلأنه افتراق عن دين بدين وقد نهي النبي ﷺ عن الكالئ بالكالئ. (الهداية: ۳/۹٦).

(3) The third form is that he pays part of the capital sum and does not pay the rest. In that case it will be permissible to the extent of the capital sum paid and will remain impermissible in the rest. However, if in the second and third forms he makes a promise rather than concluding a contract, scope for it can be found; for when a promise takes the form of a suspension upon a condition, its fulfilment becomes binding — for instance, that he says: if I hand over the dollars on such-and-such a date, you will sell me pounds at such-and-such a rate.

Qawāʿid al-Fiqh states:

المواعيد في صورة التعليق تكون لا زمة مثل أن يقول بع هذا الشيء من فلان بكذا فإن لم يعطك الثمن فأنا أعطيه. (شرح المجلة، للأتاسي، ۱/۲۲۸، شرح القواعد الفقهية، ۲٦۳).

Mullā ʿAlī al-Qārī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Sharḥ al-Niqāyah:

المواعيد قد تكون لا زمة، قال رسول الله ﷺ: العدة دين فيجعل هذا الميعاد لا زماً لحاجة الناس إليه. (شرح النقاية: ۳/۲۵۳، قبيل فصل الإقالة، بيروت).

Moreover, this is one kind of hundi in which there is scope for deferment, because it is a conventional medium of price; in a real medium of price, however, there is no scope for deferment.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
