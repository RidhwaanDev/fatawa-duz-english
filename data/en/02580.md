### TITLE
Using human urine as medicine

### QUESTION
Respected Mufti, guidance is sought concerning the making of medicine from human urine. It should also be borne in mind that the urine of animals and of human beings is used in a great many medicines.

(1) (H.C.G) Within the twelve-week period from the conception of a pregnant woman, the hormone (that substance which enters the blood) is separated out from her urine by machine. We import this medicine from China or Germany.

(2) (H.M.G) After a woman has reached the menopause, the constituents of the hormone are separated out from her urine. Both of these kinds of medicine are used for men and for women, and are used to remove infertility in a woman.

(3) (F.S.H) After a woman has reached the menopause, the constituents of the hormone are extracted from her urine.

### ANSWER
Human urine is impure, and using it in ordinary circumstances is not permissible or sound; for nowadays medicines of every kind are available with which one can manage, and one should therefore avoid using such impure things. However, if some religious and expert Muslim physician prescribes it, saying that for this patient's cure no other medicine is possible, then in such a state of dire necessity one should manage with the urine of an animal whose flesh is eaten.

Note the narration in Abū Dāwūd Sharīf:

عن أبي الدرداء رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: إن الله أنزل الداء والدواء وجعل لكل داء دواء فتداووا، ولا تداووا بالحرام. (رواه أبو داود: ۲/۱۸۵، باب الأدوية المكروهة).

Qāmūs al-Fiqh states:

Human urine is impure — this ruling is agreed upon. It is also agreed that the urine of animals whose flesh is not eaten is impure as well. It is for this reason that among the Ḥanafīs both of these fall in the category of gross impurity. (قاموس الفقہ: ۲/۳۳۷) ۔

Niẓām al-Fatāwā states:

The upshot of this distilling is only that the putrefying and harmful constituents have been taken out from within the urine, and the constituents that remain are constituents of that same urine. Urine in all its constituents is intrinsically filthy and impure with gross impurity, and so the remaining constituents too will remain intrinsically filthy and impure with gross impurity. No case of a transformation of substance is to be found in this. (نظام الفتاوی: ۱/۳۹۱) ۔

Mufti Taqī says, in his comment on the hadith of the ʿUraniyyīn:

The position of Imām Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) is that if a skilled physician decides that recovery from an illness is not possible without treatment by means of something forbidden, then in that case treatment by means of something forbidden will be permissible. According to the position on which fatwa is given among the Ḥanafīs, the explanation of this hadith is that the Prophet ﷺ had come to know by revelation that their cure lay solely in camels' urine, and for that reason he ﷺ ordered the use of camels' urine. (درس ترمذی: ۱/۲۹۲) ۔

So at a time of severe necessity one should content oneself with the urine of an animal whose flesh is eaten.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
