### TITLE
The ruling on transferring a deceased person from one place to another

### QUESTION
May a deceased person be transferred from one place to another or not?

### ANSWER
It is superior and better that the deceased be buried in the very place where the death occurred. Yes, it is permissible to transfer the body a mile or two; but transferring it a greater distance than that has been written down as disliked by the jurists, because thereby an objective of the Sharīʿah is lost: promptness, as far as possible, is required in the preparation and shrouding of the deceased, whereas in transferring the body delay occurs on account of legal procedures, and the dignity and honour of the deceased is also not maintained. Consider what ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

قوله يندب دفنه في جهة موته أي في مقابر أهل المكان الذي مات فيه أو قتل وإن نقل قدر ميل أو ميلين فلا بأس شرح المنية، قلت: ولذا صح أمره بدفن قتلى أحد في مضاجعهم مع أن مقبرة المدينة قريبة ولذا دفنت الصحابة الذين فتحوا دمشق عند أبوابها ولم يدفنوا كلهم في محل واحد

وقيده محمد بقدر ميل أو ميلين لأن مقابر البلد ربما بلغت هذه المسافة فيكره فيما زاد. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۲۳۹، سعيد).

For more, see:

مراقي الفلاح، ص ٦۱۳، والفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱٦۷، وفتح القدير: ۲/۱۰۱، والفتاوى التاتارخانية: ۲/۱۷۵، والبحر الرائق: ۲/۱۹۵.

It is recorded in Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah:

The recommended and superior method is that a person be buried in the very place where he died, whether his death occurred in his homeland or in some other place outside it. Concerning transferring the body before burial the jurists differ: transferring it a mile or two is permissible by agreement, while transferring it more than two miles has been written down as disliked by most of the jurists; the author of al-Nahr, on the authority of ʿAqd al-Farāʾid, called it "the apparent position" (the manifest transmission), and Shāmī has cited this from him. Some jurists hold it permissible within less than a journey's distance, and some hold it permissible absolutely; but the superior course is not to transfer the body, for several reasons:

1. Because of the transfer, delay occurs in the preparation and shrouding, whereas promptness is required in it.

2. At times a change occurs in the body of the deceased; the transfer is not in one's own control, legal obstacles arise, and delay occurs.

3. In transferring the body there is also a greater possibility of dishonouring the deceased: the body is moved about more than necessary, and often the corpse is placed upon ice, which is in itself a cause of distress to the deceased — and to cause distress to the deceased is forbidden.

4. Since transferring the body without a need recognised by the Sharīʿah is unnecessary, indeed disliked, the costs and expenses of the transfer will not be counted among the expenses of preparing and shrouding the deceased, and so on. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 3/485, Deoband).

For more, see: (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/208; and Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal: 4/318).
