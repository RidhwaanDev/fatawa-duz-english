### TITLE
The ruling on the pledgee taking possession of the pledge under government law

### QUESTION
If a pledgor placed his land with a pledgee as a pledge and did not discharge the debt for a long period (about twenty years), and the law of the government is that after a long period the pledgee becomes the owner of the land, will the pledgee be regarded in the sharia as the owner of the land or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, if the pledgor is unable to repay the debt within a particular period and the pledgee, under the law of the government, forcibly keeps the pledge with himself and becomes its owner; or if, under government law, a suit is brought and the pledgee wins the case — in both situations the pledgee will not become the owner of that land or item in the sharia.

A hadith states:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: "لا يغلق الرهن له غنمه وعليه غرمه". (رواه ابن حبان في صحيحه، رقم: ۵۹۳٤، قال الشيخ شعيب: رجاله ثقات رجال الشيخين غير إسحاق).

والمراد بقوله عليه الصلاة والسلام: "لا يغلق الرهن" على ما قالوا: الاحتباس الكلي والتمكن بأن يصير مملوكاً له. كذا ذكر الكرخي رحمه الله عن السلف. (الفقه الحنفي في ثوبه الجديد: ٤/٤٤۹).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم

Objection and answer:

Objection: If someone should raise the difficulty that, on account of the law of the government and its dominion, the pledgee ought to become the owner of the pledge — as though, on account of the legal supremacy of the government, the government has made the pledgee the owner, as Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written in Imdād al-Aḥkām (3/382 and 402), and the details of which have already been given in Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā, volume five — then, taking that into account, ought the pledgee not to become the owner?

Answer: The answer is that real dominion has not come about here. It is not like a case where the duty on a car has not been paid and the government seizes it. Legal dominion is not in reality dominion, for if the pledgee wishes he may return the pledge. Yes, Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) and Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) did give legal dominion the standing of real dominion in the matter of inheritance, on the grounds of necessity, of conciliating hearts, of safeguarding property, and by way of reciprocation. That is to say, when under the law a non-Muslim son inherits from a Muslim father, the Muslim father too was held to inherit from the non-Muslim son; otherwise it would be a strange thing that non-Muslims should carry off our property while we remain deprived.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
