### TITLE
The mother has the first right to custody

### QUESTION
If a woman is divorced, or the couple are separated, who is entitled to bring up the child? Who is responsible for maintenance and other expenses? And what is the order of those entitled to custody?

### ANSWER
Responsibility for the care of the child lies with the mother. Even if she has been divorced, she may keep a boy with her until he is seven and a girl until she is nine, and the expenses will be borne by the child's father. During this time the father cannot take the child away. If, however, after the divorce the mother hands the child over of her own free will, or if she marries a man who is a stranger to the child, the father will then have to take over the child's care; and he cannot compel his divorced wife to look after the child.

If the woman, God forbid, leaves Islam, the child will not be placed in her care and charge. Likewise, if she is of bad character and conduct — that is, if she has such failings as fornication, theft, or singing and music, in the presence of which the child's religion and morals are likely to be ruined — the child will not be placed in the charge of such a woman.

The order of entitlement to custody after the mother:

If the mother is not fit to take custody — that is, if she is immoral and dissolute, or she has refused, or she has married a man who is a stranger to the child — then after her the right of custody belongs to her mother, the child's maternal grandmother. If there is no maternal grandmother, then the maternal great-grandmother; if they are not available, then the paternal grandmother, then the paternal great-grandmother, then the full sister, then the sister through the mother, then the sister through the father, then the maternal aunt, and then the paternal aunt.

The conditions of custody:

Whichever of these the child is placed with, the child will be handed over only on the conditions mentioned above; that is, custody will be given to the one whose character is sound. For example, if the child has two maternal aunts or two paternal aunts, the child will be given to whichever of them is more God-fearing.

The period of custody:

The period of custody for a boy is seven years, and for a girl nine years — and in the view of some, until she begins to menstruate. For this period the responsibility for the child's care lies with the mother, and the father must bear the expenses of both mother and child.

The importance of education and upbringing alongside custody:

Responsibility for bringing up a child does not mean merely that the parents provide for its physical care and growth. Along with its physical growth, its mental and moral development and its education and training are also obligatory upon them. The sharia has stressed both kinds of upbringing, and the Noble Qur'an and the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ lay particular emphasis on children's religious reform, education and training.
