### TITLE
Using artificial devices to satisfy sexual desire

### QUESTION
A woman's husband does not have relations with her at all, and she is upset with him because of this, even though he is in good health and fit. May his wife use some kind of artificial device to satisfy her desire? Is this permissible in the sharia or not?

### ANSWER
Just as the pure sharia has permitted the satisfying of sexual desire, so too it has set out the natural way of doing so for the guidance of mankind, so that alongside the satisfying of natural desire the chain of procreation should continue. Any method adopted for satisfying desire that goes against this natural way will not only be forbidden from the point of view of the sharia; being contrary to nature, it will also lead to moral degradation. The pure sharia states plainly that as long as this natural means of satisfying desire is not available to a person, he must keep away entirely from unnatural methods so as to preserve his chastity and purity.

Thus the hadiths tell us that some of the noble Companions, in the prime of their youth and unable to marry, asked what they should do, and the Prophet ﷺ instructed them to fast frequently. This shows that as long as desire cannot be satisfied in the natural way, unnatural methods are not permitted; rather one must preserve one's chastity and purity.

So in the case in question the woman will not be permitted in the sharia to adopt any unnatural method or to use artificial devices of any kind. She should instead try by whatever means she can to bring her husband round to fulfilling her rights.
