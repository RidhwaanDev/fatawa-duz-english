### TITLE
The side from which the imam turns towards the worshippers

### QUESTION
In Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā (2/170) this ruling is recorded: that it is better for the imam to turn towards those who prayed behind him from his right. Does "the right" here mean the worshippers' right, or the right of the qiblah, which in the language of the jurists is the worshippers' left?

### ANSWER
It should be borne in mind that it is not correct to make one particular side binding upon oneself; rather, turning from either side should be regarded as correct.

In the narration of ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) it is mentioned that whoever makes one side binding — that is, the right — has left a share for Satan in his prayer. Rasūlullāh ﷺ used to turn and leave from both the left and the right. (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim: 1/247; Sharḥ Muslim: 1/247). Al-Bayhaqī's al-Sunan al-Kubrā records that he ﷺ prayed both barefoot and wearing shoes, and both standing and sitting, and that he would leave from the right side and from the left. (Al-Sunan al-Kubrā: 2/295). Sunan Ibn Mājah (p. 66) contains the same account. This was also the practice of Anas (may Allah be pleased with him), and he used to take issue with those who attached excessive importance to the right side. (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī: 1/118).

Wāsiʿ ibn Ḥabbān relates that Ibn ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) was sitting resting against a wall. When Wāsiʿ finished his prayer he turned to his left and came over to him. Ibn ʿUmar asked him why he had not come from the right, and he replied that he had wanted to turn towards him. Ibn ʿUmar said: you did right; some people regard the right side as binding, but it is up to you — turn from the right or from the left. (Muwaṭṭaʾ Mālik: 155). The same is recorded in al-Tirmidhī (1/66).

Even so, it is better to give preference to the right. Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) says that Rasūlullāh ﷺ would usually turn to the right. (Muslim: 1/247). Imam al-Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in his commentary that the right is better, because this is what the general body of hadiths indicates. (Sharḥ Muslim: 1/247). Awjaz records that al-Ḥasan preferred to turn to the right after the prayer. (Awjaz: 3/497).

Now, which side is meant by "the right"? The wording of Radd al-Muḥtār shows that, according to Sharḥ al-Munyah, it is the worshipper's own right that is taken into account:

في شرح المنية: أن انحرافه عن يمينه أولي وأيده بحديث في صحيح مسلم. (فتاوى الشامي: ۱/۵۳۱).

Muslim states:

عن البراء رضي الله عنه قال: كنا إذا صلينا خلف رسول الله ﷺ أحببنا أن نكون عن يمينه يقبل علينا بوجهه. (مسلم ۱/۲٤۷).

Marāqī al-Falāḥ states:

وإن شاء انحرف عن يمينه وجعل القبلة عن يساره، وهذا أولي. (مراقي الفلاح: ص ۱۱۷).

After that, if he wishes to go home, which side should he leave from? Imam al-Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written that if both sides are equal for him and he has no need of one side in particular, the right is better; and Radd al-Muḥtār quotes this and accepts it. (1/531). Aḥsan al-Fatāwā likewise states that he ﷺ preferred the right both when departing and when turning towards those who had prayed behind him. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 3/368).

Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) has said that Rasūlullāh ﷺ turned from the right side when facing the congregation, and took the left side — that is, the worshippers' left — when leaving for home, "since the houses lay in that direction"; and this same side is the right of the qiblah and the left of the worshipper. His view, therefore, is that if a person wishes to pray his sunnah at home, it is better for him, in order to follow the example of Rasūlullāh ﷺ, to take the right of the qiblah, which is the worshippers' left.
(اعلاء السنن: ۳/۱۸٦. ۱۹۱۳).
