### TITLE
The definition of a bank

### QUESTION
The definition of a bank.

### ANSWER
A "bank" is the name of a commercial institution that collects people's money and provides loans out of it to traders, manufacturers and others who are in need. Conventional banks today charge interest on these loans and pay interest at a lower rate to their depositors, and the difference between the two rates of interest is the banks' profit.

A bank invites people to place their deposits with it (which in the language of fiqh is in fact a loan). These are called "امانتیں" in Urdu, "ودائع" in Arabic, and "Deposits" in English. Deposits are of several kinds:

(1) The "Current Account", which is called "الحساب الجاری" in Arabic and "مدرواں" in Urdu. No interest is paid on money kept in it, and money placed in this account may be withdrawn at any time, in whatever amount one wishes, without any restriction.

(2) The "Saving Account", which is called "حساب التوقیر" in Arabic and "بچت کھاتا" in Urdu. There are usually various restrictions on withdrawing money from it, and the bank pays interest on it.

(3) The "Fixed Deposit", which is called "ودائع ثابتۃ" in Arabic. Money cannot be taken back from it before the fixed term is over. The bank pays interest on this too, and the rate of interest depends on the term: the rate is higher for a long term and lower for a short one.

The functions of a bank:

After gathering capital a bank performs a number of functions, such as financing, the creation of money, and acting as an intermediary in exports and imports. The details of these may be looked up in books such as Islām awr Jadīd Maʿīshat-o-Tijārat.
