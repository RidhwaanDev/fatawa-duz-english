### TITLE
The ruling on hunting in a game farm

### QUESTION
A Muslim man owns a farm, or the government has granted him a licence for a state farm. This man grants permission to hunt and makes arrangements for hunting. The hunter sometimes hunts an animal whose meat is eaten and sometimes one whose meat is not eaten, such as a lion, a leopard and the like, and the owner or licence-holder charges a price for each animal taken. The question now is: does the owner of the farm become the owner of these animals or not? And if he is the owner, how can their sale be permissible without his having taken possession of them? And if he sells them after they have been hunted, then in the case of a deer and the like the sale of the slaughtered animal after slaughter, or after Bismillāh has been recited, will be permissible; but how will the sale of a lion and other animals whose meat is not eaten be permissible, since such an animal has the ruling of carrion?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, if this farm has been made for the purpose of gathering animals, or if the government has placed these animals there, or if their way in and out has been closed off, then in these cases the owner of the farm or the licence-holder becomes the owner of the animals. Alternatively, if the government is the owner, then this man becomes the agent of the owner.

Consider the following. Al-ʿInāyah Sharḥ al-Hidāyah states:

وصاحب الأرض لم يعد أرضه لذلك إشارة إلى أنه لو أعدها لذلك بأن حفرها ليقع فيها أو بغير ذلك مما يصاد به كان له. (العناية في شرح الهداية: ٦/۲۵۷، مسائل منثورة، مكتبة رشيدية).

Fatḥ al-Qadīr states:

وإن علم به أي الصيد وأغلق الباب عليه أو سد الكوة كان لصاحب الدار. (فتح القدير: ٦/۲۵۷، مسائل منثورة، مكتبة رشيدية).

Sharḥ Tuḥfat al-Mulūk states:

إلا أن يغلق الباب لا حرازه، فحينئذٍ يملكه لان الحكم عند القصد يضاف إلى السبب وعلى هذا لو حفر رجل في أرضه حفيرة فوقع فيها صيد فجاء رجل وأخذه فهو له إلا أن يتخذ صاحب الأرض تلك الحفيرة لا جل الصيد، ذكره محمد رحمه الله. (شرح تحفة الملوك لمحمد بن عبد اللطيف ابن ملك، ۲/۱۷۳۲).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad ʿUthmānī, after quoting a lengthy passage from Al-Durr al-Mukhtār concerning the sale of fish, writes:

The upshot is that if this land has been designated for the gathering of fish, then the fish are the property of the owner of the land and others have no right to catch them. Likewise, if the land was not prepared for this purpose but, after the fish had entered, their way out and in was closed off, then too they are the property of the owner of the land. (امداد الاحکام: ۳/٤۱۷).

In the light of the passages set down above it appears that the owner of the farm and the government are the owners of these animals; but since they are not capable of being handed over, the sale and purchase of live animals that can run away is not permissible. Then, when the hunter has hunted them and the animal has been slaughtered, or Bismillāh was recited at the time of hunting, the sale of a lawful animal is permissible. Alternatively, a promise of sale may be made before the hunt, and after the hunt the promise may be fulfilled and the animal sold to the buyer; and at that point there will be no dispute over the price, because the price of each animal is fixed beforehand, and since the meat of a lawful animal is also sought and desired, the buyer purchases it.

As for an animal whose meat is not eaten, its meat is neither lawfully nor customarily eaten. Here too the promise will serve. Since what the buyer — the hunter — wants is the hide, the owner of the farm should make a promise that after the hide has been removed he will sell it to him alone, and then the hide may be sold at the price required. When a lion or the like is killed with a bullet from a gun and its blood flows out, then, the flowing blood having come out, its hide will be saleable just like a tanned hide.

قال في الدر المختار: وهل يشترط لطهارة جلده كون ذكاته شرعية بأن تكون من الأهل في المحل بالتسمية، قيل: نعم، وقيل لا، والأول أظهر؛ لان ذبح المجوسي وتارك التسمية عمداً كلا ذبح، وإن صحح الثاني، صححه الزاهدي في القنية والمجتبى وأقره في البحر. (الدر المختار: ۱/۲۰۵، سعيد).
ذكر أنه في المعراج نقل عن المجتبى والقنية تصحيح الثاني ثم قال: وصاحب القنية هو صاحب المجتبى وهو الإمام الزاهدي المشهور علمه وفقهه، ويدل على أن هذا هو الأصح أن صاحب النهاية ذكر هذا الشرط أي كون الذكاة شرعية بصيغة قيل معزياً إلى الخانية. (البحر الرائق: ۱/۱۰٦، كوئته، قاضيخان على هامش الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۲۰).

That is to say, the position that the hide becomes pure by a non-sharʿī slaughter is the sounder view, while the condition that the slaughter be a sharʿī one has been mentioned with the expression "it is said".

From the passages cited in Radd al-Muḥtār (1/205, Saʿīd) it is established that even if a voluntary slaughter is not a sharʿī one, the hide still becomes pure. Likewise a non-voluntary slaughter — that is, striking with an arrow or with a bullet is non-voluntary — so that if this is not sharʿī, that is, if a non-Muslim struck it without Bismillāh, or a Muslim struck it without Bismillāh, its hide will become pure and, having the ruling of a slaughtered animal, its sale will be permissible.

The details of this question have moreover already been given in volume five under Kitāb al-Buyūʿ, and may be consulted there.
