### TITLE
Whether a free adult woman's marriage to a man who is not her equal takes effect

### QUESTION
If an adult Muslim woman of sound mind marries a man who is not her equal in status, without her guardian's permission, do the guardians have the right to object to it or not? And has this marriage taken effect or not? Many scholars, after all, say that such a marriage is void.

### ANSWER
In the case described, according to the ẓāhir al-riwāyah (the authoritative narration of the school), the marriage of a free adult woman of sound mind to a man who is not her equal does take effect. However, because he is not her equal, the guardians will have the right to have it annulled so long as she has not borne a child by him. This is the fatwa of the Majmaʿ al-Fiqh al-Islāmī of India and of Muftī Kifāyatullāh (may Allah have mercy on him). After the marriage the wife is lawful for her husband. If the parents are displeased, the matter should be placed before the local Jamʿiyyah, or before a ruler or a scholar. First an effort should be made to bring the parents round; and if no way can be found of keeping the marriage in place, she should ask her husband for a divorce. If the husband refuses to divorce her, then the Jamʿiyyah, or the ruler or scholar, may annul the marriage at the guardians' request.

Consider what al-Hidāyah states:

وينعقد نكاح الحرة العاقلة البالغة برضائها وإن لم يعقد عليها ولي بكراً كانت أو ثيباً عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله وأبي يوسف رحمه الله في ظاهر الرواية وعند محمد رحمه الله ينعقد موقوفاً

ثم في ظاهر الرواية لا فرق بين الكفو وغير الكفو لكن للولي الاعتراض في غير الكفو

ويروى رجوع محمد رحمه الله إلى قولهما. (الهداية: ۲/۳۱۳، باب في الأولياء والأكفاء).

Fatḥ al-Qadīr states:

ورواية رجوعه إلى ظاهر الرواية فتحصل أن الثابت الآن هو اتفاق الثلاثة على الجواز مطلقاً من الكفء وغيره. (فتح القدير: ۳/۲۵٦، دار الفكر).

Sharḥ al-ʿInāyah states:

قوله ولكن للولي الاعتراض في غير كفء) يعني إذا لم تلد من الزوج، وأما إذا ولدت فليس للأولياء حق الفسخ كي لا يضيع الولد عمن يربيه. (شرح العناية على هامش فتح القدير: ۳/۲۵۸، دار الفكر).

Al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

ثم المرأة إذا زوجت نفسها من غير كفو صح النكاح في ظاهر الرواية عن أبي حنيفة رحمه الله وهو قول أبي يوسف رحمه الله آخراً وقول محمد رحمه الله آخراً أيضاً، حتى أن قبل التفريق يثبت فيه حكم الطلاق والظهار والإيلاء والتوارث وغير ذلك، ولكن للأولياء حق الاعتراض. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۲۹۲. وفتاوى قاضيخان: ۱/۳۵۱).

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

الحرة العاقلة البالغة إذا زوجت نفسها من رجل أو وكلت رجلاً بالتزويج فتزوجها أو زوجها فضولي فأجازت، جاز في قول أبي حنيفة رحمه الله وزفر رحمه الله وأبي يوسف رحمه الله الأول سواء زوجت نفسها من كفؤ أو غير كفؤ بمهر وافر أو قاصر، غير أنها إذا زوجت نفسها من غير كفؤ فللأولياء حق الاعتراض. (بدائع الصنائع: ۲/۲٤۷، سعيد. وكذا في البحر الرائق: ۳/۱۲۸، كوئته).

Muftī Kifāyatullāh (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

For a marriage to be valid it is in itself enough that the man and the woman are Muslims and that the woman is not one of those forbidden to him. The explicit texts of the Noble Qur'an point to this:

﴿وأحل لكم ما وراء ذلكم أن تبتغوا بأموالكم﴾، ﴿فانكحوا ما طاب لكم من النساء﴾

The Prophet's ﷺ practice then confirmed it in deed: he ﷺ married Zaynab of the Hāshimite house (may Allah be pleased with her) to Zayd, a freed slave (may Allah be pleased with him), even though Zaynab's heart was not open to it. Besides this, many other examples exist in the deeds and conduct of the noble Companions of marriages taking place despite a difference of lineage. The texts of the Qur'an and the practice of the Companions and the pious predecessors are thus conclusive proof that equality of lineage is in itself recommended and is not a condition for a marriage to take effect. It is for this reason that a marriage to a man who is not an equal becomes valid and effective once the bride and her guardian are content with it. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 5/209, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

Majmūʿah Qawānīn Islāmī states:

The right to equality of status belongs both to the wife and to her guardians. So if a woman knowingly marries a man who is not her equal, the guardians will have the right to have the marriage annulled. (Majmūʿah Qawānīn Islāmī: clause 122, p. 98).

At the eleventh seminar of the Majmaʿ al-Fiqh al-Islāmī, held in April 1999, the view that emerged from most of the participants after a detailed discussion of this question was as follows:

"A marriage without equality of status is valid, and the guardians have the right to object."

See: Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith: 17/251–266, Idārat al-Qur'ān.

The sacred law also contains other examples in which a marriage between people who were not equals took effect.

(1) The marriage of Zaynab bint Jaḥsh (may Allah be pleased with her), as already mentioned.

(2) Fāṭimah bint Qays (may Allah be pleased with her) was settled in marriage with Usāmah ibn Zayd. (Muslim Sharīf: 1/485).

(3) Bilāl (may Allah be pleased with him) married an Arab woman. (Al-Mabsūṭ: 5/23, Bāb al-Kafāʾah).

(4) Salmān al-Fārisī (may Allah be pleased with him) sent a proposal of marriage to the virtuous daughter of ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) and it was accepted, but by Allah's decree the marriage did not take place. (Al-Mabsūṭ: 5/23, Bāb al-Kafāʾah).

ʿAllāmah al-Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) transmitted from al-Ḥasan (may Allah have mercy on him) the position on marriage to a man who is not an equal and gave the fatwa accordingly; this will have been what was expedient in his own age. Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ويفتى في غير الكفؤ بعدم جوازه أصلاً، وهو المختار للفتوى لفساد الزمان، وفي الشامية: وقال شمس الأئمة: وهذا أقرب إلى الاحتياط. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ۳/۵٦، سعيد).

وفيه أيضاً: أما على رواية الحسن المختار للفتوى من أنه لا يصح. (فتاوى الشامي: ۳/۸٤، سعيد).

What is expedient in the present age, however, is that the fatwa be given according to the ẓāhir al-riwāyah, because hundreds of such marriages are taking place day after day. If these marriages were not accepted as valid, all of these people would be left in fornication. Moreover, when the essential elements of marriage and the witnesses are present, it is hard to see how the marriage would fail to take effect.

Mawlānā Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in his footnote to Kitāb al-Faskh wa al-Tafrīq:

Does a marriage to someone who is not an equal take effect or not? On this question the ẓāhir al-riwāyah is that the marriage does take effect, though the guardian has the right to object. The narration of Ḥasan ibn Ziyād (may Allah have mercy on him) is that the marriage does not take effect at all, and it is on this that the scholars of that period generally gave the fatwa, keeping in view the corruption of the age; the learned author too has adopted this position.

This humble servant differs from that view. In my opinion the ẓāhir al-riwāyah alone should be acted upon, because not a single one of the conditions for a marriage to take effect is missing. And in today's circumstances as well — when family ties have grown weak and the very notion of marrying an equal or a non-equal is slowly disappearing — the situation itself demands that a marriage already contracted be accepted as having taken effect, and that if anyone is harmed by it, a way be found to remove that harm by giving him the right to lodge his objection before a judge. (Footnote to Kitāb al-Faskh wa al-Tafrīq by Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ṣamad Raḥmānī (may Allah have mercy on him): p. 120, footnote no. 1).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
