### TITLE
Sighting the crescent moon from an aeroplane

### QUESTION
If a person flies in an aeroplane and sees the crescent moon, but nobody on the ground is able to see it, will a sighting made only from the aeroplane be taken into account or not?

### ANSWER
If a person flew in an aeroplane and saw the crescent moon while nobody on the ground could see it, then a sighting made only from the aeroplane does not count in the sharia. If, however, the aeroplane is not at a great height and someone sitting in it sees the moon, his sighting will be accepted, because the jurists have stated plainly that if a person sees the moon outside the city, or from some elevated place, his sighting is accepted.

Consider what al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

وذكر الطحاوي أنه تقبل شهادة الواحد إذا جاء من خارج المصر وكذا إذا كان على مكان مرتفع كذا في الهداية، وعلى قول الطحاوي اعتمد الإمام المرغيناني وصاحب الأقضية والفتاوى الصغرى. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۹۸، الباب الثاني في رؤية الهلال).

Fatāwā Qāḍīkhān states:

وإن جاء الواحد من خارج المصر وشهد برؤية الهلال ثمة روي أنه تقبل شهادته وإليه أشار في الأصل، وكذا لو شهد برؤية الهلال في المصر على مكان مرتفع. (فتاوى قاضيخان على هامش الهندية: ۱/۱۹٦، الفصل الاول رؤية الهلال).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

أو كان على مكان مرتفع واختاره ظهير الدين.

وفي الشامي: قلت:

وفي المبسوط وإنما يرد الإمام شهادته إذا كانت السماء مصحية، وهو من أهل المصر فأما إذا كانت متغيمة أو جاء من خارج المصر أو كان في موضع مرتفع فإنه يقبل عندنا اه. فقوله عندنا يدل على أنه قول أئمتنا الثلاثة وقد جزم به في المحيط وعبر عن مقابله بقيل، ثم قال وجه ظاهر الرواية أن الرؤية تختلف باختلاف صفو الهواء وكدورته وباختلاف انهباط المكان وارتفاعه، فإن هواء الصحراء أصفي من هواء المصر، وقد يري الهلال من أعلي الأماكن مالا يري من الأسفل فلا يكون تفرده بالرؤية خلاف الظاهر، بل على موافقة الظاهر ففيه التصريح بأنه ظاهر الرواية، وهو كذلك لأن المبسوط من كتب ظاهر الرواية أيضاً. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ۲/۳۸۸، كتاب الصوم، سعيد. وكذا في إمداد الفتاح: ص ٦۷۰، بيروت)

Islāmī Fiqh states:

When the sky is clear, no special effort is needed to see the moon. But if the sky is dusty or overcast, or the city is one in which there are nothing but buildings of ten and twenty storeys, then there is no harm in trying to see the moon with a telescope or from an aeroplane, provided that this is arranged by an Islamic government or by properly appointed, trustworthy people. It is also necessary that the moon should not be observed from a height greater than that at which the moon is ordinarily sighted in that place — that is, the aeroplane should not be flown very high, since the moon never sets and can always be seen from somewhere or other. It is therefore extremely important to keep this in mind. (اسلامی فقہ ۱/۳۸۲، نئے آلات کے ذریعہ)۔

Ālāt-e Jadīdah ke Sharʿī Aḥkām records:

The condition is that the flight should not be so high that the eyes of those on the ground could not reach that far, because in the sharia only that sighting counts which people living on the earth can make with their own eyes. So if a person flies at a height of twenty or thirty thousand feet and sees the moon, that sighting does not count for a town whose ordinary people cannot see it even though the sky is clear. (آلات جدیدہ کے شرعی احکام، ص ۱۸٦، کتب خانہ قاسمی دیوبند)۔

Niẓām al-Fatāwā states:

If the witnesses giving the report see the moon from an aeroplane and then give their report or testimony in the proper manner set out above, it will be accepted according to the rules of the sharia, and such a report or testimony can indeed be relied upon and accepted. (منتخبات نظام الفتاوی: ص ۲۲۹، اصلاحی کتب خانہ)۔

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

When the sky is overcast, a strong probability is enough; in such a case, therefore, a sighting by means of an aeroplane or a telescope should be valid, provided that the aeroplane has not been flown so high that the horizon itself changes.

Accordingly, resolution (7) of the Majlis-e Taḥqīqāt-e Sharʿiyyah of Nadwat al-ʿUlamāʾ, Lucknow, reads as follows:

"To fly in an aeroplane to such a height that the horizon is affected and to see the moon from there is not valid, nor has the sharia placed anyone under an obligation to do so. Where the books of fiqh mention climbing to high places to see the moon, what is meant is the sort of height ordinarily found in cities, so that the height of houses and trees does not block the view of the horizon — by whatever means this is achieved. So if the moon is seen from an aeroplane at such a height that the horizon changes, it will not be regarded as a valid sighting for the people of the land below." (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۲/۲٤، نعیمیہ)۔
