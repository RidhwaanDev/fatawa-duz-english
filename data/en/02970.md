### TITLE
The reward for killing the chameleon and the house lizard, and the wisdom behind it

### QUESTION
In the blessed hadiths there is a promise of reward for killing the house lizard and the chameleon. Which hadith is that, and what is the wisdom behind it? That it blew upon the fire of Ibrāhīm (peace be upon him) — is this correct or not? If it is correct, then this act was done by one particular chameleon, and the idea of punishing its entire progeny is not easy to understand. Some people say that it is a venomous creature, so much so that there is venom in its feet. Is this correct or not, given that some researchers say that there is no venom in it at all? بینوا بالدلیل۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question, mention of reward for killing the chameleon and the house lizard is found in the blessed hadiths. Consider the following few hadiths:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: من قتل وزغاً في أول ضربة كتبت له مائة حسنة وفي الثانية دون ذلك وفي الثالثة دون ذلك. (رواه مسلم، رقم: ٤۱۵٦).
وفي رواية أن رسول الله ﷺ قال: في أول ضربة سبعين حسنة. (رواه مسلم، رقم: ٤۱۵۷).

In the narration of Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah Most High be pleased with him) says: The Noble Prophet ﷺ declared: Whoever kills a lizard with a single blow, for him there are a hundred good deeds; whoever kills it with a second blow will receive fewer than that; and whoever kills it with a third blow will receive fewer good deeds still.

Another narration states that whoever kills it with the first blow, for him there are seventy good deeds.

Other narrations too have been transmitted; see Sunan Abī Dāwūd, which states:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: من قتل وزغة في أول ضربة فله كذا وكذا حسنة ومن قتلها في الضربة الثانية فله كذا وكذا حسنة أدني من الأول ومن قتلها في الضربة الثالثة فله كذا وكذا حسنة أدني من الثانية. (رواه أبو داود، رقم: ۵۲٦۵، والترمذي، رقم: ۱۵۵۸، وابن ماجه، رقم: ۳۳۵۱، وأحمد، رقم: ۸۸۹۳).

The commentators have mentioned four reasons for the difference between the narrations:

(1) The smaller number does not negate the larger number. (Imām al-Nawawī, Sharḥ Muslim: 2/236).

(2) Report was first given of seventy, and afterwards report was given of a hundred. (Imām al-Nawawī, Sharḥ Muslim: 2/236).

(3) The reward will differ according to the states and the intention of the one who kills it. (Imām al-Nawawī, Sharḥ Muslim: 2/236).

(4) The difference between the narrations is the outcome of narration by meaning. (Muftī Taqī ʿUthmānī, Takmilat Fatḥ al-Mulhim: 4/402).

The wisdom behind killing the chameleon and the house lizard:

It is mentioned in the hadiths that the chameleon and the house lizard blew upon the fire of Ḥaḍrat Ibrāhīm (peace be upon him), with the intention of making the fire fiercer. See Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, which states:

عن أم شريك رضي الله عنه أن رسول الله ﷺ أمر بقتل الوزغ وقال: كان ينفخ على إبراهيم عليه السلام. (رواه البخاري، رقم: ۳۳۵۹).
وعن سائبة مولاة الفاكه بن المغيرة إنها دخلت على عائشة رضي الله عنه فرأت في بيتها رمحاً موضوعاً فقالت يا أم المؤمنين ما تضعين بهذا قالت: نقتل به الأوزاغ فإن نبي الله ﷺ أخبرنا أن إبراهيم لما ألقي في النار لم تكن في الأرض دابة إلا أطفئت النار غير الوزغ فإنها كانت تنفخ عليه فأمر رسول الله ﷺ بقتله. (رواه ابن ماجه، رقم: ۳۲۳۱).

In the chain of this narration, Sāʾibah the freedwoman of al-Fākih is an unknown narrator; a corroborating report for it is, however, present in al-Bukhārī. (Taʿlīq Ibn Mājah: 4/619).

The real cause for the command to kill the chameleon is, nevertheless, its being harmful and venomous. See Takmilat Fatḥ al-Mulhim, which states:

والذي يظهر لي ـ والله أعلم ـ أن النبي ﷺ إنما بين هذه القصة لبيان خبث ودناءة جبلته وإنما السبب في الأمر بقتله هو كونه مؤذياً وإلا فالظاهر فعلة وزغات عهد إبراهيم عليه السلام لا يعاقب به وزغات هذا الزمان فالسبب الأصلي في الأمر بقتله هو إيذاء ه واعتداء ه ومن جملته ما فعل أبناء جنسه لسيدنا إبراهيم عليه السلام. (تكملة فتح الملهم: ٤/٤۰۰).

Maẓāhir-e Ḥaqq Jadīd records: This, as it were, sets out the vileness of the chameleon: when Nimrod cast Ḥaḍrat Ibrāhīm (peace be upon him) into the fire, this chameleon kept blowing upon that fire in order to make it blaze. In any case, experience too has shown that this creature is highly venomous and harmful; if its venomous germs reach articles of food and drink, very severe harm can come to people from it. (Maẓāhir-e Ḥaqq Jadīd: 4/62).

For further reference, see: (Tuḥfat al-Almaʿī: 4/418; Al-Durr al-Manḍūd: 6/686; and Badhl al-Majhūd: 12/453).

Research on the harmfulness and venomousness of the wazagh, that is, the house lizard and the chameleon:

ʿAllāmah al-Damīrī writes in Ḥayāt al-Ḥayawān:

واتفقوا على أن الوزغ من الحشرات المؤذيات

وزعموا أنها تسقي من الحيات وتمج في الإناء فينال الإنسان المكروه العظيم بسبب ذلك وذكر أصحاب الآثار أن الوزغ أصم قالوا: والسبب في صممه ما تقدم من نفخه النار على إبراهيم عليه السلام فصم لا جل ذلك وبرص ومن طبعه أنه لا يدخل بيتاً فيه رائحة الزعفران وتألفه الحيات كما تألف العقارب الخنافس ويلقح بفيه ويبيض كما تبيض الحيات ويقيم في حجره زمن الشتاء أربعة أشهر لا يطعم شيئاً. (حياة الحيوان: ۲/۲٤٦).

According to modern researchers, most kinds of lizard are venomous. Consider the following:

Recent studies show that a number of types of lizards including monitors and iguanas, have venom-secreting glands in their mouths but lack specialized teeth for delivering a potent bite (Microsoft Encarta 2008). For a long time, it was assumed that only two lizards; the Gilamoster and the Mexican Beaded Lizard, have venom glands. In our study in 2006 in nature, we showed the presence of venom glands in additional lizards' lineages (University of Cambridge/Google). Research Bryan Frys team at the University of Melbourne, Australia, now suggests that venomous lizards are much more widespread than anyone realised. (Google)

The upshot of these passages is that formerly people did not know this, and earlier research had established that venom is present in two kinds of lizard; but modern research has made it clear that venom is present in most kinds of lizard.

For this reason, when a lizard falls into a curry and that curry is eaten unknowingly, harm and illness befall those who eat it — and this is people's observation — to the point that there are venomous effects in its feet, and it is the only animal or creature that has venomous effects in its feet.

The finding of Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) is that the command to kill includes both the house lizard, which is found in houses, and the chameleon, which is found in the hills and the fields. See what is mentioned in Imdād al-Muftīn:

The second point is: what is the definition of wazaghah, and is the house lizard included in it or not? From an examination of the lexicons and other works on this it emerges that wazaghah is a general word which includes both the chameleon (kirliyā) and the house lizard, while the term sām abraṣ is applied only to the chameleon (kirliyā). For this reason some lexicographers have rendered wazaghah as sām abraṣ, while others have stated explicitly that sām abraṣ is one particular species of wazaghah, which is large and lives in the grass.

قال في القاموس تحت الوزغة محركه سام أبرص وقال تحت أبرص وسام أبرص من كبار الوزغ ومثله في حياة الحيوان تحت سام أبرص، وقال في مجمع البحار وهي ما يقال له أي الوزغة ثم قال: الوزغ دابة لها نوع تعدد في الحشيش. وقال في النهاية لابن أثير وهي التي يقال لها سام أبرص ومثله في الدر المنثور للسيوطي رحمه الله.

Then, after quoting a passage from a Persian lexicon, he writes: From all these passages of the Arabic, Persian and Hindi lexicons it emerges that in Arabic wazaghah, and in Persian chalpāsah, are general words which apply to both kinds — that is, to both the chameleon and the house lizard — whereas in Arabic sām abraṣ, and in Persian karbah, karsh and the like, are used only for the chameleon. The command in the hadith to kill has been given with the word wazaghah; for this reason it appears to include both kinds. (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband: 2/208, Dār al-Ishāʿat, Karachi).

Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her) kept a bamboo or a spear in the house, and it is obvious that it is the house lizard that is found in houses. Although the chain of this narration is weak, a hadith corroborating it is present in Ibn Mājah and al-Bukhārī.

Wazagh is a generic noun, and it has two species: the chameleon and the house lizard. (Tuḥfat al-Almaʿī: 4/418).
