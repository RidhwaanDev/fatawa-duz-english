### TITLE
Taking a fee for delivering talks and sermons

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to take a fee for delivering talks and sermons, or not?

### ANSWER
From the statements of the jurists it appears that taking a fee for talks and sermons is permissible, but our elders (may Allah have mercy on them) did not approve of it. There is therefore no dispute about its permissibility in itself; it is simply not something to be commended. If a speaker has set himself apart for preaching as his sole occupation, however, there is no harm in his taking a fee.

See what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ويفتي اليوم بصحتها لتعليم القرآن والفقه والإمامة والأذان. وفي الشامية: وزاد بعضهم الأذان والإقامة والوعظ. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٦/۵۵، مطلب تحرير مهم في عدم جواز الاستئجار على التلاوة

، سعيد).

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

وفي الأصل لا يجوز الاستئجار على الطاعات كتعليم القرآن والفقه والأذان والتذكير والتدريس والحج والعمرة ولا يجب الأجر، كذا في الخلاصة ومشايخ بلخ رحمه الله جوزوا

والمختار للفتوى في زماننا قول هؤلاء كذا في الفتاوى العتابية. (الفتاوى الهندية: ٤/٤٤۸).

وفي البزازية: الاستئجار على الطاعات كتعليم القرآن والفقه والتدريس والوعظ لا يجوز أي لا يجب الأجر وأهل المدينة طيب الله ساكنها جوزه قال في المحيط وفتوى مشايخ بلخ رحمه الله على الجواز قال الإمام الفضلي والمتأخرون على جوازه. (الفتاوى البزازية: ۲/۳۷).

Majmaʿ al-Anhur states:

ولا يجوز أخذ الأجرة عند المتقدمين على الطاعات وفي شرح الوافي والمذهب عندنا أن كل طاعة يختص بها المسلم فالاستيجار عليها باطل كالأذان والحج والإمامة والتذكير والتدليس

ويفتي اليوم بالجواز

كما في عامة المعتبرات وهذا على مذهب المتأخرين من مشايخ بلخ رحمه الله استحسنوا ذلك وقالوا: الأحكام قد تختلف باختلاف الزمان ألا يري أن النساء كن يخرجن إلى الجماعات في زمانه عليه الصلاة والسلام وفي زمان أبي بكر الصديق رضي الله عنه حتى منعهن عمر رضي الله عنه واستقر الأمر عليه وكان ذلك هو الصواب. (مجمع الأنهر شرح ملتقي الأبحر: ۲/۳۸۲).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Just as it is valid to be employed for teaching and instruction, so it is valid to be employed for admonition and lecturing. The work should be specified — for instance, that one hour every day, or two hours every Friday, of lecturing will be required, and that such-and-such payment will be given; or a speaker may be engaged as a permanent employee for lecturing, so that he goes about to various places, whether he is invited to gatherings or not, and delivers his talks.

What is not commendable is the practice of delivering a sermon somewhere and taking money for it, and then, if the sum falls short of what one had in mind, pulling a long face. This destroys the effect of the sermon as well; those who invite the man do so as a mere formality, and even before inviting him they begin making pointed remarks — that he was given such-and-such an amount and was displeased with it, so he should not be invited until more can be arranged, and so on. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۷/۸۵، جامعہ فاروقیہ).

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

As to whether it is permissible to take a fee for preaching, the opinion of some scholars is that it falls under taking payment for acts of obedience and is therefore not permissible, while the opinion of other scholars is that it is permissible. Reconciling the two views, Hazrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

If a man takes up the post of preacher as a job, as in the case of leading the prayer, then taking a fee is permissible; but if he has not taken up (permanent) employment and instead stipulates a fee at the very moment he is asked (that is, when someone is invited to preach on a particular occasion and he makes payment a condition), then this is not permissible. It is like a man who is not the regular imam: the time of prayer comes, he happens to be in the mosque, and when he is asked to lead the prayer he demands a fee for leading it at that very moment. That too is not permissible. (جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام: ۱/۲۱۳).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

If a man has been engaged as an employee to preach, or has set himself apart for this very work, then in that case it is permissible to take a fee for preaching; but if a scholar is requested to deliver a sermon on some particular occasion, taking a fee for that sermon is not permissible. (احسن الفتاوی: ۷/۳۰۰).

There is, however, no harm in accepting a gift or present on such an occasion.

Hazrat Mawlānā Rashīd Aḥmad Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

The later scholars have written that a fee for preaching is permissible too, on account of need. (تالیفاتِ رشیدیہ، ٤۱۸، ادارہ اسلامیات لاہور).

Qāmūs al-Fiqh states:

It is not permissible to take a fee for an occasional sermon or talk and the like, because no religious observance is impaired by that, nor is there any fear of religion being lost. But if a man is engaged specifically for this work, and preaching, admonition, propagation and calling people to Islam become his standing responsibility and duty, then it will be permissible for him to take a fee for it. (قاموس الفقہ: ۱/٤۹۷).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
