### TITLE
The ruling on a Muslim keeping an interest-bearing account and paying interest money in tax

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to pay interest money in tax or not? If it is not, what should be done with interest money? Is it permissible for a Muslim to keep an interest-bearing account in which interest is earned, with the intention that he will pay tax with the interest money? بینوا بالدلیل توجروا بالاجر الجزیل ۔

### ANSWER
1. In the case in question, if it is a fair tax — that is, one whose benefit reaches us in one form or another — then it is not permissible to pay any kind of interest money in such a tax, whether that interest is from a private bank or from a government bank. Yes, as for the tax which is imposed unjustly by the government — for example income tax, sales tax and the like — its ruling is that there is scope to pay the interest of a central and government bank in it, because this is tantamount to returning unlawful wealth to its owner; although some cautious scholars have written that it is not proper to derive any kind of benefit from interest money, and therefore giving it in tax too is contrary to caution, even though it falls within the limits of permissibility. However, paying the interest of a private bank in a government tax is not permissible and not correct. And in our country, South Africa, the banks are generally private, so paying such interest in tax is not permissible.

2. If someone has interest money, it should be returned to its owner exactly as it is; and if the owner is not known, it should be given in charity to the poor without the intention of reward.

3. It is permissible to keep money in a bank for the purpose of safekeeping; yes, it should be kept in an account other than an interest-bearing one. But if someone has kept it in an interest-bearing account by mistake, then along with repentance and seeking forgiveness he should distribute the interest among the poor without the intention of reward, and in future it is not permissible for any Muslim to become involved in an interest-based transaction. Furthermore, opening an interest-bearing account with the intention that one will pay tax with the interest money is not permissible.

The evidences may be seen in order:

1. Fatāwā al-Shāmī mentions:

ـ إذا آجر المغصوب فالأجر له، فإن تلف المغصوب من هذا العمل أو تلف لا منه وضمنه الغاصب له الاستعانة بالأجر في أداء الضمان وتصدق بالباقي. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۱۸۹، سعيد).

قال في الهداية: فلو هلك العبد في يد الغاصب حتى ضمنه له أن يستعين بالغلة في أداء الضمان، لأن الخبث لا جل المالك، ولهذا لو أدي إليه يباح له التناول فيزول الخبث بالأداء إليه، بخلاف ما إذا باعه فهلك في يد المشتري ثم استحق وغرمه ليس له أن يستعين بالغلة في أداء الثمن إليه؛ لأن الخبث ما كان لحق المشتري إلا إذا كان لا يجد غيره؛ لأنه محتاج إليه، وله أن يصرفه إلى حاجة نفسه، فلو أصاب مالا تصدق بمثله إن كان غنياً وقت الاستعمال، وإن كان فقيراً فلا شيء عليه لما ذكرنا. (الهداية: ۳/۳۷۵).

(وكذا في تبيين الحقائق: ۵/۲۲۵، إمداديه).

وفي الفتاوى السراجية: إذا آجر المغصوب يستعين بأجره في ضمان القيمة ويتصدق بالفضل لأنه كسب خبيث. (الفتاوى السراجية، ص ۳۵۸).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah records: The interest money obtained from a government bank should be given to the government itself in a non-obligatory tax, or else given to the needy poor, without making the intention of reward. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۲٤/۳٤۷) ۔

Muntakhab Niẓām al-Fatāwā mentions: Whatever interest money is received from the State Bank or the central government and all the banks belonging to it — if it is given in some un-Islamic tax of the central government itself, one's liability will be discharged, such as income tax and so on. (منتخب نظام الفتاوی: ۱/۱۹٦) ۔

2. Unlawful wealth should be returned to its owner, and if the owner is not found, it should be given in charity on his behalf.

قال في رد المحتار: لو مات الرجل وكسبه من بيع الباذق أو الظلم أو أخذ الرشوة يتورع الورثة، ولا يأخذون منه شيئاً وهو أولي بهم ويردونها على أربابها إن عرفوهم، وإلا تصدقوا بها لأن سبيل الكسب الخبيث التصدق إذا تعذر الرد على صاحبه. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۳۸۵، سعيد). وكذا في البحر الرائق: ۸/۲۰۱، كوئته).

3. Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah records: Money may be kept in a bank on account of safekeeping or legal difficulties; keeping money with the intention of obtaining interest is not correct, even if the intention be that the interest money will be given to the needy. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ۹/۲۷۰) ۔

Muntakhab Niẓām al-Fatāwā mentions: The first thing is that if money has to be deposited in a bank for the purpose of safekeeping or on account of some compulsion, one should try to deposit it in such a department or account in which interest is not calculated at all. (منتخب نظام الفتاوی: ۱/۱۹۰) ۔

Nowadays such interest-free accounts are available, so these very ones should be used, and money should not be deposited in an interest-bearing account.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
