### TITLE
The ruling on deciding by majority opinion

### QUESTION
If a difference arises between the members of the consultative council (shūrā) and the chairman, will the word of the trustee be accepted or that of the majority of the members? Is deciding by majority opinion an imitation of Western civilisation? Some religious people call it an un-Islamic method. Is this correct or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
1. If it is written in the charter of the consultative council that decisions will be taken by the majority even if that is against the opinion of the chairman, then the decision will be made in accordance with the charter, because the sharīʿah gives consideration to the majority, and the jurists have stated that the greater part carries the ruling of " الكل" (the whole). And Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) made the decision of the majority of the consultative council the means of selection after him.

2. And if it is written in the constitution that even where the chairman's decision goes against the majority it will still be accepted, then that is what will be acted upon. Its precedent is that after Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) became caliph, he decided upon fighting those who refused to pay zakāh and upon dispatching the army of Usāmah (may Allah be pleased with him) against the opinion of the people of counsel, and it was accepted, and it produced a good outcome.

But an objection may be raised against this: that in the matter of the apostates and those who would not pay zakāh, and in the matter of the army of Usāmah (may Allah be pleased with him), Ḥaḍrat al-Ṣiddīq al-Akbar (may Allah be pleased with him) decided of his own accord; we know of no seeking of the Companions' considered opinion. Yes — the Messenger of Allah ﷺ took counsel at Badr about going after the caravan, and the Companions said: we are with you; he took counsel about the place of encampment at Badr and it was acted upon; he took counsel at Uḥud and it was acted upon; he took counsel at the Trench and it was acted upon; at the Trench he took counsel about giving the confederates one third of the dates of Madīnah, and the Companions did not approve of it, and that is what was acted upon. Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Kathīr (may Allah have mercy on him) has written these incidents under "وشاورهم في الأمر". (تفسیر ابن کثیر: ۱/۴۵۳۔۴۵۴، ط: مکتبۃ دار السلام)۔

And in the noble verse:

﴿وشاورهم في الأمر فإذا عزمت فتوكل على الله﴾

there is an indication that the work should be carried out on the basis of the leader's firm resolve. One meaning of the noble verse may be that the leader changed his own opinion in the light of the majority opinion of the council and resolved upon some course — as at the Battle of Uḥud he resolved, upon the opinion of the Companions, to go out of Madīnah, and then the majority changed its opinion, saying: O Messenger of Allah, act upon your own opinion — so [the sense is]: once you have resolved, put your trust in Allah and act; do not change the decision again. And it may also mean: in carrying out the result of the consultation, place your trust in Allah the Exalted; do not rely upon the consultation, rely upon Allah the Exalted. This explanation of "resolve" is narrated from Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him): "مشاورة أهل الرأي ثم أتباعهم". (تفسیر ابن کثیر: ۱/۴۵۴)۔

If the constitution has laid down the chairman's opinion as the final word, then by virtue of that clause of the rules it may be acted upon; but the basic rule ought to be that of taking the majority.

3. And if nothing at all has been written in the constitution regarding this clause, and the majority is on one side while the leader is on the other, then the decision of the majority should be taken. Treatises have been written on this question; it is hoped that the discussion given above will prove comprehensive and beneficial. Consider a few proofs and supporting evidences for the ruling given above:

1. Under the noble verse of the Noble Qur'an:

﴿ما يكون من نجوي ثلاثة إلا هو رابعهم ولا خمسة إلا هو سادسهم ولا أدني من ذلك ولا أكثر إلا هو معهم أين ما كانوا﴾ [ المجادلة].

ʿAllāmah al-Ālūsī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Rūḥ al-Maʿānī:

وقد يقال: إن التناجي يكون في الغالب للشوري وهي لا تكون إلا بين عدد وأهلها قليلو العدد غالباً، والأليق أن يكون وتراً من الأعداد كالثلاثة والخمسة والسبعة والتسعة ليتحقق عند الاختلاف طرف يترجح بالزيادة على الطرف الآخر فيترجح إليه دونه كما هو العادة اليوم عند اختلاف أهل الشوري.

(روح المعانی: ۲۸/۲٤)۔ (و کذا فی تفسیر المظہری: ۹/۲۲۱)۔

In Tafsīr ʿUthmānī, Shaykh al-Islām Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Shabbīr Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

Note: if there are only two persons in a consultation, then in the case of disagreement it becomes difficult to give preference to one side; this is why in weighty matters an odd number is generally kept. And the first odd number after one was three, then five — perhaps that is why these two were chosen — and then the matter was made general by the words "ولا أدني من ذلك ولا أكثر". As for Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar's (may Allah be pleased with him) confining the consultation over the caliphate to six elders (even though six is not an odd number), that will have been because at that time these six alone were the most fit and deserving of the caliphate and none of them could be left out; moreover, the caliph was being chosen from among these very six, so obviously, whichever of them was named, apart from him only five remained to cast an opinion. Even so, out of caution, Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) named ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) so that in the event of a tie one side might be given preference. واللہ اعلم۔ (فوائد عثمانی، ص ۷۲۰، سورۂ مجادلہ)۔

Consider a few hadiths by way of corroboration:

(1) أخرج الإمام الطبراني في "الأوسط" (۲/۱۷۲) عن على رضي الله عنه قال: قلت: يا رسول الله! إن نزل بنا أمر ليس فيه بيان أمر ولأنهي، فما تأمرنا؟ قال: تشاورون الفقهاء والعابدين، ولا تمضوا فيه رأي خاصة. قال الهيثمي: ورجاله موثقون من أهل الصحيح. (مجمع الزوائد: ۱/۱۷۸، باب في الإجماع، دار الفكر).

The meaning is that Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī (may Allah the Exalted be pleased with him) asked the Prophet ﷺ: O Messenger of Allah, if some incident befalls us and there is no explicit ruling concerning it, what do you instruct us? The Prophet ﷺ said: gather the people of knowledge and the God-fearing and consult them; do not decide upon one particular opinion. ʿAllāmah al-Haythamī (may Allah have mercy on him) said: all the narrators of this narration are reliable.

(2) أخرج الإمام ابن ماجه (۲۸۳، باب سواد الأعظم) عن أنس بن مالك رضي الله عنه يقول: سمعت أنس بن مالك رضي الله عنه، يقول: سمعت رسول الله ﷺ، يقول: إن أمتي لا تجتمع على ضلالة، فإذا رأيتم اختلافاً فعليكم بالسواد الأعظم.

That is, the Prophet ﷺ said: my whole ummah cannot come together upon misguidance, so when you see disagreement, keep to the great body. And in another narration it is stated that the help and assistance of Allah the Exalted is with the united body.

عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: لن تجتمع أمتي على الضلالة أبداً، فعليكم بالجماعة فإن يد الله على الجماعة. (أخرجه الطبراني في الكبير: ۱۲/۴۴۷، رجاله رجال الصحيح).

Dr ʿAbd al-Karīm Zaydān writes in his treatise "The Position of the State and the Individual in Islam":

Acting upon the opinion of the majority is the second solution to a disputed matter — namely, that the head of state should accept the opinion of the majority even if it is against his own opinion. This method is exactly in accordance with the practice of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, which he adopted at the Battle of Uḥud on the question of whether to fight the polytheists by going out of the city or by remaining besieged within it. The personal opinion of the Prophet ﷺ was in favour of staying in the city and fighting; yet despite this he accepted the opinion of the majority, that the polytheists should be met in the open field by going out. This is because the majority is a source of good and benefit, and there is a comparatively greater likelihood of its being correct — even though the mere fact that the majority is on one side is no decisive proof of its being right, since at times the majority may be wrong and the opinion of the minority against it may be correct. (اسلام میں ریاست اور فرد کا مقام، ص ۷۰)۔

In Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah a detailed fatwa is recorded concerning this question. Consider it:

The matters that come before the consultative council of religious madrasahs are of several kinds:

1. Matters in which a text of the sharīʿah exists: there the text is what determines the course of action.

2. Matters in which no text exists and which have two aspects: (a) securing a benefit, (b) repelling a harm — there the consideration of repelling harm predominates.

3. Matters in which no text exists and in which the principle of repelling harm is also neither a guide nor a decisive factor: such as which of two men should be made rector, or who should be appointed head teacher, or from which shop the provisions for the kitchen should be purchased, or how many students should be admitted, or on which dates the examination should be held, and so forth. If the members of the council differ in their opinions but all the members agree that the matter should be entrusted to the discretion and considered judgement of the respected president, this too is correct. If the president does not arrive at such a settled judgement, then the majority opinion should be acted upon; this too is correct.

4. Where the members of the council agree upon one opinion and reach a consensus, and the opinion of the respected president is contrary to their agreed opinion, then the respected president should not insist upon his own opinion.

Zayd's idea — that "this deciding by majority opinion is wholly Westernism, an irreligious method, imitation and following of the English, it is not correct under any circumstances, and it should be expelled from religious institutions" — is not correct.

When the question of appointing a successor came before the second caliph, Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar al-Fārūq (may Allah be pleased with him), and the names of several persons were presented — men with whose capabilities, as well as whose weaknesses, he was acquainted (every person has some capability, and some weakness too) — he did not designate anyone; rather he set up a consultative council so that it might make the selection, and he gave the strictest instruction that in the case of disagreement the majority opinion was to be acted upon:

عن عمرو بن ميمون الأودي قال: قال عمر رضي الله عنه حين طعن لصهيب: صل بالناس ثلاثاً وليدخل على عثمان وعلي وطلحة والزبير وسعد وعبد الرحمن بن عوف، وليدخل ابن عمر في جانب البيت، وليس له من الأمر شيء فقم يا صهيب! على رؤوسهم بالسيف وإن بايع خمسة ونكص واحد، فاجلد رأسه بالسيف، وإن بايع أربعة ونكص رجلان فاجلدوه سهماً حتى يستوثقوا على رجل. (الاعتصام للشاطبي: ۲/۲٦۵)،

الطريقة الثالثة أن عمر رضي الله عنه لما ضرب وأحس بالموت، خاف أن يترك المسلمين بدون خليفة لئلا يختلفوا، ولم يكن أمام نظره من لو استخلفه يكون مطمئن النفس من قبله، فلم يشأ أن يتحمل المسلمين حياً وميتاً، فاختار ستة من كبار الصحابة وممن يري أنه لا يتطلع لا مر الخلافة غيرهم، ووضع لهم نظاماً ينتخبون به الخليفة من بينهم، فأمر أن يجتمعوا بعد وفاته في حجرة عائشة رضي الله عنه ويختاروا الخليفة في مدة لا تزيد على ثلاثة أيام، وجعل للأغلبية الرأي القبول، فيجب على الأقلية الرضوخ لحكمها، وإلا اعتبر خارجاً يستحق القتل. (تاريخ الأمم الإسلامية، ص ٦۴۴)،

Through the fine management of Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf (may Allah be pleased with him), Ḥaḍrat ʿUthmān (may Allah be pleased with him) was selected by majority opinion; then all of them gave the pledge of allegiance and came to agreement, and it turned out just as though Ḥaḍrat ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) had himself selected and appointed him.

Then, after the martyrdom of the third caliph, Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him) became caliph by the majority opinion of the people of binding and loosing. The details of both are found in Tārīkh al-Khulafāʾ. These noble men were not followers of the English.

To disregard majority opinion altogether is wrong. ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him), while setting down the principles of issuing fatwa, says:

واختلف الذين قد تأخروا ٭ يرجح الذي عليه الأكثر

وإذا لم يوجد في الحادثة عن واحد منهم جواب ظاهر، وتكلم فيه المشايخ المتأخرون قولاً واحداً، يؤخذ به، فإن اختلفوا يؤخذ بقول الأكثرين، الخ. (شرح عقود رسم المفتي، ص: ۵۲، مكتبة البشرى).

السادس: ما إذا كان أحد القولين المصححين قال به جل المشايخ العظام، ففي شرح البيري على الأشباه: أن المقرر عن المشايخ أنه متى اختلف في المسألة، فالعبرة بما قاله الأكثر، وقدمنا نحوه عن الحاوي القدسي.

(شرح عقود رسم المفتی، ص ٦۷، مکتبۃ البشری)۔

That is, it is settled on the part of the master jurists that at the time of disagreement it is the majority opinion alone that will be given consideration. If there is a disagreement over the selection of an imam and the arguments are equal, then lots should be drawn, or preference should be given by the majority opinion of the people of knowledge.

فإن استووا يقرع بين المستويين، أو الخيار إلى القوم، فإن اختلفوا اعتبر أكثرهم؛ ولو قدموا غير الأولي أساءوا بلا إثم. (الدر المختار: ۱/۵۵۸، سعيد).
قال في شرح المشكاة: لعله محمول على الأكثر من العلماء إذا وجدوا، وإلا فلا عبرة لكثرة الجاهلين، قال الله تعالى: ﴿ولكن أكثرهم لا يعلمون﴾ (مرقاة المفاتيح: ۳/۱۹۹، رشيدية، والطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح، ص ۲٤۳، بيروت).

Are the author of al-Durr al-Mukhtār, and Shāmī, and the commentator of Mishkāt, and Ṭaḥṭāwī urging that preference be given to majority opinion in imitation of the English? (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۵/۴۲۵۔۴۲۹، ط: جامعہ فاروقیہ)۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
