### TITLE
Wearing a wig of artificial hair

### QUESTION
A wig is a cap made of real or artificial hair. Is it permissible to wear one or not?

### ANSWER
Wearing a cap of artificial hair appears to be correct where there is a need. In our own times, however, because of imitation of others, excess in adornment and extravagance, one should refrain from wearing it simply for beautification and display.

All the same, it is not correct to rule that wearing a wig is impermissible on the strength of the hadith "لعن الله الواصلة والمستوصلة", because the passages of the jurists show that this hadith is to be applied to human hair alone.

قال محمد: أخبرنا أبو حنيفة رحمه الله قال: حدثنا الهيثم عن أم ثور عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنه قال: لا بأس بالوصل في الرأس إذا كان صوفاً قال محمد: وبه نأخذ وهو قول أبي حنيفة. (كتاب الآثار، ص ۳۷۸).

Fatḥ al-Qadīr states:

وهذا اللعن للانتفاع بما لا يحل الانتفاع به ألا تري أنه رخص في اتخاذ القراميل وهو ما يتخذ من الوبر ليزيد في قرون النساء للتكثير فظهر أن اللعن ليس للتكثير مع عدم الكثرة وإلا لمنع القراميل ولا شك أن الزينة حلال قال الله تعالى: قل من حرم زينة الله التي أخرج لعباده، فلولا لزوم الإهانة بالاستعمال لحل وصلها بشعور النساء أيضاً. (فتح القدير: ٦/٤۲٦، دار الفكر).

Some explanatory notes about the cap of artificial hair follow.

Fīrūz al-Lughāt states: wig — a cap of artificial hair. (ص ۱٤۱۵) ۔

"Wig" is an English word derived from "periwig", and "periwig" comes from "perruque", which is originally a French word. Both the cap and the wig are in use today, and the difference between the two is that the wig covers the whole head, while the cap only hides the baldness.

As a rule a wig looks like real hair, so that an onlooker cannot even suspect that it is a wig. Wigs come in various colours and in different kinds, and they are made of human hair and of artificial hair such as plastic and nylon, as well as of animal hair; a wig of human hair, however, is a good deal more expensive than one made of other, artificial hair. (المورد القریب، ص ٤۵۲، Cassell's French Dictionary: 536) ۔

The invention of the wig:

Historically the wig was an invention of the Egyptians. To begin with it was used out of necessity: the Egyptians kept their heads shaved, and they took to wearing wigs to protect the head from the heat of the sun. Later on affectation entered into it. In ancient times, besides the Egyptians, the custom also prevailed among other peoples such as the Greeks, the Phoenicians, the Assyrians and the Romans.

After that, by about 1600 CE its use had died out; then in 1624 CE Louis XIII wore a wig to hide his baldness, and from then on wigs became more and more common, until a substantial trade in them grew up and their use reached its height in France and throughout Europe.

Today people also wear them because of a genuine excuse — for instance, in cancer, when the head becomes completely bald, a wig is worn to hide the baldness. But most of those caught up in it are corrupt and immoral men and women, and film actors in particular are deeply involved in it.

Summarised from: Encyclopaedia Britannica: 10/668; Oxford Dictionary: 48/1885; World Book Encyclopedia: 21/251 and 4/549.

In short, where there is no need such things should be avoided altogether, especially as in this age they are the way of film actors and of corrupt, immoral people. A Muslim, precisely because he is a Muslim, must keep within the limits set by Islam and adopt the manner of the righteous. Piety, after all, is the finest garment, and there is no wisdom in following Western culture step for step.
