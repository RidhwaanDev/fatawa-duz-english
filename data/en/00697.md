### TITLE
Forgetting to join a sūrah to al-Fātiḥah and the prostration of forgetfulness

### QUESTION
If a person forgets to join a sūrah and goes down into bowing, must he return to the standing position, or should he complete the bowing and perform the prostration of forgetfulness at the end?

### ANSWER
In the case described he should return, recite the sūrah, and then perform the bowing again. But if he did not return and performed the prostration of forgetfulness at the end, the prayer is valid. Consider what Ḥāshiyat al-Ṭaḥṭāwī states:

ولوترك السورة فتذكر ما في الركوع أو بعد الرفع منه قبل السجود فأنه يعود ويقرأ السورة ويعيد الركوع، و عليه السهو لأنه بقراءة السورة وقعت فرضاً فيرتفض الركوع حتى لو لم يعده فسدت صلاته. (حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح: ٤٦۰، باب سجود السهو. وكذا في الشامي: ۲/۸٥، باب سجود السهو. وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۲٦)

Marāqī al-Falāḥ states:

وإن عاد الساهي عن القعود الأول إليه بعد ما استتم قائماً اختلف التصحيح في فساد صلاته وأرجحهما عدم الفساد لأن غاية ما في الركوع إلى القعدة زيادة قيام في الصلاة وهو وإن كان لا يحل لكنه بالصحة لا يخل

وقال صاحب البحر والحق عدم الفساد. (مراقي الفلاح: ۱۷۹، باب سجود السهو، مكة المكرمة)

This passage shows that if a man leaves out the sitting and goes up into the standing position and then returns to it, his prayer is not invalidated — even though the jurists have forbidden returning from an obligatory element to a necessary one, the prayer is still not invalidated if he does return. In the case before us likewise, the jurists have directed the man to return from the bowing so that he may make good the necessary act he had omitted; and when he does not return, the prayer should all the more not be invalidated, since not returning from the bowing is a lighter matter than a case in which a man returns from standing to the sitting.

In short, in the case described, even if he does not return from the bowing, the prostration of forgetfulness will suffice and the prayer is valid — although some muftis have said that if he does not return the prayer must be repeated. See: احسن الفتاوی ٤/۲۳. But that view is hard to follow.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
