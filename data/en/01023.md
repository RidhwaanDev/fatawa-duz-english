### TITLE
An agent paying the zakat with different money

### QUESTION
A man gave me one thousand rand and appointed me his agent to deliver his zakat to a certain person in India. I sent this sum through a transfer along with other funds, and my representative there paid the zakat out of the other money. Has the zakat been discharged or not?

### ANSWER
As long as the zakat money is still with the agent and he pays the zakat from other funds, it is discharged. But if he has already spent the original zakat money and then pays from other funds, the zakat is not discharged. In the case described the original money was still there, so the zakat has been discharged. Besides, rand are not current in India, so delivering the zakat there means delivering it after converting it; and since converting it is permitted, the zakat has been discharged.

Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ولو تصدق بدراهم نفسه أجزأ إن كان على نية الرجوع وكانت دراهم المؤكل قائمة

وفي الشامي: أي الوكيل بدفع الزكاة إذا أمسك دراهم المؤكل ودفع من ماله ليرجع ببدلها في دراهم المؤكل صح، بخلاف ما إذا أنفقها أولاً على نفسه ثم دفع من ماله فهو متبرع. (الدر المختار مع الشامي: ۲/۲٦۹، سعيد).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

The zakat is discharged in any case. Whether the conversion is permissible, however, depends on permission for it from the one who appointed the agent, given either explicitly or by implication. Present custom allows it, so explicit permission is not required; even so, it is better to obtain explicit permission. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/290).

Bihishtī Zewar states:

If you gave someone two rupees to hand to a poor person, and he did not give those very two rupees to the poor man but gave from his own money instead, the zakat is discharged, provided your money is still with him. But if he spent your money and then gave his own money to the poor man, the zakat is not discharged. (Bihishtī Zewar, the chapter on paying zakat: 245, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
