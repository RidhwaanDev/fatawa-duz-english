### TITLE
Observing the veil in front of an adopted son

### QUESTION
If a woman takes a child, raises him and makes him her adopted son, and he then grows up, will he be like a stranger or like her own child? That is, will it be necessary to observe the veil in front of him, or is there some relaxation in his case?

### ANSWER
Taking a child and bringing him up does not in reality make him like one's own offspring; he remains a stranger, and in all the questions of marriage, inheritance and veiling he has the ruling of a stranger. Thus the Noble Qur'an says:

﴿وما جعل أدعياء كم أبناء كم ذلكم قولكم بأفوهكم﴾ (سورة الأحزاب، الآية: ٤).

Translation: And He has not made your adopted sons your real sons. That is merely something you say with your mouths.

In explaining this noble verse all the commentators and the jurists of the Ummah are agreed that an adopted child does not carry the ruling of real sons and real offspring; rather he is like a stranger. So in the rules of marriage, inheritance, veiling and the like he is treated as a stranger. ʿAllāmah al-Shawkānī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

أي ليس ذلك إلا مجرد قول بالأفواه ولا تأثير له، فلا تصير المرأة به أمّاً ولا ابن الغير به ابناً، ولا يترتب على ذلك شيء من أحكام الأمومة والبنوة. (فتح القدير: ٤/۲٦۱).

This is the position of all the jurists and commentators.

Hazrat Mufti Kifāyatullāh Sahib says in answer to a question put to him:

To adopt a son is in itself correct, but adoption does not establish the rulings of real offspring, nor does the adopted son gain any right of inheritance. (کفایت المفتی: ۵/۲۷٦، ط: دار الاشاعت).

In another place he says:

An adopted son must not be regarded as sharing in the sharia rulings of a real son in marriage, inheritance, veiling and so on. (کفایت المفتی: ۵/۲۷٦، دار الاشاعت).

Yes, if he was suckled in infancy, foster-relationship is established, and then he will not have the ruling of a stranger.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
