### TITLE
The voluntary provident fund

### QUESTION
A man is an employee of a company and his salary there is two thousand rand, but two hundred rand is deducted from it every month, and this deduction is made with his own consent. The company then adds a further one hundred rand to it, and after the employment ends the whole sum is received. Is it permissible to take this money or not?

### ANSWER
A great many scholars have recorded the same ruling for both kinds of provident fund. However, in having the deduction made by one's own choice there is a resemblance to interest, and for that reason one should keep away from it, so that it does not become a means of consuming interest.

Consider what Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

However, if money is deducted into the provident fund by one's own choice, there is in this both a resemblance to interest and the danger of turning it into a means of interest; so it should be avoided. (Prōvident Fanḍ par Zakāt awr Sūd kā Masʾalah, p. 4).

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

If an employee, without compulsion or coercion and of his own will, has some money deducted into the G.P. fund and then receives the principal sum together with interest at the end of his employment, the ruling of the sharia on it is that the principal sum is lawful; but the additional sum that he receives from the government under the name of interest contains a resemblance to interest, and there is also the danger of turning it into a means of consuming interest in the future, so it should be avoided. (Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām: 1/170, citing Prōvident Fanḍ par Zakāt awr Sūd kā Masʾalah).

Fatāwā ʿUthmānī states:

This is the ruling for both the compulsory and the voluntary fund; however, it is better, out of caution, to give away in charity the increase received upon money that was deducted by one's own choice. (Fatāwā ʿUthmānī: 3/308).

Some scholars have declared the additional sum to be interest and hence impermissible. Consider what Āp ke Masāʾil states:

And if the employee has it deducted himself, it is not permissible to take profit upon it; it is interest. (Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥall: 6/225; Imdād al-Aḥkām: 3/472; and Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah: 6/213).
