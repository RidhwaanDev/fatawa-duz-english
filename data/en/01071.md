### TITLE
The ruling on the difference of horizons

### QUESTION
Is the difference of horizons taken into account in the sharia or not? And if it is, what is its limit?

### ANSWER
The difference of horizons ought to be taken into account in the sharia, because among the Hanafi jurists such eminent authorities as ʿAllāmah Zaylaʿī and ʿAllāmah Kāsānī gave it preference, and some of the leading scholars of Deoband also declared it the stronger position. As for the question of its limit, the statement of Hazrat Mufti Muhammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) appears to be clear: wherever the distance between two lands is such that taking the sighting of one place into account in the other would make the month twenty-eight days or thirty-one days long, there the difference of horizons will be taken into account; and where this is not so, it will not be. There is a further difficulty in disregarding the difference of horizons: if the moon is sighted in South Africa, the time of Fajr will already be near in Australia, and in some countries Fajr will already have come in. Will they then wait for the moon until Fajr, or break the fast after having begun it? Unity of sighting between Pakistan, India and the Arab countries is both possible and easy; indeed, if South Africa were taken as the standard — where, lying to the south-west, the likelihood of a sighting is greater and the system of sighting is also well established — then the question of the moon could be settled for Africa, Europe, and from India and Pakistan westwards.

Consider what ʿAllāmah Kāsānī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ:

هذا إذا كانت المسافة بين البلدين قريبة لا تختلف فيها المطالع، فأما إذا كانت بعيدة فلا يلزم أحد البلدين حكم الآخر لأن مطالع البلاد عند المسافة الفاحشة تختلف فيعتبر في أهل كل بلد مطالع بلدهم دون البلد الآخر. (بدائع الصنائع: ۲/۸۳، سعيد).

Al-Fiqh al-Ḥanafī wa Adillatuhu states:

إذا كان بين القطرين قريب بحيث تتحد المطالع فلا يعتبر، وإن كانت بعيدة بحيث تختلف المطالع فيعتبر فلو صام أهل قطر ثلاثين يوماً برؤية، وأهل قطر آخر تسعة وعشرين يوماً برؤية، فعليهم قضاء يوم إن كان بين القطرين قرب بحيث تتحد المطالع، وإن كانت بعيدة بحيث تختلف، لا يلزم أحد القطرين حكم الآخر، فالقرب مثل سورية، والعراق، والبعد مثل الكويت والمغرب، وجاء عن ابن عباس وعائشة رضي الله عنه ما صوم كل جماعة يوم يصومون، وفطرهم يوم يفطرون. (الفقه الحنفي وأدلته: ۱/۳۸۹، قبول خبر الواحد في روية هلال رمضان، دمشق).

ʿAllāmah Zaylaʿī says:

والأشبه أن يعتبر لأن كل قوم مخاطبون بما عندهم وانفصال الهلال عن شعاع الشمس تختلف باختلاف الأقطار، كما أن دخول الوقت وخروجه تختلف باختلاف الأقطار حتى إذا زالت الشمس في المشرق لا يلزم منه أن تزول في المغرب، وكذا طلوع الفجر وغروب الشمس بل كلما تحركت الشمس درجة فتلك طلوع فجر لقوم وطلوع شمس للآخرين وغروب لبعض ونصف ليل لغيرهم. (تبيين الحقائق: ۱/۳۲۱، إمدادية، ملتان)

Fatāwā Bayyināt states:

The question of the difference of horizons has been a matter of disagreement among the earlier and the later scholars, and the jurists hold three positions on it.

The first position, which is related from the Greatest Imam and is called the standard narration, is that no account at all is taken of the difference of horizons. (But there are great difficulties in applying this to the whole world.)

The second position is that the difference of horizons is to be taken into account everywhere and in every case.

The third position is that no account is taken of the difference of horizons between nearby lands, but it is taken into account between distant lands. (The basis of this should be common usage; and in most cases there is a difference of horizons between the northern and the southern lands: the moon is seen in the south and is not seen in the north.) ʿAllāmah Zaylaʿī and the author of al-Badāʾiʿ gave preference to this position. (فتاوی بینات: ۳/۵۸، اختلاف مطالع کا حکم، مکتبہ بینات کراچی)

Hazrat Mufti Muhammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Rūʾyat-e Hilāl:

Our respected teacher Hazrat Sayyid Muhammad Anwar Shah Kashmiri (may Allah have mercy on him) also held this position to be the stronger one, and our respected teacher Hazrat Mawlānā Shabbīr Ahmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him), in Fatḥ al-Mulhim, his commentary on Muslim, drew attention to a consideration which, once it is looked at, makes the strength of this position plain — particularly in this age, when the distances between east and west are covered in a matter of hours. It is this: it is established by explicit and decisive texts of the Qur'an and the Sunnah that no month is less than twenty-nine days or more than thirty. Now if the difference of horizons is disregarded in the case of distant lands and of the distances between east and west, it will follow, contrary to this decisive text, that in some city, on the twenty-eighth day, testimony may arrive from a far-off land that the moon has been seen there that day; and if that city is made subordinate to the other, its month will be left at twenty-eight days, which is less, or more, than the reckoning fixed by the sharia, and that is not correct. This investigation by ʿAllāmah ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) also settles the question of what the standard of nearness and distance is between nearby and distant lands, and how much distance is meant. It is this: wherever the distance between lands is such that taking the sighting of one place into account in the other would leave the month at twenty-eight days or make it thirty-one, there the difference of horizons will be taken into account; and where the distance is not so great, it will be disregarded. (ملخص از رؤیتِ ہلال: ص ۵۸ ۔ ۵۹، دار المعارف، کراچی) ۔

Mufti ʿAbd al-Munʿim says:

Among the senior scholars of Deoband, Mawlānā Anwar Shah Kashmiri (may Allah have mercy on him), Mawlānā Shabbīr Ahmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him), Mawlānā Mufti Muhammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him), Mawlānā Yūsuf Binnorī (may Allah have mercy on him) and others hold the difference of horizons to be valid, and Mufti Muhammad Farīd too gives preference to this position. (اعدل الاقوال فی مسئلۃ الہلال: ص ۷۲) ۔
