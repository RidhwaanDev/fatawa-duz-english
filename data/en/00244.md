### TITLE
The obligatory bath with caps or wires fitted on the teeth

### QUESTION
These days people have caps put on their teeth for some sound reason, or have their teeth bound with wires. What is the ruling in the obligatory bath, given that removing them causes difficulty?

### ANSWER
Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

Question: some people's teeth become loose and some people's teeth fall out altogether, after which they have a gold cap fitted. Now, when the need for the ritual bath arises, is it necessary to remove this cap at the time of the bath or not? These caps are usually very firmly fixed; they cannot be removed without a dentist and it is very difficult to do so. Can this be compared with dirt under the nails and with dough, or not? Dough is easy to remove, whereas this belongs to the category of imposing what cannot be borne.

Answer: fitting such a cap comes under necessity, and removing it causes hardship. وهو مدفوع شرعا Therefore the bath will be valid without removing it.

ونظائرها مشهورة في كتب القوم مسطورة بل نصوا على جواز اتخاذ الأسنان من الذهب وشدها به ولو كان مانعا عن صحة الغسل لما افتوا به. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā 2/32)

If a person has a cap or the like fitted purely for adornment, will his ablution and ritual bath be valid or not, given that, according to the statements of the jurists, if dough dries on the body or greasy dirt lies under the nail the bath is not valid? The answer to this is given in Kifāyat al-Muftī as follows.

Fitting a gold cap because of some defect in the teeth is not impermissible, and fitting one merely for adornment is disliked. But whether it was fitted out of necessity or without necessity, in either case it does not prevent ablution or the ritual bath, because it has the status of an inseparable part of the body — unlike dough and greasy dirt, which are not an inseparable part. (Kifāyat al-Muftī ۲/۳۱۳، بحواله شامي) الأصل وجوب الغسل إلا أنه سقط للحرج: ۱/ ۱۵۲).

Fatāwā Farīdiyyah states:

Question: if someone has had a gold cap put on his teeth, whether for adornment or because of some illness, does it now prevent the ritual bath or not? And what is the ruling of the sharia on having such a cap fitted?

Answer: if removing this gold cap — permissible or impermissible as it may be — for every bath and putting it back after the bath would cause hardship, then it does not prevent the bath.
