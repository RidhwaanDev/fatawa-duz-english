### TITLE
Collecting the school fees of children at an Islamic school from zakat money

### QUESTION
What do the scholars say about the following? Some people who feel a concern for religion wish to open an Islamic school. Children of both poor and wealthy families will study there, and to meet the school's expenses fees will be collected from the children. Can zakat be given as the fee money of the poor children? Is it necessary to state that the money is zakat? Is transfer of ownership necessary in zakat? Is there any difference in the sharia between children who have reached puberty and those who have not when it comes to receiving zakat? And if someone has a television or a VCR in his home whose value reaches the nisāb (the threshold at which zakat becomes due), is he counted as wealthy or not?

### ANSWER
In the sharia, zakat money must be given as an outright transfer of ownership to those poor and needy people who do not own the nisāb. The nisāb is 52.5 tolas of silver, or 7.5 tolas of gold, or that value in money, or goods surplus to one's needs. If the children who come to the school have reached puberty and do not themselves own the nisāb, zakat may be given to them, and there is no harm in their fathers being wealthy. It is also not necessary to state that the money is zakat; zakat may be given under the name of an Eid gift, a present, a stipend and so on. Transfer of ownership to the poor person is, however, necessary in zakat.

If the children who come to the school have not reached puberty and their fathers are needy and do not own the nisāb, then zakat money may also be given to such children, under whatever heading it may be. But children who have not reached puberty and whose fathers own the nisāb may not, in the sharia, be given zakat. Children who are entitled to zakat may be given it as an outright transfer of ownership, and this same money may then be collected back from them as fees. If someone has a television or a VCR at home whose value reaches the nisāb, he is not entitled to zakat, because both these things are surplus to need and their value amounts to the nisāb. If, however, the television and VCR do not belong to the children's father but to their mother, then the father will be regarded as needy, and those of his children who have not reached puberty but understand how to take possession of zakat will be entitled to it.

The evidence for this ruling may be seen in the previous answer.
