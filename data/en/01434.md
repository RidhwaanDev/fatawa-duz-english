### TITLE
Hajj and Umrah performed by children who have not reached puberty

### QUESTION
What is the ruling on Hajj or Umrah performed by children who have not reached puberty? Also, is sewn clothing forbidden in iḥrām, or may they be dressed in sewn clothes because of severe cold? Will the children's guardians make the intention for the Umrah, or will the children make it themselves? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
Having reached puberty is a condition for the Hajj becoming obligatory and for it to count as the obligatory Hajj; it is not a condition for the Hajj being valid and sound. So the Hajj is not obligatory upon a child who has not reached puberty, whether that child is discerning — that is, able to distinguish between pure and impure and between sweet and bitter, and aware that Islam is the means of salvation — or unable to distinguish. Therefore, if a discerning child performs the Hajj himself, or if the guardian of a child without discernment enters iḥrām on his behalf and the boy performs the Hajj, his Hajj will be voluntary; the obligatory Hajj will not be discharged, because he is not legally responsible for the obligatory Hajj. In other words, if a child performs Hajj before puberty it will not be the Hajj of Islam, that is, the obligatory Hajj, but a voluntary Hajj. His guardian should keep him from committing the things forbidden in iḥrām, such as wearing sewn clothing and applying perfume; but if the child does commit something forbidden in iḥrām, no compensation is due, neither from the child nor from his guardian. (عمدۃ الفقہ: ٤/۳۰) ۔

Muʿallim al-Ḥujjāj states:

Ruling: If the child is alert and discerning, he should enter iḥrām himself and perform the rites of Hajj, doing everything just as an adult does. If he is a small child without discernment, his guardian should enter iḥrām on his behalf.

Ruling: If a small child without discernment performs the rites himself, or enters iḥrām himself, those rites and that iḥrām will not be valid. But if a discerning child enters iḥrām himself and performs the rites himself, they will be valid.

Ruling: A guardian cannot enter iḥrām on behalf of a discerning child.

Ruling: A discerning child should himself perform whatever rites he is able to perform, and if he cannot perform them himself his guardian should perform them for him. The prayer of ṭawāf, however, the child must pray himself, and the guardian must not pray it on his behalf; and even if the child cannot pray the prayer of ṭawāf himself, the guardian should still not pray it for him: "لا يصلي أحد عن أحد ولا يصوم أحد عن أحد".

Ruling: A discerning child should perform the ṭawāf himself, while a child without discernment should be carried in his guardian's arms as the ṭawāf is performed for him. The same ruling applies to the standing at ʿArafāt, the saʿy, the stoning and the rest.

Ruling: The guardian should keep the child away from the things forbidden in iḥrām; but if the child does perform a forbidden act, no compensation for it will be due, neither from the child nor from the guardian.

Ruling: When iḥrām is entered into on a child's behalf, the sewn clothes should be taken off his body and he should be dressed in the two unsewn sheets.

Ruling: Hajj is not obligatory upon a child, so this Hajj will be voluntary.

Ruling: A child's iḥrām is not binding upon him. If he leaves out all the rites, or some of them, no compensation and no making up will be due from him.

In the light of the passages and rulings quoted above, answers to almost all of the questions have now been given. As for dressing a child in sewn clothes, warm unsewn garments of every kind are available for severe cold in place of sewn clothing, and the child should be dressed in those; even so, no compensation will be due for wearing sewn clothes.
