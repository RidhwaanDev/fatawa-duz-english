### TITLE
Attending a women's gathering (baby shower)

### QUESTION
I have received an invitation to attend a baby shower. A baby shower is a special gathering of women held for a pregnant woman of the family, attended by her close female relatives and friends. At this gathering the women present gifts to the pregnant woman for her child, and these gifts are opened in front of all the women, so that it becomes known who gave what gift. Now and then some games are also played for amusement and entertainment; for example, the pregnant woman is asked to guess what is inside before the gift is opened, and if her guess is correct, the woman who gave the gift is made to do or say something, and otherwise the pregnant woman is made to do or say something. After the gifts have been opened they all eat together and converse.

It should be borne in mind that the purpose of opening the gifts in front of everyone is not to belittle anyone, but rather mutual amusement.

Although this gathering and other functions like it are an invention of Western culture and a hallmark of non-Muslims, they have become very common in our Muslim society — indeed they have become a part of society, and avoiding them is very difficult.

What is now sought is the Sharīʿah ruling on attending such a function and gathering. Another point worth mentioning is that this invitation comes from a close relative, and in declining it there is the fear of their displeasure, which could lead to the severing of mutual ties. بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
The functions and customs described above involve the following harms and evils:

(1) There is resemblance to the ways and practices of sinful and disbelieving women.

(2) The exchange of gifts is regarded as necessary; if a woman goes empty-handed it will be counted as a fault in her.

(3) When the gifts are not equal there is resentment in the heart: if one woman gave a larger gift and then received a smaller one, there will be ill feeling in her heart.

(4) Muslim women ought to devote their capacities to good works and to avoid such futilities.

(5) In jesting and joking the limits will be exceeded, which is improper, and backbiting too will most often take place.

(6) A woman's pregnancy is not something to be put on display, that a gathering should be held for it.

(7) At times encouragement is given to an act that amounts to wastefulness — for instance, "eat the whole cake", when eating it is difficult — whereas the hadiths encourage eating little.

(8) If this becomes customary in religious households, other people will go far beyond all limits.

(9) It involves the waste of time.

(10) It involves pride, boasting, ostentation and the like.

(11) To find out by means of an electrical device (an X-ray machine) whether the child is male or female and to inform the guests of it so that they may present gifts and presents accordingly is not correct at all; it should be avoided completely. Moreover, according to the research of physicians, the use of this machine is extremely harmful for both the child and the mother.

In summary, on account of the harms mentioned above it is necessary to avoid gatherings of this kind. If there is fear of the severing of ties, then after attending one should discourage it, raise matters of faith in the gathering, and strive to reform it through daʿwah and tablīgh.
