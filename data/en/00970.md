### TITLE
Zakat on a house worth ten lakh that is rented out

### QUESTION
A man has rented out his house, which is worth 10 lakh, and this is his only source of income. The rent brings in 24,000 rand a year, and every year he pays 25,000 in zakat on the house (2.5% of 10 lakh). What we wish to know is this: in the case described, is there any lawful means by which this zakat may be avoided?

### ANSWER
A house held for trade is subject to zakat on its value; a house held for renting out is not subject to zakat on its value. However, if the rent, together with other money, reaches the nisab and a year passes over it, then zakat is due on it; otherwise not.

Al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

ولو آجر عبد ه أو داره بنصاب إن لم يكونا للتجارة لا تجب ما لم يحل الحول بعد القبض في قول أبي حنيفة رحمه الله.

(البحر الرائق: ۲/۲۰۸، كويٹه).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ولا زكاة على مكاتب

ولا ثياب البدن وأثاث المنزل ودور السكني ونحوها وكذا الكتب. قال الشامي: ونحوها أي كثياب البدن الغير المحتاج إليها وكالحوانيت والعقارات. (الدر مع الشامي: ۲/۲۶۴، ۲۶۵، سعيد).

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states:

Zakat is not due on the value of the property. It is due on the rental income, once that reaches the amount of the nisab and a full year passes over it, either on its own or together with other money in hand. (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband: 6/133).

Āp ke Masāʾil states:

I own two houses. I live in one of them myself and the other is rented out. Is the zakat due on the value of the house or on its rent?

Answer: In this case zakat is not due on the value of the house. It is due on its rent, once that reaches the nisab. (Āp ke Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal: 3/371).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

There is no zakat in any case on a house or on its value, whether it is lived in or not (since it is not wealth that grows). However, if houses are traded in, then, being trade goods, zakat is due on their value.

(2) Zakat is not on the value of the house but on its income. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 4/263, 264).

Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil states:

If a person owns a spare house or shop that he has rented out, or has hired out a vehicle, a machine or the like, zakat is not due on their value. It is due on their income once a year has passed over it. And if he already holds money or silver and the like to the amount of the nisab, then a year need not pass over the income of these things; rather, the income is added to the money already held and zakat must be paid on the total. Majmaʿ al-Anhur: 1/22; Imdād al-Fatāwā: 2/41. (Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil: p. 105, Nuʿaymiyyah).

Kitāb al-Fatāwā states:

Zakat becomes due on a house when the house has been acquired for the purpose of trade. If the house is surplus to one's needs but the aim is not trade — rather to rent it out or to put it to some other use — then no zakat is due on it. (Kitāb al-Fatāwā: part three, p. 271).

See also: Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 9/425, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah; and Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/205, "The difference between trade and renting".

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
