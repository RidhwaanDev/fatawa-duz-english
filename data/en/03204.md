### TITLE
The ruling on declaring Christianity and Judaism to be another road to Allah

### QUESTION
In America a certain mawlwī ṣāḥib said that all these non-Muslims are our brothers and that our religion is one. What the mawlwī ṣāḥib means is that Christianity, Judaism and Islam are all different roads leading to Allah Most High. This statement of the mawlwī ṣāḥib has caused many Muslims to fall into doubts and misgivings. What does the Sharīʿah say about this? Please shed some light on it. بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
Islam is a religion that teaches firmness upon its own creed together with tolerant conduct towards other religions. The Prophet ﷺ visited a Jewish boy when he fell ill, gave gifts to non-Muslims, and dealt with them with honour and respect; and the excellent treatment he ﷺ accorded to the captives of the Battle of Badr has been mentioned by all the writers of sīrah. Therefore one should help non-Muslim brethren, and there is reward and merit in this too. But the meaning of this tolerance extends only so far as this: that in human character and dealings one should show broad-mindedness towards the followers of other religions, that one should avoid interfering in their religious affairs, and that one should not revile their gods, goddesses and holy figures. Islam does not, however, permit Muslims to adopt a "give and take" attitude in the matter of creed and religion, because the foundation of all the rulings of Islam is tawḥīd, and in this there is not the slightest room for concession, nor is it permissible in any way whatsoever to welcome a polytheistic belief. (Kitāb al-Fatāwā: 1/308.)

The noble Qur'an declares that the true religion is Islam alone and that all the rest are false: "إن الدين عند الله الإسلام". (Āl ʿImrān: 19.) According to what the mawlwī ṣāḥib says, Judaism, Christianity and Islam are all three true religions, and this is contrary to the Qur'anic declaration.

قال الله تعالى: ﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا تتخذوا بطانة من دونكم لا يألونكم خبالاً ودوا ما عنتم قد بدت البغضاء من أفواههم وما تخفي صدورهم أكبر، قد بينا لكم الآيات إن كنتم تعقلون﴾ [آل عمران: ۱۱۸].

O you who believe: what faith requires is that you should not take those outside your own circle as confidants and intimates — that is, do not make non-Muslims your close companions, friends and confidants. There are several reasons for this prohibition. First: these people leave no stone unturned in stirring up mischief and in ruining you. Second: at every moment they wish that you should fall into some hardship or harm; they constantly desire misfortune for you. Third: their hearts are brimming with enmity towards you, so much so that at times the rancour of their hearts and their inward hostility escapes from their mouths involuntarily. Fourth: the rancour and enmity that their breasts conceal within them is far greater than the hostility that becomes apparent from them; if you have any sense you will understand that they are not your well-wishers but rather bitter enemies. Fifth: why are you so foolish and heedless that you hold them dear while they do not hold you dear? The sixth reason is that you believe in all the books of Allah, whereas their condition is that, out of obstinacy towards you, they do not believe in your Book despite its truth having become manifest. They ought to have loved you, since you believe in all the books of Allah and do not deny their Book; but the matter has turned out the other way round. Etc. (Maʿārif al-Qurʾān: 2/39, by Mawlānā Idrīs Ṣāḥib Kāndhlawī, may Allah have mercy on him, Maktabat al-Maʿārif.)

In another place the command of the Lord is:

﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا تتخذوا اليهود والنصارى أولياء بعضهم أولياء بعض ومن يتولهم منكم فإنه منهم إن الله لا يهدي القوم الظالمين﴾ [المائدة: ۵۱].

In Maʿārif al-Qurʾān it says:

In these verses the Muslims are commanded not to love them, for whoever loves them will be counted among them, since love and companionship have a powerful effect upon a person's heart. It is the effect of love that everything belonging to the beloved becomes beloved in the eyes of the lover. Like leprosy and cancer, unbelief and polytheism are a spiritual disease: through intimacy of the heart its germs are transmitted. Through heartfelt love for, and mixing with, those who rebel against Allah Most High, a person gradually adopts their very religion. For the present he may not inwardly be one of them, but through loving them and keeping their company there is the danger that in the future he may become one of them — that is, that he may apostatise from Islam. Just as, after becoming the subject of a government, to cavil at and criticise the law of that government is rebellion against the government, so too, after entering Islam, to cavil at and criticise the law of the Sharīʿah is apostasy from Islam; and attachment of the heart to the unbelievers is a sign of apostasy and a prelude to it. And if they conceal this and display Islam outwardly, that is hypocrisy. When a person keeps up social relations and friendly ties with the enemies and rebels against a government, his loyalty becomes suspect in the eyes of that government. (Maʿārif al-Qurʾān: 2/8, 517, Kāndhlawī.)

قال عزوجل: ﴿ولن ترضي عنك اليهود ولا النصارى حتى تتبع ملتهم قل إن هدي الله هو الهدي ولئن اتبعت أهواء هم بعد الذي جاء ك من العلم ما لك من الله من ولي ولا نصير﴾ [البقرة: ۱۲۰].

In this verse, while addressing the Prophet ﷺ, the general body of Muslims is commanded thus: give up all concern for pleasing these Jews and Christians, for until you too, like them, fall into doubt about the religion of Allah and go astray, the Jews and Christians can never be pleased with you. So leave them to their own condition and walk upon the path of guidance that Allah Most High has made clear to you. Otherwise remember that, having angered Allah, you will not be able to remain safe from His wrath by taking the support of any other protector or helper.

In Kamālāt-e Ashrafiyyah it says:

Being pleased with unbelief is itself unbelief. He said that being pleased with unbelief is unbelief, whether it be pleasure at one's own unbelief or at the unbelief of another. That is, if a person does not approve of unbelief for himself but is pleased with the unbelief of another, then this one who is pleased will immediately become an unbeliever. (Kamālāt-e Ashrafiyyah, p. 46, quoting Ashraf al-Aḥkām, p. 47.)

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم

The answer to the doubt about seeking aid from other than Allah that arises from some commentaries' explanation of fa'l-mudabbirāti amran:

Question: In some commentaries, such as Tafsīr al-Bayḍāwī (5/282) and Tafsīr Maẓharī (10/186) and others, fa'l-mudabbirāti amran has been explained as referring to the excellent souls, from which the permissibility of seeking aid from other than Allah appears to follow. Note the passage:

فالمدبرات أمراً، بصفاء النفوس الفاضلة حال المفارقة فإنها تنزع عن الأبدان غرقاً أي نزعاً شديداً من إغراق النازع في القوس، وتنشط إلى عالم الملكوت وتسبح فيها فتسبق إلى حظائر القدس فتصير لشرفها وقوتها من المدبرات أمراً. (تفسير البيضاوي: ۵ / ۲۸۲، وتفسير مظهري: ۱۰/۱۸٦).

What is the answer to this?

The answer: In the case asked about, ʿAllāmah Ālūsī (may Allah have mercy on him), after mentioning this interpretation, said that it is related from Ismāʿīl ibn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Suddī al-Kūfī, and that he stands accused of being a Shīʿī. ʿAllāmah Ālūsī (may Allah have mercy on him) further says that adopting this interpretation gives rise to the illusion that a corrupt belief is sound — namely that of the feeble-minded people who suppose that the friends of Allah and the pious elders exercise control over affairs after their passing, such as granting health to the sick, saving a drowning man, giving help against enemies and so forth; and moreover they hold the belief that Allah Most High has entrusted these matters to them. All of this is talk born of ignorance. Note the passage:

قال: وكذا في حملها على النفوس الفاضلة المفارقة إيهام صحة ما يزعمه كثير من سخفة العقول من أن الأولياء يتصرفون بعد وفاتهم بنحو شفاء المريض وإنقاذ الغريق والنصر على الأعداء وغير ذلك مما يكون في عالم الكون والفساد على معني أن الله تعالى فوض إليهم ذلك ومنهم من خص ذلك بخمسة من الأولياء والكل جهل وإن كان الثاني أشد جهلاً. (روح المعاني: ۳۰/۲۵). (ميزان الاعتدال: ۱/۲۳٦، وتقريب التهذيب، ۳٤).

The correct belief of the Ahl al-Sunnah wa'l-Jamāʿah is that the One who exercises control over affairs and directs them is the Being of Allah Most High alone; no one else has any share in this work.

قال الله تعالى: ﴿قل من يرزقكم من السماء والأرض أمن يملك السمع والأبصار ومن يخرج الحي من الميت ويخرج الميت من الحي ومن يدبر الأمر فسيقولون الله، فقل أفلا تتقون﴾ [يونس: ۳۱].

Ḥaḍrat Shāh Walī Allāh Ṣāḥib said:

لا شريك له في وجوب الوجود ولا في استحقاق العبادة ولا في الخلق والتدبير فلا يستحق العبادة أي أقصي غاية التعظيم إلا هو ولا يشفي مريضاً ولا يرزق رزقاً ولا يكشف ضراً إلا هو بمعني أن يقول لشيء كن فيكون لا بمعني التسبب العادي الظاهري كما يقال شفي الطبيب المريض ورزق الأمير الجند فهذا غيره وإن اشتبه في اللفظ. (التفهيمات الإلهية: ۱/۱٤٤).

The jurists too have written likewise, that to regard a deceased person as one who exercises control over affairs is a belief amounting to unbelief. Note the passage:

ومنها إن ظن أن الميت يتصرف في الأمور دون الله تعالى واعتقاده ذلك كفر. (البحر الرائق: ۲/۳۲۱، وفتاوى الشامي: ۲/٤۳۹، ط: سعيد، وحاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح، ص ٦۹۳).

Besides this, numerous texts of the noble Qur'an and of the hadith bear witness that the One who directs affairs and exercises control over them is solely and exclusively the Being of Allah Most High.
