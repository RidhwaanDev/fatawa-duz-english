### TITLE
Using the words qiblah and Kaʿbah as titles of honour for one's father

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to use the words qiblah and Kaʿbah for one's father in a letter or not? What difference is there in the statements of the senior scholars (may Allah have mercy on them) on this matter, and which view is the stronger? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
In the case in question it is permissible and correct to write qiblah and Kaʿbah for one's father in a letter, though it is contrary to what is preferable. The ground of permissibility is that it comes in a hadith that the Prophet ﷺ said — the sense of which is — that the rank of a believer is higher than the rank of the House of Allah.

Tadhkirat al-Rashīd states that to address one's elders with these titles is strictly disliked and unlawful, while Imdād al-Fatāwā states that when they are taken in a figurative sense they are permissible, though contrary to what is preferable.

Outwardly the view of Imdād al-Fatāwā appears the stronger, because the view that it is strictly disliked and unlawful is very severe; and since, according to the purport of the hadith, the dignity of a believer is certainly greater than that of the House of Allah, it is more fitting to call it contrary to what is preferable.

Consider what is stated in Sunan Ibn Mājah:

عن عبد الله بن عمرو رضي الله عنه قال: رأيت رسول الله ﷺ يطوف بالكعبة ويقول: "ما أطيبك وأطيب ريحك، ما أعظمك وأعظم حرمتك، والذي نفس محمد بيده لحرمة المؤمن أعظم عند الله حرمة منك، ماله ودمه وأن نظن به إلا خيراً. وفي الزوائد: في إسناده مقال، ونصر بن محمد شيخ ابن ماجه ضعفه أبو حاتم وذكره ابن حبان في الثقات. (رواه ابن ماجه، رقم الحديث: ۳۹۳۲).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

Question: In Bihishtī Zēwar the words qiblah and Kaʿbah are given among the titles for elders, whereas Tadhkirat al-Rashīd states that they are strictly disliked and unlawful, on the evidence of his saying (peace be upon him) "لا تطرونی"، (الحدیث). What is the way of reconciling the two?

Answer: Without any figurative interpretation it is strictly disliked and unlawful; taken in a figurative sense it is permissible, although contrary to what is preferable. (امداد الفتاوی: ۴/۲۷۴)۔

Consider the wording of Tadhkirat al-Rashīd:

Question: Is it permissible or not to write, as titles and forms of respect for one's father, or paternal uncle, or brother, or anyone else, expressions such as qiblah and Kaʿbah, qiblah of the two abodes, Kaʿbah of the two worlds, or religious qiblah and worldly Kaʿbah, or qiblah of wealth and needs, or qiblah of desires, or outward qiblah and inward Kaʿbah, or other expressions like these? Is it unlawful, or permitted? And if it is disliked, is it strictly disliked and unlawful, or only mildly disliked?

Answer: To utter or to write such words of praise about anyone is strictly disliked and unlawful, because of his saying (peace be upon him): "لا تطروني"، (الحدیث). When words of praise going beyond the dignity of prophethood are forbidden in his own case, how can they be correct in the case of anyone else? (تذکرۃ الرشید، ص ۱۳۷، ادارۂ اسلامیات، و فتاوی رشیدیہ، ص ٦۰٤، مکتبہ رحمانیہ)۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
