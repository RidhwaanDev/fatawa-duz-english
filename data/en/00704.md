### TITLE
Hearing a verse of prostration on the radio

### QUESTION
If someone hears a verse of prostration on the radio, is the prostration necessary or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, if the recitation had been recorded on some device — a cassette, a CD, a tape recorder and so on — and that recording is being broadcast on the radio, then the prostration is not necessary for the listener. But if a reciter is actually reciting and he recites a verse of prostration, the prostration of recitation is necessary for the listener. In short, the prostration of recitation becomes necessary through the recitation itself; it does not become necessary through a copy or a reproduction of it. Consider what Ḥāshiyat al-Ṭaḥṭāwī ʿalā Marāqī al-Falāḥ states:

ولا تجب بسماعها من الصدى وهو ما يجيبك مثل صوتك في الجبال والصحاري ونحوها، الأولي قول بعضهم الصوت الذي يسمعه المصوت عقب صياحه راجعاً إليه من جبل أو بناء مرتفع فإنه لا إجابة في الصدى إنما هو محاكاة. (حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح: ٤۸٦، باب سجود التلاوة، قديمي)

Niẓām al-Fatāwā states:

With a tape recorder, a gramophone and the like, the speaker's own voice does not reach the listener; what reaches him is a copy of the speaker's voice, as happens with an echo. So the prostration of recitation will not become necessary on account of a recitation heard from such a device.

On the radio, the talk and the voice of the speaker are usually recorded first and then broadcast. So if it is more likely than not that this is what has happened, performing the prostration of recitation on hearing that voice will not be required.

Yes: when the speaker is speaking himself, without these devices, and he recites a verse of prostration, then the prostration of recitation becomes necessary on his reciting that verse. On the radio, the difference between the speaker's own voice and a recorded voice can be established with evidence from the circumstances in which it is used, and one should act accordingly. (منتخب نظام الفتاوی: ۱/۷۲، اسلامک فقہ اکیڈمی)
