### TITLE
The ruling on saying Jazāk Allāhu khayran to a disbeliever

### QUESTION
Is it correct to say Jazāk Allāhu khayran to a disbeliever, or to say Mā shāʾ Allāh on seeing something good belonging to him, or not?

### ANSWER
The basis for uttering words such as Jazāk Allāhu khayran is, in reality, the intention. If a person intends by it that Allah Most High should grant him guidance, and takes "good" to mean guidance, then there is room for it. Likewise, Mā shāʾ Allāh is used to express wonder, so there is no harm in saying Mā shāʾ Allāh on seeing something good belonging to a disbeliever.

قال في الدر المختار: لو قال لذمي "أطال الله بقاء ك" إن نوى بقلبه لعله يسلم أو يؤدي الجزية ذليلاً فلا بأس به. (الدر المختار: ٦/٤۱۳، سعيد). (وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳٤۸، وتحفة الملوك، ص ۱۹۱، رقم المسألة: ۳٤۱، والأشباه والنظائر: ۱/۳۹).
