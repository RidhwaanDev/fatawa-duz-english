### TITLE
Imposing compensation on a company's employees for their negligence

### QUESTION
There is a company which manufactures curtains and related goods. The employees of this company manufacture these articles. Many a time the employees are negligent and careless, on account of which the goods are not manufactured properly and complaints are received from the customers. The company has warned the employees several times, but they do not desist from their negligence. Now the company wishes to impose a penalty upon the employees. Is there permission for this in the Sacred Law or not?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, the company's employees have the status of a private hired worker (ajīr khāṣṣ), and the ruling regarding a private hired worker is that if something is destroyed without transgression on his part, compensation will not be due; but if he himself destroys it, and transgression is found on their part, then compensation will be due. Accordingly, since the employees do not desist from their negligence despite the company manager's telling them repeatedly, imposing compensation and a penalty upon them is sound and correct. See Fatāwā Shāmī, in which it is transmitted:

قوله ولا يضمن ما هلك في يده، أي بغير صنعه بالإجماع وقوله أو بعمله أي المأذون فيه فإن أمره بعمل فيعمل غيره ضمن ما تولد منه. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/۷۰، سعيد).
قال في النتف في الفتاوى: فأما الأجير الخاص فهو الذي يعمل لك وحدك دون غيرك ويقال له أجير الوحد وهو لا يضمن ما يهلك على يديه إلا في ثلاثة أوجه: إذا خالف أو تعدي أو تعمد. (النتف في الفتاوى: ۲/۵٦۱، ط: لبنان).
قال في مجمع الضمانات: ولا ضمان على الأجير الخاص ولا فيما تلف من عمله اتفاقاً إذا لم يتعمد الفساد ذكره في الإصلاح نقلاً عن الخانية قال في الفصولين نقلاً عن التجريد الأجير الخاص: لا يضمن إلا بالتعدي. (مجمع الضمانات: ۱/۱۰۳).

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām records:

If something is destroyed at the hands of a private hired worker without transgression, compensation will not be due upon him; and if he himself destroys it, liability will be due. (Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām, volume one, p. 198).

For more, see: (Sharḥ-i Majallah: 2/718; ʿĀlamgīrī: 4/500; Muḥīṭ Burhānī: 12/31; and Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 5/632).
