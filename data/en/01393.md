### TITLE
Whether bleeding breaks the continuity of the expiatory fasts

### QUESTION
A woman deliberately broke a fast in the month of Ramadan, so expiation became due from her. She began keeping the sixty consecutive fasts, and then bleeding came at the time of her usual habit, so on the third day she did not fast — yet on that third day no bleeding came at all. The question is: has the continuity of her expiation been broken or not?

### ANSWER
ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

"وإذا أفطرت علي ظن أنه يوم حيضها فلم تحض الأظهر وجوب الكفارة". (رد المحتار: ۲/٤۱۳، سعيد)

The converse of this passage shows that if the woman did bleed, and to all appearances it was menstrual blood, and only later did it become clear that it was irregular bleeding, then the continuity should not be broken. For what ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) means is that the woman assumed it was a day of her period but no blood came, whereas in the case in question blood did come; so this woman has a valid excuse for breaking the fast.

ʿAllāmah al-Rāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) has also related from Qāḍīkhān that if a woman thought the sun had set when in reality it had not, or she broke her fast taking it to be a day of her period and no period came, or it was a day of illness and she broke her fast, the continuity is not lost. The words of ʿAllāmah al-Rāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) are as follows:

لكن قد صحح قاضيخان في شرح الجامع الصغير سقوط الكفارة في المسألتين وشبههما بمن أفطر وأكبر ظنه أن الشمس غربت ثم ظهر عدمه. (التحرير المختار علي رد المحتار: ۲/۱٤۹، سعيد).
