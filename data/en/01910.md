### TITLE
The ruling on selling cigarettes

### QUESTION
A man sells cigarettes in his shop along with his other merchandise. Is this permissible or not? And will the income be lawful or not? Also, what is the ruling on smoking?

### ANSWER
Physicians today are agreed that smoking is harmful to the body, and for this reason the scholars in earlier times also declared smoking to be disliked. On that basis trading in it is disliked too, so one ought to keep away from it. Its income, however, will not be called unlawful. The scholars have set out a good many proofs for the dislikedness of smoking; a few of them are given below.

(1) It involves the wasting of wealth, because it is neither a food nor a drink, nor is any benefit to the body attached to it — on the contrary, it is harmful. It is also extravagance and squandering. Allah Most High says:

﴿كلوا واشربوا ولا تسرفوا﴾. (سورة الأعراف، الآية: ۳۱).

In another place He says:

﴿ولا تبذر تبذيراً إن المبذرين كانوا إخوان الشىطين وكان الشيطان لربه كفوراً﴾. (سورة بني إسرائيل، الآية: ۲٦، ۲۷).

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ says:

إن الله حرم عليكم عقوق الأمهات ومنعاً وهات ووأد البنات وكره لكم قيل وقال وكثرة السؤال وإضاعة المال. (صحيح البخاري: ۲/۸۸٤، باب عقوق الوالدين).

(2) The second harm is that it ruins bodily health. Physicians today are agreed that it is injurious to health. Consider a few of its harms:

According to a report in the Gujarati newspaper "Sandesh", with a single puff of a cigarette or a bīṛī a million bacteria enter the body and take hold of a person's lungs, food pipe and intestines, and begin to have lethal effects on them. The poisonous substance generally found in tobacco commonly brings on cancer; and not cancer alone — many blood diseases are also born of it. In the same way ulcers, asthma, acidity and stomach disease arise from it. In short, smoking bīṛīs, cigarettes and the like is as good as driving oneself towards death, or at the very least it amounts to bringing diseases upon oneself. (Sandesh, p. 7, dated 25/1/2010).

Al-Taqaddum al-ʿIlmī has written, on the authority of the World Health Organization (W.H.O.), that at least four million people in the world are sent to their deaths every year by nothing other than smoking cigarettes and bīṛīs.

After research and experiment the world's major laboratories have shown that of the 4,700 lethal ingredients found in cigarettes and the like, there are 43 ingredients that cause cancer with complete certainty. Some of them are these:

(1) Nicotine: from it come asthma and a weakening of the brain and the heart.

(2) Carbon monoxide, which carries extreme harms.

(3) Arsenic, which is used to kill insects and vermin.

(4) Cadmium: a poisonous substance, generally used in bīṛīs.

(5) Formaldehyde, used to preserve dead bodies from decay.

(6) D.D.T., an insecticide.

(7) Ammonia, used for cleaning ground, floors, tiles and so on.

(8) Acetone, used to remove nail polish.

(9) Toluene, used in factories to clean machinery.

(10) Methanol, used in fireworks.

(11) Hydrogen cyanide, an extremely poisonous substance. (Al-Taqaddum al-ʿIlmī).

The lethal ingredients mentioned above do not harm only the person who smokes the cigarette or bīṛī; they also cause severe harm to the people around him, and they are extremely dangerous in particular for the foetus in a pregnant woman's womb. Indeed, the smoke of some cigarettes even causes miscarriage; sometimes the foetus dies, or its weight falls, or a child is born deformed in shape and form, or the foetus is born afflicted with lethal diseases. In any case the effects on the foetus and on small children — nursing infants and young children — are utterly destructive. (Al-Taqaddum al-ʿIlmī, issue 68, February 2010).

Such are the harms of smoking and the like. (Taken from the monthly al-Fārūq, pp. 56–57, Dhū al-Ḥijjah 1431 AH, "Medicine and Health: Smoking — Advancing Towards Death", by Mawlānā Ḥudhayfah Wastānwī).

A noble hadith states:

"لا ضرر ولا ضرار". (رواه ابن ماجة والدارقطني عن أبي سعيد الخدري، وابن عباس وعبادة بن الصامت رضي الله عنه مسنداً ومالك في الموطأ مرسلاً)

One of its meanings is:

لا ضرر ابتداء ً ولا ضرر للغير انتقاماً وانتصاراً، فمن ذبح شاتك فلا تذبح شاته بل خذ منه القيمة

The second meaning is:

لا ضرر أي لا تنقص حق الغير ولا ضرار أي لا تدخل ضرراً زائداً في المجازات.

And the third meaning is:

لا ضرر لنفسك ولا ضرار للغير.

It is this third meaning that is intended here.

Allah Most High says:

﴿ولا تقتلوا أنفسكم﴾. (سورة النساء، الآية: ۲۹).

"Do not kill yourselves." We learn from this that it is not correct to adopt the means of one's own destruction either. In another place He says:

﴿ولا تلقوا بأيديكم إلى التهلكة﴾. (سورة البقرة، الآية: ۱۹۵).

(3) The third harm is that its stench is injurious and repugnant. Allah Most High says:

﴿ويحرم عليهم الخبائث﴾ (سورة الأعراف، الآية: ۱۵۷).

Since it is not certain that it is foul, it is not unlawful, but it is not free of dislikedness either.

Some people say that it is an intoxicant or a stupefacient and therefore impermissible; but the verifying scholars have their doubts about its being an intoxicant or a stupefacient. They say:

"الإسكار غيبوبة العقل مع حركة الأعضاء والتخدير غيبوبة العقل مع فتور الأعضاء"

That is, intoxication is the loss of the intellect while the limbs are still moving, and stupefaction is the loss of the intellect while the limbs are sluggish, still and heavy. Doctors, however, have declared it to be a cause of cancer of the airways, the stomach and the lungs, and have said that it is harmful to the kidneys and the liver and a cause of heart disease and weakening of the eyesight, as has already been mentioned.

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muftī ʿAbd al-Raḥīm Lājpūrī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes: Whether the dislikedness is strict or mild, in either case it is something to be given up, and one should not make a habit of it. Doing it a great deal is extravagance and a cause of sin. Those who are addicted to it at all times have permanently foul-smelling mouths, and the Prophet ﷺ had a great aversion to that. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah, Kitāb al-Ḥaẓr wa al-Ibāḥah, 2/242).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Waḥīd Makkī has written a treatise on the prohibition of cigarettes. At the end of it, in the commendatory note of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā ʿĀshiq Ilāhī Bulandshahrī Madanī, former Muftī of Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi, some of the words are these:

But in present circumstances, when the harms of cigarettes have become clear and doctors have acknowledged them, then — with these harms present and known — those who issue fatwas can now find the courage to give a verdict of unlawfulness. It is necessary to save the ummah from its stench, from its harms, and from the extravagance and squandering connected with it. Let everyone reflect on this: because of the harms of smoking there is now no hesitation in issuing a verdict of unlawfulness. End of quotation.

This humble servant cannot follow Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā's verdict of unlawfulness; in my view, however, a verdict of dislikedness is appropriate.

In sum: trading in it is disliked, but the income is lawful. See what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وصح بيع غير الخمر ومفاده صحة بيع الحشيشة والأفيون. وفي الشامية: قوله وصح بيع غير الخمر أي عنده خلافاً لهما في البيع والضمان، لكن الفتوى على قوله في البيع وعلى قولهما في الضمان

قوله ومفاده أي مفاد التقييد بغير الخمر ولا شك في ذلك لا نهما دون الخمر وليسا فوق الأشربة المحرمة فصحة بيعها يفيد صحة بيعهما فافهم. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٦/٤۵٤، كتاب الأشربة، سعيد).

وفي الشامية: نظمه الشرنبلالي في شرحه على الوهبانية بقوله ويمنع من بيع الدخان وشربه وشاربه في الصوم لا شك يفطر. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۳۹۵، باب ما يفسد الصوم ومالا يفسده، سعيد).

وفي الدر المختار: قال شيخنا النجم: والتتن الذي حدث وكان حدوثه بدمشق في سنة خمسة عشر بعد الألف يدعي شاربه أنه لا يسكر وإن سلم له فإنه مفتر، وفي الشامية: قوله والتتن، أقول: قد اضطربت آراء العلماء فيه فبعضهم قال بكراهته، وبعضهم قال بحرمته، وبعضهم بإباحته، وأفردوه بالتأليف، وفي شرح الوهبانية للشرنبلالي: ويمنع من بيع الدخان وشربه

(الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٦/٤۵۹، كتاب الأشربة. وكذا في حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح، ص ٦٦۵، قديمي).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

Trading in cigarettes and tobacco is permissible, and it is lawful to make use of the profit from it. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 9/148).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

Trading in cigarettes is permissible. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 6/495).

Kitāb al-Fatāwā states:

Cigarettes, bīṛīs and gutka are injurious to health, and for that reason they are not free, at the very least, of dislikedness. (Kitāb al-Fatāwā: 5/202).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
