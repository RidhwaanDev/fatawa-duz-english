### TITLE
Insurance of cars and property

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to insure a car or not? For example, a person buys a new car and insures it for its value, so that if it is stolen the insurance company will pay its price; and likewise, if the car is damaged, the insurance company will have it repaired.

### ANSWER
The first form of insurance, in which the company pays the money over, is absolutely impermissible. In the light of the sharia it necessarily involves interest and gambling, and the prohibition of both has been stated in the Qurʾān and the hadith.

Yes, if the insurance company does not pay the money over but instead has the car repaired in return for the money, there is room for this form; still, as a precaution one should not enter into such a transaction. In this form there is indeed indefiniteness in the consideration for the price, but it is not such as leads to dispute. Some scholars declare even this to be impermissible, and in any case cooperation with an interest-based company follows from it, so one should refrain from entering into such a transaction.

Consider what Ḥakīm al-Ummat Ḥaḍrat Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

In terms of its outward form it is gambling,

لأنه تعليق الملك على الخطر والمال في الجانبين

and in terms of its reality it is interest; and both gambling and interest are forbidden. So this transaction is certainly forbidden. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 3/161).

Muftī Kifāyat Allāh says:

To insure shops, factories and buildings with insurance companies is impermissible, because insurance comprises interest and gambling, and both of these are impermissible. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 8/83).

Muftī Taqī (ʿUthmānī) says:

The position of the majority is that this insurance contains both gambling and interest. Gambling, because payment from one side is fixed while payment from the other side is uncertain: the whole of the instalments that have been paid may be lost, and one may also receive more than them — this is what is called gambling. And interest, in that here money is exchanged for money with a disparity, since a smaller amount is paid by the insured and a larger amount is received. (Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil: 7/290).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

Insurance of life and of property is in principle not permissible, since it contains both interest and gambling. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 4/124).

Islāmī Fiqh states:

The present-day scheme of insurance contains countless corrupting elements such as interest and gambling; and from the moral and economic point of view too, interest and gambling and the like eat away at human character and decency and at civilised life like a weevil. (Islāmī Fiqh: 2/360).
