### TITLE
The ruling on the establishment of revocation by the words "I love you"

### QUESTION
A man gave his wife a revocable divorce; then, nine or ten days later, the husband said to his wife over the telephone, "I love you". Did a valid revocation take place over the telephone by these words or not?

### ANSWER
Just as revocation is established by explicit expressions, so too it is established by allusive expressions when accompanied by the intention. Accordingly, in the case in question, "I love you" is an allusive expression: if the husband intended revocation when he uttered these words, then revocation has been established, and there is now no need for a fresh marriage contract.

Consider what is stated in Fatḥ al-Qadīr:

ألفاظ الرجعة صريح وكناية: فالصريح راجعتك في حال خطابها وراجعت امرأتي في حال غيبتها وحضورها أيضاً

والكنايات: أنت عندي كما كنت وأنت امرأتي فلا يصير مراجعاً إلا بالنية. (فتح القدير: ٤/۱۵۹، دار الفكر). (وكذا في فتاوى الشامي: ۳/۳۹۸، سعيد، وفتاوى الهندية: ۱/٤٦۸، وكتاب الفقه على المذاهب الأربعة: ٤/۳۳۰).

Qāmūs al-Fiqh states:

Revocation can take place by words that indicate the sense of revocation explicitly, and also by words in which there is a degree of ambiguity and which can bear a meaning other than revocation. In the terminology of fiqh such words are called allusive expressions — for example, "You are to me just as you were before". With such words the intention will be required: if revocation was intended, revocation will take place; otherwise not. (قاموس الفقہ: ۳/٤۷۳، ط: زمزم) ۔
(و کذا فی فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۳/۳٦٤، جامعہ فاروقیہ) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
