### TITLE
Ruling on ṭalāq where the husband denies the letter

### QUESTION
A man went away from home for a period. A letter reached his father-in-law which said: "Have the mahr (the dower due to the wife) remitted on my behalf, and my wife has my permission." It did not even carry his signature. When the man came to hear of it, he denied the letter. Has ṭalāq taken effect now or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question there is no apparent ground for holding that a ṭalāq has taken effect, since the man denies the letter, and the letter contains no word of ṭalāq at all; it says no more than that she has his permission. This is one of the allusive expressions, and an intention is required for it; and when the husband himself denies the letter, no ruling will be given that a ṭalāq has taken effect.

Radd al-Muḥtār states:

وإن لم يقر أنه كتابه ولم تقم بينة، لكنه وصف الأمر على وجهه لا تطلق قضاء ولا ديانة، وكذا كل كتاب لم يكتبه بخطه ولم يمله بنفسه لا يقع الطلاق ما لم يقر أنه كتابه. (فتاوى الشامي: ۳/۲٤۷، سعيد. وكذا في الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۳۷۹. وفتاوى محموديه: ۱۲/۵۹٤. والمحيط البرهاني: ۳/٤۲۹، رشيديه).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
