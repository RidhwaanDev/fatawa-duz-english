### TITLE
Looking at the face of the deceased before burial

### QUESTION
Some women are in the habit of looking at the face of the deceased, and they consider it a source of reward to look at the face of scholars and pious elders. Does the sacred law permit this or not?

### ANSWER
The scholars have written that it is permissible and correct to look at the face of the deceased after death and before burial. As for one whose face it was not permissible to look at during life — for example if the deceased is a woman who is not a maḥram — it will not be permissible to look at her face after death either. Nowadays, however, this has become a custom: a formal viewing is arranged, it is thought to bring reward, and it also causes the burial to be delayed. For this reason the custom should be given up and should not be encouraged.

Indeed, matters have now reached the point where the face of the deceased is shown by video call to relatives and family members living in other countries so that they may view it. All of this is contrary to the respect and honour due to the deceased, and it should be given up.

There is no sin in looking at the face of the deceased; simply viewing it, without intending reward, is correct. One report tells that when the father of Ḥaḍrat Jābir (may Allah be pleased with him) died, he would look at his father's face and weep. Likewise, when Ḥaḍrat ʿUthmān ibn Maẓʿūn (may Allah be pleased with him) died, the Prophet ﷺ kissed him. Another report tells that when the Prophet ﷺ passed away, Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) looked upon his blessed face and kissed it. Consider the reports:

A hadith in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī states:

عن محمد بن المنكدر، قال: سمعت جابر بن عبد الله رضي الله عنه، قال: لما قتل أبي جعلت أكشف الثوب عن وجهه أبكي، وينهوني عنه، والنبي ﷺ لا ينهاني، فجعلت عمتي فاطمة تبكي، فقال النبي ﷺ: تبكين أو لا تبكين ما زالت الملائكة تظله بأجنحتها حتى رفعتموه. (صحيح البخاري: ۱/۱٦٦/۱۲٤٤، ط: فيصل).

A report in Sunan Ibn Mājah states:

عن عائشة رضي الله عنها قالت: قبل رسول الله ﷺ عثمان بن مظعون رضي الله عنه وهو ميت، فكأني أنظر إلى دموعه تسيل على خديه. (رواه ابن ماجه، ص ۱۰۵/۱٤۵٦، ط: قديمي).

A report in the Musnad of Aḥmad states:

عن حميد بن عبد الرحمن قال: توفي رسول الله ﷺ وأبو بكر في طائفة من المدينة قال: فجاء فكشف عن وجهه فقبله وقال: فداك أبي وأمي ما أطيبك حياً وميتاً، مات محمد ﷺ ورب الكعبة. (أخرجه الإمام أحمد في مسنده: ۱/۱۹۸/۱۸، ط: الرسالة).

The hadiths quoted above establish that it is permissible to look at the face of the deceased. Permission is recorded in al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah as well:

ولا بأس بأن يرفع ستر الميت ليرى وجهه وإنما يكره ذلك بعد الدفن كذا في القنية. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳۵۱).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states: Uncovering the face and showing it before burial is permitted.

The annotations on Kifāyat al-Muftī state: Since several harms are found in it, it is better to leave it off — for example, regarding it as a means of reward, taking photographs, delaying the burial, and so on. (کفایت المفتی مع التعلیقات: ۵/٤۳۱، ط: جامعہ فاروقیہ) ۔

Fatāwā Farīdiyyah states: There is no sin in looking at the face of a dead person,

كما في الهندية

(فتاوی فریدیہ: ۳/۲۹۰) ۔

Khayr al-Fatāwā records: Whoever it was permissible to look at during his life may be looked at after his death as well. (خیر الفتاوی: ۳/۲۱۷) ۔

In our own times, however, because it has taken the shape of a custom, some muftis have encouraged people to avoid it so as to block the means to harm. A few rulings of the senior scholars are presented here. See what Aḥsan al-Fatāwā records:

Question: What is the ruling of the sacred law on the custom of showing the face of the deceased after the shrouding?

Answer: This custom involves the following harms, and it must therefore be given up:

(1) In some regions looking at the face of the deceased is considered a means of merit and reward, although there is no basis for this in the sacred law. To consider it a source of reward is therefore an innovation, and even if one does not consider it a source of reward, it still promotes and supports an innovation.

(2) The ruling of the sacred law is that as few people as possible should be present when the deceased is bathed and shrouded, and that they should be from among the relatives and close friends of the deceased, so that if — God forbid — some defect or change has appeared in the deceased, it is not made public. The custom of showing the face is contrary to this ruling of the sacred law and to its wisdom.

(3) If the deceased is a well-known personality, several hours are spent on the custom of showing his face, although it is not permissible to delay the burial of the deceased.

(4) The result of the custom of unveiling the face is that photographs of the deceased are taken and published in the newspapers, in which — besides the curse and punishment attached to picture-making — there is publicising of the change in the face of the deceased, which is unlawful.

Nowadays this ugly custom has become widespread even among distinguished scholars and spiritual guides, and it is therefore necessary to leave instructions that it be avoided. (احسن الفتاوی: ٤/۲۱۹) ۔

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah records:

Question: In our locality the face of the deceased is shown to those present after the funeral prayer. What is the ruling on this?

Answer: This custom is unnecessary and disliked, because it causes delay, whereas haste is what has been commanded. That is why the hadith commands walking briskly while carrying the bier:

أسرعوا بالجنازة،

Shāmī states:

قوله ويسرع بها بلا خبب، وحد التعجيل المسنون أن يسرع به بحيث لا يضطرب الميت على الجنازة للحديث أسرعوا بالجنازة. (شامي، مراقي الفلاح مع طحطاوي)

When a collective supplication for the deceased after the funeral prayer is forbidden on account of the delay it causes, how can gathering to view the face be correct? Fatāwā Sirājiyyah states:

إذا فرغ من الصلاة لا يقوم بالدعاء (فتاوى سراجية، وخلاصة الفتاوى)

فقط واللہ تعالی اعلم بالصواب ۔ (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ۱/۵۳٦، دیوبند) ۔

In Aghlāṭ al-ʿAwāmm, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

Ruling: In some regions looking at the face of the deceased is considered a means of reward. There is no basis for this in the sacred law, and to consider it a source of reward is an innovation. (اغلاط العوام، ص ۲۱٤، ط: زمزم) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
