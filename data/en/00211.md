### TITLE
Calling the neglect of daʿwah and tablīgh a grave crime

### QUESTION
The workers of tablīgh say that the ummah committed a grave crime by abandoning the work of daʿwah. If this is a communal obligation (farḍ kifāyah) and some people are carrying it out, has that grave crime been made good or not? And is this work recommended, a communal obligation, an individual obligation, or necessary?

### ANSWER
What the people of tablīgh mean is that in some places the work of daʿwah was not carried out from house to house, even though people capable of doing daʿwah were present there. So in those places where there was a need and nobody did the work, a grave crime was committed. And this work is a communal obligation.

قال الله تعالى: ﴿ادع إلى سبيل ربك بالحكمة والموعظة الحسنة﴾ (النحل: ۱۲۵).
﴿قل هذه سبيلي ادعوا لي الله على بصيرة انا ومن اتبعني﴾ (يوسف: ۱۰۸).
﴿ىبُني اقم الصلاة وأمر بالمعروف وانه عن المنكر واصبر على ما اصابك﴾ (لقمان: ۱۷).

Bukhārī states:

وقال عليه الصلاة والسلام: ألا هل بلّغت؟ قلنا: نعم! اللهم اشهد فليبلّغ الشاهد الغائب، فإنه ربّ مبلّغ يبلّغه من هو أوعى منه. (بخاري رقم ۷۰۷۸).

Tirmidhī states:

عن أبي بكر الصديق رضي الله عنه قال: يا أيها الناس إنكم تقرؤون هذه الآية ﴿يا أيها الذين امنوا عليكم انفسكم لا يضرّكم من ضلّ إذا اهتديتم﴾ وإني سمعت رسول الله ﷺ يقول: إن الناس إذا رأوا الظالم فلم يأخذوا على يديه أوشك أن يعمهم الله بعقاب منه. (رواه الترمذي باب في نزول العذاب رقم: ۲۱٦۸).

Al-Tafsīr al-Munīr states:

إن الدعوة إلى الإسلام ونشرها في آفاق العالم والأمر بالمعروف والنّهي عن المنكر من فروض الإسلام الكفائية. (التفسير المنير: ٤/۳۵، آل عمران، دار الفكر).

Bayān al-Qur'ān states:

The details of this question are as follows. Whoever is able to enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong — that is, from the circumstances he has a strong expectation that if he enjoins and forbids, no significant harm will come to him — for such a person it is necessary to enjoin and forbid in matters that are obligatory. (2) Whoever is not able in the sense just described, it is not necessary for him to enjoin and forbid even in obligatory matters; though if he summons the courage to do so, he will be rewarded. Then, for the one who is able, this duty is a communal obligation: if enough people are doing this work for the need to be met, it drops from the responsibility of the others who are able. (بيان القرآن: ۱/۱۰٤).

Imām Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ (may Allah have mercy on him) has also mentioned this in Aḥkām al-Qur'ān, the substance of which is as follows:

قال الله تعالى: ﴿ولتكن منكم أمة يدعون إلى الخير ويأمرون بالمعروف وينهون عن المنكر﴾ قد حوت هذه الآية معنيين أحدهما: وجوب الأمر بالمعروف والنّهي عن المنكر، والآخر: أنه فرض على الكفاية ليس بفرض على كل أحد في نفسه إذا قام به غيره لقوله تعالى: ﴿ولتكن منكم أمة﴾ وحقيقته تقتضي البعض دون البعض فدلّ على أنه فرض على الكفاية إذا قام به بعضهم سقط عن الباقين، ومن الناس من يقول هو فرض على كلّ أحد في نفسه والذي يدلّ على صحة هذا القول أنه إذا قام به بعضهم سقط عن الباقين كالجهاد وغسل الموتى وتكفينهم والصلاة عليهم ودفنهم، ولو لا أنه فرض على الكفاية لما سقط عن الآخرين بقيام بعضهم به (أحكام القرآن: ۲/۲۹).

Sharḥ Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn states:

والأمر بالمعروف والنهي عن المنكر، فرض كفاية، إذا قام به من يكفي حصل المقصود، وإذا لم يقم به من يكفي وجب على جميع المسلمين، كما قال الله تعالى: ﴿ولتكن منكم أمة يدعون إلى الخير الخ﴾. (شرح رياض الصالحين للشيخ محمد بن صالح العثيمين: ۱/٤۸۳).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Question: Is tablīgh obligatory?

Answer: Conveying the religion is obligatory in every age, and it is obligatory in this age too, but it is a communal obligation. Its importance in any place will be in proportion to the need there, and each person's responsibility will be in proportion to his own capability. Enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong is stated plainly in the Noble Qur'an. The greatest good is faith, and the greatest evil is disbelief. Every believer is charged, according to his own standing, with continuing to convey the religion that the Pure God has revealed, in accordance with the guidance of the noble Messenger. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۷/۲٤٦) ۔

Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah states:

Conveying the religion is a communal obligation. To call the creation of God to what He has commanded and to restrain them from what He has forbidden is, in the sharia, a communal obligation, so that when some carry it out the duty of all is discharged. To hold the view that it is an individual obligation is to be treated as a mistake; purifying oneself of base traits, however, is an individual obligation. (فتاوى حقانية ۲/٤۳۸).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

Tablīgh is a communal obligation. It is not an individual obligation, but there is no doubt about its being a communal obligation. (کفایت المفتی: ۲/۳٤) ۔

From the passages and texts quoted above it is clear that conveying the religion is a communal obligation, and the ruling on a communal obligation is that if some people carry it out it drops from the rest, while if nobody does it everyone is sinful.

So abandoning this blessed work of daʿwah and tablīgh is a grave crime, and if everybody abandons it, all are liable to punishment. This is supported by the sense of the hadith: if sins and disobedience to Allah are taking place somewhere and the people there do not stop it (even though they are able to), Allah's punishment will come upon them all.

Yes — when some people carry out this work, the grave crime is made good.

Those who are engaged in this blessed work are, ما شاء اللہ, engaged in a very good work.

And those who are attached to other branches of religious work should also, alongside carrying out their own duties, take part in this work in their free time. May Allah grant us all the ability to do so.
