### TITLE
Speculative trading (saṭṭā)

### QUESTION
Nowadays the business of speculation (saṭṭā) has reached its height in most of the large companies. Is there any room for it in the sacred law or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
The object of this dealing is not the purchase of shares at all. What is intended is rather that, as prices rise and fall, the profit and the loss should simply be settled and squared off against one another between the two parties. In this form the price is not paid over by the buyer, nor is the article sold handed over by the seller. Such a dealing is therefore impermissible and not allowed.

Speculation is a form of qimār, that is, gambling. In the dictionary Fīrōz al-Lughāt the meanings given for saṭṭā bāzī are: (1) a kind of gambling in which bets are laid upon the judgement given about some matter; (2) a merchant's deal. (فیروز اللغات، ۷۸۰) ۔

There are two reasons for speculation being impermissible:

(1) It is the sale of what is not owned, that is, the seller sells a thing which is not in his ownership.

(2) It is a sale before taking possession, that is, the article sold is sold on before it has been taken into the buyer's possession.
