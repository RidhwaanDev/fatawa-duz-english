### TITLE
Giving an equivalent flat when the builder does not build after taking the order

### QUESTION
A builder bought a plot somewhere and planned to put up a fifteen-storey building on it. Zayd made a deal with the builder for a flat on the tenth floor of that building. In keeping with the size of the flat and the other details, a price of 8,000,000 was settled. Zayd paid the whole price and it was agreed that the building would be ready in three years, but three years passed and the construction work had still not begun. The builder asked for a further two years' grace and Zayd agreed. Now, after five years, the builder says that the building will not be built and that Zayd should take his 8,000,000 back. Zayd is not willing, because land prices keep rising day by day and it is now hard to get such a flat at that price. The builder said, "Take the money at today's value." Even so, Zayd insisted that he wanted the flat itself. The builder said, "Find one yourself and I shall pay the whole amount." Zayd therefore found a flat, and its price was 20,000,000; but the builder then said, "I shall pay only 10,000,000 and no more."

The matters to be determined are these: (1) Is it right for Zayd to demand 20,000,000 from the builder and to take it?

(2) Is Zayd right to insist in this way? And if the builder provides such a flat, is it right to take it? Is the builder bound in the sharia to provide such a flat as demanded, or not?

Most people think that this is a contract of istiṣnāʿ, that before the flat is ready Zayd can sell it neither to the builder nor to anyone else, and that the return of the money is a cancellation and rescission of the contract, which is valid only at the original price. On this view Zayd may not take 20,000,000 but only the 8,000,000. The builder goes about showing people a fatwa of this sort.

Your guidance is needed in this matter. Kindly inform us of the ruling of the sharia and earn a great reward.

### ANSWER
When a man — Zayd, say — gives money to a company and asks it to build a house or a flat for him, and settles the matter by booking the house or the flat, the jurists of the present day have included such a transaction under istiṣnāʿ. Whether or not a date is fixed in it makes no difference to its being istiṣnāʿ, according to the preferred view.

Shaykh Muḥammad Khālid al-Atāsī writes in Sharḥ al-Majallah:

ثم ما ورد التعامل في استصناعه سواء كان مؤجلاً إلى شهر أو أزيد أو لم يكن مؤجلاً فالتأجيل يحمل على الاستعجال ولا يخرجه عن كونه استصناعاً، وهو قول الإمامين (أي الصاحبين) وعليه مشت هذه المادة لكونه أرفق. (شرح المجلة: ۱/٤۰۳، مكتبة رشيدية، كوئته).

Then, when the period had passed and the maker excused himself from building the flat at that particular place and asked the customer to take his money back, and the customer refused to take the money because it would cause him a heavy loss, the customer did not cancel the contract; and the maker has no right to cancel the contract one-sidedly. So the maker — the builder — should now give the customer a flat equivalent to the one promised.

About the salam sale, Sharḥ al-Majallah says:

ولو انقطع عن أيدي الناس بعد الحلول قبل أن يوفي المسلم فيه فرب السلم بالخيار إن شاء فسخ العقد وأخذ رأس ماله وإن شاء انتظر وجوده أي المسلم فيه. (شرح المجلة: ۲/۳۹۸، مكتبة رشيدية).

Salam and istiṣnāʿ are alike in most of their rulings. Here too, since the flat cannot be had at the promised place, the maker should give him its equivalent; and the experts in this line of business know very well what counts as an equivalent.

Our humble opinion, then, is that the man who took the order, the one called the builder, should give Zayd, the customer, a flat equivalent to the one promised.

As for the fatwa the builder shows people, namely that he will return to Zayd the money deposited with him, that is correct only if Zayd cancels the contract. If Zayd asks for the flat or its equivalent, the builder will have to give him an equivalent; in reality it is the builder who will give Zayd this flat as promised. If, because of some legal requirement, the money is paid into Zayd's account, there is no harm in that. Where an equivalent flat is to be given, the builder will buy a flat, and once the purchase is complete he will be bound to give the flat to Zayd as promised.
