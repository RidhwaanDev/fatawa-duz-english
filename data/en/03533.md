### TITLE
The ruling on the expiation for breaking an obligatory fast of Ramaḍān out of ignorance

### QUESTION
A man used to break his fasts deliberately in Ramaḍān, but he did not know that an expiation becomes binding on account of this. Now that he has come to know, will the expiation be binding upon him or not?

### ANSWER
Not knowing is not a valid excuse in the Sharīʿah. Therefore, along with making up the fasts, paying the expiation will also be binding. The expiation is that he keep fasts for sixty days without a break; and a person who is not able to fast should feed sixty poor people.

Consider what is recorded in al-Jawharah:

من جامع عامداً في أحد السبيلين أو أكل أو شرب ما يتغذى به أو يتداوى به فعليه القضاء والكفارة لأن الجناية متكاملة لقضاء الشهوة

قوله والكفارة مثل كفارة الظهارة. (الجوهرة النيرة: ۱/۱۷۲، ط: إمداديه ملتان).

وفي الاختيار لتعليل المختار: أكل أو شرب عامداً غذاء أو دواء وهو صائم في رمضان عليه القضاء والكفارة مثل المظاهر. (الاختيار: ۱/۱۳۹، كتاب الصوم، ط: بيروت).

See further: (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۲۰۵، والبناية: ۳/٦٦۵).

In a place where Islam is known and there are settlements of Muslims, ignorance of the rulings of the Sharīʿah is no excuse. Consider the following:

قال في الأشباه: الجهل في دار الحرب من مسلم لم يهاجر، وأنه يكون عذراً

وفي حاشية الحموي: قال: بخلاف الذمي إذا أسلم في دار الإسلام لشيوع الأحكام، والتمكن من السؤال. (الأشباه مع الحموي: ۳/۱۳، الفن الثالث، ط: إدارة القرآن).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states: If someone had himself cupped, or backbit someone, and then thought that his fast had been broken by this, and thereupon deliberately ate, then — if he did not take a fatwa from any jurist and no ḥadīth had reached him — both making up the fast and the expiation will be binding upon him, because this is mere ignorance and he has no legal proof for it; and ignorance is not an excuse in the land of Islam. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 3/321).

Āp kē Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥall states: An expiation is binding for deliberately breaking a fast of the blessed month of Ramaḍān, and that is to keep sixty consecutive days of fasting; and a person who is not able to keep the fasts should feed sixty needy people. A person's not knowing the ruling is no excuse. (Āp kē Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥall: 4/609, new edition).
