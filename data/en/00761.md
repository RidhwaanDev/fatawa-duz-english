### TITLE
Giving a talk before the Friday sermon

### QUESTION
A talk is given before the Friday prayer and its sermon. Does this have any basis or not?

Also, in some places time is set aside for the sunnah prayers before the sermon, while in other places people pray their sunnahs during the talk. Which of these two practices is better?

### ANSWER
There is no harm in giving a talk after the first adhan, before the sermon and the prayer, so long as the worshippers are willing. A narration in al-Mustadrak of al-Ḥākim records that Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) used to give an admonition and relate hadiths before the preacher delivered the sermon. Consider the following:

كان أبو هريرة رضي الله عنه يقوم يوم الجمعة إلى جانب المنبر فيطرح اعقاب ن عليه في ذراعيه ثم يقبض على رمانة المنبر يقول: قال أبو القاسم ﷺ قال محمد ﷺ قال رسول الله ﷺ قال الصادق المصدوق ﷺ ثم يقول في بعض ذلك ويل للعرب من شر قد اقترب فإذا سمع حركة باب المقصورة بخروج الإمام جلس. (المستدرك للحاكم: ۱/۱۰۸، كتاب العلم)

In the same way ʿAllāmah al-Zabīdī (may Allah have mercy on him), while advising that religious gatherings be held on Friday, has quoted from Ibn Abī Shaybah that the following Companions permitted such gatherings:

"قد روى ابن أبي شيبة جواز ذلك عن السائب رضي الله عنه وعبد الله بن بسر رضي الله عنه وابن عمر رضي الله عنه وأبي هريرة رضي الله عنه". (إتحاف سادة المتقين شرح إحياء علوم الدين: ۳/۲۷۷)

Furthermore, in our own time people are far from religion and have lost any eagerness to learn the rulings of the sharia or to attend gatherings of learning. A talk before the Friday prayer is therefore extremely useful as a way of bringing religious knowledge to them.

Thus, when Mufti Kifāyatullāh (may Allah have mercy on him) was asked how it is to translate the sermon into Urdu for the benefit of people who know no Arabic and do not understand it, he wrote in reply:

The better way is for the preacher to give a talk in the mother tongue before beginning the sermon and to explain the essentials of religion. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 3/214)

See also: Imdād al-Aḥkām: 1/277, on the ruling of giving an admonition before the sermon; Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 8/256, chaptered and arranged edition, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah; and Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 1/264.

It is fitting for a separate time to be set aside for the sunnah prayers before the sermon. Praying the sunnahs during the talk appears to be improper: first, because it disturbs the prayer of the one praying, and second, because it is also contrary to good manners for a person not to listen to a religious address given to everyone and to busy himself with an individual act instead.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
