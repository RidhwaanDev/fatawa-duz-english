### TITLE
Depositing money in the bank in a son's name and whether it is a gift

### QUESTION
A man deposited some of his money in the bank in his son's name in order to avoid tax. Now, because it stands in the son's name, will the son be regarded as its owner or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, if the purpose of depositing it in the bank was not to make the son its owner but it was deposited that way for some practical reason, then the son has not become the owner. Yes, if the transfer of ownership was intended, then the son has become the owner.

See what Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

If Zayd had in fact not made this house his wife's property but had had her name entered in the government papers for some practical reason, then this house did not become the wife's property, and after her death her heirs will have no right in it; rather it will remain Zayd's property as before. A person's name being entered in the government papers does not establish ownership in the sharia unless the owner of his own free will makes that person the owner and gives him possession — wa hādhā kulluhu ẓāhir min ʿāmmat kutub al-fiqh. (Imdād al-Muftīn, vol. 2, p. 738, Kitāb al-Hibah, Dār al-Ishāʿat).
