### TITLE

Giving zakat to someone believed to be entitled to it who was not

### QUESTION

A zakat organisation had an applicant fill in his details on a form, then, by estimation and investigation, determined that he was entitled to receive zakat, and he continued receiving zakat for several years. After his death his heirs informed the organisation that the deceased had not been entitled to zakat: one hundred thousand rand were deposited in his account, and the heirs are ready to return the zakat money. Now what is asked is this: should this money be returned to the organisation? And was the zakat given to this man validly discharged or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER

Organisations and bodies that collect zakat generally draw up their lists of the poor and the entitled after inquiry, investigation and careful judgement; and these bodies are agents of both the donors and the poor. Accordingly, as soon as they take possession of the zakat money, the donors' zakat is discharged. So, in the case in question, the zakat was validly discharged. Yes, if the heirs return the money, that will be a kindness on behalf of the deceased and a means of clearing his liability. Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

دفع بتحر، لمن يظنه مصرفاً فبان أنه عبده أو مكاتبه أو حربي ولو مستأمنا أعادها، وإن بان غناه أو كونه ذمياً أو أنه أبوه أو ابنه أو امرأته أو هاشمي لا يعيد، لأنه أتى بما في وسعه، حتى لو دفع بلا تحر لم يجز إن أخطأ وفي الشامية: وفيه: واعلم أن المدفوع إليه لو كان جالساً في صف الفقراء يصنع صنعهم أو كان عليه زيهم أو سأله فأعطاه كانت هذه الأسباب بمنزلة التحري كذا في المبسوط حتى لو ظهر غناه لم يعد

ولا يسترد في الولد والغني وهل يطيب له؟ فيه خلاف، وإذا لم يطب قيل يتصدق وقيل يرد على المعطي. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۲/۳۵۲، سعيد).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā records:

If the giver, after careful consideration, gave the zakat believing the recipient to be a valid recipient, then his zakat was discharged. But once Zayd (who is a Hāshimī) came to know that the thing given was zakat, it is binding on him to return it to the giver. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 4/280.)

Jawāhir al-Fiqh records: if a person gave zakat to someone believing him to be poor and entitled to it, and then it came to light that he was a non-Muslim living under Muslim rule, or a man of means; or if he gave it to someone on a dark night and then learned that it was his mother, his father, or some other relative to whom it is not correct for him to give — in all these cases the zakat was discharged and it is not necessary to give it again. However, if the recipient comes to know that it is zakat money and that he is not entitled to receive zakat, he should not take it and should return it. (Jawāhir al-Fiqh: 3/247.)

See further: (Bihishtī Zēwar, part three, p. 249, and Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 9/515, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah, and Imdād al-Muftīn, volume two, p. 392, published by Dār al-Ishāʿat).
