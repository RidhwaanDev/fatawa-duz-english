### TITLE
Setting aside a flat in a large building as a mosque

### QUESTION
These days, in large cities, there are great difficulties in buying a separate site for a mosque and in constructing it. Then, in some non-Muslim countries, an impressive mosque also becomes a target for the envy and hostility of bigoted non-Muslims. At the same time a mosque is something Muslims genuinely need, because iʿtikāf (spiritual retreat) and the reward attached to a mosque and so on are tied to a mosque. So if a flat in a large building is bought to serve as a mosque, and there are residential flats above it and below it, will this flat become a mosque or not? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
The sacred law has given the mosque a special importance, and its rulings are somewhat distinct from the rulings of other endowments. According to the ẓāhir al-riwāyah (the authoritative transmission of the school), for a mosque to be a legally valid mosque it is necessary that the land on which the mosque building is raised be endowed — that is, both the lower and the upper portion of the mosque must be endowed, and the right of every human being over it must be entirely severed. Without this, that mosque will not be called a legally valid mosque. See how ʿAllāmah Fakhr al-Dīn Zaylaʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) sheds light on this question in his commentary on al-Kanz. He writes:

ومن جعل مسجداً تحته سرداب أو فوقه بيت وجعل بابه إلى الطريق وعزله أو اتخذ وسط داره مسجداً وأذن للناس بالدخول فله بيعه ويورث عنه، لأنه لم يخلص لله لبقاء حق العبد فيه والمسجد لا يكون إلا خالصاً لله لما تلونا ومع بقاء حق العبد في أسفله أو في أعلاه أو في جوانبه محيطاً به لا يتحقق الخلوص كله أما إذا كان السفل مسجداً فلأن لصاحب العلو حقاً في السفل حتى لا يكون لصاحب السفل أن يحدث فيه شيئاً من غير رضا صاحب العلو وأما إذا جعل العلو مسجداً فلأن أرض العلو ملك لصاحب السفل وليس له من التصرفات شيء من غير رضا صاحب السفل كالبناء وغيره بخلاف مسجد بيت المقدس فإن السرداب فيه ليس بمملوك لأحد بل هو لمصالح المسجد حتى لوكان غيره مثله نقول بأنه صار مسجداً وأما إذا اتخذ وسط داره مسجداً فلأن ملكه محيط بجوانبه فكان له حق المنع من الدخول والمسجد من شرطه ألا يكون لأحد فيه حق المنع قال الله تعالى: ﴿وَمَنْ أَظْلَمُ مِمَّن مَّنَعَ مَسَاجِدَ اللَّهِ أَن يُذْكَرَ فِيهَا اسْمُهُ﴾ ولأنه لم يفرزه حين أبقي الطريق لنفسه فلم يخلص لله. (تبيين الحقائق: ۳/۳۳۰، ط: إمداديه، ملتان).

وللاستزادة ينظر: (البحر الرائق: ۵/۲۷۱، ط: دار المعرفة، والهداية: ۲/، وفتح القدير: ٦/۲۳٤، ط: دار الفكر، والعناية شرح الهداية).

Yes: if the flat above the mosque, or the one below it in the building, is endowed, and at the time of the original construction the founders excluded the portion above and below the mosque from the mosque and assigned its rent to the mosque, then in that case the jurists have stated explicitly that this mosque will come under the ruling of a legally valid mosque. See what al-Durr al-Mukhtār together with Shāmī says:

قال في الدر المختار: وإذا جعل تحته سرداباً لمصالحه أي المسجد جاز كمسجد القدس أو جعل فوقه بيتاً (فرع) لو بني فوقه بيتاً للإمام لا يضر لأنه من المصالح، أما لو تمت المسجدية ثم أراد البناء منع، ولو قال: عنيت ذلك لم يصدق، التتارخانية. وقال في الشامية: قوله أو جعل فوقه بيتاً الخ. ظاهره أنه لا فرق بين أن يكون البيت للمسجد أو لا، إلا أنه يؤخذ من التعليل أن محل عدم كونه مسجداً فيما إذا لم يكن وقفاً على مصالح المسجد وبه صرح في الإسعاف فقال: وإذا كان السرداب أو العلو لمصالح المسجد أو كانا وقفاً عليه صار مسجداً. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٤/۳۵۷، ۳۵۸، مطلب في أحكام المسجد، سعيد).

As for the statement of the jurists that the condition for a legally valid mosque is that both the lower and the upper parts be a mosque, so that the right of every human being is completely severed, this does not mean that the lower and upper parts must literally be a mosque in every respect, in the sense that all the rulings of a mosque apply to them — for example, that a menstruating woman or a person in a state of major ritual impurity may not enter, that buying and selling in them is disliked, and so on. What the jurists intend, rather, is that the right of every human being must have been entirely severed from both the lower and the upper parts. If some person retains ownership over them, that will impair the mosque's status as a mosque; but if both the lower and upper parts are endowed for the purposes of the mosque, the right of human beings comes to an end and nothing at all impairs the mosque's status as a mosque.

Accordingly Ḥakīm al-Ummat Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) explains:

This will be a mosque when he builds that cellar and upper storey for the needs of the mosque, or endows them upon the mosque. The substance of the passage in al-Baḥr is this: what is understood from the words of the jurists — that a place is a mosque only when the whole of it, above and below, is a mosque — is not to be taken to mean that the upper and lower parts must themselves be a mosque. Rather, the real object of laying down that condition is that the right of every human being should be severed from it, whether by its being a mosque or by its being endowed upon the mosque. So the stipulation that it be a mosque is by way of example, not by way of restriction; the real stipulation is the severance of the right of every human being. And if it is not by way of example, then it is by way of causation; and since the cause — the severance of the right of every human being — is shared, the ruling that follows from that cause will also be general, and wherever the severance is not found, it will not be a mosque. Since the words لینقطع الخ also conveyed the case in which there is no such severance, it is with this in view that he goes on to say بخلاف. And this statement:

لأنه مسجد إلى عنان السماء وكذا إلى تحت الثري

— this applies to the case where nothing had already been built beneath it; there everything becomes a mosque by way of subordination. But when a cellar had been built beneath it from the very outset, then by deliberate intent that part is excluded:

وللقصد ترجيح على التبع

(امداد الفتاویٰ: ۲/٦٦۰)۔

In Jawāhir al-Fiqh, Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

If a mosque is built in such a way that shops or a basement and the like are made below, and on their roof there is the courtyard of the mosque or some part of the mosque building, this is permissible on condition that the shops below are endowed just as the mosque is, and that their income is spent on the needs of the mosque. In the same way it is also permissible for living quarters to be built on the roof of the mosque for the needs of the mosque. In both these cases nothing will impair the mosque's status as a mosque, as has been quoted in Fatāwā Shāmī on the authority of al-Isʿāf. In such a case the shops below and the quarters above will not form part of the mosque, and on that basis it will all be permissible: letting them out for rent, trading in them, and the entry into them of a man who is in need of a ritual bath or of a woman in menstruation or post-natal bleeding.

A note of caution: it must be remembered, however, that this arrangement is only possible where, at the time of building the mosque, the builder formed the intention at the very outset of keeping the quarters above, or the basement or shop below, separate from the mosque, of letting them out for rent, and of endowing them upon the mosque. Otherwise, if the mosque has been built first, it is by no means permissible afterwards to build a shop beneath it or quarters above it to let out for rent, and so on. (جواہر الفقہ، جلد سوم، ص: ۱۲۳ ۔ ۱۲٤، ط: مکتبہ دار العلوم کراچی)۔

For further detail see: (مجموعۃ الفتاوی از حضرت مولانا عبد الحی لکھنوی رحمہ اللہ: ۱/۲۱۹ ۔ ۲۲۳، و فتاویٰ رحیمیہ: ۵/۱۸۹)۔

The need of the age and the reports outside the ẓāhir al-riwāyah:

On the question discussed above, besides the ẓāhir al-riwāyah, several other reports are also narrated from the imams of the school, which the author of al-Hidāyah (may Allah have mercy on him) has set out. See the passage:

وروي الحسن رحمه الله عنه أنه قال: إذا جعل السفل مسجداً وعلي ظهره مسكن فهو مسجد لأن المسجد مما يتأبد وذلك يتحقق في السفل دون العلو، وعن محمد رحمه الله على عكس هذا لأن المسجد معظم وإذا كان فوقه مسكن أو مستغل يتعذر تعظيمه وعن أبي يوسف رحمه الله أنه جوز في الوجهين حين قدم بغداد ورأي ضيق المنازل فكأنه اعتبر الضرورة، وعن محمد رحمه الله أنه حين دخل الري أجاز ذلك كله لما قلنا. (الهداية: ۲/٦٤٤، كتاب الوقف).

Imām Ḥasan ibn Ziyād (may Allah have mercy on him) narrated from Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) that if the lower storey is made a mosque and there is a dwelling above it, it becomes a mosque. The reason is that for a mosque to be a mosque it must be permanent and lasting, and the upper storey has no permanence, whereas the lower storey is the ground, which does have permanence.

The report transmitted from Imām Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him) is the exact opposite. He held that the mosque may be above, with a dwelling or income-producing shops below, because if a dwelling or shops are built above a mosque, the dignity and sanctity of the mosque will be impaired.

From Imām Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) it is reported that when he came and settled in Baghdad and saw the shortage of land alongside the dense population, and felt the need, he gave permission that whether the mosque is above or below, and even if the other storeys are put to personal use, the mosque will still be a legally valid mosque. Likewise it is reported of Imām Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him) that when he came and took up residence in Rayy and saw the hardship and constraint caused by the size of the population, he granted permission that whichever way the mosque was, above or below, and even if the other storeys were put to personal use, it would still be a legally valid mosque. It is as though the two disciples (may Allah have mercy on them) based the matter on need and necessity and granted this permission.

The researcher Ibn al-Humām (may Allah have mercy on him) sheds light on this and writes:

وهذا تعليل صحيح، لأنه تعليل بالضرورة. (فتح القدير: ٦/۲۳۵، ط: دار الفكر).

That is to say, this reasoning is sound, because necessity has been kept in view in it. So when a need or necessity of this kind arises, it may be considered in those particular circumstances. In ordinary circumstances, however, acting on the ẓāhir al-riwāyah is the stronger course, and it is also more in keeping with the principles of issuing fatwa, کما فی شرح عقود رسم المفتی۔

These statements of the two disciples (may Allah have mercy on them) are also recorded in Fatāwā Tātārkhāniyyah:

وفي رواية الحسن رحمه الله: يجوز السفل دون العلو، وعند محمد رحمه الله يجوز العلو أيضاً، وفي جامع الفتاوى: إذا كان السفل مملوكاً وفوقه مسجداً جاز وعن أبي يوسف رحمه الله أنه أجاز أن يكون الأسفل مسجداً، والأعلى ملكاً، وعن محمد رحمه الله أنه حين دخل الري ورأي ضيق الأمكنة جوز ذلك. (الفتاوى التاتارخانية: ۵/۸٤۳، أحكام المسجد).

(وكذا في الجوهرة النيرة: ۲/۲۵، ط: إمداديه، ملتان).

ʿAllāmah Ibn Nujaym al-Miṣrī (may Allah have mercy on him) declared these reports weak. See what al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq says:

وحاصله أن شرط كونه مسجداً أن يكون سفله وعلوه مسجداً لينقطع حق العبد عنه هذا هو ظاهر المذهب وهناك روايات ضعيفة مذكورة في الهداية. (البحر الرائق: ۵/۲۵۱، ط: كوئته). (وكذا في فتاوى الشامي: ٤/۳۵۸، سعيد).

In Tabyīn al-Ḥaqāʾiq, ʿAllāmah Fakhr al-Dīn Zaylaʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) has also mentioned these reports, and in the margin Imām Shihāb al-Dīn Shalabī writes:

وهذه الروايات كلها خلاف ظاهر الرواية. (حاشية تبيين الحقائق: ۳/۳۰۰، ط: إمداديه، ملتان).

The upshot is that these positions are weak and contrary to the ẓāhir al-riwāyah. So in ordinary circumstances the position adopted for fatwa will be acted upon; but in special circumstances, when there is a necessity such as the one mentioned in the question, there can be room to act on these positions, especially since the positions of the two disciples are also in accord with the principles of the school. As the legal maxims have it:

الضرورات تبيح المحظورات". (قواعد الفقه: ۸۹). والمشقة تجلب التيسير. (ص ۱۲۲). وغيره.

The jurists have granted permission to act on a weak position at a time of necessity.

Thus ʿAllāmah Ibn Nujaym al-Miṣrī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in his discussion of menstruation:

وفي معراج الدراية معزياً إلى فخر الأئمة: لو أفتي مفت بشيء من هذه الأقوال في مواضع الضرورة طلباً للتيسير كان حسناً. (البحر الرائق: ۱/۲۰۲، دار المعرفة).

(وكذا في فتاوى الشامي: ۱/۲۸۹، سعيد).

Although in acts of worship the position of the Imām is given preference as a general rule, at a time of necessity there is permission to give fatwa on the position of the two disciples. See what ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

لا يرجح قول صاحبيه أو أحدهما على قوله إلا لموجب، وهو إما لضعف دليل الإمام وإما للضرورة (شرح عقود رسم المفتي، ص ٤۱).

Muftī ʿAmīm al-Iḥsān al-Mujaddidī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

مسألة: لا يجوز العمل والإفتاء بالضعيف والمرجوح إلا عن ضرورة فلو أفتي في مواضع الضرورة طلباً للتيسير كان حسناً. (قواعد الفقه، ص ۵۷٦).

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him), in his discussion of purification, keeping in view the side of ease at a time of necessity and of widespread difficulty, says:

قال: وقال في شرح المنية: المعلوم من قواعد أئمتنا التسهيل في مواضع الضرورة والبلوي العامة كما في مسألة آبار الفلوات ونحوها. (فتاوى الشامي: ۱/۱۸۹، سعيد).

Ḥaḍrat Shāh Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) said that acting on the ẓāhir al-riwāyah is not binding, especially when a rare report is supported by hadith, in which case it is that report which should be adopted. See what he says in Fayḍ al-Bārī:

واعلم أن الروايات إذا اختلفت عن إمامنا في مسألة، فعامة مشايخنا يسلكون فيها مسلك الترجيح، فيأخذون بظاهر الرواية، ويتركون نادرها، وليس بسديد عندي، سيما إذا كانت الرواية النادرة تتأيد بالحديث، فإني أحمله على تلك الرواية، ولا أعبأ بكونها نادرة فإن الرواية إذا جاءت عن إمامنا لا بد أن يكون لها عنده دليل من حديث أو غيره (فيض الباري: ۱/۳۵۷، باب ما إذا ذكر في المسجد انه جنب خرج كما هو، كتاب الغسل).

It emerges from this that it is permissible to set the ẓāhir al-riwāyah aside for external reasons. In the same way, in our own time, in view of the circumstances and the need, if a report outside the ẓāhir al-riwāyah — one that is itself narrated from the Imām — is acted upon, there ought to be room for it.

Again, on the question of whether a ritual bath becomes obligatory when the organ is held back during a wet dream, the jurists have given fatwa at a time of need — particularly for a traveller and a guest — on the position of Imām Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him), so that people are not caught in hardship and difficulty. See:

قال في الدر: وبقوله يفتي في ضيف خاف ريبة أو استحيي وفي القهستاني التاتارخانية معزياً للنوازل: وبقول أبي يوسف رحمه الله نأخذ، لأنه أيسر على المسلمين، قلت: ولا سيما في الشتاء والسفر. وفي الشامية: وفي الذخيرة: إن الفقيه أبا الليث رحمه الله، وخلف بن أيوب رحمه الله أخذا بقول أبي يوسف رحمه الله، وفي جامع الفتاوى: إن الفتوى على قوله، قوله: قلت؛ ظاهره الميل إلى اختيار ما في النوازل فينبغي الإفتاء بقوله في مواضع الضرورة فقط، تأمل. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۱/۱٦۰، سعيد).

Muftī Jād al-Ḥaqq ʿAlī Jād al-Ḥaqq, the Mufti of the Arab Republic of Egypt, in Fatāwā Dār al-Iftāʾ al-Miṣriyyah, first set out the stronger position regarding mosques and then, in view of necessity, permitted the position of the two disciples to be adopted.

See the passage:

وعلى هذا إذا كانت هناك ضرورة تدعو إلى المشروع المسئول عنه فلا بأس بالأخذ بقول الصاحبين رحمه الله في الرواية المذكورة عنهما لأنها تتفق مع قواعد المذهب كقاعدة "الضرورات تبيح المحظورات"، وقاعدة: "المشقة تجلب التيسير". وغيرهما وهذا مقرر في قول الله عزوجل: ﴿وَمَا جَعَلَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ مِنْ حَرَجٍ﴾ [الحج: ۷۸] والله تعالى أعلم. (فتاوى دار الإفتاء المصرية: ۷/۱۵۵).

By way of a fitting conclusion, see the words of Amīr-e-Sharīʿat Ḥaḍrat Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām Qāsmī (may Allah have mercy on him).

After discussing the questions that fall outside the ẓāhir al-riwāyah, he says: The upshot, then, is that in ordinary circumstances:

1. The ẓāhir al-riwāyah is that if, at the time of building the mosque, the endower makes one storey a mosque and endows the other storey for the needs of the mosque or for its income, taking it out of his own ownership, then this mosque will be a legally valid mosque.

2. And if he makes one storey a mosque and keeps the other storey for his own personal use, the ẓāhir al-riwāyah is that it will not be a mosque.

3. According to the report of Ḥasan ibn Ziyād (may Allah have mercy on him), if the lower storey is made a mosque and the upper storey is kept for personal use, it will be valid in the view of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him).

4. From Imām Muḥammad ibn Ḥasan (may Allah have mercy on him) there is a report contrary to the ẓāhir al-riwāyah, namely that if the upper storey is made a mosque and the lower storey is kept for personal use, it will be valid.

In ordinary circumstances, acting on the ẓāhir al-riwāyah is, in the view of the present writer, the closest to sound jurisprudence, the most suited to the times, and fit to be given as fatwa, and it is easy to act upon.

All these positions and reports relate to ordinary circumstances. In special circumstances another position is also reported. When Imām Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) came and settled in Baghdad and saw the shortage of land in that densely populated city, and likewise when Imām Muḥammad ibn Ḥasan (may Allah have mercy on him) went and took up residence in Rayy and saw the constraint created in those newly settled cities by the size of their populations, both of these authorities gave the opinion that whether the mosque is above or below, and even if the other storeys are put to personal use, the mosque will still be a legally valid mosque. This position does not relate to ordinary, normal circumstances; these two great authorities adopted this opinion only in a situation of necessity and need.

Clearly this case falls under the heading of necessity and need, which may be considered in special circumstances. (مباحثِ فقہیہ، ص ۲٦۵، ۲٦٦)۔
