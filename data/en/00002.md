### TITLE
Using the word "Ṣāḥib" for Allah

### QUESTION
Can the word ṣāḥib be used for Allah Most High or not? Likewise, what is the position on using the word ḥaḍrat?

### ANSWER
Which words it is correct to use for Allah and which are not depends on customary usage, and since these words are not used for Allah in our customary speech, they should be avoided.

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Yūsuf Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

In the Urdu of an earlier age the words "Allāh Ṣāḥib says" were used, but in modern Urdu they have fallen out of use. In other words, in that earlier period this was regarded as a term of respect, but in the modern language it no longer carries enough respect for it to be used for Allah, or for the prophets, the Companions and the Successors. (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ۸/۳٤۳)۔

So, for example, we do not use the words "Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā" for the Prophet ﷺ, even though as far as the dictionary meaning goes there is nothing wrong with them. In exactly the same way, the word ṣāḥib should not be used for Allah. For Allah it is better to use the words سبحانہ و تعالی. Granted, "Allāh Ṣāḥib" was used in the past, but it later fell out of use. Besides, the use of respectful terms rests on custom. For instance, in customary speech barkhurdār is used for a son, although barkhurdār means "one who benefits". Similarly, in customary speech the word sarparast is used for a guardian and is considered a good word, although its literal meaning is "one whose head is worshipped". Since "Allāh Ṣāḥib" has fallen out of use in customary speech, it should not be used now.

In short, just as the respectful expressions handed down for the Prophet (blessings and peace be upon him) should be used, so also for Allah one should use only those words which have come down without a break from the pious predecessors or are well known.
