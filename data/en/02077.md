### TITLE
Prize bonds

### QUESTION
One form of prize bonds is that people each pay in 10 rand, and then one man or a few men receive a prize while everybody else's money is lost. That is one form of gambling. The second form is that the 10 rand each person paid in is returned to him, and some people receive a prize by way of a lucky draw. On the face of it there is no gambling in this, since no money is lost, and on the face of it there is no interest either, since the definition of interest is "الفضل المشروط الخالي عن العوض" ۔ Here no condition has been laid down with any individual that he will be given such and such an extra sum. What is the ruling of the sacred law on this second form? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
Both of these current forms of prize bonds are impermissible.

See what Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil states:

Some scholars have held prize bonds to be permissible, taking the view that here there is no suspension of ownership upon a risk (تعلیق التملیک علی الخطر), since whatever money a person has paid in he receives back in every case; so this is not gambling and therefore becomes permissible. This view, however, is not correct. Although gambling as such is not present here, interest is: for if a man's name comes out in the draw, then in return for the hundred rupees he paid, for example, he will receive one hundred thousand one hundred rupees.

Some have raised the doubt that this is not interest either, because when they bought the bond no agreement for a stipulated increase was made with them.

The answer to this doubt is that the increase is stipulated with the whole body of debtors. Although it is not stated in so many words that a profit will be paid on it, it is done in practice, and that practice is continuous and customary: when a man takes back his loan, the government gives him something more. So although there was no stipulation in words, it falls under "المعروف كالمشروط" ۔

What generally happens is that the government adds interest to every bondholder's bonds, and then all that interest is gathered together and distributed among individuals by a lucky draw. To put it another way, the interest is distributed in the form of gambling. Even though in the sacred law it is not gambling, since interest is not property in the first place, the spirit of gambling is present in it, and the gambling is being done upon interest: the interest of one man, or of a great many men, is put together and given to a single person by a draw. For this reason it is impermissible. (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ٤/۱۱ ۔ ۷۸) ۔

Fatāwā Bayyināt states:

Buying and selling prize bonds and taking the prize money that comes from them is impermissible and unlawful in the sacred law. (فتاوی بینات: ٤/۲۲۷) ۔

See further: (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۱/۲٦۳) ۔

In sum, the first form of prize bonds is forbidden and impermissible because it falls under gambling, and the second form is forbidden because interest is involved in it. It is therefore obligatory and necessary to keep away from both forms.

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

There is a scheme made up of 250 to 300 members. Each member pays 300 rupees a month, and every month there is a draw. Whoever's name comes out is given 15,000 rupees, or some other item of the same value, and no further instalments are taken from him.

In this scheme too there are two possibilities: either every member is certain to receive the prize money, or there is a risk of the original sum being lost. If receiving it is certain, then this is "interest", because the aim of the man paying in the money is that his money should bring him more money, and those running the scheme also hold out this inducement and temptation — that the member should take part in the draw and obtain an extra sum when his name comes out. And if there is a risk of the original sum being lost, then it is gambling. Interest and gambling are both unlawful, and keeping away from them is obligatory. (جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام: ۱/۲٦٤) ۔

From the detailed fatwa of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Niẓām al-Dīn Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him), however, which is recorded in (منتخباتِ نظام الفتاوی: ۱/۱۹۵), it appears that this second form, in which the whole sum is returned, does not come under interest, and that taking it is permissible and correct.
