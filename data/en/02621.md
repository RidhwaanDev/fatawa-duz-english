### TITLE
An explanation of resembling the unbelievers in dress and the like, and its ruling

### QUESTION
Please explain what is meant by resembling the unbelievers in dress and the like, and what its ruling is.

### ANSWER
عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: "من تشبه بقوم، فهو منهم. (أخرجه أبو داود، رقم ٤۰۳۱، وابن حبان في صحيحه، وابن أبي شيبة، رقم: ۱۹٤۰۱، والبيهقي في شعب الإيمان: ۱۱۹۹).

قال العراقي: أبو داود من حديث ابن عمر بسند صحيح. (۸۵۱). وقال ابن حجر: إسناده حسن. (فتح الباري: ۱۰/۲۷۱). وأورده السيوطي في الجامع الصغير (۸۵۹۳) ورمز له بالحسن، وضعفه السخاوي والزركشي وغيرهما، ولكن له شواهد.

قلت: إسناده حسن. فيه: عبد الرحمن بن ثابت بن ثوبان، قال ابن حجر: صدوق. وقال يحيى بن معين: ليس به بأس. قال أبو حاتم: ثقة. يشوبه شيء من القدر، وهو مستقيم الحديث. قال أبو داود: كان فيه سلامة، وكان مجاب الدعوة، وليس به بأس. قال النسائي: ضعيف. قال أحمد بن حنبل: لم يكن بالقوي في الحديث. قال الهيثمي: قد وثقه غير واحد، وضعفه غيرهم.

راجع: (تهذيب الكمال للمزي: ۱۷/۱٤، وتقريب التهذيب، وتهذيب التهذيب ٦/۱۳۷، ومجمع الزوائد).

وحديث حذيفة أخرجه الطبراني في الأوسط، (۸۳۲۷)، والبزار (۲۹٦٦)، وقال الهيثمي: فيه على بن غراب وقد وثقه غير واحد وضعفه بعضهم وبقية رجاله ثقات.

وللمزيد راجع: (كشف الخفاء، رقم: ۲٤۳٦، والمقاصد الحسنة، رقم: ۱۱۰۱، ونصب الراية: ٤/۳٤۷).

Mullā ʿAlī al-Qārī (may Allah have mercy on him) says in al-Mirqāt:

أي من شبه نفسه بالكفار مثلاً في اللباس وغيره أو بالفساق أو الفجار أو بأهل التصوف والصلحاء الأبرار فهو منهم أي في الإثم والخير، قال الطيبي: هذا عام في الخَلق والخُلق والشعار ولما كان الشعار أظهر في الشبه ذكر في هذا الباب، قلت: بل الشعار هو المراد بالتشبه لا غير فإن الخلق الصوري لا يتصور فيه التشبه والخلق المعنوي لا يقال فيه التشبه بل هو التخلق. (مرقاة المفاتيح: ۸/۲۵۵، إمدادية، ملتان).

ʿAllāmah al-Munāwī says in Fayḍ al-Qadīr:

"من تشبه بقوم" أي تزياً في ظاهره بزيهم وفي تعرفه بفعلهم وفي تخلقه بخلقهم وسار بسيرتهم وهديهم في ملبسهم وبعض أفعالهم

"فهو منهم" وقيل المعني من تشبه بالصالحين وهو من اتباعهم يكرم كما يكرمون ومن تشبه بالفساق يهان ويخذل كهم،

وقال ابن تيمية: هذا الحديث أقل أحواله أن يقتضي تحريم التشبه بأهل الكتاب وإن كان ظاهره يقتضي كفر المتشبه بهم

الخ. (فيض القدير: ٦/۱۰٤).

Ḥakīm al-Ummat (may Allah have mercy on him) says: This ruling is found in the text of the Qur'an itself, where Allah Most High says:

﴿ولا تركنوا إلى الذين ظلموا فتمسكم النار﴾

Translation: Do not incline towards those who have done wrong, lest the Fire touch you. From this we learn that inclining towards the people of falsehood is unlawful. To this may be added a further principle: resemblance does not come about without inclining and a leaning of the heart. First there is a sense of the greatness of that thing in the heart, then it comes to be regarded as fine, and then there is an inclination towards it. So when leaning and inclining are unlawful, resembling is unlawful too. (Al-Iḍāfāt: 8/326, as cited in Fiqh Ḥanafī ke Uṣūl o Ḍawābiṭ, p. 148).

In another place he says:

The definition of resemblance, and how to recognise when it has been removed: the criterion is that wherever something is the distinctive style of a particular group, and it is known that this thing belongs to the unbelievers, and the mind turns to it as something peculiar to the unbelievers, then there is resemblance; otherwise there is not. (Ḥusn al-ʿAzīz: 3/213, as cited in Fiqh Ḥanafī ke Uṣūl o Ḍawābiṭ, p. 150).

The way to recognise it is that on seeing these things there should be no misgiving in the minds of ordinary people that this is the style of such-and-such a people; but so long as this distinctiveness remains, it will be forbidden. (Ḥayāt al-Muslimīn, p. 224, as cited in Fiqh Ḥanafī ke Uṣūl o Ḍawābiṭ, p. 150).

The kinds of resemblance and their rulings:

(1) Resembling the unbelievers in beliefs and acts of worship is unbelief, and in religious ceremonies it is unlawful.

(2) Resemblance in social life, in acts of worship and in the distinctive marks of a people is disliked to the degree of prohibition.

(3) Things that are neither the national style nor the religious style of other peoples, even if they are their invention, and things of general need such as matches, a watch, new weapons or new forms of exercise for which our own people have no substitute — using these is permissible. It is permissible to adopt the ways of other peoples in inventions, administration, armaments and the equipment of war. Yes, where the Muslims already possess a substitute for something, resembling them in it is disliked in the lighter sense.

(4) To adopt the style of those Muslims who are openly sinful or given to innovation is also sinful.

(Ḥayāt al-Muslimīn and Anfās-e-ʿĪsā, as cited in Fiqh Ḥanafī ke Uṣūl o Ḍawābiṭ, p. 151).

Muftī Kifāyatullāh (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

What is meant by "من تشبه بقوم فهو منهم" is that if resemblance is adopted with a people in something that is peculiar to that people or is a special badge of theirs, then such resemblance is impermissible. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 9/169, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

In another place he says:

Where the sari is part of the dress of Muslim women themselves, it is permissible; and where the sari is not customary among Muslims but belongs only to the dress of non-Muslim women, there it is disliked. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 9/160, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

In yet another place he writes:

An English hairstyle, a hat, and a coat and trousers — each of these is enough for resemblance; but the ruling of resemblance applies only where a person seeing it is led to suppose that this person belongs to that community — for instance, that someone wearing a hat is taken to be a Christian. So things that have come to be used generally among other people as well — boots, trousers, a coat, for example — the aspect of resemblance in them remains weak and slight. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 9/164, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

Regarding a coat and trousers, Muftī Maḥmūd Ḥasan Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

Nowadays there is not so much severity in this; this much is certain, that in these parts this is not the dress of the righteous, so one should avoid it — it is at the level of dislikedness.

In the annotation it is recorded:

Although the pant and shirt (coat and trousers) and the English hairstyle have nowadays become widespread among Muslims, they are still regarded as English dress. In short, even if there is no resemblance to the unbelievers, there is no doubt about resemblance to the openly sinful, so avoiding such dress is necessary. This detail regarding trousers applies where they do not reveal the shape and size of the parts that must be covered. If the trousers are so tight and close-fitting that the shape and size of the limbs is visible — as such trousers have become very common nowadays — then wearing them, displaying them to people, and looking at them are all impermissible. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah maʿa al-Taʿlīqāt: 19/280, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

In another place it is recorded:

In a region where this is a badge of the unbelievers and the openly sinful, it should be avoided; and where it is not a badge and everyone wears it, this ruling does not apply. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 19/281, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

Qāmūs al-Fiqh states:

It is disliked for Muslims to adopt a dress that creates a likeness to non-Muslims. It is related concerning ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him) that the Prophet ﷺ said while delivering a sermon: "O people, keep away from the dress of the monks; whoever adopts monasticism and resemblance is not of me." It is related from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar and Ḥudhayfah (may Allah be pleased with them) that whoever adopts the resemblance of a people is one of them.

Clearly, resemblance has various degrees. Resemblance in the religious symbols of non-Muslims is a grave sin — so much so that some jurists have ruled that tying the zunnār and the like amounts to unbelief. Although in the view of the leading scholars a person will not be declared an unbeliever on that account, this attitude of the jurists shows how unlawful and how severely forbidden such resemblance is. As for a dress that is not connected with religious ideas and concepts, if that dress becomes so widespread that seeing a person in it does not give rise to the thought that he belongs to such-and-such a people, then the resemblance in it becomes slight.

A dress that is worn because of the requirements of climate and geography and is not connected with religious symbols — even if it is the invention of some particular people — involves no resemblance in its use. (Qāmūs al-Fiqh: 4/575).

See further: (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/344).

So if someone in a non-Muslim country wears a coat-and-trousers type of dress because his employment requires it, or because of the hardship of repeated searches, or for some other particular consideration, then he should make its cut somewhat different — for example, the coat or shirt should be long enough to cover the buttocks, and the trouser-like shalwār should be above the ankles.
