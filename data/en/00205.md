### TITLE
Not being saved from harm despite reciting the supplications for protection

### QUESTION
What is the reason that a person is not saved from someone's harm even though he recites the supplications for protection from harm?

### ANSWER
A good opinion of Allah and certainty have a great deal to do with a supplication being accepted. The Prophet ﷺ said that you should pray to Allah in such a way that you are certain of being answered.

عن عبد الله بن عمر رضي الله عنه أن رسول الله ﷺ قال: القلوب أوعية بعضها من بعض فإذا سألتم الله عز وجل أيها الناس فسلوه وأنتم موقنون بالإجابة فإن الله لا يستجيب لعبد دعاه عن ظهر قلب غافل. [رواه أحمد وإسناده حسن]. (مجمع الزوائد: ۱۰/۱٤۸).

Another hadith says that every supplication a servant makes is answered, provided he does not become hasty. He was asked what haste meant, and he said: haste is that a man starts to think, "I have prayed a great deal and my prayers are simply not being answered," and, worn out, gives up praying altogether. (Reported by Muslim, no. 2735)

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ilyās (may Allah have mercy on him) says in one of his recorded sayings: "The reality of supplication is that you place your needs before an exalted court. So the more exalted that court is, the more one should turn one's heart towards it when praying and utter the words with humility and pleading. And one should pray with certainty and firm conviction that it will surely be answered, because the One being asked is exceedingly generous and noble, merciful to His servants, and the treasuries of the earth and the heavens are all in the grasp of His power." (Malfūẓāt of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muḥammad Ilyās, may Allah have mercy on him, p. 55)

Āp kē Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal states:

Question: Why do the transmitted supplications have no effect when we recite them?

Answer: What the Prophet ﷺ said is entirely true, but sometimes when we recite these supplications we do not have the presence of mind that we ought to have, and sometimes our own evil deeds get in the way of the object. It is like this: physicians describe the properties of a medicine that has been tested many times over, yet sometimes the desired effect of the medicine does not appear. That does not mean that the medicine has no effect; the reason is that some impediment has come in the way. (Āp kē Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal: 8/329)

It should also be borne in mind that the acceptance of a supplication takes more than one form. There is a hadith in Musnad Aḥmad:

"The Prophet ﷺ said: whenever a Muslim servant makes a supplication, Allah certainly grants him, through the blessing of that supplication, one of three things: either He gives him the very thing he asked for, or He stores that supplication up for him as a treasure for the hereafter, or through the blessing of that supplication He turns away some calamity from him."

Or a person is given something better than what he asked for. The mother of Maryam, for example, had asked for a child to serve the Sacred House in Jerusalem, and she was given Maryam, who was far superior to a male servant.

عن أبي سعيد أن النبي ﷺ قال: ما من مسلم يدعو بدعوة ليس فيها إثم ولا قطيعة رحم إلا أعطاه الله بها إحدى ثلاث: إما أن تجعل له دعوته وإما أن يدخرها له في الآخرة وإما أن يصرف عنه السوء مثلها، قالوا: إذا نكثر، قال: الله أكثر. (أخرجه الإمام أحمد في مسنده، رقم: ۱۱۱٤۹). قال الشيخ شعيب الأرناؤوط: إسناده جيد.
قال الهيثمي في المجمع (۱۰/۱٤۸): رواه أحمد وأبو يعلي بنحوه والبزار والطبراني في الأوسط ورجال أحمد وأبي يعلي وأحد إسنادي البزار رجاله رجال الصحيح غير علي بن علي الرفاعي وهو ثقة.

The upshot is that supplications certainly are answered, but acceptance takes different forms. It is therefore the servant's duty to keep asking Allah, and to rest completely content that Allah will deal with him in whatever way is best for him.
