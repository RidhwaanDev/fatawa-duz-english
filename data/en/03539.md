### TITLE
Keeping restaurants open during the day in the blessed month of Ramaḍān

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to open a restaurant in the blessed month, or not — given that the majority of the customers are non-Muslims, or that Muslims and non-Muslims are mixed together? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In Muslim areas, restaurants should be kept closed during the daytime in the blessed month of Ramaḍān without exception, out of respect for Ramaḍān, so that helping one another in sin does not come about. And a Muslim whose restaurant is in a non-Muslim area should also keep it closed out of respect for the blessed month of Ramaḍān. It is like this: if a man comes from Saudi Arabia to India or Pakistan and his thirty fasts are already complete, he will still keep the fast in order to be in step with the people. In the same way, if a traveller becomes a resident, or a child reaches puberty during the day, he will abstain; and if a menstruating woman becomes pure during the day, she will abstain. In just the same way, Muslims too should honour Ramaḍān and should keep their restaurants closed.

Consider what the Glorious Qurʾān says:

﴿وتعاونوا على البرّ والتقوى ولا تعاونوا على الإثم والعدوان﴾. [المائدة: ۲].

The noble ḥadīth says:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ صومكم يوم تصومون وأضحاكم يوم تضحون. (السنن الكبرى للبيهقي، رقم: ۸٤٦۷، كذا في الدارقطني، رقم ۳٤، ومصنف عبد الرزاق، رقم ۷۳۰٤).

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

لو صام رائي هلال رمضان وأكمل العدة لم يفطر إلا مع الإمام لقوله عليه السلام صومكم يوم تصومون وفطركم يوم تفطرون

والناس لم يفطروا في مثل هذا اليوم فوجب أن لا يفطر، نهر. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/ ۳۸٤، سعيد).

Fatāwā Hindiyyah states:

الصبي إذا بلغ في بعض النهار وأسلم الكافر وأفاق المجنون وطهرت الحائض وقدم المسافر مع قيام الأهلية يجب عليه الإمساك بقية اليوم. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۲۱٤).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states: There are several principles governing when it becomes obligatory to abstain from the things that break the fast, and under each of them there are some subsidiary rulings. The first principle is this: a person upon whom the fast of Ramaḍān became obligatory because he possessed the capacity for obligation, but who was overtaken at the beginning of the day (before the break of dawn) by some excuse on account of which fasting was not obligatory upon him at that moment, or on account of which it was permissible for him not to fast, and then that excuse was removed during the day and he came into a state such that, had he been in that state at the beginning of the day — that is, before the break of dawn — fasting would have been obligatory upon him: such a person must, for the remaining part of that day, abstain from food, drink and sexual intercourse like those who are fasting. For example, if a child who had not reached puberty reached puberty just as dawn broke, or in some part of the day after the break of dawn; or an unbeliever became a Muslim; or a madman regained his senses; or a woman in menstruation or in post-natal bleeding became pure; or a traveller returned from his journey — upon such a person it is obligatory to abstain from the things that break the fast for the remaining part of that day, so that in all these cases the sanctity of the time may be upheld as far as it possibly can be. So abstaining from the things that break the fast, in imitation of those who are fasting, is a way of giving the time its due. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh, vol. 3, p. 359).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī ʿAbd al-Raḥīm Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) says: Out of respect for the blessed month it is necessary to keep restaurants for food and drink closed during the day, whoever the people eating and drinking may be. This blessed month is one of the symbols of Allah, and honouring the symbols of Allah is necessary. The Glorious Qurʾān says:

﴿ومن يعظم شعائر الله فإنها من تقوى القلوب﴾،

So if a person opens his restaurant during the day to serve food or tea, the sanctity of the blessed month will not remain, and the one who opens it will be sinful. However, if in the evening, a little before the time of breaking the fast, people buy items for iftar such as ḥalīm, firnī, samosas and the like and take them home, there is no harm in that, since it is provision for breaking the fast. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 4/81, al-Iḥsān, Deoband).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Salmān Ṣāḥib Manṣūrpūrī writes: Keeping restaurants and paan shops open during the hours of fasting in the blessed month of Ramaḍān is against the respect due to Ramaḍān. In Muslim neighbourhoods shops of this kind ought to remain closed, so that those who do not fast are not encouraged. (Kitāb al-Nawāzil: 17/392, al-Markaz al-ʿIlmī, Moradabad).

Out of respect for the blessed month of Ramaḍān it is necessary to keep a thirty-first fast. Consider what Kitāb al-Masāʾil states:

If a person comes from Saudi Arabia to India during Ramaḍān and takes up residence there, and his thirty fasts are completed here, he will not give up fasting until the moon of ʿĪd is sighted in India, even if he has to keep thirty-one or thirty-two fasts. (Kitāb al-Masāʾil: 2/138, Nuʿaymiyyah, Deoband; and likewise in Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 8/225, Saeed; Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 2/744, Zamzam edition; and Rozē kē Masāʾil kā Insāʾiklopīḍiyā, p. 211, Bayt al-ʿAmmār).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
