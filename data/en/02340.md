### TITLE
The ruling on machine slaughter

### QUESTION
What is the ruling on machine slaughter? In this mechanised age of industrial progress man is carrying out more and more of his work with the help of machines rather than with his own hands. In some countries the procedure of this machine slaughter is that a great many animals are stood beneath the machine and, at a single press of a button, all their necks are cut. This electric machine keeps running all day long and, as a result, chickens keep being cut. The matter to be determined is this: when the neck is cut by a revolving blade in an electric machine, will the animal be regarded as slaughtered or not? Furthermore, if the person pressing the button is a Muslim or a scripturist and presses it saying "Bismillāh, wallāhu akbar", will the slaughtered animal be lawful or not? بینوا توجروا.

### ANSWER
The opinions of the senior scholars (raḥimahumullāh) differ regarding machine slaughter. (1) Muftī Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib and others hold that machine slaughter is lawful subject to the details set out below.

Consider first the explanation and details of the first view:

The first point is that severing the animal's neck completely — whether with a hand-held knife or by means of a machine — is contrary to the sharʿī method of slaughter and is disliked. As for the lawfulness of the meat of an animal slaughtered in this way, the detail is as follows. If, at the press of the button, the blade came down upon the necks of all the animals at one and the same moment, and a Muslim or a scripturist pressed the button after reciting Bismillāh, then this one Bismillāh sufficed for them all and the slaughtered animals are lawful. But if the necks were cut one after another, then this Bismillāh will suffice for the first animal only; for the remaining animals it will not be valid, and those animals will have the ruling of carrion.

Sometimes, for one reason or another, a hen's neck is not cut at all, and sometimes it is cut so slightly that it becomes doubtful whether all its vessels have been severed. In both these cases it will not be called a sharʿī slaughter; rather it will have the ruling of carrion.

Consider the following. Al-Hidāyah states:

من بلغ بالسكين النخاع أو قطع الرأس كره له ذلك وتؤكل ذبيحته

أما الكراهة فلما روي عن النبي ﷺ أنه نهي أن تنخع الشاة إذا ذبحت

وإن ذبح الشاة من قفاها فبقيت حية حتى قطع العروق حل لتحقق الموت بما هو ذكاة ويكره. (الهداية: ٤/٤۳۸، كتاب الذبائح).
(وكذا في الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٦/۲۹٦، سعيد، وبدائع الصنائع: ۵/٤۲، سعيد).

الاختيار لتعليل المختار
states:

والذكاة اختيارية، وهي الذبح في الحلق واللبة؛ واضطرارية، وهي الجرح في أي موضع اتفق؛ وشرطهما التسمية، وكون الذابح مسلماً أو كتابياً، فإن ترك التسمية ناسياً حل، وإن أضجع شاة وسمي فذبح غيرها بتلك التسمية لم تؤكل، وإن ذبح بشفرة أخري أكل

ويكره أن يبلغ بالسكين النخاع، أو يقطع الرأس وتؤكل. (الاختيار لتعليل المختار: ۱۰. ۱۱، ۱۳، كتاب الذبائح، دار الكتب العلمية، بيروت).

الفتاوى الهندية
states:

ولو أضجع إحدى الشاتين على الأخرى تكفي تسمية واحدة إذا ذبحهما بإمرار واحد، ولو جمع العصافير في يده فذبح وسمي وذبح آخر على إثره ولم يسم لم يحل الثاني ولو أمر السكين على الكل جاز بتسمية واحدة كذا في خزانة المفتين. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۲۸۹، وكذا في تحفة الملوك: ۲/۱۷۰۰).

The meaning of this passage is not that the machine may keep running all day long and that a single tasmiyah at the first press of the button will suffice. Rather it means that only those animals which are actually present upon the machine's blade at the moment the button is pressed, and over which the blade passes at one and the same time, will be lawful. As for the animals that come along afterwards and are cut by that blade, the tasmiyah of the one who pressed the button will not suffice for them. Furthermore, it is also necessary that the slaughter take place immediately after the tasmiyah; if an appreciable interval occurs, then too the slaughtered animal will not be lawful.

Consider the following. Al-Jawharah al-Nayyirah states:

ولو أضجع شاة وسمي وكلمه إنسان أو استسقي ماء فشرب أو شحذ السكين قليلاً ثم ذبح على تلك التسمية الأولي أجزأه وأما إذا طال الحديث أو أخذ في عمل آخر واشتغل به ثم ذبح بتلك التسمية الأولي لم تؤكل. (الجوهرة النيرة: ۲/۲۷۵، ط: إمدادية، ملتان).

وفي المحيط البرهاني: وليس في ذلك تقدير بل ينظر فيه إلى العادة.

إن استكثر الناس في العادة يكون كثيراً وإن كان يعد قليلاً فهو قليل. (المحيط البرهاني: ٦/٤٦۵، الفصل الرابع فيما يتعلق بالتسمية على الذبيح، مكتبه رشيدية).

The summary of the detailed fatwa of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (raḥimahullāh) on machine slaughter is as follows.

He says: The method of slaughter customary in the cities of Europe is contrary to the sharīʿah and is a cause of sin. Muslims should avoid it so far as they are able and should put a stop to its spread in their own countries. As for the Muslims living in the regions of Europe who are unable to change this method and who in any case have need of meat, it will be permissible for them to use this meat subject to the conditions set out below; and if even one of these conditions is not met, it will be unlawful.

(1) The person carrying out the slaughter by means of the machine must be a Muslim, a Christian or a Jew.

(2) At the moment of bringing the machine's blade down to the animals' necks he must have pronounced the name of Allah alone,
"بسم الله والله أكبر".
(It has been mentioned earlier that Bismillāh alone also suffices; indeed, in the view of Shaykh al-Islām Abū al-Ḥusayn ʿAlī al-Saghdī this is preferable.)

(3) The animals upon whose necks this blade fell at one and the same time must be kept distinct and separate; the animals upon which the blade fell later, and which are dead, must not have had their meat mixed with the meat of the first animals.

But it is obvious that it is not easy for Muslims who come from outside and who live in different regions to know that these conditions have been fulfilled; therefore abstention is better. واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم. (Taken from Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām: 3/107.)

For further details on machine slaughter, see the following books: Aḥkām al-Dhabāʾiḥ; Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil, volume two; Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām, volume three; Muntakhabāt-e Niẓām al-Fatāwā: 1/369 and 404–407; and Ḥalāl o Ḥarām, pp. 137–144.

The second view:

The second opinion is that of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Maḥmūd Ṣāḥib, Shaykh al-Ḥadīth of Madrasah Qāsim al-ʿUlūm, Multan. His detailed fatwa follows.

He says: My dear sir! I hold that even if the man who presses the button is a Muslim, and even if he recites the tasmiyah as he presses it, the machine slaughter that is current cannot be called lawful. Rather it is simply carrion.

Consider this: all that the man pressing the button has done is that the connection between the electric power and the machine had been broken, and he removed the barrier that stood between the two and joined the connection once more — that is all. In reality it is the electric current that drives the machine's blade and cuts the animal's throat, and not the motive force of a Muslim's hand; and this cutting of the throat is the act of the electric power and of the machine, not the act of that Muslim.

Now in voluntary slaughter it is a condition that the act of the slaughterer (his cutting of the throat with his own hand) and the effectiveness of his motion be present. Here, however, the act of the one pressing the button is nothing more than the removal of an obstacle. How can the act of slaughtering be ascribed to the one who merely removes an obstacle? And how can he be called the slaughterer?

Understand this by way of the following examples. (1) A Magian took a knife in his hand and wished to slaughter an animal, but someone seized his hand and prevented him from slaughtering. Now a Muslim, saying "Bismillāh, Allāhu akbar", pulls away the hand of the one who was restraining him and frees the Magian's hand, whereupon the Magian at once draws the knife across the animal's neck. Will this slaughtered animal become lawful?

Observe that in this example it is a Muslim who performed the act of removing the obstacle, and he did so having recited the tasmiyah, and he is himself qualified to slaughter. But since the one who actually performed the slaughter, and whose motion was the effective one, is the Magian, the ruling of unlawfulness was necessarily passed upon this slaughtered animal by looking at the real mover and effective agent, and no account was taken of the act of the one who removed the obstacle.

(2) In the same way, suppose a sharp-edged instrument — a knife, for instance — is hanging suspended from a rope above, and directly beneath it stands a hen, a kid, or some other animal. Now if a Muslim recites the tasmiyah and cuts the rope, and that instrument falls by its own natural weight and cuts the animal's throat, will this slaughtered animal be lawful? Will this act of slaughter be ascribed to that Muslim who removed the obstacle, and will he be called the slaughterer of the animal and the animal be called the slaughtered animal of a Muslim?

If in these two examples the ruling upon the slaughtered animal is not one of lawfulness — and this animal is certainly not lawful — then how can the ruling of lawfulness be passed upon animals slaughtered by machines? And what is the difference between the two?

The second point worth pondering is this: even if this reality is set aside and it is conceded for a moment that pressing the button is an effective and voluntary act, still the act of pressing the button comes to an end the very moment the button is pressed. At the time when the machine is running and cutting the throats, his act is not present at all. The machine goes on running and throats go on being cut, so that he has finished his act before ever the throats are cut.

This state of affairs is tolerated in the sharīʿah in the case of necessitated slaughter, for the act of the archer comes to an end the moment he releases the arrow, and at the time the arrow strikes his act is to all appearances no longer present. But in this case the sharīʿah, solely on account of the excuse of necessity, has attached the striking of the arrow to the archer and declared him to be the slaughterer. In reality his act is only the "shooting" — that is all. Indeed, it is not even necessary that the archer remain qualified at the moment the arrow strikes, so long as he was qualified at the time of the "shooting". Imām Abū Bakr al-Kāsānī writes in Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ:

ولو رمي أو أرسل وهو مسلم ثم ارتد أو كان حلالاً فأحرم قبل الإصابة وأخذ الصيد يحل ولو كان مرتداً ثم أسلم وسمي لا يحل لأن المعتبر وقت الرمي والإرسال فتراعي الأهلية عند ذلك. (بدائع الصنائع: ٤/۱۷۲، ط: إحياء التراث العربي).

Likewise Al-Hidāyah states:

ولأن الكلب والبازي آلة والذبح لا يحصل بمجرد الآلة إلا بالاستعمال وذلك فيهما بالإرسال فنزل منزلة الرمي وإمرار السكين. (الهداية: ٤/۵۰۰).

This is precisely the fundamental difference between necessitated slaughter and voluntary slaughter: in voluntary slaughter it is the drawing of the knife that is itself the act of slaughter, whereas in necessitated slaughter the shooting of the arrow and the releasing of a trained hunting animal stand, in the eyes of the sharīʿah, in place of the act of slaughter.

Observe too that Imām al-Shāfiʿī (raḥimahullāh) also lays down "the human act" as a condition in voluntary slaughter. In Kitāb al-Umm, vol. 2, p. 198, he says:

والذكاة وجهان وجه فيما قدر عليه الذبح والنحر وفيما لم يقدر عليه ما ناله الإنسان بسلاح بيده أو رميه بيده فهي عمل يده وما أحل الله عز وجل من الجوارح المعلمات التي تأخذ بفعل الإنسان كما يصيب السهم، فأما الحفرة فإنها ليست واحداً من ذا كان فيها سلاح أو لم يكن ولو أن رجلاً نصب سيفاً أو رمحاً ثم اضطر صيداً فأصابه فزكاه لم يحل أكله لا نها ذكاة بغير فعل أحد.

And there is no doubt that the cutting of animals' throats by an electric machine is certainly not the act of a man, nor has the strength of his hand any part in it. This is precisely why no one possessing even the slightest understanding can call it the act of a man — and this is why it is called machine slaughter.

I therefore respectfully request Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (raḥimahullāh) to review this fatwa and to correct it. (فتاوی بینات، جلدِ چہارم، ص ۵٤۰ ۔ ۵٤۵، کتاب الذبائح و الاضحیہ).

The upshot is that in the view of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Maḥmūd Ṣāḥib (raḥimahullāh) a sharʿī slaughter is not realised; and this is the correct view, and we concur with this fatwa. Accordingly it is obligatory and necessary to abstain entirely from machine slaughter. Moreover, it is recorded in the introduction to Fatāwā Muftī Maḥmūd Ṣāḥib Multānī (raḥimahullāh), p. 114, that Ḥaḍrat Muftī Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (raḥimahullāh) had retracted his fatwa. واللہ تعالی اعلم بالصواب.

A sound and unquestionable alternative to machine slaughter:

The blade should be removed from this machine and, in its place, a number of Muslims or People of the Book should be stationed; then, as the hens pass before them, each one of them should in turn recite the tasmiyah over each hen and slaughter it in the sharʿī manner.

Note: There are several other defects in machine slaughter as well — for example, the stunning brought about by the electric current, the immersion in hot water for cleaning, and so on. But since these details have already been mentioned under the question of "Rainbow Chicken", it was thought unsuitable to mention them again, so as to avoid undue length; anyone who desires these details may refer to that place.

An objection to the fatwa of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Maḥmūd Ṣāḥib (raḥimahullāh), and its answer:

The objection: Some people used to raise the following objection against the fatwa of Ḥaḍrat Muftī Maḥmūd Ṣāḥib (raḥimahullāh). The Muftī Ṣāḥib said that the machine is the direct agent and that the man is merely the remover of an obstacle, so that the animal is the machine's slaughtered animal and not the man's, and is therefore unlawful. Yet the jurists have accepted slaughter by fire and have given a verdict that it is lawful — and there too the man merely performs the act of applying the fire while the act of slaughter is performed by the fire, which stands between him and the animal. Despite this, why did the jurists give a verdict that the slaughtered animal is lawful?

The answer: When this objection was placed before Ḥaḍrat Muftī Maḥmūd Ṣāḥib (raḥimahullāh), he smiled and said: This is a strange thing — that an animal should be made to stand there and a fire lit, and the animal thereby become slaughtered! That would only scorch the animal; its vessels would certainly not be severed. The verdict of the jurists that an animal is lawful by slaughter with fire means that a blunt piece of iron is heated red-hot in fire and then drawn across the animal's throat, so that by the fierce heat of that fire all the animal's vessels are severed and slaughter is duly realised.

This incident was related by Ḥaḍrat Muftī Walī Ḥasan Ṣāḥib (raḥimahullāh); Ḥaḍrat ʿAllāmah Banūrī (raḥimahullāh) also greatly approved of this answer.
