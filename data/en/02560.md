### TITLE
Heart transplantation

### QUESTION
Is heart transplantation permissible or not? Also, if there is a difference of opinion, please present the evidence of both parties, and record the view that Muftī Riḍāʾ al-Ḥaqq gives preference to. بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
It should be understood that there are two differing views and positions among the scholars on the transplantation of human organs. Some hold that it is impermissible, while others hold that it is permissible. In our times a large body of scholars, in the light of established necessity and various proofs, has given preference to its permissibility. The details of the evidence of both parties have already been given earlier; they may be consulted there.

However, removing one person's heart and fitting it into another amounts to killing that person, so permission cannot be given for heart transplantation. As for organs other than the heart and the brain, upon which a person's life does not depend — for example an eye, one kidney and so on — there is room for difference of opinion regarding them, and one may act upon the view of those who permit it.

But if it is removed immediately after death, or, as the doctors put it, after brain death has occurred, so that there is no suspicion of killing, then according to the view of those who permit it, heart transplantation will be allowed, as is current practice in our times.

For further detail see: (Majallah Fiqh Islāmī, compiled by Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām Qāsimī (may Allah have mercy on him): 1/169–248, "Transplantation of organs").

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
