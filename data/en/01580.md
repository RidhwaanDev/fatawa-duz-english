### TITLE
Reconciling the hadiths on the contagion of disease

### QUESTION
Reconciling the hadiths on the contagion of disease.

### ANSWER
Two kinds of hadith are found on the question of whether disease is contagious: some deny contagion, while others indicate that it does occur.

For example, the hadith "لا عدوی ولا طیرۃ الخ" (رواه البخاري: ۲/۸۵۰/۵٤۸۸، باب الجذام) gives the sense that disease is not contagious. The hadith "فر من المجذوم فرارک من الأسد" (رواه البخاري: ۲/۸۵۰/۵٤۸۸، باب الجذام. وكذا في مسند احمد برقم ۹۳٤۵) — that is, "flee from a leper as you would flee from a lion" — shows that disease is contagious. Likewise "لا یوردن ممرض علی مصح" (رواه البخاري: ۲/۸۵۹/۵۵٤۵، باب لا هامة) — that is, a sick camel should not be brought to a healthy one — also points to contagion. Again, when taking the pledge of allegiance the Prophet ﷺ did not touch a leper but took his pledge from a distance; yet elsewhere it is reported of a leper that the Prophet ﷺ ate a meal with him and said, "ثقۃ باللّٰہ وتوکلاً علیہ" — that is, out of confidence in Allah and reliance upon Him (رواه أبو داود في الطيرة. والترمذي في باب ما جاء في الأكل مع المجذوم. وابن ماجه في باب الجذام).

The scholars of hadith have reconciled these reports in several ways. The easiest of them, and the one that settles most readily in the mind, is that some diseases are indeed contagious, but their contagion takes place by the command of Allah. In the age of ignorance people believed that certain things possessed an effect inherent in themselves, in which the command of Allah had no part — as when they held the stars to govern a person's fortune and thought Allah's command unnecessary. The hadith "لا عدوی" is therefore meant to deny that belief: disease is not contagious of itself, but by the command of Allah. This is why, when a Bedouin said after hearing "لا عدوی" that they bring a healthy camel to a mangy one and it falls ill, the Prophet ﷺ replied, "Then who gave the mange to the first one?" (بخاری شریف: ۲/۸۵۲) — that is, the disease did pass from other camels, but its passing came about by the command of Allah, just as Allah's command was at work in the first camel as well.

In short, in our own time the contagiousness of many diseases has moved beyond opinion and conjecture and become a matter of observation. The correct position, therefore, is that some diseases are transmitted by germs; but this ranks as one cause among many. The onset of illness does not depend on contact with a sick person, nor does contact with a sick person necessarily bring the illness on. Whether a person is affected by such causes or not remains subject in every case to the will and decree of Allah, for without His command not even a leaf on a tree can stir.
