### TITLE
An operation without severe need

### QUESTION
Is it sound to deliver a child by an operation without need, when the ordinary manner of childbirth is possible?

### ANSWER
In the pure sharia the human body is inviolable, whether it is alive or dead, and so cutting it about without a legal excuse is not sound. The jurists have permitted the opening of the belly in particular excusing circumstances; in ordinary circumstances there ought to be no permission for it. If, however, according to what the doctors say, there is a fear that some harm will come to the mother because of a delay in the birth, then it is sound. But women sometimes agree to an operation merely out of fear of labour pains; this should not be done.

Note what Fatāwā Hindiyyah states:

في فتاوى أبي الليث رحمه الله في امرأة حامل ماتت وعلم أن ما في بطنها حي فإنه يشق بطنها من الشق الأيسر وكذلك إذا كان أكبر رأيهم أنه حي يشق بطنها كذا في المحيط

لا بأس بقطع اليد من الآكلة وشق البطن لما فيه كذا في الملتقط. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳٦۰).

(وكذا في الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۲/۲۳۸، سعيد، والبحر الرائق: ۸/۲۳۳، ط: بيروت، وبدائع الصنائع: ۵/۱۳۰، سعيد).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

In Islam the human body is a thing worthy of respect, and to cut and trim it to no purpose is a sin. But if the need for it arises in order to protect a human being himself and to treat him, then it is permitted. (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۱/۳۲۷) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
