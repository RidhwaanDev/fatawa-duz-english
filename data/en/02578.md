### TITLE
A dressing made from pigskin

### QUESTION
Here in Chile we get a dressing made from pigskin which contains medicine and the like. It is extremely effective and heals a wound very quickly. There are other treatments too, but they are not effective and are also more expensive. Will there be permission to use the pig dressing or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In ordinary circumstances it is not permissible to treat oneself with forbidden and impure substances, and for this reason using a dressing made from pigskin will also be impermissible. If, however, a religious and expert Muslim physician prescribes it, saying that a cure is not possible without it and that no lawful medicine is sufficient for this illness, then to the degree of compulsion there will be permission to use it.

Note what the Durr al-Mukhtār states:

اختلف في التداوي بالمحرم وظاهر المذهب المنع كما في رضاع البحر ولكن نقل المصنف ثمة وهنا عن الحاوي، وقيل: يرخص إذا علم فيه الشفاء ولم يعلم دواء آخر كما رخص في الخمر للعطشان وعليه الفتوى، قال الشامي: ففي النهاية عن الذخيرة يجوز إن علم فيه الشفاء ولم يعلم دواء آخر. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۱/۲۱۰، سعيد).

ʿĀlamgīrī states:

قال أبو حنيفة رحمه الله: ولا ينتفع من الخنزير بجلده ولا غيره إلا الشعر للاساكفة وقال أبو يوسف رحمه الله: يكره الانتفاع أيضاً بالشعر وقول أبي حنيفة رحمه الله أظهر كذا في المحيط، وإذا كان برجل جراحة يكره المعالجة بعظم الخنزير والإنسان لأنه يحرم الانتفاع به كذا في الكبري. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۳۵٤).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

If a pig, a dog, carrion, or those crawling creatures of the earth that have flowing blood are cooked in oil and made into an oil, these will remain impure and their use will not be sound. These rulings apply in ordinary circumstances; a case of outright dire necessity and compulsion is an exception, for at such a time the sharia grants permission to use even impermissible things to the extent of the need. (جدید فقہی مسائل: ۱/۳۳۵) ۔

Al-Ashbāh wa'l-Naẓāʾir states:

الضرورات تبيح المحظورات. (الأشباه والنظائر: ۱/۱۱۸).

Ḥalāl aur Ḥarām states:

In comparison with other impure substances, the jurists have taken the path of greater caution over using parts of the pig, because the pig is intrinsically filthy. Bazzāziyyah states:

ويكره معالجة الجراحة بإنسان أو خنزير لانهما محرم الانتفاع

In my view the prohibition of parts of the pig too applies where some other means of treatment is available, since the pig, on account of its forbiddenness and its intrinsic filth, is absolutely unusable. But it is Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) himself who holds the use of pig bristle for stitching leather and the like to be permissible, and the jurists declare this view of the Imām (may Allah have mercy on him) to be the more evident in the light of the evidence and of the interests recognised by the sharia. So, in order to safeguard human health and to preserve human life, permission to use parts of the pig will have to be given all the more readily. (حلال و حرام، ص ۱۷۷) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
