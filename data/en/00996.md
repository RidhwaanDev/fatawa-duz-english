### TITLE
Whether zakat deducted by a bank discharges the obligation

### QUESTION
In Pakistan people keep their money in banks, and the law of the land is that the government deducts zakat from that money. Depositors are aware of this law — indeed it is probably set out in the bank's own paperwork. Is zakat discharged by this deduction or not?

Some people say that it is not discharged, and they raise the following objections:

(1) The bank is a debtor, and it has taken the zakat out of its own money; that is, the money is owned by the bank and not by the depositor.

(2) In reality the bank has deducted a portion of the interest. For instance, if the rate of interest is seven and a half per cent and the bank pays the depositor 5 per cent instead, then the deduction has come out of the interest and not out of zakat.

So what is your view on this matter?

### ANSWER
(1) A passage of al-Hidāyah sheds light on this question. Suppose a man is owed a thousand rand by another, and the creditor tells the debtor to buy an unspecified slave with that thousand. The debtor buys the slave, and the slave then dies in the debtor's possession before the creditor has taken possession of him: the loss falls on the debtor's property. But if the creditor had taken possession of him and he died after that, he perished as the creditor's property. This is the ruling in the view of Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him). In the view of his two companions, in both cases the slave perishes as the creditor's property (that is, the debtor is the creditor's agent for taking possession, and an agent's possession is the principal's possession).

Then, a few lines further on, the author of al-Hidāyah says: this is unlike the case where the creditor instructs the debtor to give the money away in charity (that is, the charity is paid out of the creditor's property), because in that case he has given the money in charity for Allah, and Allah is known.

Reasoning by analogy with that ruling, in the case before us it is as though the depositor, when he placed his money in the bank, said of his own free will and consent, "Pay my zakat for me." So when the bank pays the zakat in this way it is valid, and the zakat is discharged. The poor person is the agent for taking possession: he takes possession as a deputy on Allah's behalf and then takes possession for himself, as though he were receiving it from the real owner. The bank's payment of the zakat is therefore just like the owner paying his zakat himself.

Consider the passage of al-Hidāyah:

ومن له على آخر ألف درهم فأمره أن يشتري بها عبد اً بغير عينه فاشتراه، فمات في يده قبل أن يقبضه الآمر مات من مال المشتري، وإن قبضه الآمر فله ولهذا عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله، وقالا: هو لا زم للآمر إذا قبضه المأمور

.. بخلاف ما إذا أمره بالتصدق، لأنه جعل المال لله تعالى وهو معلوم. (الهداية: ۳/۱۸٦، ۱۸۷).

This is, moreover, one of the matters debated by the Majlis-e Taḥqīq-e Masāʾil-e Ḥāḍirah of Karachi, which issued the decision that the deduction of zakat by a bank is valid.

The answer to this objection is also set out in detail in Aḥsan al-Fatāwā; see (احسن الفتاوی: ٤/۳۱٤ ۔ ۳۲٤). But once the matter is viewed in the light of the ruling quoted above, no difficulty remains.

(2) The second objection was that the bank in fact deducts a portion of the interest, so the payment is made out of interest money and not as zakat.

The answer is that the money placed in a bank is for the most part not interest money. Where the extra interest paid by the bank does become mixed with the lawful money and zakat is paid out of the total, the zakat is attributed to the lawful wealth, and the interest money is treated as something that must be given away in charity. A man is in any case obliged to give away the whole of the interest money in charity without any intention of reward.

For example, a man deposits 100,000 in a bank and 10,000 of interest accrues on it. The bank takes a fortieth of the whole sum as zakat, that is 2,750. Of this, the 2,500 is the zakat on the 100,000, while the 250 is not zakat but charity paid from the interest money. The man is still obliged to give the remaining 9,750 away in charity, and since interest too is to be spent on the poor, there is no harm in this. (مستفاد از فتاوی حقانیہ: ٤/۹۸).

Bear in mind that the detail given above applies where the bank deducted the man's zakat after the interest had been credited to him.

If the interest came after the zakat had been deducted — for example, the bank took 2,500 as zakat on the 100,000 and placed it in the zakat fund, and interest then accrued on that 2,500 — the increase belongs to the poor and not to the account holder.

ونظيره إبل الزكاة والأضحىة إذ ولدت. (مستفاد از فتاوی فریدیہ: ۳/۳۸۰).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ولدت الأضحية قبل الذبح يذبح الولد معها، وعند بعضهم يتصدق به بلا ذبح. (الدر المختار: ۵/۳۲۳، سعيد).
