### TITLE
Some questions concerning the difference of horizons

### QUESTION
What do the elders of the faith and the muftis of the noble sharia say concerning the following matter?

There is a great deal of talk nowadays about the sighting of the crescent, and people are whispering all sorts of things. What is the reality of the matter? Kindly favour us with an answer and earn a great reward.

(1) If the people of one city have seen the moon, upon the people of how many cities is it necessary to follow them? What is the area covered by a sighting of the crescent, and up to how many kilometres is the horizon taken into account?

(2) In this country, the sighting of which of the neighbouring countries will be taken into account? And is the horizon of Johannesburg and Cape Town one and the same, or are they separate?

(3) Can we take the sighting of Madinah Munawwarah into account or not? If not, why not?

### ANSWER
In the Hanafi school, according to the standard narration, the testimony of the east is sufficient for the west, provided it reaches there in a sound manner or falls within the definition of a widely known and widely circulated report. As for those who reckon the difference of horizons in kilometres, quite apart from the contradictions in their statements, this does not fall under any general rule; it rests for the most part on estimation. The scholars of careful investigation, however, do take the difference of horizons into account in the case of distant lands, the details of which have already been given.

(2) In this country, if the moon has genuinely been sighted and there is no flaw or defect in it according to the Hanafi school, then it should be accepted; and the testimony of the neighbouring countries will also be accepted.

(3) One reason for not accepting the testimony of Saudi Arabia is that their testimony is generally contrary to what is evident, since the moon is reported as seen on one and the same day both towards the east and towards the west, whereas in the view of the astronomers, ancient and modern, this cannot happen. Furthermore, no sighting takes place anywhere else; indeed, on many occasions there is no general sighting of the moon — and sometimes not even an individual sighting — on the day after the reported sighting, even though the sky is clear. Again, at times the moon is only five or six hours old and a sighting is claimed; indeed, sometimes a sighting is claimed even before the moon is born. How, then, is testimony contrary to what is evident to be accepted?

The second reason is that with them, in the light of Hanbali fiqh, the testimony of one man is enough for Ramadan and that of two trustworthy men for Eid and the like, whereas in the view of the Hanafis a large body of people is required when the sky is clear. Even so, this difference is easy to avoid, for when a Hanbali qāḍī accepts such testimony and gives his ruling upon it, the Hanafis are able to accept it. But it is very difficult to accept testimony that is contrary to what is evident. Moreover, most people have no knowledge of the particulars of their testimony; indeed, since the government there is a personal one, it is difficult — well-nigh impossible — even to reach the particulars of these matters. It should not be imagined that the Saudi sighting is always wrong; nevertheless, it is not regarded as very reliable among the scholars. And the scholars of careful investigation who hold that the difference of horizons applies in distant lands do not follow them, for these and other reasons.

After these questions had been answered, since there was a fear of division and disunity in this country, and certain winds were blowing from those given to novelty to the effect that Saudi Arabia should be followed in Ramadan, Eid and the like — while the three councils here (Cape Town, Natal and the Transvaal) were constantly engaged in this service and were issuing an agreed decision on the sighting of the crescent — Hazrat Mufti Sahib (may his shade endure) wrote a comprehensive and well-grounded treatise gathering together the statements and passages of the jurists, which is set down here.
