### TITLE
Spending a child's money on his own expenses

### QUESTION
Someone, or the father himself, gave a sum of money as a gift to a man's minor son. May clothes and the like be bought for the child out of this money, or not? Is the father to bear the child's expenses himself?

### ANSWER
In the case described, when the child has money of his own, the father may meet the child's expenses out of that money; but the father may not spend the child's money on extravagances. Great care is needed where children's property is concerned.

See what al-Durr al-Mukhtār says:

فإن نفقة المملوك على مالكه والغني في ماله الحاضر. وفي الشامية: قوله في ماله الحاضر، يشمل العقار والأردية والثياب، فإذا احتيج إلى النفقة كان للأب بيع ذلك كله وينفق عليه لأنه غني بهذه الأشياء بحر، وفتح. (الدر المختار مع فتاوى الشامي: ۳/٦۱۲، سعيد).

وفي البحر الرائق: الصغير إذا كان له مال فنفقته في ماله. (البحر الرائق: ٤/۲۰۱، كوئته).

وفي الفتاوى الهندية: إرضاع الصغير إذا كان يوجد من ترضعه إنما يجب على الأب إذا لم يكن للصغير مال وأما إذا كان له مال فتكون مؤنة الرضاع في مال الصغير كذا في المحيط. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۵٦۰).

Imdād al-Fatāwā states:

Question: If minor children's maternal or paternal grandfather gives them something, how are the children's parents to spend that gift on them? If it is spent on food and clothing, that is the parents' own responsibility until the children come of age. So should the gift be kept in safe keeping as a trust until they reach maturity, or should it be spent on sweets and delicacies? What should be done?

Answer:

في الدر المختار: ولطفله الفقير الحر لأن نفقة المملوك على ملكه والغني في ماله الحاضر،

From this passage it is clear that when a minor owns property, his maintenance comes in the first place out of that property; as long as the property exists it is not obligatory upon the father. So in the case mentioned, these gifts should be spent on the minor's necessary maintenance. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 3/480, Kitāb al-Hibah).

In another place it is stated:

If the children, whether a boy or a girl, are people of means — that is, property has come into their ownership in some way, whether by gift or by inheritance — then in that situation their food and maintenance are due out of their own property, and the parents' responsibility is only to administer it. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 2/533).
