### TITLE
Doubts raised about the prohibition of wine

### QUESTION
Some misguided and irreligious people say that the prohibition of wine is not in the Noble Qur'an but only in the hadiths. Is what they say correct or not?

### ANSWER
The prohibition of wine is in the Qur'an. Allah Most High says:

يا أيها الذين آمنوا إنما الخمر والميسر والأنصاب والأزلام رجس من عمل الشيطان فاجتنبوه. الخ. (سورة المائدة)

Translation: O you who believe! Wine, gambling, idols and divining arrows are all filth, part of Satan's work; so keep away from these things, that you may prosper. Satan only wants to stir up enmity and hatred among you by means of wine and gambling, and to keep you back from the remembrance of Allah and from prayer. So will you not abstain?

In the above verses wine, gambling, idols and divining arrows are declared to be filth and the work of Satan, which means that these things are foul, that they are neither suitable nor lawful for human life, and that they are satanic works, not pleasing to Allah and His Messenger. Muslims must keep away from Satan and satanic works and obey Allah and His Messenger. It is for this reason that Allah Most High has declared the things mentioned above unlawful.

Some doubts raised about the prohibition of wine, and their answers:

Among these doubts are the following:

(1) The word "unlawful" does not occur anywhere in the Noble Qur'an?

الجواب:

(1) The answer that turns their own argument against them is that the word "unlawful" does not occur for adultery either, and yet everyone accepts that adultery is forbidden.

(2) The reasoned answer:

Tafsīr Ḥaqqānī states: The author of al-Kashshāf writes that in this verse the prohibition of wine has been emphasised in several ways. (1) The sentence is introduced with إنما. (2) It is placed alongside idol worship. (3) It is called rijs, that is, impure. (4) It is called the work of Satan, which is the fountainhead of every corruption. (5) A command is given to keep away from it. (6) Success is stated to lie in avoiding it — so how could there be success in committing it? (7) The ground of its prohibition is that a man is deprived of his senses, which is injurious both to his worldly life and to his hereafter: to his worldly life by creating mutual resentment and enmity, and to his hereafter by making him heedless of prayer and of the remembrance of God. After this, from "أطيعوا الله" to "المبين" the command is emphasised still further. So applying the word "unlawful" was not necessary in order to establish its prohibition. (تفسیر حقانی، صفحہ ۵٤، ج ٤، سورہ مائدہ پارہ ۷)

In Āyāt al-Aḥkām, Shaykh al-Ṣābūnī says:

التعبير بقوله تعالى: فاجتنبوه (المائدة، الآية: ۹۰) أبلغ في النهي والتحريم من لفظ حرم لأن معناه البعد عنه بالكلية فهو مثل قوله تعالى: ولا تقربوا الزنا. (الإسراء: ۳۲) لأن القرب منه إذا كان حراما فيكون الفعل محرما من باب أولي فقوله فاجتنبوه معناه: كونوا في جانب آخر منه وكلما كانت الحرمة شديدة جاء التعبير بلفظ الاجتناب كما قال تعالى: فاجتنبوا الرجس من الأوثان، واجتنبوا قول الزور (حج: ۳۰) ومعلوم أنه ليس هناك ذنب أعظم من الإشراك بالله. (آيات الأحكام للصابوني: مأخوذ من فتاوى الشبكة: ٤/۹۳٦).

Moreover, the blessed person of the Prophet ﷺ stood in relation to the Qur'an as its exposition and commentary, and so in the hadiths the Prophet ﷺ spoke of wine as unlawful — and that is the Qur'an's own explanation. It is impossible that permissibility should be derived and drawn out from the Qur'an while the Messenger ﷺ declares the thing unlawful. Allah Most High says in the Qur'an:

﴿وأنزلنا إليك الذكر لتبين للناس ما نزل إليهم﴾ (النحل، الآية: ٤٤).

In Maʿārif al-Qurʾān, Muftī Muhammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

In this verse "the Reminder" means, by common agreement, the Noble Qur'an, and in this verse the Messenger of Allah ﷺ is commanded to set out and explain to the people the verses of the Qur'an that were revealed. In this there is clear proof that a correct understanding of the realities, the insights and the rulings of the Noble Qur'an depends upon the explanation of the Noble Messenger ﷺ. If every person, merely by knowing the Arabic language and Arabic literature, were able to understand the rulings of the Qur'an as God intended them, there would be no sense in entrusting the work of exposition and clarification to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. (معارف القرآن: ۵/۳۳٦، سورۂ نحل: ٤٤) ۔

Accordingly, explaining and expounding the verses mentioned above, the Master of Creation ﷺ says:

عن أبي سعيد الخدري رضي الله تعالى عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: إن الله تعالى حرم الخمر فمن أدركته هذه الآية وعنده منها شيء فلا يشرب ولا يبع قال: فاستقبل الناس بما كان عندهم منها في طريق المدينة فسفكوها. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۲۲).

It is narrated from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī (may Allah be pleased with him) that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: Allah Most High has declared wine unlawful; whoever has wine in his possession when the verse of its prohibition reaches him should neither drink it nor sell it. Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī (may Allah be pleased with him) said: those who had wine brought it out onto the road of Madinah and poured it away.

(۲) عن جابر بن عبد الله رضي الله عنه أنه سمع رسول الله ﷺ يقول عام الفتح وهو بمكة: إن الله ورسوله حرم بيع الخمر والميتة والخنزير والأصنام. (رواه مسلم: ۲/۲۳).

(۳) عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: إن الله حرم الخمر والميسر والكوبة

(مشكوة: ۲/۳۸٦).

It is narrated from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) that Allah Most High declared wine, gambling and the beating of the drum unlawful.

(٤)

عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه قال خطب عمر رضي الله عنه على منبر رسول الله ﷺ فقال: إنه قد نزل تحريم الخمر

(رواه البخاري).

ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) says that ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him), while delivering a sermon from the pulpit of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, said: The prohibition of wine has certainly been revealed.

The texts above establish the prohibition of wine beyond any doubt.

Al-Hidāyah calls the denial of the prohibition of wine unbelief and rejection:

ومن الناس من أنكر حرمة عينها

وهذا كفر لأنه جحود الكتاب فإنه سماه رجساً والرجس ما هو محرم العين وقد جاء ت السنة متواترة أن النبي ﷺ حرم الخمر وعليه انعقد الإجماع. (هداية آخرين، ٤۹۳).

The second doubt:

The second doubt they raise is that some verses appear to indicate that wine is lawful. For example:

(۱)

﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا تقربوا الصلاة وأنتم سكاري حتى تعلموا ما تقولون﴾. (سورة النساء: ٤۳).

(۲) ﴿ومن ثمرات النخيل والأعناب تتخذون منه سكراً ورزقاً حسناً﴾. (سورة النحل: ٦۷).

(۳) ﴿يسئلونك عن الخمر والميسر قل فيهما إثم كبير ومنافع للناس﴾. (سورة البقرة: ۲۱۹).

الجواب:

The rulings on wine were revealed gradually. In the earliest period of Islam wine was lawful; then, in connection with various incidents, it was made unlawful step by step through various verses. The verses cited as indicating lawfulness are therefore to be understood as belonging to the earliest period of Islam, after which abrogation took place.

Consider what ʿAllāmah Ibn Kathīr (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

وقال الإمام أحمد

عن عمر بن الخطاب رضي الله عنه أنه قال: لما نزل تحريم الخمر قال اللهم بين لنا في الخمر بياناً شافياً فنزلت الآية التي في البقرة: ﴿يسئلونك عن الخمر والميسر قل فيهما إثم كبير﴾ فدعي عمر رضي الله عنه فقرء ت عليه فقال: اللهم بين لنا في الخمر بياناً شافياً فنزلت الآية التي في سورة النساء: ﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا تقربوا الصلاة وأنتم سكاري﴾ فدعي عمر رضي الله عنه فقرء ت عليه، فقال: اللهم بين لنا في الخمر بياناً شافياً، فنزلت الآية التي في المائدة، فدعي عمر رضي الله عنه فقرء ت عليه فلما بلغ قوله تعالى: ﴿فهل أنتم منتهون﴾ قال عمر رضي الله عنه انتهينا انتهينا. (ابن كثير: ۲/ ۱۰٤، وابن عربي: ۲/۱٦۳).

Some people, however, say that abrogation is not possible in the Noble Qur'an. They deny abrogation only because they have not been able to grasp its reality and its wisdom.

In Maʿārif al-Qurʾān, Muftī Muhammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

There is a third possibility as well: that the one who issues a command knew from the very beginning that circumstances would change and that at that time this command would no longer be suitable and another command would have to be given. Knowing this, he gave one command today, and when, in accordance with his knowledge, circumstances changed, he changed the command too in accordance with his earlier decision. This is like a physician or doctor who, looking at a patient's present condition, prescribes a medicine while knowing that after two days of taking it the patient's condition will change and he will then have to prescribe another medicine. On the first day he prescribes a medicine suited to that day, and after two days, when the condition has changed, he prescribes a different one. In the commands of Allah, exalted be His majesty, only this last form of abrogation is possible. (معارف القرآن: ۱/ ۲۸۳) ۔

The third doubt:

In the noble verse, they say, "فاجتنبوه" means no more than that you should be careful in drinking so that no harms follow; or, in their view, it is merely a piece of advice.

الجواب: The exegetes are agreed that "اجتنبوه" carries the sense of giving something up completely and staying away from it. Thus the careful scholar Ibn Kathīr (may Allah have mercy on him) said:

"فاجتنبوه" أي اتركوه. (۱/۱۰٤، وكذا في تفسير السمرقندي: ۱/٤۵۷).

In Aḥkām al-Qurʾān, Imam Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ al-Rāzī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

اقتضت هذه الآية تحريم الخمر من وجهين: ـ أحدهما قوله رجس لأن الرجس اسم في الشرع لما يلزم اجتنابه

والوجه الآخر قوله تعالى فاجتنبوه وذلك أمر والأمر يقتضي الإيجاب فانتظمت الآية تحريم الخمر من هذين الوجهين. (أحكام القرآن: ۲/٤٦۱).

Ibn al-ʿArabī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

قوله تعالى: "فاجتنبوه" يريد ابعدوه، واجعلوه ناحية، وهذا أمر باجتنابها، والأمر على الوجوب لا سيما وقد علق به الفلاح. (أحكام القرآن لا بن العربي: ۲/۱٦۵).

ʿAllāmah al-Qurṭubī says:

قوله تعالى: (فاجتنبوه) يريد ابعدوه واجعلوه ناحية، فأمر الله تعالى باجتناب هذه الامور، واقترنت بصيغة الامر مع نصوص الأحاديث وإجماع الأمة فحصل الاجتناب في جهة التحريم، فبهذا حرمت الخمر، ولا خلاف بين علماء المسلمين أن سورة مائدة نزلت بتحريم الخمر، وهي مدنية من آخر ما نزل، وورود التحريم في الميتة والدم ولحم الخنزير في قوله تعالى: "قل لا أجد" وغيرها من الآي خبراً، وفي الخمر نهياً وزجراً، وهو أقوي التحريم وأوكده. (الجامع لا حكام القرآن: ٦/۱۸٦).

Lisān al-ʿArab states:

جنب الشيء

واجتنبه: بعد عنه، وهذا مفهوم عدم القرب. (لسان العرب: ۱/۲۷۸).

Qāmūs al-Waḥīd states:

اجتنب الشيء:

to avoid, to keep away from, to shun, to withdraw from. (قاموس الوحید: ۱/۲۸٤) ۔

The context of the noble verse likewise shows that the imperative is for obligation and that prohibition is what is meant. For details see:

(صفوة التفاسير: ۱/۳٦٦ وأحكام القرآن للجصاص: ۲/٤٦۱، ومعارف القرآن: ۲/۵۸۳).

The fourth doubt:

The Qur'an states:

﴿إنما حرم عليكم الميتة والدم ولحم الخنزير وما أهل به لغير الله﴾. (سورة البقرة، ص ۱۷۳).

This noble verse, they say, shows that only these things are unlawful; and since the word khamr is not mentioned among the things listed, wine is lawful — as though إنما were for restriction.

الجواب:

The exegetes have given a great many answers. Among them are the following:

(1) إنما comes for emphasis, not for restriction.

(2) The restriction here is relative, not absolute: that is, these things are unlawful, while the sāʾibah, baḥīrah, waṣīlah and ḥām that you regard as unlawful are not unlawful.

(3) The meaning is not that unlawfulness is confined to these things; rather it is that in these things there is nothing but unlawfulness and impurity — that is, no benefit at all.

(4) The unlawfulness of these things is on account of their impurity; so where impurity is found in other things, unlawfulness will certainly be found as well.

(5) It contains qaṣr al-qalb: that is, you consider these things lawful, whereas it is precisely these things that are unlawful.

The kinds of wine and their rulings:

According to the three imams every intoxicant is unlawful and entails the ḥadd punishment, whether it is little or much.

Imam Muhammad (may Allah have mercy on him) agrees with the three imams as to unlawfulness, but is with the two shaykhs as to the obligation of the ḥadd punishment — that is, in drinks other than khamr the criterion is actual intoxication.

According to the two shaykhs, drinks are of three kinds:

(۱) الخمر: ني من ماء العنب إذا غلا واشتد وقذف بالزبد.

Both a little of it and a great deal of it are unlawful, and drinking it entails the ḥadd punishment even if the amount is small.

(2) Cooked grape juice when less than two-thirds of it has evaporated, and wine cooked from dates or raisins once froth has risen upon it. Drinking it is unlawful even in a small quantity; the ḥadd punishment, however, becomes due only after intoxication.

(3) Drinks other than the four just mentioned — that is, other than raw grape wine, cooked grape wine, cooked date wine and raisin wine — such as nabīdh of dates and of raisins cooked to the least degree, grape juice two-thirds of which has evaporated in cooking, and drinks made from wheat, rice, barley and the like.

Drinking a small, non-intoxicating quantity of these in order to gain strength for worship is lawful according to Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him), while drinking them for intoxication and idle amusement is impermissible. If intoxication results, then according to the preferred position the ḥadd punishment applies. Taking them as medicine, however, is permissible. In ordinary circumstances the fatwa is on the position of Imam Muhammad (may Allah have mercy on him), that drinking them is impermissible; and in the matter of medical treatment the fatwa is on the position of the two shaykhs, that is, that the non-intoxicating quantity mixed into medicines is lawful.
