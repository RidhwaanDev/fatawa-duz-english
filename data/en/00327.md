### TITLE
An imam or teacher serving as trustee of the mosque

### QUESTION
Can an imam or a teacher who draws a salary from the mosque fund become the mutawallī (trustee) of the mosque, or not?

### ANSWER
Any man may be appointed mutawallī of a mosque who is religious, trustworthy and familiar with the rules of endowments, and these qualities are generally found in the fullest measure in the imams of mosques. As for the point that the imam draws a salary from the mosque fund, drawing a salary from the fund is no bar to his becoming mutawallī of the mosque, because he is paid for leading the prayers and for teaching, not for being the trustee. And if we suppose that no one is willing to serve as trustee without payment, then whoever discharges the duty properly may be given a suitable fixed allowance agreed upon for it — even if he happens to be the imam of the mosque.

Al-Fatāwā al-Hindiyyah states:

سئل الفقيه أبو القاسم عن قيم مسجد جعله القاضي قيما على غلاتها وجعل له شيئاً معلوماً يأخذ كل سنة حل له الأخذ إن كان مقدار اجر مثله كذا في المحيط. (الفتاوى الهندية ۲/ ٤٦۱).

Besides, what reason could there be for an imam or a teacher not to become a trustee or a committee member? When worldly people who are openly sinful and corrupt can become trustees, it seems strange even to ask whether an imam or a teacher may serve as trustee or member.
