### TITLE
A motorcycle and ʿumrah welfare scheme

### QUESTION
An advertisement issued by a company is attached to this question. The gist of the advertisement is that a company selling motorcycles has drawn up a scheme by which buyers will purchase motorcycles in instalments; in the meantime a draw will be held, and whoever's name comes out in the draw will be given the motorcycle in return for the instalments already paid, and the remaining instalments will be waived. In the draw some will also receive a ticket for ʿumrah. Some muftis call this dealing impermissible, and they state some of the reasons for its being impermissible as follows:

(1) If this is a salam sale, then the whole price ought to be paid in advance, whereas here the price is paid in instalments.

(2) The price is unknown: for one person it is one amount, and for another it is another.

(3) If the money handed over is not the price but a trust, and the company has permission to use it, then it has become a loan; and then, on account of it, in the case of the draw the buyer, who is the lender, obtains a reduction in the price. This has come under "كل قرض جر نفعاً فهو ربا". What is your fatwa in this connection? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
باسمه سبحانه وتعالى:

In the case asked about, in the view of this humble servant this scheme is permissible, and there are two ways of making it sound:

(1) One is that the instalments of the price should be given as a trust and permission also given to use them; then it will become a loan, and in the case of the draw these same instalments will become the price. The answer to

"كل قرض جر نفعاً فهو ربا"

is that a benefit in a loan which is stipulated and known for every one of them is forbidden, while a benefit that is merely hoped for is not forbidden; and here the benefit is merely hoped for by every one of them, it is not known.

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Niẓām al-Dīn Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him), chief mufti of Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, writes in Niẓām al-Fatāwā:

What do the learned scholars say about this matter: here a scheme is running in the post office by which people deposit one pound or five pounds, and they are given a receipt for the deposit; whenever they wish to withdraw the money, they may withdraw it by handing in the receipt. But every month certain numbers come out in the newspapers, and a hundred pounds is received for one pound. The question is whether it is permissible to take the extra sum received by keeping money in this way or not?

Answer: Ḥaḍrat Muftī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) declared it permissible. We quote only one part of the answer: Then, when there is no certainty of this excess being received, nor is it demanded, nor does everyone in fact receive this excess — rather the department, at its own discretion and merely in accordance with its own rules, announces an additional sum for certain numbers and gives it only to those whose names come out, and no one has any right to demand this additional sum. Then Ḥaḍrat Muftī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) writes further: if someone were to say that this additional sum is received by the one who gives the loan, so that this excess has been drawn by that loan, and that it therefore falls under

"كل قرض جر نفعاً فهو ربا"

and this excess has become interest —

the answer to this is that since not every lender receives this additional sum, but only the one whom the department wishes — so much so that if one of the lenders whose name did not come out were to demand this excess, he would have no right of demand on the basis of the earlier contract — this excess is therefore not an instance of

جر نفعاً

because, under

"كل قرض جر نفعاً"

Shāmī (۱۷٤) states:

إذا كان مشروطاً كما علم مما نقله عن البحر ومن الخلاصة وفي الذخيرة: إن لم يكن النفع مشروطاً في القرض فعلى قول الكرخي رحمه الله لا بأس به.

He then goes on to explain the difference between jarr and injirār. (نظام الفتاوی: ۱/ ۱۹٤ و۱۹۵) ۔

Muftī Rashīd Aḥmad Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Aḥsan al-Fatāwā:

Nowadays a motorcycle company, in order to make itself known, has adopted the following method: it sells motorcycles in instalments. Twenty-one instalments have been fixed, and each monthly instalment of 550 rupees has to be paid. If, before the instalments are completed, a buyer's name comes out in the draw — a draw is held every month — the motorcycle is given to him and all the remaining instalments are waived. If a buyer's name does not come out in the draw for twenty months, then after twenty-one months the motorcycle is given to him; and the sum of these twenty-one instalments is that price of the motorcycle which is current in the market, no more. Is this method of buying and selling permissible or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

الجواب باسم ملهم الصواب:

This is a concession in the price from the company's side, and the company makes its choice of which buyer is to be given the concession by means of a draw. There is no loss to anyone in this, so this buying and selling is permissible. واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم ۔ (احسن الفتاوی: ٦/ ۵۱۸) ۔

From this passage it is also learnt that in the case asked about the whole price is not unknown but known; it is only that by means of the draw the remaining price is waived for some people. The whole price should therefore be stated in the advertisement — yes, and by means of the draw some of the price is waived for some people.

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Niẓām al-Dīn Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) was asked the following question: a man, in order to promote his business, adopts this arrangement. The market price of a watch is one hundred rupees. For it fifty members are enrolled, who will each pay ten rupees a month, and this scheme will be run for ten months. In the first month the one whose name comes out in the draw will get the watch for ten rupees; in the same way, for nine months whoever's name comes out will go on receiving a watch; in the tenth month the 41 persons who are left will each be given a watch and the scheme will be brought to an end. Is this permissible?

Answer: Ḥaḍrat Muftī Niẓām al-Dīn Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) declared this scheme permissible, on condition that if a customer dies before receiving the watch, his instalments are returned. We have given the gist of this question and answer. (نظام الفتاوی: ۲/۳۱۹) ۔

Muftī Khālid Sayf Allāh Ṣāḥib too has declared such an arrangement invalid at the outset and permissible in the end. See: (جدید فقہی مسائل: ٤/۱٦٦). But the reason he gives for calling it invalid at the outset is the unknownness of the price, whereas here the actual price is not unknown; it is only that afterwards, because of the draw, the remaining price is waived.

The upshot is that here the benefit is not stipulated for every contracting party but depends on the name coming out in the draw, and this is called a conditional gratuity — that is, if the name comes out in the draw the remaining instalments will be waived. A conditional gratuity is permissible, and there are many examples of it in the sharia. Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) bought a camel-saddle from Ḥaḍrat ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him) and said to ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him): tell your son Barāʾ (may Allah be pleased with him) to carry this saddle along with me. ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him) said: he will carry it on condition that you relate the story of the hijrah. Ḥaḍrat ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him) thus made the favour of carrying the saddle conditional upon the story being related, and Ḥaḍrat Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) accepted the condition of relating the story.

Imdād al-Aḥkām states: the admission fee and the monthly fee in madrasahs are not wages but a contribution, and a condition is permissible in a contribution. (امداد الاحکام: ۳/ ٦۲۳، کتاب الاجارہ) ۔

For details on this question see Kitāb al-Hibah, under conditional gratuity.

A simple way of putting a conditional gratuity is: laying down a condition in doing a favour — as when a draw and the coming out of a name are laid down as a condition for waiving the price.

(2) The second way of making the above contract sound is that it should be treated not as salam but as istiṣnāʿ, and that this should be stated explicitly in the advertisement. The basic reason for not treating it as salam is that in salam it is necessary to hand over the capital sum, and mention of the term is also necessary, whereas here the capital sum is paid in instalments.

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

أما الذي يرجع إلى رأس المال

ومنها: أن يكون مقبوضاً في مجلس السلم لأن المسلم فيه دين والافتراق لا عن قبض رأس المال يكون افتراقاً عن دين بدين

ولأن مأخذ هذا العقد دليل على هذا الشرط فإنه يسمي سلماً وسلفاً

والسلف ينبئ عن التقدم فيقتضي لزوم تسليم رأس المال. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/۲۰۲، سعيد).

Tabyīn al-Ḥaqāʾiq (تبيين الحقائق) states:

فإن أسلم في مائتي درهم في كر بر مائة دين عليه ومائة نقد فالسلم في الدين باطل أي في حصة الدين لأنه دين بدين. (تبيين الحقائق: ٤/۱۱۸، إمدادية).

But in istiṣnāʿ it is not necessary to pay the price at once. The Majallah states:

لا يلزم في الاستصناع دفع الثمن حالاً لأن هذا بيع والمستصنع مشترٍ والمشتري لا يلزمه دفع الثمن قبل إحضار المبيع. (مجلة وشرح المجلة لمحمد خالد الأتاسي: ۲/٤۰۵، باب الاستصناع).

The simple meaning of istiṣnāʿ is placing an order for something, or having it booked. In istiṣnāʿ it is not necessary that the thing be made afterwards; the person who places the order may also be given something already made. کذا فی کتب الفقہ ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم ۔
