### TITLE
Money left over from a patient's treatment fund after his death

### QUESTION
A man was suffering from a fatal illness, and a large sum was needed for his treatment. A collection was made for this, and friends, acquaintances and relatives gave generously. All this money was transferred into the patient's account and was spent on his treatment as the need arose, until the patient died. What should now be done with the money that is left over? Must it be returned to the donors, or will it be included in the deceased's estate?

### ANSWER
In the case described, the donors paid the money into the patient's account intending it as charity, so the patient became the owner of all of it. Now, after his death, it will form part of his estate and will be divided among all the sharia heirs according to the shares fixed by the sharia. It is not obligatory or necessary to return it to the donors; they have already earned the reward of their charity and their gift.

See what a hadith states:

عن أنس رضي الله عنه قال: لما نزلت: ﴿لن تنالوا البر حتى تنفقوا مما تحبون﴾ [آل عمران: ۹۲] جاء أبو طلحة إلى رسول الله ﷺ فقال: يا رسول الله! يقول الله تبارك وتعالى في كتابه: ﴿لن تنالوا البر حتى تنفقوا مما تحبون﴾ [آل عمران: ۹۲] وإن أحب أموالي إلى بيرحاء، قال: وكانت حديقة كان رسول الله ﷺ يدخلها، ويستظل بها ويشرب من مائها فهي إلى الله عزوجل وإلي رسوله ﷺ، أرجو بره وذخره، فضعها أي رسول الله حيث أراك الله، فقال رسول الله ﷺ: بخ يا أبا طلحة ذلك مال رابح، قبلناه منك، ورددناه عليك، فاجعله في الأقربين، فتصدق به أبو طلحة على ذوي رحمه قال: وكان منهم أبي، وحسان، قال: وباع حسان حصته من معاوية، فقيل له: تبيع صدقة أبي طلحة، فقال: ألا أبيع صاعاً من تمر بصاع من دراهم، قال: وكانت تلك الحديقة في موضع قصر بني حديلة الذي بناه معاوية. (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۲۷۵۸).

This hadith shows that a thing given as a gift leaves the ownership of the donor, and that the recipient becomes entitled to deal with it as its owner.

Sharḥ al-Majallah states:

لكن في رد المحتار (۵/٦۸۹، سعيد) أيضاً، أول كتاب الهبة، ما نصه: أعطي لزوجته دنانير لتتخذ بها ثياباً وتلبسها عنده فدفعتها معاملة فهي لها (قنية)، اتخذ لولده الصغير ثوباً يملكه وكذا الكبير بالتسليم (بزازية)، قلت: فقد أفاد أن التلفظ بالإيجاب والقبول لا يشترط، بل تكفي القرائن الدالة على التمليك، كمن دفع لفقير شيئاً وقبضه ولم يتلفظ واحد منهما بشيء

ويظهر لي من مجموع هذا النقول أن الهبة كما تنعقد بالألفاظ الدالة على التمليك مجاناً لغة أو عرفاً، تنعقد أيضاً بالفعل بطريق التعاطي

لكن مع قرينة لفظية أو حالية. (شرح المجلة لمحمد الأتاسي: ۳/۳٤۹، المادة: ۸۳۸، ط: مكتبه رشيديه).

These passages of fiqh make it clear that words are not necessary for a gift to be concluded; it is also concluded by the simple handing over of the thing. So as soon as the money was transferred into the patient's account with the intention of gift and charity, the patient became its owner. Furthermore, a gift is not rendered invalid by an invalid condition. Consider the following:

قال في الجامع الصغير: رجل وهب لرجل داراً، أو تصدق عليه بدار على أن يرد عليه بشيء منها، أو يعوضه شيئاً منها، أو وهب له جارية على أن يردها عليه، أو على أن يعتقها، أو على أن يتخذها أم ولد، فالهبة جائز ة، والشرط باطل. وفي النافع الكبير: قوله والشرط باطل، لأنه يخالف موجب العقد، والهبة لا تفسد بالشروط الفاسدة. (الجامع الصغير مع شرحه النافع الكبير، ص ٤۳۵، ط: عالم الكتب). وينظر أيضاً: (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۵/ ٦۸۸، سعيد، وشرح العيني على الكنز: ۲/۲٦٦، إدارة القرآن، والفتاوى الهندية: ٤/۳۹٦، دار الفكر).

Since the occasion is fitting, an interesting incident concerning some of our elders (may Allah have mercy on them) is related here.

Fatāwā Khalīliyyah records: On a journey, Ḥaḍrat Sahāranpūrī and another elder (whose name has not been disclosed) were travelling together. In the course of the journey a devotee presented the latter with a very valuable pocket watch as a gift. He already had a watch, but out of his gracious kindness he accepted it.

After a while Ḥaḍrat Sahāranpūrī said, "If this watch is more than you need, I should like to buy it; do sell it to me." The other elder replied, "What need is there to sell it? I am yours and the watch is yours too. Here, please accept it as a gift." Ḥaḍrat said, "Had you offered it earlier I would have accepted it; but I have already formed the intention to buy it, so a gift is now out of the question." After some discussion back and forth, a sale was concluded: the price was paid and the watch was taken.

But somehow the man who had given the watch came to hear that his watch had been sold, and he was hurt by it, because his wish had been that the watch should remain in that elder's own use.

When this elder learnt of his displeasure, he said to Ḥaḍrat Sahāranpūrī (may Allah illuminate his resting place), "Please return the watch and I will give the price back." Ḥaḍrat said, "Why? Was there a stipulated option in the sale?" The elder said, "No, there was no stipulated option; but the man who gave it is hurt, and his hurt would be removed." Ḥaḍrat Sahāranpūrī said, "Was it a condition of the sale that if he were hurt the watch would be returned?"

The elder said, "That was not a condition either. The sale was perfectly clear and the matter was settled. Let us now cancel it by mutual agreement." Ḥaḍrat Sahāranpūrī said, "Cancellation by agreement requires the consent of both parties, and I am not willing." The elder said, "You are the senior and I am the junior, and seniors do consent for the sake of their juniors." Ḥaḍrat Sahāranpūrī said, "For your sake I would certainly have consented; but I did not buy this watch for myself. A friend had appointed me his agent to buy a watch, and I bought this watch on his behalf, so the watch has now become his. I have no right to dispose of it, because an agent may act only within the limits of the authority his principal has given him, and I was appointed only as an agent to buy, not as an agent to sell."

At this the elder ended the conversation and fell silent.

Later, at another gathering and in the presence of the man who had given the watch, Ḥaḍrat Sahāranpūrī presented the watch to that elder with the words, "Here, take this watch and return my money to me."

The elder said, "But you were saying that you are only an agent to buy and not an agent to sell; how then are you selling it?"

Ḥaḍrat Sahāranpūrī said, "The matter is exactly as I stated it; but I have complete confidence in that friend. When I tell him that I bought the watch for him under his authorisation and then returned it for a good reason of my own, he will not mind." At this the elder (may Allah illuminate his resting place) took the watch back and paid the price. (فتاوی خلیلیہ، ص 62-64، مکتبۃ الشیخ، کراچی)۔

This incident shows that a gift leaves the ownership of the donor and enters the ownership of the recipient, and that the recipient becomes entitled to dispose of it entirely as he wishes.
