### TITLE
Dealing on the black market

### QUESTION
Is it permissible to sell foreign goods behind the government's back — what we call "smuggling" and black-marketeering (shady dealing)? And may one buy from and sell to people who do this?

### ANSWER
For every Muslim his self-respect comes first, and selling goods in breach of the law carries the risk of disgrace at the hands of the government. For this reason one should keep away from trade of this kind, even though in itself it is permissible, though disliked. Yes, if it also involves deception and lying, then it becomes altogether impermissible.

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

If the goods are not impure, not forbidden to use and not forbidden to sell, and have been bought from their owner, then trading in them is lawful in itself. But since it is against the law of the government, and an offender is liable to punishment and is disgraced, and it is not permissible to bring disgrace upon oneself, one should not take up such a business. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ۹/۲۲۲) ۔

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Whoever buys goods becomes their owner and acquires the right to use them himself, or to give them away, or to sell them; and whoever then buys them from him may lawfully use them, because he has become the owner. But when a person lives under a government, he is legally bound to obey its law. To act against it is theft in the eyes of the law, and that puts both his honour and his wealth at risk; and it is not wise to put one's honour and wealth at risk. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱٦/۱٤۸، مبوب و مرتب) ۔

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

The reality of smuggling is this: bringing goods in from other countries, or taking goods out to other countries, is permissible in the sharia so long as the goods are lawful. But since the government has placed a ban on it, breaking that ban involves committing many sins. For instance, one usually has to lie, one has to pay bribes, and one has to put one's life, property, honour and good name at risk — all of which the sharia is very careful to protect — and often one has to endure physical suffering and the hardship of imprisonment. One should therefore obey the law of the government and keep away from such a business. Even so, buying and selling lawful and permitted goods that have come in through smuggling is permissible, using them oneself is valid, and the income from them is lawful. (جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام: ۱/۱۰۵) ۔
