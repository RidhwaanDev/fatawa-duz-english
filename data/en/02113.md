### TITLE
A debtor referring his creditor to a third party

### QUESTION
Bakr owes money to Khālid, and Khālid in turn owes money to ʿUmar. Khālid said to ʿUmar: do not take the debt from me, take it from Bakr, who is my debtor. Bakr accepted this. Now ʿUmar says that he will recover his debt from Khālid, while Khālid refuses to pay and says that ʿUmar had already agreed to recover it from Bakr. Is Khālid's refusal correct or not? In short: if a debtor transfers his debt to his own debtor, may the creditor still claim from the debtor or not?

### ANSWER
When a debtor refers his creditor to some third party and that third party accepts, the creditor can no longer make a claim against his own debtor. So in the case in question ʿUmar will recover from Bakr — the debtor of his debtor, who is the third party — and will not make a claim against Khālid. The exception is where the money is lost, in which case he may claim from the debtor: for example, if the third party dies leaving no property or goods, or if he denies the transfer and there is no witness to it.

Consider what the author of al-Hidāyah says:

قال وإذا تمت الحوالة بالقبول بري المحيل من الدين قال في الفتح هذا قول طائفة من المشايخ وهو الصحيح من المذهب

ولا يراجع المحتال على المحيل إلا أن يتوى حقه والتوى عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله أحد الأمرين إما أن يجحد الحوالة ويحلف ولا بينة له عليه أو يموت مفلساً لأن العجز عن الوصول يتحقق بكل منهما وهو التوي في الحقيقة. (الهداية مع فتح القدير: ٦/۳٤۷).
