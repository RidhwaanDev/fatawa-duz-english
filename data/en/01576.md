### TITLE
Judicial separation when a husband fails to maintain his wife

### QUESTION
A woman says of her husband that he does not give her what the household needs: he hands over so little that it is hard to manage, and then he demands an account of it. He also refuses to show any hospitality to her relatives. Quarrelling is a daily occurrence and the wife is in great distress. She does not want to live with him at all, and because his demands are so unjust a khulʿ is difficult too. Can any scholar grant a separation in this case? A separation has already been granted in the court.

### ANSWER
If a husband is causing his wife hardship over maintenance, and matters are so desperate that there is no remedy left but separation, then in such circumstances the Hanafi scholars give their fatwa according to the Mālikī school in order to rescue the woman from her plight. That is, once there is no room left for any reconciliation or khulʿ, the woman is entitled to put her case before a judge or the Jamiatul Ulama and have a separation granted.

Al-Ḥīlah al-Nājizah states:

The wife of an obstinate husband — one who, though well able to do so, does not give his wife her rights of maintenance and the like — must first of all try somehow to obtain a khulʿ or a similar settlement from him. But if no arrangement can be reached despite every effort, then in a case of dire necessity there is room to act on the Mālikī school. Dire necessity takes two forms:

(1) That no provision at all can be made for the woman's expenses: nobody is providing for her, and she herself is not able to earn a living while keeping her honour safe.

(2) That although provision can be made for her expenses, easily or with difficulty, there is strong reason to fear that living apart from her husband will lead her into sin.

The way the separation is carried out is this: the woman puts her case before a judge or a Muslim ruler, or, where there is none, before a body of Muslims. Whoever the case is brought before must investigate it fully through legally acceptable testimony and so on. If the woman's claim is proved — that he does not provide for her although he has the means — her husband is to be told: give your wife her rights or divorce her; otherwise we shall separate you. If even then the wrongdoer will not act on either course, the judge, or whoever stands in his place in sacred law, is to pronounce the divorce. The Mālikīs are agreed that no waiting period or period of grace is required for this.

"للرواية الثانية والعشرين من الفتوي للعلامة سعيد بن صديق المالكي". (الحيلة الناجزة للحليلة العاجزة: ص ٦۳، حكمِ زوجه متعنت في النفقة، ط: دار الاشاعت، ديوبند).

If a body of Muslims investigates in accordance with the directions given above and reaches a decision, that decision is binding.

Conditions for such a body of Muslims

The following points must be kept in mind concerning a body of Muslims:

(1) The Mālikī fatwas use the words "جماعة المسلمين العدول", and by "upright" is meant a person who is not a sinner.

(2) If the decision is entrusted to a body, it must not be a council or gathering of ordinary people: either all its members should be scholars, or there should be at least one scholar with a sound grasp of such matters.

(3) The body must consist of at least three people.

(4) If such a body grants a separation, its decision is binding; but the decision must be unanimous. So if the members differ among themselves, the case is to be dismissed.

(Summarised from "الحيلة الناجزة", p. 135, the section on how a judge exercises jurisdiction in India.)

The fatwa of the Mālikī mufti reads:

الجواب من العلامة سعيد بن صديق الفلا تي متعنا الله بعلومه:

بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم، ولا حول ولا قوة إلا بالله العلى العظيم، سبحانك لا علم لنا إلا ما علمتنا إنك أنت العليم الحكيم، أتم الصلاة وأتم التسليم على سيدنا محمد الهادي الحليم، وعلى آله وصحبه والآتي ربه بقلب سليم

أما الجواب عن المتعنت الممتنع عن الإنفاق ففي مجموع الأمير ما نصه: إن منعها نفقة الحال فلها القيام، فإن لم يثبت عسره أنفق أو طلق، وإلا طلق عليه. قال محشّيه: قوله وإلا طلق أي طلق عليه الحاكم من غير تلوم إلى أن قال: وإن تطوع بالنفقة قريب أو أجنبي قال ابن عبد الرحمن: لا مقال لها، لأن سبب الفراق هو عدم النفقة قد انتفي. (الحيلة الناجزة: ص ۱۱۵، ۱۱۹، ط: دار الاشاعت، ديوبند).

See also: Majmūʿah-e Qawānīn-e Islāmī (Muslim Personal Law Board), by Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām: p. 198, section 79; and Kitāb al-Faskh wa al-Tafrīq, by Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ṣamad Raḥmānī, Maktabah Imārat-e Sharʿiyyah: p. 89.

The decision of a non-Muslim judge in court carries no weight.

Īḍāḥ al-Nawādir states:

If a non-Muslim judge gives a decision about divorce or the like, his decision is not sound or valid in sacred law, and it does not set the wife free in a matter of divorce. ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) recorded this ruling in these words: "لم ينفذ حكم الكافر على المسلم، وينفذ للمسلم على الذمي" ۔ (ایضاح النوادر: ص ۱۵۲) ۔
