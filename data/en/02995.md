### TITLE
Whether a judge appointed by the Muslims can enforce the ruling of liʿān

### QUESTION
A judge who has not been appointed by the government, but rather the Muslims have jointly made someone their Amīr al-Muʾminīn and he has appointed a judge — can such a judge enforce the ruling of liʿān or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, it appears from the books of fiqh and the fatwa collections that a judge appointed by the Muslims can enforce rulings such as annulment and separation. But since liʿān stands in place of the prescribed punishment for false accusation in the case of the man, and in place of the prescribed punishment for fornication in the case of the woman, and since the prescribed punishments and retaliation can be enforced in dār al-Islām by a judge appointed by the government, a governor or judge appointed by the community of Muslims cannot carry out this task.

Muftī Khālid Sayf Allāh Raḥmānī writes in the book compiled on the life of Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām Ṣāḥib:

Before the arrival of Qāḍī Ṣāḥib, it was the practice in the Dār al-Qaḍāʾ of the Imārat-e Sharʿiyyah that if a husband levelled a charge of misconduct against his wife, liʿān would be carried out and then the marriage would be annulled. Qāḍī Ṣāḥib's view was that liʿān, in the opinion of the jurists, stands in place of the prescribed punishment for false accusation in the case of the husband and in place of the prescribed punishment for fornication in the case of the woman, and that the prescribed punishments are carried out in dār al-Islām and not in dār al-kufr. (حضرت مولانا قاضی مجاہد الاسلام قاسمی، حیات و افکار و آثار، ص ٦۲)۔

Moreover, it is recorded in the fatwas of the senior scholars too that for liʿān an Islamic government or a Muslim ruler is necessary, and that in dār al-kufr the ruling of liʿān will not be enforced. Consider a few references:

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Imdād al-Muftīn:

Question: Since there is no Islamic government in India there are no judges. In place of a judge, does anyone have the right to bring about separation between a husband and wife after liʿān? Can the scholars, or the ruler of the time, or some body of Muslims — for example a panchayat and the like — be authorised for this or not?

Answer: Liʿān cannot take place in dār al-ḥarb; for it, being in dār al-Islām is a condition.

صرح به الشامي، وقال في الدر المختار: فمن قذف بصريح الزنا في دار الإسلام زوجته الحية بنكاح صحيح الخ، قال الشامي: أخرج دار الحرب لانقطاع الولاية.

However, if the ruler of the time is a Muslim — even though he is appointed by and in the employment of a non-Muslim government — and he carries out liʿān according to the rules of the sharīʿah, the liʿān will be valid, because he too has the status of a judge. (کما صرح بہ فی رد المحتار من باب القضاء) And the ruling that liʿān does not take place in dār al-ḥarb, which is mentioned in Shāmī, is on account of the severance of authority; when a Muslim is appointed by the government and carries out liʿān, the severance of authority no longer remains.

This humble one also presented this view of his before my master Ḥaḍrat Ḥakīm al-Ummah Mawlānā Muḥammad Ashraf ʿAlī Ṣāḥib (may his blessings endure), and Ḥaḍrat also approved it, and it was clarified and supported by the wording of Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ. واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم۔ (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند، جلد دوم، ص ۵۷۵، ط: دار الاشاعت)۔

The above question and answer have been reproduced in Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, volume one, p. 526, on the authority of Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib. They have also been reproduced in the footnote of Kifāyat al-Muftī (٦/۲۷۲). Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah records:

Dār al-Islām is a condition for liʿān; therefore liʿān is not obligatory in India:

و يشترط أيضاً كون القذف بصريح الزنا، وكونه في دار الإسلام. رد المحتار: ۳/٤۸۳، سعيد.

(فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۳/۳۳۸، جامعہ فاروقیہ)۔ See further: (کتاب الفتاوی: ٦/۵٤)۔

ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

وأما بلاد عليها ولاة كفار فيجوز للمسلمين إقامة الجمع والأعياد ويصير القاضي قاضياً بتراضي المسلمين فيجب عليهم أن يلتمسوا والياً مسلماً منهم

وفي الفتح: وإذا لم يكن سلطان ولا من يجوز التقلد منه كما هو في بعض بلاد المسلمين غلب عليهم الكفار كقرطبة الآن يجب على المسلمين أن يتفقوا على واحد منهم يجعلونه والياً فيولي قاضياً ويكون هو الذي يقضي بينهم وكذا ينصبوا إماماً يصلي بهم الجمعة وهذا الذي يطمئن النفس إليه فليعتمد. (فتاوى الشامي: ۵/۳٦۹، سعيد). (وكذا في فتح القدير: ۷/۲٦٤، ط: دار الفكر).

Al-Fatāwā al-Tātārkhāniyyah states:

وأما بلاد عليها ولاة كفار يجوز للمسلمين إقامة الجمعة والأعياد ويصير القاضي قاضياً بتراضي المسلمين ويجب عليهم أن يلتمسوا والياً مسلماً. (الفتاوى التاتارخانية: ۵/۳۲٦).

From the wording of Fatāwā Tātārkhāniyyah it appears that a judge may be appointed by the mutual agreement of the Muslims.

The same is recorded in (البحر الرائق: ٦/۲۹۸،
و فتاوی الشامی: ۲/۱٤٤، سعیدو ٤/۱۷۵، سعید،
و حاشیۃ الطحطاوی علی مراقی الفلاح، ص: باب الجمعۃ،
و فتاوی ہندیہ: ۱/۱٤٦، بحوالہ معراج الدرایہ)۔

Qāḍī Mujāhid al-Islām Ṣāḥib has written in Islāmī ʿAdālat:

The fourth case is that the disbelievers have gained dominance over the country, and power lies in the hands of a non-Muslim king and absolute ruler, or of some democratic government in which power is in practice in the hands of non-Muslims. In this case it will be obligatory upon the Muslims to elect a leader of their own so that they may live a collective life, and the delegation of judicial authority by this leader will be valid. In this way, too, the Islamic sharīʿah will be able to be implemented among the Muslims as far as possible, and the sin of suspending the rulings of the sharīʿah, as well as of dissension and division, will be avoided. And if — may God forbid — the Muslims do not agree upon the election of a leader, but the people of binding and loosing agree upon appointing a particular person as judge, this too will be valid. (اسلامی عدالت، حصہ اول، ص ۱۷۸، ۱۷۹، ط: قاضی پبلشرز)۔

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states: Where no arrangement of this kind exists on the part of the government, and the general body of Muslims establishes, for the settlement of matters of this kind, a panchayat consisting of at least three persons of knowledge and understanding of the matter, then its decision too takes the place of the decision of a judge. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ٤/۵٤۸، ط: مکتبۃ الاحسان، دیوبند)۔

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states:

From the wording of the Tātārkhāniyyah and other works, which ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) has quoted, it appears that a judge who is appointed by the mutual agreement of the Muslims has, in the settlement of disputes, the standing of a sharʿī judge. The author of Fatḥ al-Qadīr, however, has verified in this matter that first a Muslim governor should be appointed, and then that governor should appoint a judge; and there is no doubt that this is a matter of complete peace of mind,

كما قال صاحب النهر

۔ (فتاوی دار العلوم دیوبند: ۱۰/۲٤۷، مدلل و مکمل)۔

But Fatāwā Khalīliyyah records that a sharʿī judge will not come into being by the mutual agreement of the Muslims, because he does not possess the authority and awe of government. Consider what Fatāwā Khalīliyyah records:

It is necessary for a judge to be a holder of governmental authority, since the very meaning of qaḍāʾ is a binding ruling; and both lexically and in the sharīʿah, governmental authority is a pillar of the office of judge. Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وأركانه ستة على ما نظمه ابن الفرس: حكم ومحكوم به وله ومحكوم عليه وطريق. در مختار مع الشامي (٤/٤٦۱)

And it is obvious that a person whom the subjects themselves make a judge will not be a holder of governmental authority; hence he will also not be a sharʿī judge. ʿĀlamgīrī states:

وإذا اجتمع أهل بلدة على رجل وجعلوه قاضياً يقضي بينهم لا يصير قاضياً. (٤/۱٦٤)

It is therefore obligatory upon the Muslims of India that they petition the government to appoint a Muslim judge who may decide, in accordance with the sharīʿah, those matters in which the decision of a judge is required by the sharīʿah; and it is necessary for these judges to be holders of governmental authority. (فتاوی خلیلیہ: ۱/۲۵۳، ط: مکتبۃ الشیخ)۔

For further detail see: Imdād al-Aḥkām: ٤/۸۸۔ ۹٦، the treatise "القول الماضي في نصب القاضي".
