### TITLE
Saying "Tell everyone that I have divorced you"

### QUESTION
A woman said to her husband, "Divorce me; there is nothing but disgrace in this marriage." In reply the husband wrote her a note: "Your disgrace will pass; tell everyone that I have divorced you." To all appearances he did not mean by this to bring about a divorce. Has a divorce taken effect in this case or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, if he did not in fact give a divorce and merely said to the woman, "Tell everyone that I have divorced you", then as a matter of conscience no divorce has taken effect, though as far as legal judgement goes it has. If he had taken witnesses to this beforehand, however, it will not take effect judicially either. And if the husband's purpose in these words was to give a divorce, then the divorce has taken effect.

Consider what al-Shāmī states:

وأما ما في إكراه الخانية: لو أكره على أن يقر بالطلاق فأقر لا يقع، كما لو أقر بالطلاق هازلاً أو كاذباً، فقال في البحر: إن مراده لعدم الوقوع في المشبه به عدمه ديانة، ثم
نقل عن البزازية والقنية: لو أراد به الخبر عن الماضي كاذباً لا يقع ديانة، وإن أشهد قبل ذلك لا يقع قضاءً أيضاً. (فتاوى الشامي: ۳/۲۳۸، كتاب الطلاق، سعيد).
