### TITLE
Saying "Take your things and go to your parents' house"

### QUESTION
A husband said the following words:

"Pack your things and go to your father's house, I don't want you anymore, get out."

That is, "Go away; I have no need of you now; take your things and go to your parents' house." He says, however, that he did not intend by these words to bring about a divorce. Does a divorce take effect or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, since the husband denies having had any intention of divorce, no divorce has taken effect. The reason is that these are among the allusive expressions, and a divorce takes effect by an allusive expression only when the man intends it, or when he says it during a discussion of divorce. If he says it during a discussion of divorce, the divorce takes effect as far as legal judgement goes, even without an intention; as a matter of conscience, though — between him and Allah — no divorce takes effect.

Consider what al-Hidāyah states:

الكنايات لا يقع بها الطلاق إلا بالنية أو بدلالة الحال، لا نها غير موضوعة للطلاق بل تحتمله وغيره فلا بد من التعيين أو دلالته وهي على ضربين

وبقية الكنايات إذا نوى بها كانت واحدة بائنة، وإن نوى ثلاثا كان ثلاثا

إلا أن يكون في حالة مذاكرة الطلاق فيقع بها الطلاق في القضاء، ولا يقع فيما بينه وبين الله تعالى إلا أن ينويه. (الهداية ۲/۳۷٤، باب إيقاع الطلاق. وكذا في بدائع الصنائع: ۳/۱۰٦. والشامي: ۳/۳۰۱، سعيد).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
