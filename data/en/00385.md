### TITLE
Speaking during the gaps left after responding to the adhan

### QUESTION
Suppose the adhan is called so slowly that a good deal of time is left over after responding — responding to one phrase takes about three seconds, while the muezzin takes fifteen or twenty seconds over it. May one speak in between or not?

### ANSWER
The essential thing is to respond to the adhan. That is why the jurists have forbidden giving a greeting while the adhan is being called: it disrupts the response. But if the gaps are so long that time is left over after responding, then in those gaps one may engage in remembrance of Allah or in other speech.

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī records:

عن النبي ﷺ قال: إذا سمعتم النداء فقولوا مثل ما يقول المؤذن. (رواه البخاري: ۱/۸٦)

Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah states:

Even worldly talk is permissible during the muezzin's pauses. (فتاوی رشیدیہ: ص ۲۹۰)

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states:

Doing these things — giving a greeting and returning one — in the gaps between the phrases does not conflict with responding to the adhan. (عمدۃ الفقہ: ۲/٤۲) ۔
