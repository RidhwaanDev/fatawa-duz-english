### TITLE
An investigation into the grave of the great Successor Ḥaḍrat Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabīḥ

### QUESTION
It is well known of Ḥaḍrat Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabīḥ (may Allah have mercy on him) that he died in a village called Bhāṛbhūt near Bharuch in the province of Gujarat in India, a place on the sea coast. Two questions are put here: (1) In our own time, is this area in Sindh or in India? (2) Is Ḥaḍrat Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabīḥ (may Allah have mercy on him) weak or trustworthy in the transmission of hadith? بینوا بالدلیل توجروا بالاجر الجزیل ۔

### ANSWER
(1) Most books of history and of the names of hadith transmitters record that Ḥaḍrat Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabīḥ (may Allah have mercy on him) died in Sindh. Ḥāfiẓ al-Dhahabī (may Allah have mercy on him) in Siyar Aʿlām al-Nubalāʾ and in al-Kāshif, and Imām al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) in Kitāb al-Ḍuʿafāʾ, wrote that he died in Sindh. Some, however, have written that it was in India: Ibn Saʿd wrote in al-Ṭabaqāt al-Kubrā

"خرج غازياً إلى الهند"

There are two ways of reconciling the two:

(a) It may be that the place lies at the meeting-point of India and Sindh, so that one writer called it Sindh and another India — just as some write of Kabul that it is a city of Sijistān and others that it is a city of Khurāsān, as though it lies at the meeting-point.

(b) Or Sindh may be a part of Greater India, just as Uttar Pradesh is a part of India and Sindh today is a part of Pakistan — although Sindh also had a standing of its own because of its scholarly importance.

Consider the references:

قال الإمام الذهبي في سير أعلام النبلاء: توفي بالسند سنة ستين ومائة. (۷/۲۸۸، رقم الترجمة: ۸۷).

وقال في الكاشف: الربيع بن صبيح السعدي عن الحسن وعطاء وعنه، ابن مهدي، وعلي بن الجعد وكان صدوقاً غزا عابداً ضعفه النسائي، توفي ۱٦۰ هـ بالسند. (الكاشف: ۱/۲۳٦، ط: بيروت).

وقال الإمام المزي في تهذيب الكمال: قال محمد بن المثني وغيره مات سنة ستين ومائة بأرض السند.

وقال في حاشية تهذيب الكمال: وكذلك قال ابن سعد، ويحيى بن معين، وخليفة، والبخاري، وابن حبان، وابن زبر، وغيرهم، وكان المهدي قد سير جيشاً في البحر بقيادة عبد الملك بن شهاب المسمعي إلى بلاد الهند، فحاصر الجيش مدينة باربد وفتحها عنوة، وكان الربيع بن صبيح رحمه الله في هذا الجيش، فأصابهم مرض في العودة توفي فيه الربيع، فدفن في إحدى جزر البحر، قال ابن سعد: خرج غازياً إلى الهند في البحر، فمات فدفن في جزيرة من جزائر البحر سنة ستين ومائة في أول خلافة المهدي، أخبرني بذلك شيخ من أهل البصرة كان معه. [الطبقات: ۷/۲۷۷]، وانظر تاريخ الطبري [۸/۱۲۸]، ووفيات ابن زبر الورقة ۵۱. (تهذيب الكمال مع التعليقات: ۹/۹٤).

وقال ابن سعد: الربيع بن صبيح ويكني أبا حفص مولي لبني سعد بن زيد مناة بن تميم خرج غازياً إلى الهند في البحر فمات فدفن في جزيرة من جزائر البحر سنة ستين ومائة في أول خلافة المهدي

وكان ضعيفاً في الحديث وقد روي عنه الثوري وأما عفان فتركه فلم يحدث منه. (الطبقات الكبري: ۷/۲۷۷، دار صادر، بيروت).

See further: (تهذيب التهذيب: ۳/۲۲۲، وكتاب الضعفاء الصغير للإمام البخاري، ص ٤۷، ونزهة الخواطر: ۱/۷۰، والمغني في ضبط الأسماء للشيخ محمد طاهر بن على الفتني، ص ۱۷٤).

Mawlānā Qāḍī Aṭhar Mubārakpūrī writes in Islāmī Hind kī ʿAẓmat-i Raftah:

The place of Imām Rabīʿ's death and burial: from the details of the expedition of Bārbad it is perfectly clear that this expedition took place at Bārbad, a city of the land of India which was in those days the capital of a raja, and that Imām Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabīḥ, along with a thousand other Muslim fighters, died of an epidemic disease at that very place or somewhere near it, and that they were washed and shrouded for burial at the very place of death. The evidence that these men died at Bārbad in India, or in its neighbourhood, is as follows:

Al-Ṭabarī wrote:

وفيها وجه المهدي عبد الملك بن شهاب المسمعي في البحر إلى بلاد الهند.

That is, in 159 AH al-Mahdī despatched ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Shihāb al-Mismaʿī by the sea route towards the lands of India.

Then, further on, he wrote:

فمضوا لوجههم حتى أتوا باربد من بلاد الهند.

These men set out and came straight to Bārbad, a city of the lands of India.

Ibn al-Athīr wrote in al-Kāmil that in 159 AH al-Mahdī despatched a military expedition to the lands of India by the sea route, whose commander was ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Shihāb al-Mismaʿī. Many soldiers and volunteers took part in it, and Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabīḥ was among them; and these men came to Bārbad.

Imām al-Dhahabī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote in al-ʿIbar fī Khabar man Ghabar that in 160 AH the Muslims, under the command of ʿAbd al-Malik al-Mismaʿī, conquered a very great city of India.

In the same way other historians have recorded the account of the Indian expedition of Bārbad and have mentioned the death of Imām Rabīʿ in connection with it; but it has not been possible, in the light of historical proofs and evidence, to determine his burial place — at what spot he died and where he was buried. ʿAllāmah Ibn Saʿd (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote in the Ṭabaqāt:

خرج غازياً إلى الهند

الخ.

Rabīʿ went to India for the Indian expedition, and in the meantime, in 160 AH, in the early period of al-Mahdī's caliphate, he died and was buried on one of the islands of that sea. A shaykh of Basra who was with them gave me this report.

This statement of Ibn Saʿd is highly trustworthy and dependable because he heard it from the lips of an elder of Basra who had himself taken part with Imām Rabīʿ in the battle of Bārbad. ʿAllāmah al-Balādhurī accepted this same finding and report of ʿAllāmah Ibn Saʿd in Futūḥ al-Buldān in these words:

وكان خرج غازياً إلى الهند في البحر فمات فدفن في جزيرة من الجزائر سنة ستين ومائة. (فتوح البلدان، ص ۳٦۲)

Rabīʿ set out by the sea route towards India for jihad, and in the meantime, in 160 AH, he died and was buried on an island.

Imām al-Dhahabī (may Allah have mercy on him) stated expressly, in these words, that Rabīʿ died on the return from the Indian expedition:

وتوفي في غزوة الهند في الرجعة بالبحر الربيع بن صبيح صاحب الحسن.

Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabīḥ, the companion of al-Ḥasan (may Allah have mercy on him), died on the return by sea after the Indian expedition.

ʿAllāmah Ibn al-ʿImād al-Ḥanbalī wrote this same passage in Shadharāt al-Dhahab among the events of 160 AH.

From the statements of all these historians and biographers it appears that the place of Imām Rabīʿ's death and burial is Bārbad itself, or some island or islet between it and the sea. It is still generally well known among the Muslims of Gujarat that there is the tomb of some Successor at Bhāṛbhūt in the district of Bharuch and at Rāndēr in the district of Surat; indeed, at Rāndēr one particular tomb is ascribed to a Successor, though this is not established. Clearly, it is in one of these two places, or near them, that Ḥaḍrat Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabīḥ (may Allah have mercy on him) and thousands of other Muslim fighters lie at rest, and the strong likelihood is that this sacred spot is Bhāṛbhūt or somewhere near it.

In spite of all these express statements that Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabīḥ died in India, Imām al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) gave Sindh as his place of death. He says in al-Tārīkh al-Kabīr:

مات سنة ستين ومائة بارض السند.

Rabīʿ died in 160 AH in the land of Sindh.

Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (may Allah have mercy on him) also wrote in Tahdhīb al-Tahdhīb:

قال محمد بن المثني وغيره مات ۱٦۰ هـ بأرض السند.

Muḥammad ibn al-Muthannā and others said that Rabīʿ died in the land of Sindh.

In our view the reason for this is that down to the third century Arab travellers and geographers, when they said "Sindh", included in it the coastal regions and cities as far as Gujarat — just as Ibn Khurradādhbih (living in 250 AH) counted several cities of Gujarat as belonging to Sindh in al-Masālik wa al-Mamālik — although historians and geographers generally recognised India and Sindh as two separate countries and placed the boundary of India after Sindh, at a place called Qāmuhul, within which the coastal regions of Gujarat fell.

In the same way all our Indian biographers have with one voice given Sindh as the place of Ḥaḍrat Rabīʿ's death. So far as we know, the first to state expressly in al-Mughnī that Rabīʿ died in Sindh was ʿAllāmah Muḥammad Ṭāhir Gujarātī (may Allah have mercy on him); after him all the Indian biographers kept this before them and wrote Sindh as his place of death, and none of them paid attention to the express statements of Ibn Saʿd, al-Balādhurī, al-Ṭabarī, al-Dhahabī, Ibn al-Athīr, Ibn Khaldūn, Ibn al-ʿImād al-Ḥanbalī and others. Thus ʿAllāmah Ghulām ʿAlī Āzād in Subḥat al-Marjān fī Āthār Hindūstān, p. 26, Mawlawī Raḥmān ʿAlī in Tadhkirah-i ʿUlamāʾ-i Hind, p. 3, and Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy (may Allah have mercy on him) in Nuzhat al-Khawāṭir (vol. 1, p. 32) have all given Sindh as the place of Imām Rabīʿ's death. Imām al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) gave Sindh as Rabīʿ's place of death because, according to the usage of his own time, he counted this region of India as part of Sindh; but these Indian biographers, living in India itself, still placed it in Sindh. The reason for this is apparently the misunderstanding that in their view the lands of India meant the regions of Sindh, that Bārbad lay somewhere within them, and that down to the second century of the Hijrah the only centre of general Muslim activity was the region of Sindh — whereas Arab historians counted Sindh and India as separate countries, and it is not without reason that they repeatedly described the expedition of Bārbad as being to the lands of India and to India. Moreover, by that time the coastal regions of India had already felt the effect of Muslim activity, and several ventures had already been undertaken before the expedition of Bārbad. (Islāmī Hind kī ʿAẓmat-i Raftah, from p. 157 to p. 160, the notice of Imām Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabīḥ al-Baṣrī al-Hindī, published by Nadwat al-Muṣannifīn).

Gujarāt kī ʿIlmī wa Adabī Shakhṣiyyāt records:

After the conquest of Bhāṛbhūt the Muslim army made ready to return; but at that very time the sea grew rough, so that the fighters could not return at once and were obliged to remain there until the sea grew calm. As ill luck would have it, an epidemic broke out at that very time, and a thousand fighters fell victim to the disease. According to the statement of researchers generally, Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabīḥ was among those who were martyred, and he was buried there. (Gujarāt kī ʿIlmī wa Adabī Shakhṣiyyāt, citing al-Bidāyah wa al-Nihāyah: 9/132).

The same book also records:

"Bārbad" (now Bhāṛbhūt) is a small town on the Arabian Sea at the bank of the river "Narmadā", situated 48 kilometres from the city of Bharuch. The tomb of Ḥaḍrat Rabīʿ ibn Ṣabīḥ remained for some time at the water's edge; later it was struck by the waves and disappeared into the river, and now even its traces have been effaced. Some men of spiritual insight, however, have pointed out its position in the river "Narmadā". (Gujarāt kī ʿIlmī wa Adabī Shakhṣiyyāt, p. 547, citing Sawāniḥ Munshī ʿĪsā Bhāʾī Kāwī, 1).
