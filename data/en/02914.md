### TITLE
The ruling on calling people together for a recommended act

### QUESTION
We hold a gathering of remembrance once a week. For the convenience of our friends we have fixed it for Sunday night. Fixing Sunday night is purely for people's convenience. The first time, an announcement was made that a gathering of remembrance would be held on Sunday night, in which, besides an admonition and reminder, there would be remembrance; after that no announcement is needed. Some of the Deobandi scholars raise two objections to this. The first is that calling people together for a recommended act — that is, inviting people or making an announcement — is an innovation or is disliked.

The second is that Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) called gatherings of this kind inside the mosque an innovation and considered them objectionable. Since you write on the subject of collective remembrance, we wish to have the honour of putting these two questions before you. بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
حامداً و مصلیاً و مسلماً:

Answer to the first question: A recommended act is one whose performance is better than its omission. It is of two kinds: one for which inviting people and calling them together is established, and another for which such calling together is not established. Where calling people together is established for a recommended or sunnah act, it is correct to invite people to it — as it is correct to invite people to a walīmah feast, an ʿaqīqah feast, the prayer for rain and the like.

Regarding the prayer for rain, Mullā ʿAlī Qārī (may Allah have mercy on him) has quoted from Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her):
شكي الناس قحوط المطر فأمر بمنبر فوضع له في المصلي، ووعد الناس يوماً يخرجون فيه، قالت عائشة رضي الله عنه: فخرج رسول الله ﷺ حين بدا حاجب الشمس

الخ.

In this there is a promise to come out on a particular day for the prayer for rain. (شرح نقایہ: ۱/۳٤۹) At the end Mullā ʿAlī Qārī (may Allah have mercy on him) said:
رواه أبو داود (رقم: ۱۱۷۵) وقال: غريب وإسناده جيد ورواه الحاكم في المستدرك (۱/۳۲۷/۱۲۲۵)

Moreover, the prayer for rain in congregation is established.

In the same way, congregation is a sunnah in the prayer at an eclipse, and it is narrated from Rasūlullāh ﷺ: "فإذا رأيتم ذلك فافزعوا إلى الصلاة" — and this is a calling together.

In the same way, calling people together for gatherings of remembrance is established from the hadiths of his sayings. Rasūlullāh ﷺ did not keep the fast of Dāwūd, yet its being recommended is established from his sayings; and he did not perform an ʿumrah in Ramadan, yet its being recommended is established from the hadiths of his sayings. In just this way, encouragement towards gatherings of remembrance is established from the sayings of Rasūlullāh ﷺ. We now quote a few hadiths from which the encouragement towards gatherings of remembrance, and the calling of people to them, will become clear:

(۱) عن أبي الدرداء رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: ليبعثن الله أقواماً يوم القيامة في وجوههم النور، على منابر اللؤلؤ يغبطهم الناس ليسوا بأنبياء ولا شهداء قال: فجثا أعرابي على ركبتيه فقال: يا رسول الله! حلهم لنا نعرفهم قال: هم المتحابون في الله من قبائل شتي وبلاد شتى يجتمعون على ذكر الله يذكرونه. (مجمع الزوائد: ۱۰/۷۷، ۱۰۲۲۱. إسناده حسن).

The gist of this hadith is that Allah will seat people with radiant faces on pulpits of light — people who had gathered from different tribes and different cities for the remembrance of Allah. Clearly, these people will not have gathered by chance, nor will anyone have driven them out with sticks; rather they will have gathered as the result of some announcement.

(2) In the narration of Ḥaḍrat ʿAmr ibn ʿAbasah (may Allah be pleased with him) these words are reported:
قيل: من هم؟ قال: هم جماع من نوازع القبائل يجتمعون على ذكر الله. (مجمع الزوائد، رجاله موثقون)
These people will be gathered from different tribes like strangers drawn together for the remembrance of Allah. In these words there are indications — indeed an explicit statement — of calling people together. جماع means people of different tribes mixed together, and نوازع is the plural of نازعہ, meaning groups drawn out from their places. Hadiths with this content are also found in Muṣannaf ʿAbd al-Razzāq and Musnad Aḥmad. (مسند احمد۳/ ۵۳۰/ ۲۲۸۹، و مصنف عبد الرزاق: ۱۱/۲۰۱) ۔

(3) A hadith in Bukhārī Sharīf states: "إن لله ملائكة يطوفون في الطرق يلتمسون أهل الذكر فإذا وجدوا قوماً يذكرون الله تنادوا هلموا إلى حاجاتكم". (رواه البخاري: ۲/۹٤۸).

Allah has angels who roam the roads searching out those who remember Him; and when they see a group of people engaged in remembrance, they announce and call out to one another: come, what you were seeking is here. The angels' announcement and calling together in this hadith is not hidden from people of understanding. It should be borne in mind that Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written that congregation for a voluntary prayer is permissible occasionally. Imām Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him) accordingly wrote in Kitāb al-Adhkār:
اعلم أنه كما يستحب الذكر يستحب الجلوس في حلق أهله، وقد تظاهرت الأدلة على ذلك. (كتاب الأذكار، ص: ۲٦، مع تعليق الشيخ بشير محمد عيون. مكتبة دار البيان)،
Its full text comes below.

(4) A hadith in Shuʿab al-Īmān of Bayhaqī states:
عن أبي رزين أنه قال له رسول الله ﷺ: ألا أدلك على ملاك هذا الأمر الذي تصيب به خير الدنيا والآخرة عليك بمجالس أهل الذكر وإذا خلوت فحرك لسانك ما استطعت بذكر الله. (أخرجه الامام البيهقي في شعب الإيمان: ۱۱/۳۲۹، رقم: ۸٦۰۸. وقال محشيه: إسناده ضعيف؛ عثمان بن عطاء هو ابن أبي مسلم أبو مسعود المقدسي، ضعيف).

Rasūlullāh ﷺ said to Abū Razīn (may Allah be pleased with him): shall I not point you to that foundation of this religion by which you may attain the good of this world and the next? Keep to the gatherings of the people of remembrance. In this hadith Rasūlullāh ﷺ is himself inviting people to attend gatherings of remembrance.

(5) It is narrated from Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) that Rasūlullāh ﷺ passed by ʿAbd Allāh ibn Rawāḥah (may Allah be pleased with him), who was engaged in preaching and reminding. Rasūlullāh ﷺ said: you are in such a company that Allah, glorified be His majesty, has commanded me to sit with you. Then the Prophet ﷺ recited the noble verse
﴿واصبر نفسك مع الذين يدعون ربهم بالغداة والعشي﴾
— that Allah sends angels equal in number to those engaged in remembrance, telling them to keep reciting glorification, praise and Allāhu akbar along with them. The angels say to Allah: O Allah, these servants of Yours were reciting Your glorification, magnification, praise and laudation, and we were reciting along with them. Allah will say: O angels, I make you witnesses that I have forgiven them. الخ ۔ In its chain of narration Muḥammad ibn Ḥammād Kūfī is weak.

Ṭabarānī's shaykh Mūsā ibn ʿĪsā al-Ḥimṣī has also been severely criticised. (المعجم الصغیر للطبرانی، رقم: ٤۷٤، مجمع الزوائد: ۱۰/۷٦،
حلیۃ الاولیاء لابی نعیم: ۵/۱۱۸، و جامع الحدیث للسیوطی ۲۹۷) ۔

We have presented this narration only by way of corroboration. Mūsā ibn ʿĪsā al-Ḥimṣī is not in the chain of Ḥilyat al-Awliyāʾ; but the weak narrator Muḥammad ibn Ḥammād al-Kūfī is present in the chain of Ḥilyat al-Awliyāʾ too.

(٦) إذا مررتم برياض الجنة فارتعوا قيل: ما رياض الجنة؟ قال: حلق الذكر. (رواه الترمذي وقال هذا حديث حس غريب.
ذکر اجتماعی و جہری ص ٤۰) ۔
وفي تعليق جمع الفوائد قال الألباني: حسن. (٤/٤۳).

In this hadith Rasūlullāh ﷺ is urging people to sit in the gathering of remembrance and to take their fill of it. فارتعوا is a calling together, an announcement and an encouragement.

And another hadith states:
إذا مررتم برياض الجنة فارتعوا قلت: يا رسول الله وما رياض الجنة قال المساجد. وما الرتع يا رسول الله قال: سبحان الله والحمد لله ولا إله إلا الله والله أكبر. (رواه الترمذي "كذا في الترغيب والترهيب: ۲/۲۸٤" وقال وهو مع غرابته حسن الإسناد).
(ذکر اجتماعی و جہری، ص ۵۲) ۔
In this hadith the mosques are called the gardens of Paradise, and "Subḥān Allāh" and the other formulae of remembrance are called grazing and taking one's fill. Putting the two hadiths together, the conclusion is that the Noble Prophet ﷺ is inviting and urging people to take part in the circles of remembrance in the mosques. Imām Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Kitāb al-Adhkār:

اعلم أنه كما يستحب الذكر يستحب الجلوس في حلق أهله، وقد تظاهرت الأدلة على ذلك وسترد في مواضعها إن شاء الله تعالى ويكفي في ذلك حديث ابن عمر رضي الله عنه. قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: إذا مررتم برياض الجنة فارتعوا. قالوا: وما رياض الجنة يا رسول الله قال: حلق الذكر فإن لله تعالى سيارات من الملائكة يطلبون حلق الذكر فإذا أتوا عليهم حفوا بهم. (كتاب الأذكار، ص: ۲٦، مع تعليق الشيخ بشير محمد عيون. مكتبة دار البيان).

In his notes on it Bashīr Muḥammad ʿUyūn writes:
أبو نعيم في الحلية: ٦/۳۵٤، وأخرجه الترمذي من حديث أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه وأبو يعلي والبزار والحاكم من حديث جابر وفي إسناده عمر بن عبد الله مولى عفرة وهو ضعيف وأيوب بن خالد ليس بذاك،
and at the end he writes:
وهو حديث حسن بشواهده ولتمام الفائدة انظر: (نتائج الأفكار: ۱/۱٦ تا ۲۳).

This narration mentions the angels going about in search of gatherings of remembrance.

(۷) عن أنس بن مالك رضي الله عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: لان أقعد مع قوم يذكرون الله من صلاة الغداة حتى تطلع الشمس أحب إلى من أن أعتق أربعة من ولد إسماعيل، ولأن أقعد مع قوم يذكرون الله من صلاة العصر إلى أن تغرب الشمس أحب إلى من أن أعتق أربعة. (رواه أبو داود: ۵/۵۰۷/۳٦٦۷، الرسالة).
قال الشيخ شعيب الأرناؤوط في تعليقاته على سنن أبي داود: إسناده حسن من أجل موسي بن خلف العمي، فهو صدوق حسن الحديث.

Rasūlullāh ﷺ said: for me to sit with people who are engaged in the remembrance of Allah from the Fajr prayer until sunrise is dearer to me than freeing four Arab slaves; and for me to sit with those who remember Allah from the ʿAṣr prayer until sunset is dearer to me than freeing four Arab slaves.

In this hadith Rasūlullāh ﷺ expresses his longing to sit in a gathering of remembrance after Fajr. If someone makes the longing of Rasūlullāh ﷺ his own longing and invites another to take part in such gatherings, how can that be called an innovation?

(2) The second kind of recommended act is that for which calling people together, announcing and assembling is not established — as congregation for voluntary prayers is not established as a permanent practice, although Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written that it is permissible occasionally. Where assembling and announcing is not established for a recommended act, then if a person makes an announcement while regarding the assembling itself as a sunnah and an act of worship, that will be an innovation. But if he does not regard such an assembly as a sunnah, an act of worship or an end in itself, and makes the announcement regarding the gathering of people as a matter of benefit and expedience, and does not revile those who do not attend or consider them blameworthy, then there is no harm in making the announcement. On this basis, even if we were to suppose that calling people together for gatherings of remembrance is not established, then so long as a person announces it as a matter of expedience and not as a sunnah or an act of worship, there should be no harm in it.

We present one parallel case so that this matter may settle in your mind and become completely clear.

Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) has written in Majmūʿ al-Fatāwā (۲۱/۱۱٦ ۔ ۱۱۹) that shaving the head is of four kinds:

(1) Shaving as an act of worship, which takes place in Hajj and ʿumrah.

(2) Shaving out of necessity, as when lice appear in the hair and one has to have the head shaved out of need; this too is permissible.

(3) Shaving as a matter of custom, which ordinary people have done all along; in the view of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) and Imām Shāfiʿī (may Allah have mercy on him) this is permitted.

(4) Shaving as an innovation: if a person makes shaving the head outside Hajj and ʿumrah his distinguishing mark and has it done regarding it as an act of worship, then this shaving is blameworthy and an innovation. This same shaving is permitted in ordinary circumstances, but to do it regarding it as an act of worship is an innovation. In the same way, if people were to regard an assembly for some recommended act, or a particular form of it, as an act of worship and a sunnah, it would be blameworthy: the turban-tying ceremony, the Tablīghī gathering, the gathering for spending the night, the gathering of the older workers, the centenary gathering of Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, the gatherings of Maẓāhir al-ʿUlūm of former times which Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) also used to attend, the reformative gatherings of those attached to the Sufi orders, the turban-tying gathering of Miftāḥ al-ʿUlūm Jalālābād, the Khulafāʾ-e Rāshidīn Conference, the annual Khatm-e Nubuwwat gathering, the Ahl-e Sunnat Conference, the Tawḥīd and Sunnah Conference of the people of England, the various conferences of the Salafīs in Saudi Arabia, the conferences and gatherings of the Ishāʿat al-Tawḥīd people, the Imām Aʿẓam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) Conference, the Ahl-e Ḥadīth Conference, the gathering of reciters for beautiful recitation, the Ḥaqq Chār Yār gathering, the gatherings for the completion of Bukhārī, prize-giving gatherings, and conferences on fiqh.

The definition of a recommended act — "one whose performance is better than its omission" — applies to all the gatherings mentioned above. Those who hold them regard them as a good work, and for that reason communities or governments spend millions of dollars on them. These gatherings are not established from the golden age of prophethood; but since our scholars and spiritual guides do not regard them as a sunnah or as ends in themselves, but rather as matters of expedience and benefit, they hold these conferences and gatherings, and they do not criticise those who do not hold them in the slightest. Gatherings of remembrance may, by way of concession, be counted in this same category — though in reality they belong to the first kind.

As for those who count these conferences and gatherings under enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong, that is, among the necessary duties, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) has forcefully refuted them. We present a short extract from his lengthy discourse; let readers take it as a collyrium for their insight. In his sermon entitled Ḥuqūq al-ʿIlm, Ḥaḍrat said: the jurists have stated expressly in Kitāb al-Siyar — and reason too accepts this — that where Islam and its rulings have already reached, conveying the message is not necessary, though it is recommended. (حقوق العلم، ص۳۱، طبع سہارنپور) ۔

Answer to the second question: The questioner's second question was that Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) did not look upon gatherings of remembrance with approval, and that he therefore came into the mosque and forbade collective remembrance. We have discussed the various answers to this incident, the various explanations of Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd's (may Allah be pleased with him) prohibition, and the chains of these narrations in our book Dhikr-e Ijtimāʿī wa Jahrī Sharīʿat ke Āʾīne Meṉ. Here we confine ourselves to a single answer, and in our view this answer is the better and the easier one:

Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) criticised the fixing of a particular number, and there can be two reasons for this. One is that he did not regard the remembrance of Allah as being bound by numbers; rather he wished the remembrance of Allah to be without count and without limit, which is why a weak narration reports from him a prohibition of keeping prayer beads. The second reason, which is the more reasonable, is that if the shaykh of some circle fixed a particular number for the remembrance, it was quite possible that later on that number would come to be regarded as a legal sunnah — just as some followers of the spiritual path begin acting upon a certain hadith, and then after some time it comes to light that the narration is fabricated or close to it, yet even so the people of that order continue to act upon it and call it following the shaykhs. We now mention a few indications and pieces of evidence which make it clear that the aim of Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) was to reject the fixing of a particular number:

1. In Muṣannaf Ibn Abī Shaybah it is narrated from Ibrāhīm Nakhaʿī:
كان عبد الله يكره العدد ويقول: أيمن على الله حسناته. (مصنف ابن أبي شيبه: ۵/۲۲۰، باب من كره عقد التسبيح، وفي إسناده: الخضر بن أبان وفيه كلام).
Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) disliked counting the remembrance and would say: are they reminding Allah of the favour of their good deeds?

2.
مر ابن مسعود رضي الله عنه بامرأة معها تسبيح تسبح به فقطعه وألقاه ثم مر برجل يسبح بحصي فضربه برجله ثم قال: لقد سبقتم ركبتم بدعة ظلماء ولقد غلبتم أصحاب محمد ﷺ علماً. (سلسلة الأحاديث الضعيفة للألباني: ۱/۱۱۲)، رواه الصلت عن ابن مسعود رضي الله عنه ولم يلقه.

Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) passed by a woman who had a rosary with which she was reciting the formulae of glorification; he cut it and threw it away. Then he passed by a man and struck him with his foot and said: Companions have gone before you; you have committed a dark innovation and have outstripped the Companions in knowledge — saying this by way of rebuke. It should be remembered that Shaykh Albānī has accepted this disconnected narration.

3. In the narration of Dārimī these words occur:
فوقف أي ابن مسعود رضي الله عنه عليهم فقال: ما هذا الذي أراكم تصنعون قالوا: يا أبا عبد الله حصاً نعد بها التكبير والتهليل والتسبيح قال: فعدوا سيئاتكم فأناْ ضامن أن لا يضيع من حسناتكم. (سنن الدارمي: ۱/۵۷۹). وفي إسناده عمرو بن يحيى وفيه كلام.

Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) stood over the people engaged in remembrance in the mosque and said: what is this that I see you doing? They said: we are counting the takbīr, the tahlīl and the tasbīḥ with pebbles. He said: count your sins; I guarantee that Allah will not let your good deeds go to waste — that is, remembrance is among the good deeds and there is no need to count it; what needs to be noted, counted and repented of are one's sins. This narration makes it plain that he was objecting to the counting.

4. The narration of Ṭabarānī's Al-Muʿjam al-Kabīr also shows that he was objecting to the counting. In it these words occur:
ذكر لابن مسعود رضي الله عنه قاص يجلس بالليل ويقول للناس قولوا كذا وقولوا كذا. وفي إسناده كلام
— that is, a preacher would sit at night and say to the people: so-and-so, recite this formula so many times; so-and-so, recite this formula so many times. Then, in the light of another narration of the Muʿjam of Ṭabarānī, he asked which group had come first, and when he was told, he raised the second circle and joined it to the first circle. And in one narration of Al-Muʿjam al-Kabīr the words
و أحصوا سيئاتكم
occur. Putting these two narrations together, it becomes clear that Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) was not against a circle of remembrance in the mosque, but against counting and reckoning; otherwise why did he join the two circles into one?

The texts of these narrations and the discussion of them may be seen in our book Dhikr-e Ijtimāʿī wa Jahrī, pp. 176 to 182.

Note: We had written in Dhikr-e Ijtimāʿī wa Jahrī that if the meaning of Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd's position is taken to be that a particular number was being treated as a legal ruling and dealt with as though it were a sunnah, then his rejection of it will not be a position peculiar to him. It should also be borne in mind that the followers of the Sufi orders do not regard the numbers of the formulae prescribed by their shaykhs as legal rulings; rather they prescribe different numbers to different disciples, and they tell some of them to keep remembering Allah abundantly, as much as they can, and that abundance has no limit. واللہ تعالی اعلم بالصواب ۔

Objection: Some people quote a hadith to forbid remembrance aloud:
عن قيس بن عباد كان أصحاب رسول الله ﷺ يكرهون رفع الصوت عند الجنائز وعند القتال وعند الذكر. (رواه البيهقي في السنن الكبري ٤/۱۲٤/۸۱۸۲، دار الكتب العلمية).
ورواه الإمام الوكيع في الزهد (رقم: ۲۱۱، دار ابن حزم)
Shaykh Shuʿayb al-Arnāʾūṭ has written in his documentation of Sunan Abū Dāwūd:

وروي ابن أبي شيبة (۳/۲۸٤)، وابن المنذر في الأوسط (۵/۳۸۹)، والبيهقي (٤/۸٤)، من طريق الحسن البصري عن قيس بن عباد قال: كان أصحاب رسول الله ﷺ يكرهون رفع الصوت عند ثلاث عند القتال وعند الجنائز وعند الذكر. وإسناده صحيح، وهو عند عبد الرزاق (٦۲۸۱) ومن طريق ابن المنذر (۵/۳۸۹) عن الحسن قال: أدركت اصحاب رسول الله ﷺ، دون ذكر قيس. (سنن أبي داود: ۵/۸۲، دار الرسالة العالمية).

Answer: The meaning of the hadith is that Rasūlullāh ﷺ forbade raising the voice at a funeral, in battle and at the time of remembrance. The sense of the hadith may be that when a funeral is being carried, one should not make a clamour but keep silent; and when fighting is going on, one should not make a clamour then either, because shouting at the time of battle is sometimes a sign of weakness and cowardice. Mawlānā Khalīl Aḥmad Sahāranpūrī (may Allah have mercy on him) quoted from ʿAllāmah Shawkānī:
ولعل وجه كراهتهم لذلك أن التصويت في ذلك الوقت ربما كان مشعراً بالفزع والفشل بخلاف الصمت فإنه دليل الثبات ورباط الجيش. (بذل المجهود: ۹/۲٦۷، ط: دار البشائر الإسلامية).

Just as in a debate it is the weaker party that raises a clamour, so too, when people are engaged in the remembrance of Allah, one should not make a clamour at that time or disturb their remembrance. Otherwise, you have already seen the proofs for remembrance aloud.

It should be borne in mind that raising the voice in remembrance during jihad is reported in the hadiths. In the battle of Khaybar the words "الله أكبر خربت خيبر وإنا إذا نزلنا بساحة قوم فساء صباح المنذرين" occur; and at the digging of the trench "والله لولا الله ما اهتدينا" is transmitted. Yes — the remembrance should not be at the top of one's voice, tearing the throat; and where concealment is intended, one should not raise the voice there either.

A detailed enquiry in the form of an objection was sent from South Africa to Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muftī Ismāʿīl Ṣāḥib Bhaḍkodrawī (may Allah have mercy on him) concerning collective and audible remembrance in the mosques. Setting the objector's difficulties aside, Ḥaḍrat Muftī Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) gave, in a positive style, an eye-opening answer establishing remembrance both audible and silent, collective and individual, in the light of the Qur'an and the hadiths. Regarding the question as unnecessary and to avoid lengthiness, only the answer is set down here. It is as follows:

Answer: حامداً و مصلیاً: ۔ The remembrance of Allah is such an important act of worship that the Qur'an and the hadith command it emphatically and in abundance, and great merit has been mentioned for it.
كما قال تعالى: ﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا اذكروا الله ذكراً كثيراً﴾ [سورة الأحزاب: ٤۱].

After this it should be borne in mind that there are four forms of remembrance, whether the remembrance takes place in the mosques or elsewhere. The four forms are as follows:

(1) Audible remembrance in a group; (2) silent remembrance in a group; (3) audible remembrance in private; (4) silent remembrance in private.

Proof for each one is present in the Qur'an and the hadith. First consider the Qur'anic verses:

﴿واصبر نفسك مع الذين يدعون ربهم بالغداة والعشي﴾ [سورة الكهف: ۲۸].
﴿في بيوت أذن الله أن ترفع ويذكر فيها اسمه يسبح له فيها بالغدو والآصال﴾ [سورة النور: ۳٦].
﴿فإذا أفضتم من عرفات فاذكروا الله عند المشعر الحرام﴾ [سورة البقرة: ۱۹۸].
﴿الذين يذكرون الله قياماً وقعوداً وعلي جنوبهم﴾ [سورة آل عمران: ۱۹۱].
﴿ادعوا ربكم تضرعاً وخفية﴾ [سورة الأعراف: ۵۵].
﴿واذكر ربك في نفسك تضرعاً وخيفة ودون الجهر من القول﴾ [سورة الأعراف: ۲۰۵].
﴿إذ نادي ربه ندائً خفياً﴾ [سورة مريم: ۳].

Secondly, consider the blessed hadiths:

(۱) إن لله تعالى ملائكة يطوفون في الطرق يلتمسون أهل الذكر فإذا وجدوا قوماً يذكرون الله عزوجل تنادوا هلموا إلى حاجتكم.
(بخاری شریف: ۲/۹٤۸، و مسلم شریف: ۲/۳٤٤) ۔

(2) خرج معاوية رضي الله تعالى عنه على حلقة في المسجد فقال: ما أجلسكم قالوا: جلسنا نذكر الله.
(مسلم شریف: ۲/۳٤۲) ۔

(3) عن أبي هريرة رضي الله تعالى عنه وأبي سعيد رضي الله تعالى عنه أنهما شهدا على رسول الله ﷺ أنه قال: لا يقعد قوم يذكرون الله إلا حفتهم الملائكة وغشيتهم الرحمة وذكرهم الله فيمن عنده.
(مسلم شریف: ۲/۳٤۵) ۔

(4) عن أبي هريرة رضي الله تعالى عنه قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ يقول الله عزوجل: أنا عند ظن عبدي بي وأناْ معه حين يذكرني فإن ذكرني في نفسهٖ ذكرته في نفسي وإن ذكرني في ملإ ذكرته في ملإ خير منهم.
(مسلم شریف: ۲/۳٤۳) ۔

(5) عن أنس رضي الله تعالى عنه عن رسول الله ﷺ قال: ما من قوم اجتمعوا يذكرون الله لا يريدون بذلك إلا وجهه إلا ناداهم مناد من السماء أن قوموا مغفوراً لكم قد بدلت سيئاتكم حسنات. طبراني.
(فضائل اعمال، ص ۳۲۷) ۔

(6) عن أبي هريرة رضي الله تعالى عنه قال: سمعت رسول الله ﷺ يقول: سبعة يظلهم الله في ظله يوم لا ظل إلا ظله وذكر منها رجل ذكر الله خالياً ففاضت عيناه.
(مسلم شریف، فضائل اعمال، ص ۳٤۱) ۔

After considering these Qur'anic verses and prophetic hadiths, bear in mind that the Qur'an and the sunnah establish certain conditions and restrictions for remembrance, which the jurists (may Allah have mercy on them) have mentioned in their books, and which it is necessary to observe. They are as follows:

(1) His remembrance should not disturb anyone who is sleeping or anyone who is praying and the like.

(2) It should not be adhered to in the way that the obligatory duties are adhered to.

(3) No one who does not sit in the gathering should be reproached. (کفایت المفتی: ۲/۵٤، معارف القرآن: ٤/۱٦۸، فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱/۱۸۵)

(4) This remembrance should be in accordance with the sunnah. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ٦/۲۱۹) ۔

(5) The pleasure of Allah should be the aim; it should not be for show or reputation. (معارف القرآن: ٤/۱٦۸) ۔

The conditions and restrictions whose absence is mentioned in the case asked about — for example, paying no regard at all to the others present in the mosque, looking down upon those who do not join in the collective remembrance, and adhering to it as a fixed practice — must be observed; and provided these conditions are observed and the ugliness of abandoning them is avoided, collective remembrance aloud in the mosques or elsewhere is permissible and correct.
