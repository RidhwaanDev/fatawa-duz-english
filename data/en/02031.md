### TITLE
Selling dried dates in exchange for fresh dates

### QUESTION
If a man sells one ṣāʿ of tamr — dried dates — in exchange for ruṭab — fresh dates — Imām Abū Ḥanīfah holds it permissible, while the other imams and the two companions hold it impermissible. The position of the two companions rests on a hadith that most hadith scholars and jurists have recorded. The wording of the hadith is:

"عن سعد بن أبي وقاص رضي الله عنه أنه قال: سمعت رسول الله ﷺ يسأل عن شري التمر بالرطب، فقال: أينقص الرطب إذا يبس، قالوا: نعم، فنهاه عن ذلك رواه أصحاب السنن الأربعة، وقال الترمذي حسن صحيح. (شرح النقاية للملا علي القاري رحمه الله: ۳/ ۲٦٤، أبواب الربا).

What answer does the Imam give to this hadith? Kindly provide a satisfying reply.

### ANSWER
(1) Imām Abū Ḥanīfah says that, since in common usage fresh dates are dates, the two may be sold in equal quantities. When Imām Abū Ḥanīfah went to Baghdad and people put this question to him, he said that Abū ʿAyyāsh in the chain of the hadith quoted above is weak, and he added that if fresh dates are dates, then, both being dates, they may be sold in equal quantities; and if they are not dates, it ought to be permissible because the two are then of different kinds.

Mullā ʿAlī, however, raised an objection to this: sometimes the kind is one and yet a sale in equal quantities is still not permissible, because the measure is not equal — as when roasted wheat may not be sold in exchange for unroasted wheat, since the one settles down in the measure and the other does not. That is, even if there were no hadith, selling fresh dates for dried dates by volume ought to be impermissible, just as roasted wheat may not be sold by volume in exchange for unroasted wheat even in equal quantities.

(2) Imām Abū Ḥanīfah criticised the hadith quoted above, holding that it contains Zayd ibn ʿAyyāsh, also called Abū ʿAyyāsh, who is unknown: he is said to be al-Zuraqī, or the client of Banū Zuhrah, or the client of Banū Makhzūm. Ibn Ḥazm therefore called him
"رجل مجهول"
and al-Ḥākim said:
إن الشيخين لم يخرجا هذا الحديث لما خشيا من جهالة زيد بن عياش"
For this reason Ibn Ḥazm, Ṭaḥāwī, Ṭabarī and ʿAbd al-Ḥaqq al-Ishbīlī judged the narration to be defective.

Ibn ʿAyyāsh has two students: (1) ʿImrān ibn Anas and (2) ʿAbd Allāh ibn Yazīd. Having two students removes the unknownness of his identity, but the unknownness of his standing still remains. Furthermore, ʿImrān ibn Anas differed from ʿAbd Allāh ibn Yazīd on two points:

The first is that he calls him the client of Banū Makhzūm and not the client of Banū Zuhrah.

The second is that ʿImrān ibn Anas added the qualification "on deferred terms". The narration is therefore not to be relied upon. But an objection arises against this: Imām Mālik took a narration from Zayd ibn ʿAyyāsh, and he generally does not take narrations from weak transmitters; Imām Tirmidhī graded the hadith authentic; and Ibn al-Jawzī says:

روي عنه عبد الله بن يزيد وعمران بن أبي أنس فكيف يكون مجهولاً مع تصحيح الترمذي لحديثه قال: فقد عرفه أئمة النقل، قلت: وقد صححه ابن حبان أيضاً وابن خزيمة والدارقطني وذلك يقتضي أنهم عرفوا حاله. (الدراية على الهداية: ۳/ ۸۳، باب الربا).

The sum of the objection is that, since a great many scholars have declared Zayd ibn ʿAyyāsh acceptable, the weakness of the hadith is not severe; and the Hanafis do draw rulings even from weak narrations, as in the hadith of loud laughter and of wiping the neck. Moreover, equality by volume is in any case hard to achieve between these two, because fresh dates leave gaps in the measure while dried dates pack down. So according to Hanafi principles this ought to be impermissible.

(3) Another answer given on behalf of the Hanafis is that, since the word "deferred" occurs in ʿImrān ibn Anas's narration, the qualification "on deferred terms" is an addition by the transmitter; so it should be declared impermissible on deferred terms and permissible for immediate payment.

An objection may be raised against this too: if both narrations are accepted, then when the kind is one the sale is impermissible on deferred terms, and when equality cannot be achieved it ought to be impermissible for immediate payment as well.

This servant found a satisfying answer in support of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah's position in the words of ʿAllāmah ʿAynī, which are as follows.

(4) ʿAllāmah ʿAynī wrote in his commentary on al-Kanz:

وصح أيضاً بيع الرطب بالرطب متساوياً أو بيع الرطب بالتمر حال كونه متماثلاً في الوزن عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله وقالا: لا يجوز لأنه عليه الصلاة والسلام نهى عن بيع الرطب بالتمر وبه قالت الثلاثة. (رمز الحقائق في شرح كنز الدقائق: ۲/ ۶۰، باب الربا، إدارة القرآن).

That is, if fresh dates are sold for dried dates in equal quantities by weight, this is permissible, because equality by weight is entirely achievable. So if the two companions' ruling of impermissibility is taken to refer to measurement by volume, and Imām Abū Ḥanīfah's ruling to measurement by weight, the difficulty disappears.

Other jurists have also quoted ʿAllāmah ʿAynī:

قال ابن نجيم رحمه الله: وصح أيضاً بيع الرطب بالتمر حال كونه متماثلاً كيلاً كذا في غير كتاب، وقال العيني رحمه الله: وزناً. (النهر الفائق: ۳/ ٤۷٦).
وقال الفقيه أبو السعود المصري: قوله كيلاً، كذا في غير كتاب خلافاً للعيني رحمه الله حيث اعتبر بالوزن. (فتح المعين شرح ملا مسكين: ۲/ ٦۰۳).

It is worth mentioning that the hadiths contain an indication that fresh dates are among the goods sold by weight.

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī states:

عن أبي البختري سألت ابن عباس رضي الله عنه فقال: نهي النبي ﷺ عن بيع النخل حتى يوكل منه أو يأكل منه وحتى يوزن. (رواه البخاري: ۱/ ۲۹۹، كتاب السلم).

Before this comes the following passage:

عن أبي البختري الطائي قال: سألت ابن عباس عن السلم في النخل فقال: نهي النبي ﷺ عن بيع النخل حتى يوكل منه وحتى يوزن فقال الرجل: وأي شيء يوزن فقال رجل إلى جانبه: حتى يحرز. (رواه البخاري: ۱/ ۲۹۹، كتاب السلم).
