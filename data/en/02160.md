### TITLE
Customary possession is enough in a gift

### QUESTION
My father gifted me a building and it was put in my name in the papers. There are two houses and four shops in this building, and tenants are living in them. During my father's lifetime I collect their rent, and the matter of taxes and so on is also my responsibility; that is, I am the one who deals with the property. In the situation described, is my possession enough for the gift to be complete, or is outward possession necessary by evicting the tenants? It is obvious that under the tenancy agreement there is no way of evicting the tenants.

### ANSWER
Possession of anything is according to what suits that thing: possession of a car is that the key be handed over to the buyer. In this case possession will be effected by handing over the papers to the person to whom the gift is made, and after the father's death the other heirs cannot claim any right of inheritance in this building.

See what Radd al-Muḥtār says:

وحاصله أن التخلية قبض حكماً لو مع القدرة عليه بلا كلفة لكن ذلك يختلف بحسب حال المبيع،

قال أجمعوا على أن التخلية في البيع الجائز تكون قبضاً

الخ. (رد المحتار: ٤/۵٦۲، ۵٦۳، مطلب في شروط التخلية، سعيد. وكذا في الفتاوى البزازية على هامش الهندية: ٤/٤۹۹. والفتاوى الهندية: ۳/۱۹).

Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states:

The Qur'an and the Sunnah have not laid down any one particular definition of taking possession; rather the hadiths mention various forms of it. In one report of ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) the instruction is given to move the goods from the place of purchase to another place. It is reported from Zayd ibn Thābit (may Allah be pleased with him) that traders should move goods they have bought into their own saddle-packs. In the report of Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) measuring and weighing is treated as taking possession. This is why the jurists agree that in this matter people's custom is the standard: whatever degree of handling is regarded as possession in people's custom for a particular thing will also be regarded as possession for it in the sharia.

ʿAllāmah al-Kāsānī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

ولا يشترط القبض بالبراجم، لأن معني القبض هو التمكين والتخلي وارتفاع الموانع عرفاً وعادة حقيقة. (بدائع الصنائع: ۵/۱٤۸، سعيد. جديد فقهي مباحث: ۱۵/۱۲).

Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil states:

Imam Abū Ḥanīfah's (may Allah have mercy on him) position is that physical possession is not necessary; clearing the way (takhliyah) is enough. Takhliyah means that the buyer is given the ability to come and take possession of the goods whenever he wishes; when no obstacle to taking possession remains, we hold that takhliyah has taken place. For instance, there is a box with a number of things kept inside it, and its key has been handed over to him; once the key has been handed over, then whether he picks the box up or not, possession has been realised. Imam al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) adopted Imam Abū Ḥanīfah's position here and narrated with a connected chain the incident of Jābir (may Allah be pleased with him): the Prophet ﷺ bought a camel from Jābir (may Allah be pleased with him), and then Jābir (may Allah be pleased with him) travelled on that very camel as far as Madīnah Munawwarah. Jābir (may Allah be pleased with him) did not dismount from it, yet takhliyah had been realised. Imam al-Bukhārī (may Allah have mercy on him) says that this shows that possession took place through takhliyah. (Islām awr Maʿāshī Masāʾil, 2/98, 100).

ʿIṭr-e-Hidāyah states:

Possession means gaining the ability to deal with a thing by another's permission. Now if this ability comes from the owner's permission or from a right established in the sharia, it is called lawful possession. (ʿIṭr-e-Hidāyah, p. 88).

For further detail see: Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām, 1/40, 42; and Qāmūs al-Fiqh, 4/466, 467).

In short, the reality of possession rests upon custom, and it will also differ according to the nature of the thing. So in the case in question, collecting the rent of the building and paying its taxes and the like — that is, having full authority to deal with it — amounts to possession.
