### TITLE
Giving interest money to one's grandson

### QUESTION
A man has some interest money, and his grandson is poor. May he give this interest money to his grandson or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question it is permissible and correct to give the interest money to one's poor grandson. This is because interest money, since its owner is not known, must be given away in charity: that is, it is a charity given on behalf of its owner and not on behalf of the person handing it over, and that owner is an unrelated party with respect to the giver's grandson.

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband states:

Question: If unlawful wealth or corrupt profits accumulate in the hands of a Muslim, and it is impossible to convey their dues to the owners of that wealth and return it to them, then, in order to free himself of liability for this tainted wealth, the jurists (may Allah have mercy on them) write that he should give it away in charity. The question here is whether its recipients will be only the poor and needy, as with the ordinary obligatory charities, or whether he may give such wealth in charity to his mother, father, children and wife as well, or whether, as with zakat, it must be given in charity to someone unrelated.

Answer: The ruling that unlawful wealth and tainted profit are to be given away in charity rests on a particular principle, namely that when the owners of wealth are unknown, or conveying it to them is impossible, that wealth takes the ruling of lost property; and the ruling for lost property is that once there is no hope of finding the owner it is given away in charity on the owner's behalf. So when the owners of unlawful wealth are unknown, or conveying it to them is impossible, giving that wealth in charity will likewise, as with lost property, be on behalf of the owners of the wealth. It is for this reason that calling it a charity is sound, and for this reason too that taking it is lawful for the poor. Otherwise, just as consuming unlawful wealth was forbidden to him, it would be forbidden to the poor as well; for this wealth has come to the poor on behalf of its owner, and not on behalf of the one who earned it unlawfully. The following texts bear witness to this:

وفي العشرين من بيوع الهندية

وإنما طاب للمساكين على قياس اللقطة.

In short, corrupt profits and unlawful wealth that must be given away in charity are to be spent, like lost property, on the poor and needy. It has become clear that the unlawful wealth which is declared to require being given away in charity is not every kind of unlawful wealth, but only that wealth which cannot be returned to its owner because the owner is unknown or has disappeared. Further, in such a case this wealth takes the ruling of lost property and is given in charity on behalf of its real owner, which is why it is permissible for the poor to take it and why this wealth is not unlawful for them. On this basis a person may also give such wealth in charity to his own mother, father, children and wife, since the charity is not his own but the real owner's,

كما في الهندية: ان تصدق به على أبيه يكفيه ولا يشترط التصدق على الأجنبي (۵/۳٤۹). وفيه من متفرقات الغصب سئل يوسف بن محمد عن غاصب ندم على ما فعل وأراد أن يرد المال إلى صاحبه وقع له اليأس عن وجود صاحبه فتصدق بهذا العين هل يجوز للفقير أن ينتفع بهذا العين فقال: لا يجوز أن يقبله ولا يجوز له الانتفاع وإنما يجب عليه رده إلى من دفعه إليه قال: إنما أجاب بهذا الجواب زجراً لهم كيلا يتساهلوا في أموال الناس أما لو سلك الطريق في معرفة المالك فلم يجده فحكمه حكم اللقطة، كذا في التاتارخانية. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۵/۱۵۷). مأخوذ ا ز فتاوى دار العلوم ديوبند، جلد دوم، ص ۳۸٦. ۳۸۳).
