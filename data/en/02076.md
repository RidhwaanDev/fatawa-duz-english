### TITLE
Accepting a prize given by a third party

### QUESTION
A function is being held at a school, and there is an entrance fee of 50 rand for a seat in the hall — in effect a contribution to the school. One or several prizes, a mobile phone for example, are given out by a lucky draw, not by the owner of the school but by a third party. If a person who has paid the fee wins such a prize, is it permissible for him to take it or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, if a person who has paid the fee wins this prize, taking it is permissible and correct, for two reasons.

(1) The prize is not given in exchange for the 50 rand fee, because the 50 rand is an entrance fee — in other words, it is a contribution to the school — and is not the price of the prize. For this reason it is permissible and correct.

(2) The second reason for its permissibility is that this prize comes from a third party and not from the owner of the school, and taking a prize from a third party is permissible and correct.

See what Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

وكذلك (أي لا بأس به) ما يفعله السلاطين وهو أن يقول السلطان لرجلين: من سبق منكما فله كذا فهو جائز لما بينا أن ذلك من باب التحريض على استعداد أسباب الجهاد خصوصاً من السلطان. (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/۲۰٦، كتاب السباق، سعيد. وفتاوى الشامي: ۵/۳۵٤، سعيد).

In this passage the ruler occupies the position of a third party. So just as it is permissible for the ruler to give a prize, in the case in question it will likewise be permissible for a third party to give a prize.

Jadīd Muʿāmalāt ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

One permissible form of a horse race with a stipulated payment is that the two parties racing their horses against each other have nothing to give to or take from one another, but rather a sum of money is fixed as a prize for the one who comes first by the government of the day, or by some third person or body. This is permissible and correct. (جدید معاملات کے شرعی احکام: جلد دوم، ۷۱) ۔
