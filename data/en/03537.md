### TITLE
The ruling on the fast being broken by angiography while fasting

### QUESTION
A man had an angiography carried out during the day in the blessed month of Ramaḍān while he was fasting. Has his fast been broken or not? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
Angiography while fasting: in this procedure a tube is passed into a person's veins and the vessels of the heart, and the blood vessels and the internal organs are examined and the result is preserved in the manner of an X-ray. The word is made up of the Greek words Angein, meaning a vessel, and Graphein, meaning to write or to put on record; these preserved images of the blood vessels are called an Angiongraph. It should be borne in mind that what is aimed at by this procedure is an X-ray. Its method is that a cap-like instrument is inserted into the veins, and in this instrument there is a kind of fluid substance; because of this fluid substance the X-ray comes out perfectly clear.

Since the tube which is inserted in this procedure contains a fluid substance, the fast will be broken by having this procedure carried out, and making up the fast will be binding; the expiation will not be obligatory.

Consider the proofs and the supporting evidence. Al-Baḥr al-Rāʾiq states:

وكذا لو أدخل أصبعه في إسته أو أدخلت المرأة في فرجها هو المختار إلا إذا كانت الأصبع مبتلة بالماء أو الدهن فحينئذ يفسد لوصول الماء أو الدهن. (۲/۳۰۰، ط: دار المعرفة).

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

(أو أدخل أصبعه اليابسة فيه) أي في دبره أو فرجها (لم يفسد) ولو مبتلة فسد. (فتاوى الشامي: ۷/٤۰۳، باب ما يفسد الصوم وما لا يفسده).

Muḥīṭ Burhānī states:

وأكثر المشايخ اعتبروا الوصول إلى الجوف في الجائفة والآمة إن عرف أن اليابس وصل إلى الجوف يفسد صومه بالإتفاق، وإن عرف أن الرطب لا يصل إلى الجوف لا يفسد صومه. (المحيط البرهاني: ۲/٦٤۱، ط: دار إحياء التراث العربي).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states: The general rule is that if an arrow, a spear, a piece of wood, a piece of meat tied with a thread, a cloth, cotton wool or any other such thing enters one of the openings of the fasting person's body and disappears completely inside, with no end of it left sticking out, then his fast is broken — that is, when the fasting person inserted it by an act of his own, or when there is a benefit in it for his body. If one end of it, or some part of it, is left sticking out, his fast will not be broken. And this general rule applies when the thing which has been inserted is dry; but if he wetted that thing with water or oil or the like, then, even though one end of it may remain outside, his fast will be broken on account of the water or oil reaching inside, provided that he remembered his fast at the time of inserting it. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 3/309, Zawwār Academy edition).

Muftī Salmān Manṣūrpūrī writes: If, in order to test the stomach or the like, a tube with a viewing device was passed by way of the throat or the nose, and there was no medicine or lubricant on it, and one end of it was outside, then the fast will not be broken merely by the insertion of this tube. But if some other substance is present along with the tube, then the fast will be broken on account of its entering inside. (Kitāb al-Masāʾil: 2/155, Nuʿaymiyyah, Deoband; and likewise in al-Masāʾil al-Muhimmah fīmā Ibtulat bihi al-ʿĀmmah: 1/107, Akkalkuwa edition).
