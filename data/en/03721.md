### TITLE
The ruling on buying and selling with Bitcoin currency

### QUESTION
Nowadays a currency is at its height in the market which is called Bitcoin currency. I wish to know its reality, and also what the ruling is on trading with it. Explain and be rewarded.

### ANSWER
A few observations concerning Bitcoin currency are presented:

1. Bitcoin is a digital currency for the payment of money, that is to say, an invisible coin.

2. Bitcoin is like cash for buying and selling on the internet.

3. A particular feature of the Bitcoin network is that no country or institution owns it or controls it; rather, the users control it. For this reason, if it is lost, no country and no institution can provide a replacement for it.

4. Bitcoin is stored in an electronic device. It can be lost only if that electronic device breaks down with no hope of its being repaired, or is mislaid, or the code is forgotten.

5. Bitcoin currency came into existence in the form of numbers, as a digital currency.

6. The determination of the value of this currency was left to the free play of supply and demand, on account of which its value at the outset was far below one dollar; but afterwards, passing through rises and falls, its value has now reached five thousand dollars.

7. In some Western countries it has been partially recognised for the collection of taxes, for private accounts and for the supervision of this currency, while in certain other countries a ban has been imposed upon it.

8. Because there is no regular supervision by any country, and because of the fluctuation in this currency, institutions that conclude transactions internationally under this currency have been warned a number of times.

The ruling, from the point of view of fiqh, on the permissibility or impermissibility of dealing in Bitcoin, together with its evidences:

If the government does not recognise Bitcoin and there is no established practice with it, then it is not a price (thaman), and there will be no permission to deal with it. But if the government has recognised it, and it has been put into practice, and custom and established usage are found, then in the view of some muftīs it falls within the category of a customary price, while some muftīs have written treatises arguing for its impermissibility; in their view this currency is not a price, and one ought therefore to keep away from dealing with it.

In the Arab lands many muftīs have issued a fatwa of permissibility for the transactions carried out with this currency, while certain others have given the fatwa that one should remain cautious until the government formally recognises it.

What is required for something to become a customary price? Consider the following. Qāmūs al-Fiqh states:

In terms of our own age, there can be two ways in which something becomes a price in the technical sense and agreement comes about upon its being a price. One is that it comes into circulation among the general public of itself, as is mentioned in the old books of fiqh — in our time this way is not possible. The second way is that the government declares something to be a price and the public is thereby obliged to accept it; and it is this form that is current throughout the whole world today. It is clear from the details given by the jurists that the coming into being of price-status rests in reality upon custom and usage: what reason is there, other than usage and established practice, for the fulūs in circulation, and such dirhams and dinars in which base metal predominates, being accepted as prices? (Qāmūs al-Fiqh: 3/59).

Therefore, in a country where the government has legally recognised it, it has there the status of a price in the technical sense.

The jurists have given various definitions of wealth (māl). Consider the following. ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) states:

وقدمنا أول البيوع تعريف المال بما يميل إليه الطبع ويمكن ادخاره لوقت الحاجة، وأنه خرج بالادخار، المنفعة فهي ملك لا مال، لأن الملك ما من شأنه أن يتصرف فيه بوقف الاختصاص كما في التلويح، فالأولي ما في الدرر من قوله المال موجود يميل إليه الطبع

(فتاوى الشامي: ۵/۵۱، سعيد).

قال في البحر الرائق: المال ما يميل إليه الطبع ويمكن ادخاره لوقت الحاجة والمالية إنما ثبت بتمول الناس كافة أو بتقوم البعض والتقوم يثبت بها وبإباحة الانتفاع له شرعاً فما يكون مباح الانتفاع بدون تمول الناس لا يكون ما لا كحبة حنطة وما يكون مالاً بين الناس ولا يكون مباح الانتفاع لا يكون متقوماً كالخمر وإذا عدم الأمران لم يثبت واحد منهما كالدم. (البحر الرائق: ۵/۲۵٦، ط: الماجدية).

وفي منحة الخالق: لأن المال ما يميل إليه الطبع ويدخر لوقت الحاجة أو ما خلق لمصالح الآدمي ويجري فيه الشح والضنة. (منحة الخالق حاشية البحر: ۵/۲۵۷، ط: الماجدية).

وقال في بدائع الصنائع: وأما التبر فهل يصلح رأس مال الشركة؟ ذكر في كتاب الشركة وجعله كالعروض وفي كتاب الصرف جعله كالأثمان المطلقة؛ لأنه قال فيه: إذا اشتري به فهلك لا ينفسخ العقد، والأمر فيه موكول إلى تعامل الناس، فإن كانوا يتعاملون به فحكمه حكم الأثمان المطلقة، فتجوز الشركة بها وإن كانوا لا يتعاملون بها فحكمها حكم العروض، ولا تجوز فيها الشركة. (بدائع الصنائع: ٦/۵۹، سعيد).

For further detail see: (Sharḥ al-Majallah of Salīm Rustam Bāz al-Lubnānī: 1/70, Dār al-Kutub al-ʿIlmiyyah; Durar al-Ḥukkām fī Sharḥ Majallat al-Aḥkām of ʿAlī Ḥaydar: 1/101, Beirut; Tabyīn al-Ḥaqāʾiq: 3/317, published by Imdādiyyah; and Majmaʿ al-Anhur: 1/720, Dār Iḥyāʾ al-Turāth al-ʿArabī).

Some institutions of Pakistan — Jāmiʿat al-ʿUlūm al-Islāmiyyah, ʿAllāmah Banūrī Town, and Jāmiʿat al-Rashīd — have declared that adopting this currency as a business investment, in the absence of established practice and a government directive, is impermissible.

In the monthly Al-Ṣadīq, after the evidences of those who hold it permissible and those who hold it impermissible, a judgement has been given between the two. Some summaries of it are presented here.

Analysis of the evidences for permissibility:

In connection with the second evidence it was stated that a sale in which none of the causes of impermissibility is found is permissible, and that if some cause of impermissibility exists then it is impermissible; and since in Bitcoin and other digital currencies none of the causes of impermissibility is found, dealing in it is therefore permissible.

Two things in this argument call for consideration. The first: that none of the causes of impermissibility is found in it — this we do not accept, because one important cause of a sale being impermissible is that the thing is not wealth, and Bitcoin too is not wealth. For wealth is the name of a thing that is desired and can be stored, and this quality is not found in it. The reason for this is that the term "wealth" is applied to physical objects and to usufructs, and Bitcoin is neither among physical objects nor among usufructs. It is therefore not wealth; and the ruling that applies to the buying and selling of what is not wealth will apply to it also.

And if in the view of some people it is a desired and storable thing, even then, in the view of those who do not regard it as a desired and storable thing, it is not wealth. And just as a thing which in the view of some is not wealth and in the view of others is wealth — such as wine and the like — the sale of which is defective, so the same ruling will apply to Bitcoin.

The second: in this argument the entire attention has been focused upon whether there is a defect in the Sharīʿah in dealing in Bitcoin, whereas our subject is the establishing of its being a currency. When its being a currency is established, then we shall discuss the rulings on the permissibility or impermissibility of dealing in it. That is to say, the claim is one thing and the evidence another: the claim concerns whether Bitcoin is or is not a currency, while the evidence is that there is no defect in the Sharīʿah in dealing in Bitcoin.

Analysis of the evidences for impermissibility. Analysis of the first evidence for impermissibility:

The substance of this evidence is that the basis of price-status is gold and silver, or else a receipt for them, and in the third case a government able to guarantee it. In answer to this it can only be said that in a Sharīʿah price the basis is indeed that it be gold and silver, or a receipt for them, or else a government able to guarantee it, as in the case of fulūs and the like; but in a customary price, established practice and the permission of the government appear to be sufficient. For this reason it may be said that we too are not calling Bitcoin a Sharīʿah price; rather we may call it a customary price.

As for the point that in Bitcoin and digital currency the strength of the price is reckoned by the strength of the software — that is, the stronger the software, the greater its value — this same thing is found in ordinary paper currency too, for the currency of a people and a government that are strong is itself strong.

And as for the point that in Bitcoin and other digital currencies there is the danger of hacking and the like, this same danger exists in the usurping of paper notes and in the production of counterfeit copies of them; and since that is permissible, what harm is there in this?

In the light of the above evidences for permissibility and impermissibility and of the analysis made of them, a few things have become clear:

The first point: the substance of the matter is that a currency depends upon its figures and numbers, and figures and numbers are recorded upon this currency too; so it will have the ruling of a currency.

Dealing in this currency by companies and by the public has become current, so much so that it can be preserved by means of particular numbers and accounts and records can be kept of it; and when there is the patronage of the government and acceptance among the public, then it is possible for it now to become a currency.

The substance of the second point: there appears outwardly to be no objection to using Bitcoin as a price; however, since there is no established practice with regard to gold and silver, or a receipt for them, or guaranteeing governments or their recognised institutions, using it as a currency does not appear to be correct. (Summarised from the monthly Al-Ṣadīq, pp. 135–137, the organ of Maʿhad al-Ṣadīq li al-Dirāsāt al-Islāmiyyah, Muḥarram al-Ḥarām to Rabīʿ al-Awwal 1439 AH, October to December 2017 CE).
