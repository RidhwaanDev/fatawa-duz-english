### TITLE
Zakat money for a madrasah stolen from the collector

### QUESTION
Someone gave zakat money to a madrasah's collector, and the money was then lost or stolen from him. Two things need to be established: (1) Has the zakat been discharged or not? (2) Is the collector liable for the loss or not?

### ANSWER
(1) Our senior scholars (may Allah have mercy on them) are agreed that the collector is the agent of the needy students of the madrasah, and an agent's possession is the same as the possession of the one who appointed him. On this basis the zakat has been discharged.

(2) The collector is an agent and a trustee. So if he made proper arrangements to safeguard the money and it was still lost or stolen, he does not have to make it good; but if he was negligent, he must make it good.

Consider what Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil states:

If zakat money is stolen from madrasah collectors, or is stolen or lost from the principal, and there was no shortcoming on their part in safeguarding it, they are not liable to make it good, and the owner's zakat is discharged; for in practice and by custom these men are the agents of the poor students, and an agent's possession is as good as the possession of the poor themselves.

But if these men were negligent in safeguarding the money, or made changes to the zakat money, or mixed it with their own money, then they are liable to make it good, and they must give the poor an equal sum out of their own pockets. (Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil: p. 120, Nuʿaymiyyah).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

If the principal of the madrasah is the students' agent, then his possession is the students' own possession. The zakat is therefore discharged, and no one is liable for the loss. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 9/513, chapter-arranged edition).

Imdād al-Muftīn states:

The principals of a madrasah, and the donation collectors they appoint, come under the ruling of the officials appointed to collect charity, and so they are agents of the poor. They are agents of the donors only to this extent, that the donors accepted them as agents and handed their donations over to them. So when the money passes into their possession in their capacity as agents of the poor, it becomes the property of the poor, and the zakat of those who gave it is discharged. Mawlānā Rashīd Aḥmad Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him) also said, in answer to a question: these students and poor people are unknown both in number and in identity, yet in spite of this the principals of the madrasah are established by custom as their agents, and the principals' possession has become the possession of the poor. (Imdād al-Muftīn, vol. 2: 1085, Ikhtiyār al-Ṣawāb, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

See further: Fatāwā Khalīliyyah, vol. 1: 154, Bāb al-Maṣrif, Maktabat al-Shaykh; and Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil: 1/226, Nuʿaymiyyah.
