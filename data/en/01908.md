### TITLE
The ruling on trading in black hair dye

### QUESTION
A man makes black hair dye and sells it. Is this permissible or not, given that using it is not permissible?

### ANSWER
The principle of the noble sharia concerning whether trade is permissible or not is this: whatever can possibly be put to a permissible use may be bought and sold, while whatever cannot possibly be put to any permissible use and is used only and solely in disobedience may not be traded in and is impermissible. On this basis it is clear that black dye can also be used on permissible occasions. For example, a warrior may use it in jihad to strike awe into the disbelievers. Likewise, in the view of Imām Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) it is permissible for an old man whose wife is young. And if a young man's hair has turned white before its time because of some ailment, using black dye is permissible in order to remove the blemish.

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ẓafar Aḥmad ʿUthmānī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

Making black dye for trade and selling it is permissible.

لأن الحرمة ليست بقائمة بعينه

وإنما الحرمة في الاستعمال إذا استعمله خادعاً ومن شاب قبل أوان المشيب أو خضب لا رهاب العدو في الحرب يجوز له الخضاب بالسواد كما صرح به في الهندية وغيرها.

(Imdād al-Aḥkām: 3/376).

Al-Muḥīṭ al-Burhānī states:

وأما الخضاب بالسواد: فمن فعل ذلك من الغزاة ليكون أهيب في عين العدو فهو محمود منه، اتفق عليه المشايخ، ومن فعل ذلك ليزين نفسه للنساء، وليحبب نفسه إليهن فذلك مكروه عليه عامة المشايخ، وبنحوه ورد الأثر عن عمر رضي الله عنه، وبعضهم جوزوا ذلك من غير كراهية، روي عن أبي يوسف رحمه الله أنه قال: كما يعجبني أن تتزين لي يعجبها أن أتزين لها، هذه الجملة من شرح "السير الكبير". (المحيط البرهاني: ٦/۱۲۲، فصل في الزينة، رشيدية).

وفي فتاوى الشامي: قوله ويكره بالسواد، أي لغير الحرب، قال في الذخيرة: أما الخضاب بالسواد للغزو، ليكون أهيب في عين العدو فهو محمود بالاتفاق

الخ. (فتاوى الشامي: ٦/٤۲۲، كتاب الكراهية، سعيد).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

Preparing black dye and selling it is permissible, since there is also a situation in which its use is permissible, namely to strike awe into the enemy. Making it and selling it is therefore contrary to what is preferable, but it is not permissible to sell it to a person about whom it is certain that he will use it in an impermissible way, as is stated in Radd al-Muḥtār and elsewhere. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 8/375).

Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil states:

The principle regarding sales is that whatever can possibly be put to some permissible use may be sold, even if that thing is generally used for an impermissible purpose; it is then the buyer's business to use it for a permissible purpose. For instance, ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her) had bought a cloth with pictures on it. When the Prophet ﷺ saw the pictures he showed his displeasure, but he did not order the sale to be cancelled — which shows that the sale was not impermissible. Later ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her), at the Prophet's ﷺ suggestion, made a cushion out of that cloth and used it in a cushion. (Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil: 4/17).
