### TITLE
Praying on a patterned prayer mat bearing pictures

### QUESTION
Nowadays prayer mats have pictures of the Kaʿbah and of the Prophet's Mosque upon them: is it correct to pray on such a prayer mat or not? أفيدونا بارك الله فيكم.

### ANSWER
The greatness of the House of Allah and of the Prophet's Mosque, and reverence for them, are present in the heart of every Muslim; and the design which is on a prayer mat does not carry the ruling of the original. Praying upon it is therefore permissible and correct without any dislikedness. Moreover, this is a picture of something without a soul, which does not fall under the forbidden kind of picture. Consider what Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī Sharīf states:

عن أبي طلحة صاحب رسول الله ﷺ قال: إن رسول الله ﷺ قال: إن الملائكة لا تدخل بيتاً فيه الصورة قال بسر: ثم اشتكى زيد فعدناه فإذا على بابه ستر فيه صورة فقلت لعبيد الله ربيب ميمونة زوج النبي ﷺ ألم يخبرنا زيد عن الصور يوم الأول فقال عبيد الله ألم تسمعه حين قال: إلا رقماً في ثوب. (صحيح البخاري، رقم: ۵۹۵۸، باب من كره القعود على الصورة).

قال العيني في شرح أبي داود: وأما تصوير صورة الشجر ونبات الأرض وغير ذلك مما ليس فيه صورة حيوان، فليس بحرام. (شرح أبي داود: ۱/۵۰۵، ط: مكتبة الرشد).

وفي الفتاوى الهندية: ولا يكره تمثال غير ذي الروح كذا في النهاية. (۱/۱۰۷).

Sharḥ Munyat al-Muṣallī states:

وأما صورة غير ذي روح فلا خلاف في عدم كراهية الصلاة عليها أو إليها.

(۳۵۹، ط: سهيل اكيڈمي).

(وكذا في فتاوى الشامي: ۱/٦٤۹، سعيد، وحاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح، ص ۳٦۲، قديمي).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah records:

There is no harm in the Sacred Law in praying on these prayer mats; there is no need to place a cloth over them, nor any need to sell them. Nor does this picture make any difference to the reverence due to the Kaʿbah, because a picture does not carry the ruling of the thing itself. Secondly, when prayer is performed inside the Kaʿbah, there too the ground is beneath one's feet; and when that is not contrary to reverence, then for a picture to be beneath the feet will all the more not be contrary to reverence. (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۱۱/۹۲، محمودیہ) ۔

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

Since the design of the Kaʿbah and the like on a prayer mat is not the original but only an artificial representation resembling it, reverence for it is not necessary; and Muslims have a sense of its greatness in their hearts, and there is no thought of dishonouring it. Therefore, if a foot should fall upon it unknowingly and by chance, there will be no sin. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ۵/٤۸۲، مکتبۃ الاحسان، دیوبند) ۔

Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil states:

There is no objection in the Sacred Law to placing one's feet on these prayer mats and standing on them to pray, because a picture does not carry the ruling of the original thing but is of a lower rank than the original thing. Yes, it is better for the prayer mat to be plain — but both are permissible. (ایضاح المسائل، ص ۱۳۳) ۔

An objection and its answer:

Objection: Some muftīs have declared this to be a dishonouring of the symbols of Allah; what is the answer to this? Consider what Niẓām al-Fatāwā records: If the House of Allah and the Prophet's Mosque are seen upon it clearly and distinctly, then both placing one's feet upon it and praying upon it are impermissible, because the fact that the House of Allah and the Prophet's Mosque are among the symbols is accepted and manifest, and reverence for the symbols is obligatory, کما قال اللہ تعالی:

﴿ومن يعظم شعائر الله فإنها من تقوى القلوب﴾

And to trample a venerated thing underfoot entails dishonouring it; and although these two designs are not the Kaʿbah itself, they are certainly a reflection of it, and therefore dishonouring them will also entail dishonouring those two. To prostrate upon these designs will be impermissible and unlawful; and if this thing is not polytheism, it is certainly a semblance of polytheism, and a semblance of polytheism too will be forbidden. (نظام الفتاوی: ۱/٤۸ بتغیر یسیر) ۔

Answer: The ruling of the original and that of the derivative are distinct; the ruling of the original is not identically the ruling of the derivative, but rather there is a difference between the two. Numerous parallels to this are found in the books of fiqh and legal theory:

قال في الهداية: وإن أبرأ الكفيل لم يبرأ الأصيل عنه، لأنه تبع، ولأن عليه المطالبة وبقاء الدين على الأصيل بدونه جائز، وكذا إذا أخر الطالب عن الأصيل فهو تأخير عن الكفيل، ولو أخر عن الكفيل لم يكن تأخيراً عن الذي عليه الأصل. (الهداية: ۳/۱۱۸).

وينظر: (السراجي في الميراث، ص ۳۳، ط: بشرى).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

Nor does this picture make any difference to the reverence due to the Kaʿbah, because the ruling of a picture is not that of the thing itself. (۱۱/۹۲، محمودیہ) ۔

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah records: Since the design of the Kaʿbah and the like on a prayer mat is not the original but only an artificial representation resembling it, reverence for it is not necessary. (فتاوی رحیمیہ: ۵/٤۸۲، مکتبۃ الاحسان، دیوبند) ۔

Īḍāḥ al-Masāʾil states: A picture does not carry the ruling of the original thing, but is of a lower rank than the original thing. (۱۳۳) ۔

The upshot is that there is a difference between the original and its reflection; the rank which the House of Allah and the Prophet's Mosque possess is not the rank and ruling of a picture, and on this basis reverence for the picture is not binding either. Moreover, the picture of the Kaʿbah or of the Prophet's Mosque is at the place of prostration; it is not at the place of the feet.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
