### TITLE
The ruling on pointing out mistakes of tajwīd in the tarāwīḥ

### QUESTION
Is it correct to point out the ḥāfiẓ's mistakes of tajwīd during the tarāwīḥ prayer or not?

### ANSWER
Prompting in the prayer is permissible at a time of need; it should not be done without need, nor should one be hasty in it. Mistakes of recitation are of two kinds: (1) manifest error (laḥn jalī); (2) hidden error (laḥn khafī).

A manifest error is a mistake by which the meaning and sense of the Qurʾān is altered. At such a time one should prompt; indeed, it is sometimes necessary, when the invalidity of the prayer would otherwise follow.

However, a mistake by which the meaning is not altered, though the word is wrong — as in some regions where people pronounce kāf as chīm, saying "Allāhu achbar" for "Allāhu akbar"; or as the people of Cape Town turn ḥāʾ into khāʾ, saying, for instance, "al-khamd" for "al-ḥamd" — does not invalidate the prayer, and so there is no need to prompt. Consider what is stated in Ashraf al-Aḥkām:

If, through reciting wrongly again and again, it becomes a habit, then that becomes his own manner of pronunciation. It is the statement of Imām al-Faḍlī (may Allah have mercy on him) that when a person has, through repeatedly reciting a word wrongly, made it his habit, it becomes his dialect; therefore the prayer of one who recites the Qurʾān correctly behind such a person will be valid. Thus, on one occasion in Makkah the Venerated, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Qāsim Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) and several scholars prayed behind a Turkish imām. The Turks pronounce chīm in place of kāf, and this imām too recited "iyyācha naʿbudu" in place of "iyyāka naʿbudu". All the people repeated their prayer, but Mawlānā Qāsim Ṣāḥib did not repeat it, and gave this very ruling. (Ashraf al-Aḥkām, supplement to Imdād al-Fatāwā, p. 59, quoting Anfās-i ʿĪsā: 2/434, Islāmiyyāt).

Consider what is transmitted in Fatāwā Shāmī:

وإن كان الخطأ بإبدال حرف بحرف فإن أمكن الفصل بينهما بلا كلفة كالصاد مع الطاء بأن قرأ الطالحات مكان الصالحات فاتفقوا على أنه مفسد وإن لم يكن إلا بمشقة كالظاء مع الضاد والصاد مع السين فأكثرهم على عدم الفساد لعموم البلوى. (فتاوى الشامي: ۱/٦۳۱، سعيد).

(2) The hidden error is a mistake by which no change comes about in the meaning, such as omitting the secondary prolongation, the concealment (ikhfāʾ), the nasalisation (ghunnah) and the like. In these one should not prompt, because no invalidity comes into the prayer on their account. Consider what is stated in Fatāwā Shāmī:

قوله وإلا لا، أي وإن لم يغير المعنى فلا فساد إلا في حرف مد ولين إن فحش فإنه يفسد، وإن لم يغير المعنى، وحروف المد واللين هي حروف العلة الثلاثة: الألف، والواو والياء، إذا كانت ساكنة وقبلها حركة تجانسها، فلو لم تجانسها فهي حروف علة ولين لا مد. (فتاوى الشامي: ۱/٦۳۰، سعيد).

قال في الهندية: ذكر في الفوائد: لو قرأ في الصلاة بخطأ فاحش ثم رجع وقرأ صحيحاً قال: عندي صلاته جائزة وكذلك الإعراب ولو قرأ النصب مكان الرفع والرفع مكان النصب أو الخفض مكان الرفع أو النصب لا تفسد صلاته. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۸۲).

Prompting in the prayer at a time of need does not invalidate the prayer. See: (al-Hidāyah: 1/136, al-Miṣbāḥ; al-Mabsūṭ: 1/193, Idārat al-Qurʾān; and al-Muḥīṭ al-Burhānī: 1/377).
