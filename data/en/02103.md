### TITLE
Taking an interest-bearing loan in order to avoid income tax

### QUESTION
May one take an interest-bearing loan in order to avoid income tax, given that the government levies less tax on those who carry larger debts? بینوا توجروا ۔

### ANSWER
In the case described, the preferable and better course is certainly to keep away from every interest-based dealing. But if the government levies income tax unjustly at a very high rate, then in a time of need there should be room for an interest-bearing loan in order to ward off the harm. The jurists have permitted even the giving of a bribe in order to ward off harm.

Consider what Jadīd Fiqhī Mabāḥith states, under the heading "The ruling on paying a bribe or interest in order to avoid income tax and to buy costly goods":

Answer: This arrangement is beyond doubt permissible, because there is a benefit for the Muslim in it as well as the warding off of harm. Even on the view of those who say it is not permissible there ought to be room for it, because advancing through trade, and buying goods of any kind such as a truck and so on, is lawful for us and is our right. As soon as we go forward to secure that right, all the wrangling over tax and the like is thrown in our way. So although this is not a case of dire necessity, it is certainly an injustice in which our loss is plain, and in order to escape that injustice and harm, arrangements of this sort ought to be permissible as well. (جدید فقہی مباحث: ۲/۴۹۹) ۔

By way of support and corroboration, a maxim of the jurists is cited here. Consider: ʿAllāmah Ibn Nujaym al-Miṣrī (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

"الضرر يزال" أصلها قوله ﷺ: "لا ضرر ولا ضرار"

ويبتني على هذه القاعدة كثير من أبواب الفقه، فمن ذلك الرد بالعيب وجميع أنواع الخيارات والحجر بسائر أنواعه على المفتي به

الخ. (الأشباه والنظائر: ۱/۲۵۰، القاعدة الخامسة).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
