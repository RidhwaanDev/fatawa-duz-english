### TITLE
The saʿy after the widening of Ṣafā and Marwah

### QUESTION
These days Ṣafā and Marwah are being widened considerably. Some people say that the widened area lies away from the original place of saʿy, so that after the widening the place of saʿy is open to question. Is this correct or not?

### ANSWER
First of all it should be understood that Ṣafā and Marwah are the names of two hills, and a hill is long and broad. So even after the widening there will be no defect in the place of saʿy. Indeed, at one time there were houses between these two hills, and people used to perform saʿy on the outer side of those houses.

Lisān al-ʿArab states:

الصفا: العريض من الحجارة الأملس جمع صفاة يكتب بالألف

ومنه الصفا والمروة وهما جبلان بين بطحاء مكة والمسجد

الصفا اسم أحد جبلي المسعي والصفا موضع بمكة. (لسان العرب: ۷/۳۷۱).

Bear in mind that the word jabal is used for a long and broad mountain.

Lisān al-ʿArab states:

الجبل اسم لكل وتد من أوتاد الأرض إذا عظم وطال. (لسان العرب تحت مادة الجبل: ۱۱/۹٦).

A small thing is not called a jabal. Since Ṣafā and Marwah are long, broad hills, the place of saʿy will be broad as well.

It also states:

المرو

واحدتها مروة

ومروة المسعي التي تذكر مع الصفا وهي أحد رأسيه الذين ينتهي السعي إليها سميت بذلك

والمروة جبل مكة شرفها الله تعالى في التنزيل العزيز ﴿إن الصفا والمروة من شعائر الله﴾. (لسان العرب: ۱۳/۸۹).

See also:

المعجم الوسيط: ۸٦۵. والمنجد في الأعلام: ۳٤۵. ومعجم البلدان: ۳/٤۱۱. وعمدة القاري: ۷/۲۲۸. والجامع لا حكام القرآن: ۲/۲).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states:

For the saʿy to be between Ṣafā and Marwah is an essential element of the saʿy, in the sense that one should not go outside the actual width of Ṣafā and Marwah on either side while performing it. Al-Mansak al-Kabīr has declared this to be an essential element of the saʿy, and this is correct. So if someone performed saʿy outside the masʿā (the place of saʿy), it is not valid.

An important note: Shaykh ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Murshidī (may Allah have mercy on him) mentions in his commentary on al-Kanz that the distance between Ṣafā and Marwah is seven hundred and fifty cubits; on this reckoning the complete saʿy — that is, all seven circuits — comes to five thousand two hundred cubits. Al-Shumunnī says that the distance between Ṣafā and Marwah is seven hundred and sixty-six cubits. As for the width of the masʿā, ʿAllāmah Shaykh Quṭb al-Dīn al-Ḥanafī, quoting from his own Tārīkh al-Fākihī, writes that it is thirty-five cubits, and that the masʿā in which the Messenger of Allah ﷺ performed saʿy was broad. Later, houses were built within the width of that old masʿā; then the caliph al-Mahdī (may Allah have mercy on him) had those houses pulled down, incorporating some of them into the Sacred Mosque and leaving the rest. Whatever width of the masʿā was left at that time is what remains today, and it is in this that saʿy is performed nowadays. (از منحة، ملخصاً عن حاشية المدني، وغنية، ملخصاً عن منحة).

(The Saudi government has now expanded the Sacred Mosque and has also rebuilt the masʿā in a very fine new style, putting a partition down the middle so that the route from Ṣafā to Marwah is separate from the route from Marwah to Ṣafā, so that those performing saʿy are not inconvenienced. — the author, may Allah have mercy on him)

(ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: part four, Kitāb al-Ḥajj: 200, Mujaddidiyyah).
