### TITLE
Spending interest money on public welfare projects

### QUESTION
Is it allowed to dig a well for the poor and needy with interest money, or not? In the same way, may it be used to build a madrasah wall and so on, or not? And is it permissible to spend it on other public welfare and charitable projects or not?

بينوا بالتفصيل توجروا بأجر جزيل.

### ANSWER
The great scholars and muftis differ over this question. Most muftis hold that interest money should not be spent on digging a well, but should be given away in charity to the poor without any intention of earning reward. Some Indian muftis, however — such as Sayyid Mufti ʿAbd al-Raḥīm Lājpūrī (may Allah have mercy on him) — hold that interest money may be spent on public welfare projects. Caution lies in acting on the position of the majority, though if someone has already spent it in this way there is room for that. If it is genuinely necessary to spend the money on digging a well or on some other good work, the following course may be adopted: the interest money is given to a poor person and the work is then carried out through him.

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

The question is a disputed one. Giving the money to a poor, needy person is preferable. There is room to use it for public welfare works such as roads. It cannot be used for a mosque, but it may be used to repair a mosque's lavatory. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 9/279).

When Mufti Ismāʿīl Kacchōlwī raised an objection to this fatwa of Mufti ʿAbd al-Raḥīm (may Allah have mercy on him), Mufti ʿAbd al-Raḥīm (may Allah have mercy on him) attached the fatwas of senior scholars in support of it: for example the fatwas of the chief mufti, Mawlānā Muḥammad Kifāyatullāh; the fatwa of Mufti Saʿīd Aḥmad, the chief mufti of Maẓāhir ʿUlūm; the fatwa of Shaykh al-Islām Ḥusayn Aḥmad Madanī (may Allah have mercy on him); the fatwa of Mufti Maḥmūd Ḥasan Gangōhī (may Allah have mercy on him); and others. For detail see: (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 9/256–260).

Islāmī Fiqh states:

If a person is compelled to deposit money in a bank or the like and receives interest on it, he should not use it for himself or for his children, but should give it to the poor without any intention of earning reward, or have it put through some reliable person into a welfare project — for instance into the roads of the neighbourhood or village, public lavatories and the like. (Islāmī Fiqh: 2/369).

In one place in Fatāwā ʿUthmānī, Mufti Muḥammad Taqī said:

Give the interest money away in charity to a poor or needy person with the intention of ridding yourself of it; and here too it is necessary to make the recipient its owner. This money cannot be used for construction or other welfare works. (Fatāwā ʿUthmānī: 3/279).

In another place, however, he wrote a researched fatwa stating that spending interest money on public welfare is permissible and correct. He also argued from the fatwa of Ḥakīm al-Ummat, showing that in his view too interest money must be given away in charity but need not be transferred into someone's ownership; and Mufti Rafīʿ agreed with this fatwa as well.

The following points may be noted by way of summary:

• Ḥakīm al-Ummat (may Allah have mercy on him) said that wealth of this kind takes the ruling of lost property, and the ruling for lost property is that it is a voluntary charity given on behalf of its real owner, in which transferring ownership is not necessary.
(الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۲/۳۳۸، سعيد. وشرح منظومه ابن وهبان: ۱/۸۸).

• Nowhere in the books of the jurists of the school is it explicitly stated that tainted wealth must be transferred into someone's ownership; that has merely been inferred from the word "give in charity".

• Tainted wealth that must be given away in charity is not like zakat as regards those who may receive it; it differs in several respects. The jurists have written, for instance, that this wealth may even be given to one's own wife and children.
(حاشية الحموي على الأشباه: ۲/۱۰٦. والدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٤/۲۷۸).

(There is, however, this difficulty in giving it to one's wife and children: the charity is given on behalf of the owner of the wealth, and the one who finds it is merely an intermediary. Still, the permissibility of spending it on the shrouding and burial of a deceased person shows that transfer of ownership is not necessary here, since no transfer of ownership takes place in shrouding and burial.)

• According to the apparent narration of the school, this wealth may also be given to the Banū Hāshim.
(الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۲/۳۵۱، سعيد).

• Zakat may not be given to a non-Muslim, but this wealth may be given to a non-Muslim too.
(شامي: ٤/۳۵۱، سعيد. وفتاوى دار العلوم زكريا: ۳/۲۲۰).

• Restricting the disposal of tainted earnings to the poor is found only among the Ḥanafīs; the other imams allow such wealth to be spent on general public interests.
(المعيار المعرب: ٦/۱٤٦، بيروت. والذخيرة: ۵/۱٦۷، بيروت. وشرح المهذب: ۱۰/۵۲۰، بيروت. ونهاية المحتاج: ۵/۱۸۵).

For further detail see: (Fatāwā ʿUthmānī: 3/128–140).

Other scholars, on the other hand, have held it impermissible, and their fatwas may also be considered:

The chief mufti of Pakistan and India, Mufti Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him), says:

Transferring the money into the ownership of the poor and needy is necessary. It is not permissible to spend such wealth on building madrasahs or on other endowments and charitable works. The evidence is as follows:

(1) The statements of the jurists concerning wealth of this kind are transmitted in two forms: in some of them "تصدق به" or "وجب عليه التصدق", and in others "تصدق على الفقراء والمساكين". Spending it on endowments and charitable works is nowhere transmitted.

(2) When the words ṣadaqah and taṣadduq are used, in the usage of the jurists they refer to something that must be transferred into the recipient's ownership.

For the detailed evidence see: (Imdād al-Muftīn, vol. 2, pp. 383–386, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah states:

The building of a school, urinals and the like are not entitled recipients, which is what giving in charity requires; that is why this has been forbidden. The money should be handed over to an entitled recipient so that he owns it, and he may then spend it wherever and however he wishes. (Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 16/386, the arranged and classified edition).

Āp kē Masāʾil states:

Give interest money to some needy, destitute person without making the intention of charity; it is not permissible to put this money into any good cause. (Āp kē Masāʾil awr un kā Ḥal: 6/242).

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

Interest money may be used to meet the individual need of some needy person, or to carry out a work of collective benefit such as digging a well. This money should not be put into mosques and madrasahs, but the scholars have permitted its use for building lavatories. (Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil, vol. 1, p. 253).

In other words, in Mawlānā Khālid Sayfullāh's view spending it on public welfare is permissible, and only mosques and madrasahs are excluded — although he then permits it to be used for building the lavatory of a mosque or madrasah.

Maḥmūd al-Fatāwā states:

Interest money should be given to the poor and needy as their own property, with the intention of removing its evil consequences and without any intention of earning reward; it should not be given to a public scheme. (Maḥmūd al-Fatāwā: 3/64, the chapter on interest).

In any case, senior scholars stand on both sides, so there is room to act upon whichever position one wishes. Nevertheless, acting on the more cautious course is preferable, and spending the money on public welfare by means of the arrangement described above is the cautious course.

Niẓām al-Fatāwā states:

Once the device of transferring ownership to an entitled recipient has been used, it will be correct to use the money even for cleaning a mosque's lavatory and the like. The device of transferring ownership is that the whole of the interest money is given to poor and needy people who are entitled to charity, so that they become its owners, and the recipient then gives it to the mosque as a donation of his own.
