### TITLE
Blood that appears in vomit

### QUESTION
If blood comes up in vomit, does the ablution break or not?

### ANSWER
The detail on this question is as follows. If the blood that comes up in the vomit is thin and flowing, the ablution breaks, whether the blood is little or much and whether it fills the mouth or not. If the blood is clotted, in pieces, and fills the mouth, the ablution also breaks; but if it is less than a mouthful, the ablution does not break.

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

(قوله أو علق الخ) العلق لغة: دم منعقد كما هو أحد معانيه، لكن المراد به هنا سوداء محترقة كما في الهداية وليس بدم حقيقة كما في الكافي ولهذا اعتبر فيه ملأ الفم وإلا فخروج الدم ناقض بلا تفصيل بين قليله وكثيره على المختار. (فتاوى الشامي: ۱/۱۳۷).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states:

If blood comes up in vomit, it has either come down from the head or come up from the stomach, and it may be of the flowing kind. If it has come down from the head and is flowing, then by agreement the ablution breaks. If the blood is clotted, then by agreement the ablution does not break. When the blood is of the flowing kind it is like a nosebleed, so what counts for breaking the ablution is that it flows and that it predominates over the saliva. If the saliva and the blood are equal — that is, the saliva is a reddish yellow (orange) in colour — the ablution still breaks, as a precaution. If the saliva predominates and the blood is overcome — that is, the saliva is a pale yellow — the ablution does not break. The same ruling applies to blood coming from the teeth. Clotted blood does not break the ablution because it has already lost the character of blood. If the blood has come up from the stomach and is clotted, then, as long as it is less than a mouthful, by agreement it does not break the ablution either; but if it fills the mouth, the ablution breaks. If the blood coming from the stomach is flowing, then according to the position of Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) the ablution breaks just as it does when blood comes out of any other place, even if it does not fill the mouth, because it has come out of a wound in the belly, the stomach not being a natural site for blood. This is the chosen view, and most of the scholars have declared it to be the correct one. (ʿUmdat al-Fiqh: 1/150).
