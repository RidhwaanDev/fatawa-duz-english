### TITLE
Saying "If I listen to that lecture, my wife is divorced"

### QUESTION
Someone told Zayd not to listen to a certain scholar's lectures, saying that his talks were full of poison. Zayd replied, "If I listen to that scholar's lecture, my wife has three divorces." Zayd then listened to the talk on a tape recorder. Has divorce taken place or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, if he listened to the talk on a tape recorder, no divorce falls on Zayd's wife. What he heard was a reproduction of the lecture, not the lecture itself — just as the prostration of recitation is not required when a verse of prostration is heard from a tape recorder, and is not required when it is heard as an echo. The question of divorce works the same way.

Ālāt-e Jadīdah ke Sharʿī Aḥkām states:

A verse of prostration heard through a tape recorder carries the same ruling as one heard from a gramophone record: hearing it does not make the prostration of recitation necessary, because a genuine recitation is a condition for the prostration of recitation to fall due, and recitation is not what is intended from a lifeless, unconscious machine. (آلاتِ جدیدہ کے شرعی احکام، ص ۲۲۳) ۔

For further detail see:

حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح، ص ٤۸٦

(و نظام الفتاوی: ۱/۷۲، و فتاوی محمودیہ: ۷/٤۷۲، و احسن الفتاوی: ٤/٦۵، و فتاوی دار العلوم زکریا: ۲/٤۷۸) ۔

Note: The books generally state that a conditional divorce has an oath hidden within it. That is so only in this sense: just as breaking an oath makes expiation due, so once the condition is met the divorce takes effect. Otherwise a conditional divorce is not an oath in the real or the technical sense. The divorce therefore turns on the reality of the matter, and hearing a reproduction of the lecture will not bring a divorce into effect. An oath is different: the chapter on oaths states that a person does break his oath by hearing a reproduction of the speech.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
