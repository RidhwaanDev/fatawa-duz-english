### TITLE
Informing people of an invalid prayer after a long time has passed

### QUESTION
A man used to lead the prayers in a mosque, and a good deal of time has passed since. Once or twice it happened that during the prayer he suspected, or thought it likely, that a drop of urine had come out. On another occasion, on the first night of Ramadan, the sighting of the moon had not yet been announced and the imam had already prayed his own witr; then, once the moon had been announced, he led the people in witr again after the tarāwīḥ, even though the tarāwīḥ had been led by someone else. At the time he made no announcement that the prayer should be repeated on account of the drop of urine, which was either doubtful or merely thought likely. He now has no connection at all with that place, and most of the worshippers of those days will either have died or moved elsewhere. This happened 30 years ago, and there would be no benefit in informing anyone now. How can the imam free himself of this responsibility?

### ANSWER
In the case described, since the breaking of the imam's ablution by a drop was doubtful or merely thought likely, he should have made the announcement at the time so that the prayer could be repeated. The established Ḥanafī position is that the imam's prayer carries the follower's prayer with it; this has been explained already in volume two, p. 234. In view of the difficulties in the situation described, however, there is a ruling among the jurists which shows that the announcement is not necessary.

Tuḥfat al-Mulūk states:

ولو ظهر حدث الإمام أعاد المأموم لقوله ﷺ: أيما رجل صلى بقوم ثم تذكر جنابة أعاد، وأعادوا. (فصل في الجماعة: ۱/۸٤٦)

In his commentary on it, Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd al-Laṭīf ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn Malak writes:

هذا إذا علم المأموم حدث إمامه وإن لم يعلموا لا يجب عليهم الإعادة ولا على الإمام الإعلام بأنه صلي على غير طهارة ولا يأثم بتركه الإعلام. (شرح تحفة الملوك: ۱/۸٤۷. بتعليق عبد المجيد الدرويش).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār and Shāmī have also reported from some jurists the view that there is no need to inform anyone:

وصحح في مجمع الفتاوى عدمه أي الإخبار مطلقاً لكونه عن خطأ معفو عنه لأنه لم يتعمد ذلك. (ص: ٥۹۱/۱).

As for the hadith cited in the base text, "أيما رجل صلى بقوم ثم تذكر جنابة أعاد، وأعادوا", the annotator Dr ʿAbd al-Majīd al-Darwīsh quotes from Naṣb al-Rāyah that this narration is uncommon, and Ibn Ḥajar said لم اجدہ مرفوعاً. It is, however, narrated in broken form from Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab:

"أن رسول الله ﷺ صلى بالناس وهو جنب وأعاد وأعادوا"

This narration is broken, and its chain contains Abū Jābir al-Bayāḍ, who is abandoned; Yaḥyā ibn Maʿīn called him a liar. Against it, al-Dāraquṭnī reports from al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him):

قال رسول الله ﷺ: أيما إمام سها فصلى بالقوم وهو جنب فقد مضت صلاتهم ثم ليغتسل هو ثم ليعد صلاته الخ ۔

But this narration too is weak; it contains Juwaybir, who is abandoned.

(تعليق عبد المجيد الدرويش على شرح تحفة الملوك: ۱/۸٤٦).

The author of Sharḥ Tuḥfat al-Mulūk is Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd al-Laṭīf (d. 854), well known as Ibn Malak. Writing about his life in his introduction to Munyat al-Ṣayyādīn, Sāʾid Bakdāsh (may Allah preserve him) says:

"هو الإمام المحدث الفقيه الحنفي المعتبر محمد بن عز الدين عبد اللطيف بن عبد العزيز بن أمين الدين بن فرشته الرومي".

He was a scholar with many works to his name. Some of them are:

(۱) شرح مصابيح السنة للبغوي، (۲) شرح الوقاية، (۳) شرح تحفة الملوك للرازي، (٤) منية الصيادين في تعلم الاصطياد وأحكامه، (٥) روضة المتقين في مصنوعات رب العلمين في المواعظ والعبادات.

In the notes to Munyat al-Ṣayyādīn the following books are given as the sources for his life:

(۱) كتائب اعلام الاخيار من فقهاء مذهب النعمان المختار للكفوي رحمه الله، (۲) الشقائق النعمانية في علماء الدولة العثمانية، (۳) كشف الظنون لحاجي خليفة، (٤) الفوائد البهية في تراجم الحنفية لعبد الحي اللكنوي رحمه الله، السعاية، وعمدة الرعاية، (٥) هدية العارفين لا سماعيل باشا البغدادي، (٦) الاعلام للزركلي، (۷) معجم المؤلفين لعمر رضا كحالة وغيره. (مقدمة منية الصيادين لسائد بكداش، ص ۱۱).

His father, ʿIzz al-Dīn ʿAbd al-Laṭīf, was likewise a careful scholar with works to his name, and the editor has recorded his life as well in the introduction to Sharḥ Tuḥfat al-Mulūk.

This introduction has been given because on one occasion, when this humble servant cited Sharḥ Tuḥfat al-Mulūk in a fatwa, some muftis objected and asked where this little-known book had come from to be used as a source. Yet some of the author's works (may Allah have mercy on him), such as Munyat al-Ṣayyādīn, are regarded as masterpieces. It should also be kept in mind that the base text, Tuḥfat al-Mulūk, is the work of Muḥammad ibn Abī Bakr al-Rāzī (d. 666).

The upshot is that the imam should inform the worshippers as far as he possibly can; if that is altogether impossible, this other view may be acted upon.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
