### TITLE
An animal slaughtered in veneration of other than Allah

### QUESTION
In some non-Muslim areas it is the custom that at a special family gathering, or on the occasion of a death, an animal is slaughtered in the name of other than Allah — that is, in the name of the deceased forefathers — and a great many people are invited. At such a gathering there may be one or two Muslims in the family as well, and their attendance is expected; otherwise there is a serious risk that ties will be cut off with them. What I wish to know is this: if the Muslim attends in order to avoid conflict, and himself slaughters the animal that has been dedicated to other than Allah after saying "Bismillāh", will that animal be lawful or not?

### ANSWER
In the case described, it is the owner's intention that counts. An animal that has been dedicated to other than Allah will not become lawful even if it is slaughtered with "Bismillāh" pronounced over it; rather, it comes under ما اہل لغیر اللہ and is unlawful. Accordingly, if it is a gathering of non-Muslims, he may attend for a short while where there is good reason to do so, but he should not eat the slaughtered animal and should excuse himself from eating at that time. Alternatively, attending the gathering would be proper if it is done with the intention of conveying and calling to the message of Islam.

Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ذبح لقدوم الأمير ونحوه كواحد من العظماء يحرم لأنه أهل به لغير الله ولو ذكر اسم الله تعالى ولو للضيف لا يحرم

والفارق أنه

كان لتعظيم غير الله فتحرم. وفي رد المحتار: قوله والفارق أي بين ما أهل به لغير الله بسبب تعظيم المخلوق وبين غيره. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ٦/۳۰۹، سعيد).

See further:

(الأشباه والنظائر مع شرح الحموي: ۲/٤۵۹. ٤٦۰، كتاب الصيد والذبائح).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

An animal that is slaughtered as a vow to other than Allah and with the intention of drawing near to other than Allah is unlawful and is carrion, even if it is slaughtered while saying "Bismillāh, Allāhu Akbar", because in such cases it is the intention of the true owner that counts, and his intention was that its life should be offered up for other than Allah. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 8/23, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Muḥammad Shafīʿ Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) states:

The second case is that an animal is slaughtered in order to draw near to other than Allah — that is, the shedding of its blood is meant as a means of nearness to other than Allah — but the name of Allah is pronounced over it at the time of slaughter. By the agreement of the jurists this case too is unlawful, and the slaughtered animal has the status of carrion, though there is some difference over how the evidence is derived. Some of the commentators and jurists have held that this too falls under the plain sense of "ما أهل به لغير الله", as appears in the glosses on al-Bayḍāwī and in al-Durr al-Mukhtār, Kitāb al-Dhabāʾiḥ. Others have not treated it as the plain sense of "ما أهل به لغير الله", because as a matter of Arabic usage that is strained; but on the ground of the shared underlying cause — namely, seeking nearness to other than Allah — they have attached it to "ما أهل به لغير الله" and declared it unlawful, and in this humble one's view this is the safer and more cautious position. Moreover, another independent verse also bears witness to the prohibition of this case, namely the noble verse "وما ذبح على النصب"; for because of the conjunction the plain sense is that "ما أهل به لغير الله" and ذبح علی النصب are two distinct cases. Thus "ما أهل به لغير الله" is an animal over which the name of other than Allah is called out at the time of slaughter, while "وما ذبح على النصب" is an animal slaughtered in veneration and honour of the stone altars, even if the people mention no name other than Allah's over it and in fact mention the name of Allah alone. (Imdād al-Muftīn, vol. 2, p. 774, Dār al-Ishāʿat).
