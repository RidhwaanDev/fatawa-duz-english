### TITLE
Assessing the statement of Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him): "امصص بظْر اللات"

### QUESTION
Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī records that when ʿUrwah said to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that he was bent on destroying his own people, and that if he were overcome this mixed crowd of people would abandon him and run away, Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) said on that occasion: "امصص بظر اللات". The commentators give its meaning as "go on sucking the private part of al-Lāt" (صحيح البخاري ۱/۳۷۸ باب الشروط في الجهاد). On the face of it, such an insult is contrary to the truthfulness and dignity of Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him). Besides, there is the verse of the Noble Qur'an ﴿ولا تسبّوا الذين يدعون من دون الله فيسبّوا الله عدوا بغير علم﴾, and this too conflicts with it. What is the solution to this difficulty?

The Qadianis use this sentence to argue that Mirza's foul abuse was sound. For instance, Mirza called those who did not accept him the offspring of prostitutes, called Ḥusayn (may Allah be pleased with him) a heap of dung, and called his enemies the swine of the wilderness. Mirza Qadiani wrote all this abuse in his books. The Shia likewise use this sentence to argue that Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) was foul-tongued — نعوذ باللہ.

### ANSWER
The word بظر has two meanings:

(1) The protruding part of the private part.

(2) The raised middle portion of the upper lip (القاموس الوحید: ۱/۱۷۱). Accordingly بظر also carries the meaning "الشفة العليا" (المعجم الوسيط ٦۲). Here the second meaning is intended, and the indications for this are as follows:

(1) The word امصص is itself an indication, because what is sucked is the lip, not the private part.

(2) The same hadith mentions that the Companions would apply the blessed saliva of the Prophet ﷺ to their faces and their bodies, and would drink the water he left over:

"قال فو الله ما تنخم رسول الله ﷺ نخامة إلا وقعت في كف رجل منهم فدلك بها وجهه وجلده" (صحيح البخاري ۱/۳۷۹)

The sense is: you keep sucking the spittle of al-Lāt; as for us, we will keep rubbing the saliva of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ on our faces and sucking it.

(3) ʿUrwah did not accuse Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) of abuse and insult, which shows that this was not an insult.

(4) The dignity of Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) also requires that his words be taken in the better sense.

An objection and its answer:

A scholar has criticised this answer in a respected journal of one of the Dār al-ʿUlūms. Someone showed me that critical article a long while later. This humble servant had written an explanation befitting the standing of Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him), so there was no need to reply to the article; but at the request of some friends a few further submissions are being presented. The writer of the article cited about 17 commentaries in support of the meaning he prefers. This humble servant already knew that meaning from the commentaries, but in his own view he adopted a more fitting meaning, one that also agrees with the dictionaries. This servant tried to keep the words of Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) clear of abuse and insult by taking them in the other sense. The writer has stated that abusing someone in a state of war is a kind of fighting with the tongue, and that at the treaty of Ḥudaybiyah a state of war existed, so fighting with the tongue was resorted to. It is better that we briefly quote the kinds of jihad from some well-known book and explain what jihad with the tongue is. We quote the kinds of jihad from Ḥāfiẓ Ibn al-Qayyim's Zād al-Maʿād, and to avoid length we leave out the Arabic passages and confine ourselves to the translation — indeed, to the gist of it.

ʿAllāmah Ibn al-Qayyim (may Allah have mercy on him) has written that there are four kinds of jihad: (1) against the self; (2) against Satan; (3) against the obstinate, that is, the disbelievers and hypocrites who are enemies of the religion; (4) against sinful believers. Jihad against the self then has four branches: jihad through knowledge, jihad through action, jihad through calling others, and jihad through patience — that is, to struggle against the self and acquire knowledge, then to subdue the self and act upon it, to call others to it, and to bear patiently the hardships and trials that come on this path.

(2) Jihad against Satan has two kinds: repelling satanic doubts and repelling satanic desires. Remember that the desires of the lower self are one thing and satanic desires are another, though at times the two come together. What demand of the self is there in drinking foul-smelling liquor? It is Satan who has made it beloved by creating an imaginary pleasure in it.

(3) For jihad against the enemies of the religion, ʿAllāmah Ibn al-Qayyim (may Allah have mercy on him) has mentioned four branches; instead of four we may make six: 1. jihad with the heart; 2. jihad with wealth; 3. jihad with life and body; 4. jihad with the pen; 5. jihad through rank and office; 6. jihad with the tongue.

Jihad with the heart is to detest their disbelief in one's heart. Jihad with wealth is obvious. Jihad with body and life is to devote oneself entirely against falsehood. Jihad with the pen is to dedicate one's pen against falsehood. Jihad through position is to benefit the religion through one's rank and office, and to bring and call those under one's authority towards the religion — this kind of jihad may also be carried on with Muslims. Jihad with the tongue means to strengthen the truth with a tongue that calls people to Allah.

(4) Jihad against sinful and immoral believers has three kinds: jihad with the hand, jihad with the tongue, and jihad with the heart. The well-known hadith points to these kinds:

من رأي منكم منكراً فليغيره بيده فإن لم يستطع فبلسانه فإن لم يستطع فبقلبه وذلك أضعف الإيمان. (صحيح مسلم، رقم: ٤۹، باب بيان كون النهي عن المنكر من الإيمان).

Among these kinds of jihad, jihad with the tongue has now been set before the reader. Jihad with the tongue is calling people to the religion, or meeting them in debate with proofs. Jihad with the tongue is not abuse and foul language.

This humble servant submits that abuse and insult are not fitting towards any Muslim, and the rank of Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) is the highest of all after the prophets (peace be upon them). A hadith states:

ليس المؤمن بالطعان ولا اللعان ولا الفاحش البذي. (سنن ترمذي: ۲/۱۸).

Taunting and cursing do not befit a Muslim. Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim states:

المستبان ما قالا فعلي البادي مالم يعتد المظلوم. (صحيح مسلم: ۲/۳۲۱)

When two people trade insults, the sin falls on the one who began, as long as the wronged party does not go beyond the limit. Musnad Aḥmad narrates from Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him):

إن رجلاً شتم أبابكر والنبي ﷺ جالس فجعل النبي ﷺ يعجب ويتبسم فلما أكثر رد عليه بعض قوله فغضب النبي ﷺ وقام فلحقه أبوبكر رضي الله عنه فقال: يا رسول الله! كان يشتمني وأنت جالس فلما رددت عليه بعض قوله غضبت وقمت قال: إنه كان معه ملك يرده عنك فلما رددت عليه بعض قوله وقع الشيطان فلم أكن لأقعد مع الشيطان. (مسند احمد: ۱۵/۳۹۰/۹٦۲٤) قال الشيخ شعيب الأرناؤوط: حسن لغيره،

That is, a man was abusing and insulting Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) while the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was smiling. When the man abused him a great deal, Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) answered him back. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ became angry and got up and left. Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) went after him and said: "Messenger of Allah, while he was abusing me you remained seated; when I answered him back you became angry." He said: "While you were silent an angel was answering on your behalf; when you began to trade insults, the angel left and Satan came, and I cannot sit with Satan." This hadith shows how displeased the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was at returning abuse for abuse.

The writer of the article said that at Ḥudaybiyah a state of fighting existed, and that in a state of fighting abuse falls under jihad with the tongue. There was no state of fighting at Ḥudaybiyah. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ had come with the intention of ʿUmrah, was clothed in the garments of iḥrām, had brought the sacrificial animals with him, and had set out after making the intention of ʿUmrah.

وأحرم بالعمرة ليأمن الناس من حربه وليعلموا أنه إنما خرج زائراً للبيت ومعظماً له. (عمدة القاري، باب الشروط في الجهاد، والتمهيد لابن عبد البر: ۱۲/۱٤۹، ط: مؤسسة القرطبة).

Abū Qatādah (may Allah be pleased with him) hunted a wild donkey; he had come without iḥrām, and the commentators of hadith have given several explanations of this. Similarly, if some person came on that occasion in order to accept Islam and had no intention of ʿUmrah, that is a separate matter. But the Companions had come with the intention of ʿUmrah, and when the ʿUmrah did not take place, all who had taken part came the following year for ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ. Abū Qatādah (may Allah be pleased with him) says:

انطلقنا مع النبي ﷺ عام الحديبية فأحرم أصحابه ولم أحرم. (صحيح البخاري: ۱/۲٤۵، باب إذا رأى المحرمون صيداً).

The Companions had not come to fight. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to Budayl, the envoy of the polytheists:

إنا لم نجئ لقتال أحد ولكنا جئنا معتمرين.

And when Budayl returned to the polytheists he said:

إنكم تعجبون على محمد إنه لم يأت للقتال إنما جاء زائراً لهذا البيت.

Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) said to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ:

يا رسول الله! خرجت عامداً لهذا البيت لا تريد قتل أحد ولا حرباً فتوجه له فمن صدنا قاتلناه.

All these narrations show that the Companions had not come to fight. Even when the polytheists sent out a party to attack, the Muslims still held fast to patience. So there was no state of fighting; the Muslims were waiting for a peaceful settlement. Moreover, when an envoy of the polytheists came and the Companions began to recite the talbiyah and brought the sacrificial animals out before him, he said:

رأيت البدن قد قلدت وأشعرت فما أري أن يصدوا عن البيت.

These passages may be seen in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, باب الشروط فی الجہاد و المصالحہ مع اہل الحرب.

Even if we were to suppose that the ground of Ḥudaybiyah was a battlefield, abuse would still not be jihad. Jihad with the tongue is calling people to Allah. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him):

لأن يهدي الله رجلاً واحداً خير لك من أن يكون لك حمر النعم. (رواه مسلم في أبواب فضائل علي، رقم: ٦۲۲۳).

That is, for Allah to guide one man through you is better for you than red camels.

Mufti Taqi ʿUthmānī says in Takmilat Fatḥ al-Mulhim:

فكان النبي ﷺ في أول الأمر مأموراً أن يجاهد الكفار بلسانه لا بيده فيدعوهم ويعظهم ويجادلهم بالتي هي أحسن. (تكملة فتح الملهم: ۳/۸).

In another place he writes:

فالمرحلة الأولى هي الصبر على أذى المشركين مع الاستمرار في دعوتهم إلى دين الحق. (تكملة: ۳/۵).

Qurrat al-ʿAynayn states:

قال أبوبكر ابن العربي: اتفق العلماء على أن معنى الآية لا تسبوا ٓلهة الكفار فيسبوا آلهتكم وكذلك هو فإن السب في غير الحجة فعل الأدنياء فمنع الله في كتابه أحداً أن يفعل فعلاً يؤدي إلى محظور. (قرة العينين على الجلالين، ص ۱۸۰، للدكتور محمد احمد كنعان).

To say, then, that "امصص بظر اللات" is not an insult to their deities but an insult to the polytheists is very strange indeed. Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (may Allah have mercy on him) has also written that al-Lāt has here been put in the place of "your mother", so the بظر would belong to al-Lāt and not to the polytheists. And even if the polytheists were meant, the commentators hold that the prohibition in "لا تسبوا الذين يدعون من دون الله فيسبوا الله عدواً بغير علم" also covers abusing the polytheists. Rūḥ al-Maʿānī states:

والمراد من الموصول أما المشركون على معني لا تسبوهم من حيث عبادتهم لآلهتهم كأن تقولوا تباً لكم ولآلهتكم. (روح المعاني: ۷/۲۵۱).

In another place he writes: لا تخرجوا من دعوة الكفار ومحاجتهم إلى أن تسبوا ما يعبدونه من دون الله. (روح المعاني: ۷/۲۵۲).

Islam is not a religion of abuse. Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

عن عبد الرحمن بن عائذ كان النبي ﷺ إذا بعث بعثاً قال: تألفوا الناس وتأنوا بهم ولا تغيروا عليهم حتى تدعوهم فما على وجه الأرض من أهل بيت مدر ولا وبر إلا وأن تأتوني بهم مسلمين أحب إلى من أن تقتلوا رجالهم وتأتوني بنسائهم. (المطالب العالية: ۲/۱٦٦، والإصابة: ۳/۱۵۲).

When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ sent out a company for jihad he would say: win people over, deal patiently with them, and do not attack them before you have called them. For that you should bring me the people of every house of clay and every tent on the face of the earth as Muslims is dearer to me than that you should kill their men and bring me their women as captives. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ also sent Muʿādh (may Allah be pleased with him) to Yemen and said:

إنك ستأتي قوماً أهل كتاب فإذا جئتهم فادعهم إلى أن يشهدوا ألا إله إلا الله وأن محمداً رسول الله. (صحيح البخاري باب اخذ الصدقة من الأغنياء).

This shows that jihad with the tongue is calling people to Allah, and that abuse is not jihad. Abuse is in fact a mark of the hypocrite: إذا خاصم فجر.

Mawlānā Aḥmad ʿAlī Sahāranpūrī (may Allah have mercy on him) has given the meaning of فجر as abuse and insult. (بخاری: ۱/۱۰) Mawlānā Fakhr al-Dīn, the hadith scholar of Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, said: because fujūr is an expression for losing all self-restraint and stooping to abuse when quarrelling. (ایضاح البخاری: ٤/۳۳۵) Shaykh al-Ḥadīth Mawlānā Muḥammad Zakariyyā (may Allah have mercy on him) translated it thus: when he quarrels with someone, he resorts to abuse. (سراج القاری: ۱/۱۷۷).

The Noble Qur'an speaks of arguing in the best manner when the need arises. Allah says:

ادع إلى سبيل ربك بالحكمة والموعظة الحسنة وجادلهم بالتي هي أحسن. (النحل: ۱۲۵).

Mufti Muḥammad Shafīʿ (may Allah have mercy on him) writes: the substance of admonition is to say a word of sincere good will in such a way that the listener's heart is softened, and to present it under a heading and in a manner that sets the heart at rest. (خلاصہ معارف القرآن: ۵/٤۰۹). Tafsīr ʿUthmānī states: wisdom means thoroughly sound and weighty matter; good admonition means effective and moving counsel; and if it comes to debate and discussion, then discussion carried on with courtesy and good manners, in which there is no wounding or hurting of hearts. (تفسیر عثمانی، ص۳۷۲). Tafsīr Maẓharī states:

وجادلهم ؛ المناظرة على وجه لا يتطرق إليه طغيان النفس ولا وسواس للشيطان. (مظهری: ۵/۳۹۰)

And in explaining جاهد الكفار والمنافقين واغلظ عليهم. (التوبة: ۷۳) it is written: when they took to obstinacy at Tabūk — now be firm and stern with them. (Tafsīr ʿUthmānī.) Mawlānā Khālid Sayf Allāh says: while staying within the bounds of good character, firmness and a degree of blunt speech are also necessary. (آسان تفسیر، ص۵۹۲).

In my view واغلظ عليهم means to speak firmly and to answer in a way that silences the opponent. In another place غلاظ carries the sense of stern and severe. The upshot is that insulting their deities and insulting the polytheists should not be understood in the sense of abuse and foul language. Yes, if "insult" means to deny of their deities the attributes that belong to Allah alone, then this is permissible — indeed it has occurred:

إن الذين تدعون من دون الله عباد أمثال لكم، والذين تدعون من دونه لا يستطيعون نصركم ولا أنفسهم ينصرون

That is, it is permissible to state their helplessness. (كذا في تعليق زاد المعاد: ۱۳، للشيخ شعيب الأرناؤوط والشيخ عبد القادر الأرناؤوط)

Sometimes it is reported that the Companions insulted one another; what is meant there is reproach and condemnation of a position, as in Takmilat Fatḥ al-Mulhim in the chapter on the merits of ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him).

As for the objector's argument that ʿUrwah ibn Masʿūd said, "Had I not owed you a favour that I have not yet repaid, I would have answered you," and that this shows that Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) had abused him — the words speak of an answer, not of abuse, and an answer is not confined to abuse. The polytheists said اعل هبل، اعل هبل, and in reply it was said الله أعلي وأجل، الله أعلي وأجل; that is not abuse. The polytheists said إن لنا العزي ولا عزي لكم، and in reply came الله مولانا ولا مولي لكم; that is not abuse. For instance, had ʿUrwah replied, "We love our idols; they — نعوذ باللہ — fulfil our needs; we kiss their lips," that would have been an answer, and there would have been no abuse on either side.

An objection: if, in this humble servant's view, these words of Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) are to be taken in the sense of "go on sucking the lips of al-Lāt, which can bring you no benefit — they are only idols you have made yourselves — while we shall remain lost in the love of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ", then how did all the great scholars take بظر in the sense of the private part? These men were mountains of knowledge and practice and standard-bearers of piety and purity; we are only gleaners in the field of their learning and gatherers of the crumbs from their table, and the sum of our lives is our love and devotion for them.

There are two answers to this objection. (1) When ʿUrwah ibn Masʿūd used improper words about the Companions — that they would leave the Prophet ﷺ alone and run off in all directions — Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (may Allah be pleased with him) became intensely angry, and in that state of anger these words came from his tongue involuntarily; in severe anger such words can sometimes escape a person's tongue without his choosing them. When the Angel of Death came to Mūsā (peace be upon him) to take his soul, Mūsā (peace be upon him) struck him such a blow that his eye came out. Explaining and interpreting this, Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

إن موسى دفعه عن نفسه لما ركب فيه من الحدة. (فتح الباري: ٦/٤۳۲).

Mūsā (peace be upon him) pushed the Angel of Death away from himself because there was a sharpness of temper in his nature — that is, he did so overcome by anger. Fayḍ al-Bārī records:

وفي العيني: إنه كان في طبع موسى عليه السلام حدة،

Commenting on this, Mawlānā Badr-e-ʿĀlam writes:

روي أنه كان إذا غضب اشتعلت قلنسوته. (فيض الباري: ۲/٤۷٦).

That is, because of the intensity of his anger his cap would catch fire. When Mūsā (peace be upon him) came down from Mount Ṭūr and saw the Children of Israel fallen into misguidance, the tablets of the Torah fell from his hand out of the intensity of his anger. ʿAllāmah al-Ālūsī (may Allah have mercy on him) described this state as follows:

والصواب أن يقال إنه عليه السلام لفرط حميته الدينية وشدة غضبه لله تعالى لم يتمالك ولم يتماسك أن وقعت الألواح من يده بدون اختيار فنزل ترك التحفظ منزلة الإلقاء الاختياري فعبر به. (روح المعاني: ۹/٦۷).

That is, because of the intensity of his anger the hands of Mūsā (peace be upon him) were no longer under his control and the tablets fell.

The commentators have given the same interpretation and explanation for his seizing the beard of Hārūn (peace be upon him). The Shia also raise an objection against ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him): how could he deny the passing away of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, until Abū Bakr (may Allah be pleased with him) recited the verse إنك ميت وإنهم ميتون? In answer to this ʿAllāmah al-Ālūsī (may Allah have mercy on him) has written:

والجواب أن ذلك من شدة دهشته بموت قرة عينه وعيون المؤمنين،

That is, ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him) was stunned by the passing of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ; grief robbed him of his senses and these words came from his tongue. The jurists have written that if a man pronounces ṭalāq while beside himself and dazed with extreme anger, his ṭalāq likewise does not take effect. (فتاوی دار العلوم زکریا: ٤/۲۱۳).

If بظر اللات is taken as an insult, this is the fitting explanation. And if abuse and insult are to be counted as jihad with the tongue, as the objector has said, then the students and teachers of that scholar's own madrasah may as well abuse non-Muslims instead of holding khatm-e-khwājagān, publish it abroad, and go on collecting the reward of jihad with the tongue.

Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Ḥajar (may Allah have mercy on him) said in Fatḥ al-Bārī (5/340):

وحمله على ذلك ما أغضبه من نسبة المسلمين،

By this he pointed to the explanation given above; and Ḥāfiẓ al-ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) too pointed to the above explanation at (9/64).

In the fatwas of our elders (may Allah have mercy on them) there is a question and answer whose substance is this: a man has taken the pledge at the hands of a certain shaykh, and in a dream he takes that shaykh's name and says, "So-and-so is the Messenger of Allah." Then, on waking, the very same words keep coming to his tongue involuntarily. The man wants to recite blessings on the Prophet ﷺ, but "So-and-so is the Messenger of Allah" comes out involuntarily. Like other elders (may Allah have mercy on them), Mufti Kifāyatullāh (may Allah have mercy on him) also wrote a detailed answer to this question, and it seems fitting to quote it, since at times, in a state of helplessness and compulsion, or in severe anger, the tongue slips out of control and some improper words come out. Mufti Kifāyatullāh writes in Kifāyat al-Muftī (1/63):

As for the second incident, which took place while awake: the person concerned states that, ashamed and distressed at his lapse in the dream, he wants to make up for it by reciting blessings on the Prophet ﷺ, and he does recite them; but in these too, instead of the blessed name of the Prophet ﷺ, the name of Zayd comes out of his mouth. He was aware of this mistake, yet he says that he had no control over his tongue, that he was helpless and compelled. Two points about this incident need to be clarified.

First: is such helplessness and compulsion — that the tongue is not under control while a man is awake — possible and conceivable without there being intoxication, insanity, coercion, or feeble-mindedness? For the question does not mention that any of these was present, so it appears that none of these causes of helplessness existed. Second: if such helplessness and compulsion are possible and conceivable, will the statement of the person concerned be accepted as true or not?

The answer to the first point is that there are a great many causes of helplessness; they are not confined to intoxication, insanity, coercion, and feeble-mindedness. The fact that the books of legal theory and law restrict themselves to mentioning these causes is not based on definition and limitation; they have been mentioned as the most common causes. The proof of this is that several books of fiqh state explicitly that whenever the intellect is overpowered, for whatever reason, that state of being overpowered lifts legal responsibility.

إلا في بعض المواقع فيكون الزجر مقصوداً.

Al-Fatāwā al-ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

من أصابه برسام أو أطعم شيئاً فذهب عقله فهذي فارتد لم يكن ذلك ارتداداً وكذا لو كان معتوهاً أو موسوساً أو مغلوبا على عقله بوجه من الوجوه فهو على هذا كذا في السراج الوهاج. (هنديه مطبوعة مصر صفحه ۸۲ ج ۲).

In this passage the word موسوس and the phrase بوجہ من الوجوہ deserve attention. In the case asked about, however, there is no explicit statement of loss of awareness or loss of reason, nor do I regard it as correct, on the basis of the facts mentioned, to hold that awareness was lost; for the person concerned states that he was conscious of his mistake even as he spoke, and pleads only helplessness, compulsion, and his tongue not being under control. I therefore regard only this much as correct: that in spite of awareness and understanding he utters a statement of disbelief, but does so helplessly and under compulsion, and even at the very moment of speaking he regards it as a mistake and contrary to his belief, and after he has finished speaking he is remorseful about it, distressed, and weeps. Besides the known causes (intoxication, insanity, coercion, feeble-mindedness), old age, illness, calamity, overwhelming joy, overwhelming grief, excessive love and the like can also become causes of loss of reason or loss of choice; and where there is loss of reason or loss of choice, a ruling of apostasy cannot be established.

(2) A narration of Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim reports from the Prophet ﷺ that Allah is even more pleased at the repentance of His sinful servant than a traveller whose camel, loaded with his provisions, is lost in the wilderness; after searching and searching he despairs, sits down resigned to death, and in that state falls asleep; a little later he opens his eyes and sees his camel standing beside him with all his provisions. In the same narration the Prophet ﷺ says that in the height of his joy these words slip from the man's tongue:

أنت عبدي وأناْ ربك.

That is, he cried out before Allah: "You are my servant and I am your Lord." The Messenger of Allah ﷺ says:

خطا من شدة الفرح

— that is, these words escaped him by mistake because of the intensity of his joy.

This hadith proves explicitly that overwhelming joy too becomes a cause of loss of choice; and since the Prophet ﷺ did not go on to say that this utterance of words of disbelief, which occurred by mistake, was disbelief and destroyed faith, his silence shows that uttering words of disbelief by mistake does not establish apostasy.

(3) In the account of the treaty of Ḥudaybiyah narrated in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and elsewhere, it is mentioned that when the treaty was concluded the Prophet ﷺ ordered the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) to shave their heads and slaughter their sacrificial animals. Out of the intensity of their grief and sorrow at not being able to reach the Kaʿbah, contrary to their longing and hope, the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) were so beside themselves that although the Prophet ﷺ gave the command a second and a third time, not one of them carried out his noble order. Grieved, he went into the tent to Umm Salamah (may Allah be pleased with her). She asked the reason for his distress and submitted that he should go outside, shave his own head, and say nothing to anyone. He came out, called the barber, and had his head shaved. When the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) saw this, they came to their senses and at once began shaving one another's heads. This narration proves that overwhelming grief too becomes a cause of loss of choice, for one cannot even entertain the thought that the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) deliberately and by choice failed to carry out the command of the Prophet ﷺ.
