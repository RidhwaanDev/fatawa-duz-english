### TITLE
Answer to the objection raised against Tafsīr ʿUthmānī's explanation of wa iyyāka nastaʿīn

### QUESTION
Objection: Shāh ʿAbd al-Qādir Dihlawī (may Allah have mercy on him) translated "إياك نعبد وإياك نستعين" thus: "You alone do we worship, and from You alone do we seek help." (Mūḍiḥ al-Qurʾān.) Explaining this, it is written: from this noble verse we learn that in reality it is not permissible to seek help from anyone besides His pure Being. Yes, if a person seeks outward help from some accepted servant of Allah, regarding him merely as a channel of the divine mercy and as having no independent power, then this is permissible, because in reality this seeking of help is a seeking of help from Allah Most High alone. (Tafsīr ʿUthmānī, p. 2.)

Objectors raise this objection against this passage of Ḥaḍrat Shaykh al-Hind: if by "outward help" is meant help that is above the ordinary means, then such seeking of help is exclusive to Allah Most High, so how can it be permissible to seek it from other than Allah? And if by "outward help" is meant asking someone to make supplication, then what need is there to add the qualification of "having no independent power," since one who supplicates and asks is in any case not independent? What is the solution to this difficulty?

### ANSWER
Sometimes the common people, in spite of the friends of Allah and the pious elders being needy and being mere supplicants, regard them as possessing power, believing that Allah Most High cannot reject their supplication; and some ignorant people, in spite of their neediness, regard them as independent — just as a king cannot reject the word of certain particular ministers, since they would bring the government down, or as a lover cannot reject the word of his beloved, since the beloved would be displeased. So Shaykh al-Hind (may Allah have mercy on him) cut away the root of every kind of polytheism in this passage: if someone asks a pious elder to make supplication for him and holds the belief that his supplication is like an intercession that compels, and that Allah Most High is, as it were, helpless and constrained to accept his supplication, then this too is a polytheistic belief.

Note how Shāh ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz (may Allah have mercy on him) sets out the details of "independent" and "not independent but merely a channel":

One kind is that a person seeks aid in this manner even from living men of religion, and that manner is this: that he considers that the supplication of these elders is quickly accepted and is accepted in most cases, and with this thought he makes them a channel for requesting his own needs, and considers only that these elders are merely a channel and stand in the position of an instrument; and he entertains no other thought besides this — Allah forbid — that these elders are absolutely powerful. Rather he regards them merely as being in the position of a pair of spectacles. This is undoubtedly permissible.

The second kind is that a person seeks his desire from the men of religion in an independent manner, and considers that they have independent power either to have his desire fulfilled or to fulfil it themselves, and believes that these elders hold such a rank of nearness to the Real (Most High) that they can make the plan of Allah Most High subject to their own will. And this is precisely the manner in which the common people seek aid, and this manner is sheer polytheism. (Fatāwā ʿAzīzī, pp. 180 and 175, published by Saʿīd Kampanī.)

Shāh Ismāʿīl Shahīd (may Allah have mercy on him) has described three kinds of intercession:

1. Intercession by way of standing: this means that a king, in order to save the system of his kingdom, accepts against his own will the intercession and recommendation of some minister of standing and awe, because if he did not accept it, disorder and disturbance would arise in the system of the kingdom. This kind of intercession is negated and non-existent.

2. Intercession by way of love: that a person accepts the recommendation of some beloved one against his own will, lest the beloved be displeased; so the intercession is accepted because of the heart's constraint. This kind of intercession too is non-existent.

3. The third kind is intercession by permission: Allah Most High, of His own will, will grant permission to some prophet or friend of Allah to intercede. This kind of intercession is established. (Taqwiyat al-Īmān, pp. 34–40, chapter on polytheism in the exercise of control.)

See further: (Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah, p. 141; Imdād al-Muftīn, p. 181; Kifāyat al-Muftī: 1/170; and Tafsīr Jawāhir al-Qurʾān: 1/10.)

We have heard that the Saudi government has placed a ban on the printing and distribution of Tafsīr ʿUthmānī because of the exegesis and explanation of this noble verse. This is the result of their lack of reflection; otherwise there is in it only an indication that one particular kind of supplication falls under polytheism.
