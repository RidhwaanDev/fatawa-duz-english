### TITLE
Taking shares in a company that runs hotels

### QUESTION
There is a company engaged in the business of building and running hotels. Some of the hotels are involved in selling liquor or in other wrongs, but roughly ninety per cent or more of the income comes from room rentals and the like, and the company is not in debt either. Further, if a shareholder encourages someone else to take shares in the company, he receives a percentage of the amount by way of commission. Is there room for taking shares in such a company or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question the greater part of the company's income comes from lawful sources, and for that reason there is room for taking shares in it. One should, however, raise one's voice at the annual meeting against the wrongs that occur.

Consider what Islām awr Jadīd Muʿāshī Masāʾil states:

Dealing in the shares of a company is acceptable subject to the following conditions:

(1) The company's principal business must not be contrary to the sharia — such as providing financial services on the basis of interest, that is, the shares of banks and insurance companies, or the shares of companies involved in some other impermissible business, such as those that produce or sell liquor, pork or unlawful meat, or that are involved in gambling, night-club activities, indecency and so on. Taking shares in such companies, and dealing with them, is impermissible.

(2) If the company's principal business is lawful — the automobile or textile business, for example — but the company places its surplus capital in interest-bearing accounts or takes interest-bearing loans, then the shareholder must express his disapproval of dealings of that kind. The better way of doing this is to raise his voice against such activities at the company's annual general meeting.

(3) If some income obtained from interest-bearing accounts is included in the company's income, then that proportion of the profit paid to the shareholder should be given away in charity, and the shareholder should not benefit from it himself. For example, if five per cent of the company's total profit was obtained from interest-bearing accounts, then five per cent of the profit should be given away in charity. (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ۵/۱٦٤) ۔
