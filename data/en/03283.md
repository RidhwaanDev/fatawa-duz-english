### TITLE
Ablution and the ritual bath when a special substance has been applied to the hair

### QUESTION
Nowadays a special kind of substance is used to treat the hair, which sets it firm; in English it is called (Brasil Cacau Keratin). It generally remains for up to three months, and there is no easy way of removing it. What, then, is the ruling regarding the purity, ablution, ritual bath, prayer and so on of those who use it? Is its use permissible or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, if a layer forms from this substance like a plaster, then according to the well-known view held by the majority of the scholars it is a barrier to ablution and the ritual bath; ablution and the bath will therefore not be valid without removing it, and for that reason the prayer too will not be valid. According to some scholars, however, if there is hardship in removing it, the ablution and the bath will be valid; its ruling is like that of nail polish. But if no layer like a plaster forms and it is rather like the colour of henna, then in no one's view is its removal necessary. In any case, one ought to avoid using such doubtful things.

See what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

ولا يمنع ما علي ظفر صباغ ولا طعام بين أسنانه أو في سنه المجوف به يفتى. وقيل: إن صلبا منع وهو الأصح. وفي رد المحتار: قوله وإن صلبا، أي إن كان ممضوغاً مضغاً متأكداً بحيث تداخلت أجزاؤه وصار له لزوجة وعلاكة كالعجين شرح المنية، قوله وهو الأصح صرح به في شرح المنية وقال: لامتناع نفوذ الماء مع عدم الضرورة والحرج. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۱/۱۵٤، سعيد).

Fatāwā Hindiyyah states:

والدرن اليابس في الأنف يمنع تمام الغسل كذا في الزاهدي، والعجين في الظفر يمنع تمام الاغتسال والوسخ والدرن لا يمنع

والتراب والطين في الظفر لا يمنع والصرام والصباغ ما في ظفرهما يمنع تمام الاغتسال وقيل كل ذلك يجزيهم للحرج والضرورة ومواضع الضرورة مستثناة عن قواعد الشرع كذا في الظهيرية. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱۳).
قال في البحر: ولو لصق بأصل ظفره طين يابس وبقي قدر رأس إبرة من موضع الغسل لم يجز. (البحر الرائق: ۱/۱٤، ط: دار المعرفة، وفتح القدير: ۱/۱٦، دار الفكر).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

When something is present that prevents water from reaching the body, ablution and the ritual bath are not valid; if even a hair's breadth of the area remains dry, the ablution and the bath will not be valid. The jurists (may Allah have mercy on them) declared dried kneaded dough to be a barrier to the validity of ablution, even though it is not as hard as nail polish and there is a genuine need for it — so how can nail polish be allowed? Whatever prayers have been performed with nail polish applied must be repeated, and along with that one should also repent and seek forgiveness. (احسن الفتاوی: ۲/۲٦، ط: سعید) ۔

Jadīd Fiqhī Masāʾil states:

To apply, without a genuine need, something that does not let water reach the body is an obstacle to the validity of ablution. The ablution will be valid only when it is scraped off. Paints of this kind, which women apply, are not a necessity; they are mere adornment. Therefore, when performing ablution, it will be necessary to scrape them off and make the water reach the surface beneath, as is understood from this passage of Fatāwā ʿĀlamgīriyyah:

أو لزق بأصل ظفره طين يابس أو رطب لم يجز. (
جدید فقہی مسائل: ۱/۸۷، ط: نعیمیہ) ۔ مزید ملاحظہ ہو : (کتاب المسائل: ۱/۱۷۷، ط: کتب خانہ نعیمیہ) ۔

According to some scholars, if there is hardship in removing it, it is not a barrier. Consider the following. Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah states:

The renowned scholars of the present age hold that nail polish is not permissible, because with nail polish the body of the nail becomes covered and it is no longer possible for water to reach it in ablution and the bath; it is therefore a barrier to ablution and the bath, just like kneaded dough. According to certain other scholars, however, if nail polish is regarded as an adornment of a woman, then in such a case, if there is no difficulty in removing it, its removal will be necessary for ablution and the bath; and if there is hardship in removing it but no layer has formed from it, then its ruling will be like that of henna; and where a layer has formed and there is hardship in removing it, then, on account of that hardship, making the water reach the nail is not necessary. (فتاوی حقانیہ: ۲/۵۰۱) ۔

Fatāwā Farīdiyyah records:

If nail polish cannot be removed without a knife or some similar instrument, then, on account of the hardship, it will not be a barrier to ablution and the bath. In another place it states: The cautious course lies in removing it, though there is room for leniency even if it remains. (فتاوی فریدیہ: ۲/۵۲ و ۵٤) ۔

Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi accepts the validity of ablution and the bath in a case of genuine constraint. Consider the following:

Nail polish is a liquid substance which sets after being applied to the nails and prevents water from reaching them; removing it is therefore necessary for ablution and the bath, and while it is present the ablution and the bath will not be valid. Nowadays a chemical is also available in the market with which nail polish is easily cleaned off. If, however, no such chemical by which the nail polish could be cleaned off is available at the time, and despite one's efforts the colour is not coming off, while on the other hand there is a danger of the prayer being missed and removing it is not possible at that moment, then in such a case, on account of the constraint, the ablution and the bath will be valid despite the nail polish. (فتاوی دار العلوم کراچی: ۱/۵۱۹) ۔

According to most of the scholars, however, it is a barrier to ablution and the bath. One should therefore act upon the more cautious view, so that this important obligation of worship is discharged from one's responsibility with certainty.

قال العلامة السرخسي رحمه الله في المبسوط: وفي العبادات يؤخذ بالاحتياط. (۱/٤۵۲، ط: دار الفكر، و۳۰/۱۹٤).
وللاستزادة انظر: الفتاوى الهندية ۱/۱۲٤، والبحر الرائق: ۱/۳۲، دار المعرفة، واللباب في شرح الكتاب: ۱/۲۹، ط: دار الكتاب العربي، وفتاوى الشامي: ۲/۳٦٦، سعيد، وحاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح، ۱۵۲، ط؛ مصر، والعناية شرح الهداية).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Saʿīd Aḥmad Pālanpūrī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes:

It is the way of Imam al-Aʿẓam (may Allah have mercy on him) that in the chapter of acts of worship he rests the ruling upon that text in which greater caution lies; and here caution lies in acting in accordance with the opinion of the majority. (تحفۃ الالمعی: ۱/٤٤۰، ط: زمزم، بتغیر یسیر) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
