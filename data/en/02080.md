### TITLE
Winning money through a bank draw

### QUESTION
A certain bank in South Africa has devised a new kind of account. It pays no interest on this account; instead, whoever opens the account is automatically entered into a competition and stands a chance of winning one million rand. If someone wins, will it be permissible for him to take that money or not?

### ANSWER
If the sum deposited is certain to remain intact and an extra sum is received along with it, then in the view of some scholars this is interest; and if the sum deposited can be lost, then it is plain gambling. Both are unlawful and impermissible in the sight of the sacred law.

قال الله تعالى: ﴿يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا تأكلوا الربا أضعافاً مضاعفة، واتقوا الله، لعلكم تفلحون﴾ (سورة آل عمران، الآية: ۱۳۱).

Fatāwā Shāmī states:

وسمي القمار قماراً لأن كل واحد من المقامرين ممن يجوز أن يذهب ماله إلى صاحبه، ويجوز أن يستفيد مال صاحبه، وهو حرام بالنص. (رد المحتار: ٦/٤۰۳، كتاب الحظر والإباحة، سعيد).

In Islām awr Jadīd Maʿāshī Masāʾil, Ḥaḍrat Muftī Taqī Ṣāḥib (may his shade endure) says:

Some scholars have held prize bonds to be permissible, taking the view that here there is no suspension of ownership upon a risk (تعلیق التملیک علی الخطر), since whatever money a person has paid in he receives back in every case; so this is not gambling and therefore becomes permissible. This view, however, is not correct. Although gambling as such is not present here, interest is: for if a man's name comes out in the draw, then in return for the hundred rupees he paid, for example, he will receive one hundred thousand one hundred rupees.

Some have raised the doubt that this is not interest either, because when they took the bond no agreement for a stipulated increase was made with them.

The answer to this doubt is that the increase is stipulated with the whole body of debtors. Although it is not stated explicitly that any profit will be paid on it, it is done in practice, and that practice is continuous and customary: when a man takes back his loan, the government gives him something more. So although there was no stipulation in words, it falls under "المعروف کالمشروط" ۔ (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ٤/۱۱ ۔ ۷۸) ۔

Certain other scholars, however, say that this money is not interest, because interest has been ruled out; the prize that was received is simply a prize and not interest. The rule

"كل قرض جر نفعاً فهو ربا"

is to be understood of a stipulated benefit, and here no condition was laid down that the prize would come to me in particular; indeed not everyone receives this prize, so it is not stipulated. Under the rule kull qarḍ jarra nafʿan the words إذا كان مشروطاً are written.

See what ʿAllāmah Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) states:

و في الذخيرة: إن لم يكن النفع مشروطاً في القرض فعلى قول الكرخي رحمه الله لا بأس به. (فتاوى الشامي: ۵/۱٦٦، مطلب كل قرض جر نفعاً حرام، سعيد).

قال الجرجاني رحمه الله في التعريفات: الربا: هو في اللغة: الزيادة، وفي الشرع: هو فضل خالٍ عن عوض شرط لأحد العاقدين. (التعريفات، ص ۱۱۲، بيروت).

وفي الهداية: لأن الربا: هو الفضل المستحق لأحد المتعاقدين في المعاوضة الخالي عن عوض شرط فيه. (الهداية: ۳/۷۸، باب الربا).

Ḥaḍrat Muftī Niẓām al-Dīn Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

The substance of the question is this: a scheme is running here at the post office by which people deposit one pound, two pounds or five pounds and are given a numbered receipt. No interest is paid on it, but every month certain numbers are published in the newspapers, and for one pound a hundred pounds is received. Is it permissible to take the extra sum or not, given that the whole of the sum deposited is returned?

The substance of the answer is that taking this extra sum is permissible. For at the time of depositing the money the agreement is that no extra sum will be given on it at the time of repayment and that the transaction will be free of interest. Nor is there any certainty of receiving an increase, nor can it be demanded, nor does everyone receive an increase. Rather, the department simply announces, at its own discretion and purely according to its own rules, that an extra sum will be given for certain numbers, and gives it only to those whose names come out. For this reason this increase is not an instance of jarra nafʿan, and in the sacred law this increase will not be called interest.

If someone adopts the way of God-fearingness, that is another matter and is better. God-fearingness here would be that he should not leave the money with that department but withdraw it and spend it on the poor and needy, or on some good work or public welfare. (منتخباتِ نظام الفتاوی: ۱/۱۹٤ ۔ ۱۹۵) ۔

In sum, although some scholars have not included this under interest, it is better to keep away from a transaction of this kind.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
