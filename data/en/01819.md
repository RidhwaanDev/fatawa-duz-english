### TITLE
Laying a drainage pipe under a mosque

### QUESTION
In a village, rainwater and the waste water from the houses used to flow across one man's land and fall into a large drain. That man built a wall and a room on his land and blocked the water off, and he also won the court case. Adjoining this land there is now part of a mosque where the prayer is performed in congregation. Some people are asking that a pipe be laid in the lower part of the mosque and a floor be laid over it, so that the water will keep draining away underground, no harm will come to the mosque above, and an outlet for the water will be provided. What is the ruling of the sharia on this?

### ANSWER
It is not permissible at all to lay a pipe in the lower part of the mosque and then put a floor over it. Once a place becomes a mosque, its character as a mosque does not come to an end until the Day of Judgement; and a mosque extends downwards to the depths of the earth and upwards to the sky. The sharia therefore gives no permission for anything that is contrary to the respect due to a mosque.

Consider what ʿĀlamgīriyyah states:

قيم المسجد لا يجوز له أن يبني حوانيت في حد المسجد أو في فناء ه لأن المسجد إذا جعل حانوتاً ومكاناً تسقط حرمته وهذا لا يجوز والفناء تبع المسجد فيكون حكمه حكم المسجد كذا في محيط السرخسي. (الفتاوى الهندية: ۲/٤٦۲).

Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

لو تمت المسجدية ثم أراد البناء منع ولو قال عنيت ذلك لم يصدق تاتارخانية، فإذا كان هذا في الواقف فكيف بغيره فيجب هدمه ولو على جدار المسجد، ولا يجوز أخذ الأجرة منه ولا أن يجعل شيئاً منه مستغلاً ولا سكني، بزازية. (الدر المختار: ٤/۳۵۸، سعيد. وفتاوى قاضيخان: على هامش الهندية: ۳/۲۹۳).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

The word "courtyard" is used of a mosque in two senses. In the first, it refers to that uncovered part of the mosque which is prepared for prayer — that is, it is made for performing the prayer in congregation — but is left open without a roof. In the second, it is also used of that part which is left as bare ground or as a paved surface beyond the roofed and unroofed area prepared for prayer, but which is not made for performing prayer and congregation.

In the first sense the courtyard is a part of the mosque itself and the rulings of the mosque apply to it. It is not permissible to build a water tank or a channel for ablution in it, because a place that has once become a mosque and been set aside for prayer cannot then be put to any other use. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 3/182, Kitāb al-Ṣalāh, Dār al-Ishāʿat).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
