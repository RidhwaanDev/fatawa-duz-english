### TITLE
Reinforcing a grave when there is a danger of it collapsing

### QUESTION
If there is a fear that a grave may collapse, may bricks and the like be placed around it to make it firm, or not?

### ANSWER
If the grave itself is left as plain earth in the middle and bricks are placed around it for fear of its collapsing, that is correct. Otherwise, the prohibition of building up the grave itself in solid masonry is established in the hadiths, so it must be avoided.

Consider what Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim states:

عن جابر رضي الله عنه قال: نهى رسول الله ﷺ أن يجصص القبر وأن يقعد وأن يبني على ه. (رواه مسلم: ۱/۳۱۲. والترمذي: ۱/۲۰۳)

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

Building of every kind over a grave is haram if it is done for adornment, and strictly disliked (makrūh taḥrīmī) if it is done to make the grave firm; and as regards sin, what is strictly disliked stands on a par with what is haram. A boundary wall — even one a single brick high — is plainly a construction, and so is a raised platform; indeed, even heaping on more earth than the grave's own earth comes under construction. (احسن الفتاوی: ٤/۱۸۹)

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

Building a grave up in solid masonry on all four sides is permissible, provided that alongside the body of the deceased it stays plain earth from bottom to top; that is, if the body of the deceased remains surrounded by earth on all four sides and the masonry lies beyond that, there is no harm in it. (کفایت المفتی ٤/۵۰، فصل چہارم قبر و دفن، دار الاشاعت) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
