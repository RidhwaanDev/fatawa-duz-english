### TITLE
An answer to a doubt about ﴿أفلا يتدبرون القرآن﴾

### QUESTION
Consider the noble verse

﴿أفلا يتدبرون القرآن ولو كان من عند غير الله لوجدوا فيه اختلافاً كثيرا﴾ (سورة النساء الآية ۸۲)

If the word "much" is taken as a restriction here, would the meaning not be that a slight discrepancy does exist in the Noble Qur'an, just as is the case with the Torah and the Gospel in the hands of the Jews and Christians after they were distorted and altered?

### ANSWER
It is not necessary that every qualifying word in every place is meant to exclude what falls outside it; in many places the qualification is merely incidental. Take, for instance, the words of Allah:

﴿لا تأكلوا الربوا أضعافا مضاعفة﴾

This does not mean that taking a small amount of interest is permissible; the wording simply reflects what people commonly did. Sometimes, too, the purpose of adding such a qualification is to bring out how much worse the act is: how evil it is that you take interest, and take it doubled and redoubled! It is like saying, "Do you swear at people inside the mosque?" That does not mean that swearing outside the mosque is permissible; it is said to bring out how much worse the act is. The same applies to:

﴿لا تكرهوا فتىتكم على البغاء إن أردن تحصنا﴾

That is, the slave girls themselves wish to remain chaste, and even so you force them into immorality — how vile that is! So this noble verse should be understood in the same way. It does not mean that a slight discrepancy exists; it means only that if the Qur'an were from someone other than Allah, there would be a great deal of discrepancy in it and its whole order would fall apart.

An easy illustration can be drawn from our own way of speaking. Suppose a wealthy man's son is foolish and inexperienced, with no ability to tell the genuine from the counterfeit. People say of him, "If the son becomes the chief after his father, great disorder will spread through the country." This does not mean that there is already some disorder at present. It merely gives news of what is to come: that if the boy becomes the ruler, great disorder will break out — the present state of affairs being left out of account.

The verse also carries this sense: if the Qur'an were from someone else and not from Allah, then — since it is Allah's way that the speech of a false claimant to prophethood is full of contradictions and inconsistencies, as the writings of Ghulām Aḥmad Qādiyānī are full of contradictions — it would be the same here. Further, if the Qur'an were from other than Allah and were to foretell future events, its predictions would have turned out different from what actually happened, since its author would not know the unseen. The mention of "much discrepancy", then, is a statement of what would actually be the case; it is not a qualification meant to exclude anything.
