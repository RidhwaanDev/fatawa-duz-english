### TITLE
The ruling on a WhatsApp group for conveying reward

### QUESTION
Some time ago a man's parents and his brother and sister passed away in an accident. He formed a group on WhatsApp in which family members and close relatives took part of their own accord. In this group one juzʾ each is distributed to every participant every month, and in this way two completions of the Noble Qurʾān take place each month and three in Ramaḍān; then we make the intention of conveying the reward to our parents and to all the deceased. A few days ago someone told us that this practice is an innovation, that it is showing off, and that it is not part of the religion. Now the request to you is that you inform us of the Sharīʿah ruling. بینوا بالبرہان توجروا عند الرحمن ۔

### ANSWER
The Ahl al-Sunnah wa al-Jamāʿah are agreed that a person may give the reward of his bodily and financial acts of worship, of his recitation of the Qurʾān and so forth, to someone deceased or living, and that Allah Most High conveys the reward so given. Further, the superior and better way of conveying reward is that each person should recite, or give charity, individually and in his own capacity, and convey the reward. Yes, the scholars have also permitted collective Qurʾān recitation from time to time, subject to several conditions:

1. It should not be regarded as binding and necessary.
2. Money and the like should not be given to those who recite the Qurʾān, nor should food be served in return for it.
3. Pride, ostentation and fame should not be the object.
4. It should not be for the sake of arranging the third day, the fortieth day, the annual anniversary and so forth.
5. There should be no mingling of men and women.

On this basis, subject to the conditions mentioned, the distribution of the parts on a WhatsApp group, the completion of the Noble Qurʾān and the conveying of its reward are permissible and correct. Moreover, no collective recitation takes place on WhatsApp; each person may recite at his own convenience, and the distribution of the parts is for the sake of ease, with no compulsion upon anyone. This distribution is also for the reason that no part should be repeated and that the Qurʾān should be completed in order. In any case, this distribution is for the sake of ease, and it is not correct to call it an innovation. Consider the following pieces of evidence:

Ḥaḍrat ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd

رضي الله عنه

used to deliver an admonition every Thursday, and this fixing of a day was not an innovation; rather it was on account of ease.

عن أبي وائل قال: كان عبد الله يذكر في كل خميس فقال له رجل: يا أبا عبد الرحمن لوددت أنك ذكرتنا كل يوم، قال: أما أنه يمنعني من أني أكره أن أملكم وأني أتخولكم بالموعظة كما كان النبي ﷺ يتخولنا بها مخافة السآمة علينا. (رواه البخاري، رقم: ۷۰، باب من جعل لا هل العلم أياما معلومة).

Ḥaḍrat Shaykh (may Allah have mercy on him) says that fixing times, lessons and hours in teaching and instruction is not an innovation, because it is for the sake of ease; yes, if someone were to intend greater reward on account of it, then that would be an innovation.

Consider what is in the Ḥāshiyah of al-Lāmiʿ:

فإن البدعة هو التعيين الذي يعد فيه ثواب وأجر خاص بهذا المعين وأما التعيين لساعات الدروس مثلاً فلا يعده أحد أجراً وثواباً. (حاشية لامع الدراري: ۱/٤۷، ط: سعيد).

The Pole of Poles, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Rashīd Aḥmad Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him), says:

Conveying reward is valid on any day and is a cause of reward; no date or time is fixed for it by the Sharīʿah, and the day of birth and the day of death are also valid. So if one does not regard any particular day as necessary but treats it like any other day for conveying reward, and no harm comes to the common people through such conveying of reward, then there is no objection to it; it is valid in the view of all. (Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah, p. 118, published by Islāmī Kutub, Karachi).

It is also mentioned: If people gather without fixing a day and complete the Qurʾān, or recite the kalimah ṭayyibah and convey its reward, this is permissible in the view of most scholars. (Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah, p. 137).

In another place he writes concerning the completion of Bukhārī Sharīf:

Question: Is having Bukhārī Sharīf completed at a time of some calamity established from the first three generations or not, and is it an innovation or not? Answer: Bukhārī Sharīf had not been compiled in the first three generations; but its completion is correct, since supplication is accepted after the mention of good, and its basis is established from the Sharīʿah, so it is not an innovation. And that is all. Rashīd Aḥmad, may he be pardoned. (Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah, p. 147).

It should be borne in mind that the fixing of a day that is forbidden means that one regards the fixing as an act of reward, a sunnah, or as necessary; otherwise, fixing a day for the sake of ease is not forbidden. Consider what Ḥaḍrat says:

The restrictions attached to a mawlid gathering, to conveying reward and the like are in reality permissible; if someone regards them as a sunnah or as necessary, then it is an innovation and a transgression, and without that they are in the category of the permissible. Yes, we have ascertained from the habit of the common people that they regard these things as necessary and as sunnah, and for that reason we forbid them. (Adapted from Fatāwā Rashīdiyyah, p. 131).

(Likewise in Kifāyat al-Muftī: 4/127, Dār al-Ishāʿat; and Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah: 3/86, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

For further detail on this issue, see: (Dhikr-i Ijtimāʿī wa Jahrī Sharīʿat ke Āʾīnah Meṉ, pp. 249–291).

In Siyar Aʿlām al-Nubalāʾ, Imām Dhahabī (may Allah have mercy on him) has related from the pious predecessors a great many practices of this kind, which fall into the category of things now abandoned but which are not forbidden:

(1) Ḥaḍrat Abū al-Dardāʾ (may Allah Most High be pleased with him) used to recite a hundred thousand tasbīḥs daily. (2/348).

(2) Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah Most High be pleased with him) used to fast on the first three days of the month. (2/609).

(3) Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah Most High be pleased with him) used to recite twelve thousand tasbīḥs daily. (2/610).

(4) Ḥaḍrat Abū Hurayrah (may Allah Most High be pleased with him) had a thread in which there were twelve thousand knots; before sleeping he would recite tasbīḥs upon them. (2/623).

(5) ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Abī Laylā used to recite the Noble Qurʾān after the morning prayer until sunrise. (4/265).

(6) ʿAlī ibn Ḥusayn Zayn al-ʿĀbidīn (may Allah have mercy on him) used to perform one thousand rakʿahs in twenty-four hours. (4/392).

(7) Ṭāwūs ibn Kaysān (may Allah have mercy on him), together with his students and associates, used to set aside the time after ʿAṣr until Maghrib for supplication. (5/48).

(8) ʿAlī ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) used to perform one thousand rakʿahs daily. (5/253).

(9) Imām Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal (may Allah have mercy on him) used to perform three hundred rakʿahs by day and night; when the trials had weakened him, he would then perform one hundred and fifty rakʿahs. (5/212).

(10) Baqī ibn Makhlad (may Allah have mercy on him) used to complete the Noble Qurʾān in thirteen rakʿahs, would perform a hundred rakʿahs during the day, and used to fast perpetually. (13/292).

(11) Junayd ibn Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him) used to perform three hundred rakʿahs daily and would recite several thousand tasbīḥs. (14/67).

(12) ʿAllāmah Ibn al-Qayyim (may Allah have mercy on him) has written concerning ʿAllāmah Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) that ʿAllāmah Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) used to say that whoever recites يا حي يا قيوم، لا إله إلا أنت forty times daily and regularly between the sunnah and the farḍ of Fajr, Allah Most High will keep his heart flourishing and fresh on account of these words. (Madārij al-Sālikīn: 3/264).

All of these are practices now abandoned; those who made them their regular practice observed them personally, but did not object to one who did not do so — hence no one called them an innovation.

Ḥaḍrat Sahāranpūrī (may Allah have mercy on him) said: Insistence in the sense of holding something binding is an innovation, while constancy without holding it binding is recommended. (Barāhīn-i Qāṭiʿah, p. 67). Further detail on this issue is mentioned in the treatise of Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥayy Lakhnawī (may Allah have mercy on him),

إقامة الحجة على أن الإكثار في التعبد ليس ببدعة

and it may be consulted there. (Taken from Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 7/807).

The upshot of the entire discussion is that if the distribution and allocation of the parts on WhatsApp is for the sake of ease, we will not call it an innovation. Yes, if people regard it as binding and necessary, or accept that there is greater reward in it, then it will be an innovation — although the matter is not so. For the evidence relating to collective Qurʾān recitation and the conveying of reward, see: (Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Zakariyyā: 8/680, published by Zamzam).

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
