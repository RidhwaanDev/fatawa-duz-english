### TITLE
The rules governing the broadcasting of news on the radio

### QUESTION
What are the principles and rules of the Sacred Law governing the news items that are broadcast on the radio?

### ANSWER
On this subject the Ḥakīm al-Ummat, Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him), wrote an essay entitled "Akhbār Bīnī" (Newspaper Reading), in which he pointed out the joyless sins of newspaper reading and, along with that, collected a number of principles and rules of the Sacred Law by adhering to which these evils can be removed from newspapers. These same principles and rules will therefore apply to the radio as well:

The most comprehensive and exclusive principle in this connection is that whenever a person intends to write or broadcast something, he should first put the question to himself: is it permissible for me to write this or not? If it is established as permissible, he should go forward; and if he is not himself expert in the rulings of the Sacred Law, then it is necessary to put the question to someone who is. This is a summary legal rule of the Sacred Law which should be kept in view by every Muslim in every kind of writing. After this we set out the details under a number of headings:

(1) A report that consists of censure of a person or of the misfortunes that have befallen him should on no account be published until sufficient proof of it is obtained by a legally recognised proof of the Sacred Law, for it is not permissible to level a false accusation or to fabricate a charge even against an unbeliever.

(2) It is also necessary to remember that in this matter the spread of a rumour, or the fact that some newspaper has printed it, is by no means sufficient as a legally recognised proof; rather, testimony conforming to the Sacred Law is necessary. Hundreds of experiences with all the newspapers of the present day have made it undeniable that many articles and reports are published in newspapers of which the poor man in whose name they are published has no knowledge whatsoever. Sometimes this is done deliberately, and sometimes the mistake occurs inadvertently. Therefore, if some article or report is quoted in a newspaper on the authority of a person, it cannot be regarded as established in the Sacred Law. If, however, the report does not consist of censure of anyone, or of harm to him or fault-finding with him, then even this weak proof is sufficient and it may be quoted and published.

(3) Even if a person's fault or an instance of his sin is established by a legally recognised proof, it is still not permissible to publicise it. Even then the Islamic duty is that he should be admonished in private out of goodwill; if he does not accept the admonition and you have the power, then he should be stopped by force; otherwise, having conveyed the word of truth, you are discharged of your duty. Experience proves that publicising it and disgracing him, far from being beneficial, is always harmful. It is for this reason that the Mercy to the Worlds ﷺ emphasised in numerous hadiths that if some fault or sin of a Muslim brother becomes established, one should not disgrace him but should rather cover it over and admonish him privately, for this is the way that has proved more effective and more beneficial.

(4) If, however, such a fault or sin of a Muslim is established by a legally recognised proof as causes harm to a person himself, so that he is a wronged party in the matter, then he may publicise the wrongdoer's misdeed openly. Concerning this Allah Most High has said:

﴿لايحب الله الجهر بالسوء من القول إلا من ظلم﴾

that is, Allah Most High does not like evil to be spoken of openly, except by one who has been wronged (for he may make the wrongdoer's oppression known). (Rūḥ al-Maʿānī.) But in such a case it is better that, instead of making a general announcement and publishing it abroad, he should state it only before those people who are able to redress his grievance.

(5) If some erroneous article calling for refutation is printed in a newspaper in a person's name, then in reply it is enough to say only that such-and-such a newspaper has written this and that the answer to it is such-and-such. No attack should be made upon that person himself, because it has not yet been established by any legally recognised proof that the article really is that person's.

(6) A news item that does not consist of censure of a person or of harm to him may lawfully be published, but on condition that it is not contrary to the particular interest of any Muslim or to the general interest. And where there is even a slight possibility of that, it should not be disclosed to the general public, but only to those who are handling the matter in accordance with reason and the Sacred Law, for it is possible that the person concerned has not perceived the harm that may result. In the verse

﴿وإذا جاء هم أمر من الأمن أو الخوف أذاعوا به﴾

the harm and blameworthiness of precisely such news reports and gatherings has been set forth. It is fitting for a Muslim, however, that he should not relate even such an item merely as a piece of news, but should derive from it some religious or worldly benefit. In the first place, there is hardly any event or item of news in the world that is without consequence or from which no religious or worldly benefit can be conceived. But even if there should be such an item, there is no harm in mentioning it simply by way of lightening the spirits; indeed this too is to a degree required by the Sacred Law, and the Noble Prophet's ﷺ jesting on some occasions was based upon this very wisdom.

(7) Articles contrary to the Sacred Law and the false beliefs of the heretics should in the first place not be published at all; and if some need arises for them to be published, then their refutation and a satisfactory reply must also be published in the very issue (or in the very gathering) in which they appear. It should not be held over to the next issue (or to another gathering), because there are many people who do not see the following issue, and if — God forbid — they are seized by some doubt as a result, the cause of it will be the one who published it.

(8) If news of the oppression of Muslims by unbelievers is to be published, then so long as the ascription of the oppression to the unbelievers is not established by a legally recognised proof, it should be published in this manner: that oppression is being inflicted upon the Muslims of such-and-such a place, and that the Muslims should put a stop to this oppression and assist them, by lawful means, with their lives and their wealth.

(9) The editor of a newspaper should always be a person who has a thorough command of all the Islamic sciences, or who at the very least is bound to refer to the scholars and who is sympathetic to the religion. Otherwise it is obvious that newspapers are a highly effective instrument for spreading irreligion and lawlessness.

(10) No advertisement should be given for a book that is harmful to religion, or for a medicine that is unlawful in the Sacred Law, or for a transaction that is invalid in the Sacred Law.
