### TITLE
Mentioning Muʿāwiyah (may Allah be pleased with him) in the sermon

### QUESTION
Should Muʿāwiyah (may Allah be pleased with him) be mentioned in the sermon or not?

### ANSWER
Muʿāwiyah (may Allah be pleased with him) should be mentioned in the sermon, especially in this age, when people have fallen prey to ill thoughts about him. However, if there is a fear of discord, one should first accustom people to it and then quietly begin mentioning him in the sermon. Consider what Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

In every age, care has been taken in the arrangement of the sermon's content to protect the position of the Ahl al-Sunnah against the schisms that have arisen within Islam. Thus the aim of including in the sermon the blessed names of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), the supplication made for them and their merits is to refute the Rāfiḍīs and the Khārijites and to proclaim the position of the Ahl al-Sunnah. For the schisms of earlier times it was enough to mention those Companions (may Allah be pleased with them) who appear in the printed sermons. One of the new schisms of the modern age is the emergence of a group that claims to belong to the Ahl al-Sunnah and professes devotion to the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them), while their hearts are poisoned with hatred of the Companions (may Allah be pleased with them). In particular, the filth in their hearts concerning ʿUthmān (may Allah be pleased with him) and Muʿāwiyah (may Allah be pleased with him) pours out constantly from their tongues and pens. In the position of the Ahl al-Sunnah, even the slightest ill thought about any Companion brings down the anger of Allah and leads to Hell; these people are therefore outside the Ahl al-Sunnah and are following the very path of the Rāfiḍīs in their unbelief. The Rāfiḍīs also bear hatred towards the noble daughters of the Prophet ﷺ (may Allah be pleased with them) other than Fāṭimah (may Allah be pleased with her). So, with the refutation of these schisms in view, one should make it a regular practice in the sermon to mention the merits and virtues of Muʿāwiyah (may Allah be pleased with him) and of the noble daughters (may Allah be pleased with them), and to pray for Allah's pleasure upon them. This does not entail departing from the position of Ḥaḍrat Thānwī (may Allah sanctify his secret) and the other senior scholars (may Allah have mercy on them); rather, it supports their position, because this addition has been made in line with the very principle on which their sermons are based, as has been explained above. فقط واللہ تعالی اعلم ۔ (احسن الفتاوی: ٤/۱٤٦، باب الجمعہ و العیدین)

Fatāwā Farīdiyyah states:
Neither is insisting on it appropriate, nor is refusing it becoming — especially since many people have fallen prey to ill thoughts about Muʿāwiyah (may Allah be pleased with him). (فتاوی فریدیہ: ۳/۱۸۸، فصل فی الجمعہ) ۔

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
