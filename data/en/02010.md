### TITLE
Selling currency contrary to the rate fixed by the government

### QUESTION
If a person sells rand for dollars at more or less than the rate fixed by the government, what is the ruling?

### ANSWER
In the case in question there is no dispute about permissibility as such, since selling a thing for something of a different genus with a difference in amount is permissible. It will, however, entail a violation of the government's law; and safeguarding one's honour is necessary — if there is a danger to one's honour, such a thing should not be done, and putting one's honour at risk for a few pennies is no act of wisdom.

Consider what Fiqhī Maqālāt states:

Exchanging with a difference in amount in contravention of the rate fixed by the government will not entail interest, because the two currencies differ in genus, and a difference in amount in the exchange of different genera is permissible; nor has any limit for this difference been laid down in the sharīʿah — rather it depends on the mutual consent of the two parties. The rulings of price-fixing will, however, apply to it. Hence, according to those jurists in whose view price-fixing of goods by the government is permissible, it will be permissible in currency too, and so it is not permissible to contravene this order of the government (provided that it is an Islamic government bound by Islamic principles and not like the governments of today). But on the other hand it is also not correct to call this increase interest and declare it unlawful. (فقہی مقالات: ۱/٤۰) ۔
