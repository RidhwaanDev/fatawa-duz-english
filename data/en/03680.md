### TITLE
The ruling on returning land after buying it when it turns out not to be fit for building

### QUESTION
1. A person bought a piece of land for the purpose of building. Later it came to light that there is no permission from the government to build upon it, because building is not possible on account of a defect in the land. Is it correct for him to return it or not? 2. He has also incurred some expenses on this land; is it correct for him to recover them or not? 3. Furthermore, monthly payments have to be made on the security of the land to the estate. Now, because the land is useless, is it still binding to pay them?

### ANSWER
In the case in question an important purpose of land is building. When there is a prohibition from the side of the government, and the buyer had no prior knowledge of it, then this falls under the heading of a defect. The buyer may therefore return the land to the seller and recover his money.

2. In the option of defect, permission is given upon return only to recover the price; the expense of the return and so forth is upon the buyer himself. Yes, if the seller deliberately concealed the defect and thereby deceived the buyer, then, by analogy with a recalcitrant debtor, he may recover the expenses from the seller.

3. When the land was useless and could not be used for building or any other purpose, then paying the security on it is also not binding in the Sharīʿah. Consider the proofs for number 1: Fatḥ al-Qadīr mentions:

والعيب: ما تخلو عنه أصل الفطرة السليمة مما يعد به ناقصاً، قوله وإذا اطلع المشتري على عيب في المبيع، ولم يكن شرط البراءة من كل عيب، فهو بالخيار، إن شاء أخذ، ذلك المبيع بجميع الثمن، وإن شاء رده، هذا إذا لم يتمكن من إزالته بلا مشقة. (فتح القدير: ٦/۳۵۵، ط: دار الفكر).

In Fatāwā Shāmī it is transmitted:

لا بد في العيب أن لا يتمكن من إزالته بلا مشقة

وأن يكون عند البائع ولم يعلم به المشتري، ولم يكن البائع شرط البراءة منه خاصاً أو عاماً ولم يزل قبل الفسخ

الخ. (فتاوى الشامي: ۵/۵، سعيد).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā mentions:

After becoming aware of a defect, the buyer has the option: if he wishes he may keep it for the whole of the price, and if he wishes he may return it. (احسن الفتاوی: ٦/٤۹۳) ۔

2. The expenses of returning the sold item are upon the buyer. Consider (فتاوی دار العلوم زکریا، ۵/۲٤۲، ط: دیوبند) ۔

3. It is correct to recover the expenses of litigation from a recalcitrant debtor.

ʿAzīz al-Fatāwā states:

In the case in which the debtor is recalcitrant and, despite having the capacity, is negligent in and refuses the payment of the debt, and the creditor out of compulsion recovers the loan by bringing a suit, in this situation it is correct to take the court expenses from the debtor, because the cause of this expense has been the debtor. (عزیز الفتاوی: ۱/٦۲۵، ط: دار الاشاعت) ۔

For further detail consider: (فتاوی دار العلوم زکریا: ۵، ۱۷۱، ط: زمزم، کراچی) ۔
