### TITLE
The ruling on taking part in trade on the day of Black Friday

### QUESTION
I have heard from some people that Muslims do not take part in trade on Black Friday. I would like to know whether Muslims may take part in trade on this day or not.

### ANSWER
Black Friday is observed in America on the Friday after Thanksgiving Day (the last Thursday of November); it is an informal name (that is, it has no connection with any custom or creed), and since 1952 CE this day has been regarded in America as the beginning of the Christmas shopping season, while the term itself is not very old. On this day discounts and reductions in prices are generally offered in commercial centres, and this day is regarded as the busiest day of shopping. The reason for the name "Black" is that on this day merchants make ample profits, and in the terminology of merchants black is the sign of profit while red is the mark of loss and deficit. (Summarised from Wikipedia).

A Muslim may form this intention regarding the word "Black": that since this trading begins in the darkness of night and people have to set out early, therefore its name is Black Friday.

It is permissible and correct for Muslims to take part in trade on this day; there is no harm in it, nor is any false creed or any religious symbol connected with it. It is also correct for merchants to grant a reduction in price on this day.

Consider what the author of al-Hidāyah says:

ويجوز للمشتري أن يزيد للبائع في الثمن ويجوز للبائع أن يزيد للمشتري في المبيع ويجوز أن يحط عن الثمن. (الهداية: ۳/۵۹).

قال في العناية: قال ويجوز للمشتري أن يزيد البائع في الثمن، إذا اشتري عيناً بمائة ثم زاد عشرة مثلاً أو باع عيناً بمائة ثم زاد على المبيع شيئاً أو حط بعض الثمن جاز. (العناية: ٦/۵۱۹).

It is transmitted in Fatāwā Shāmī:

ثم ذكر حط الثمن وهبته وإبراء ه، وحاصل ما ذكره في البحر عن الذخيرة: أنه لو وهبه بعض الثمن أو أبرأه عنه قبل القبض فهو حط، وإن حط البعض أو وهبه بعد القبض صح، ووجب عليه للمشتري مثل ذلك. (فتاوى الشامي: ۵/۱۵٦، سعيد).

It is mentioned in Aham Masāʾil jin meṉ Ibtilāʾ ʿĀmm Hai:

Nowadays the method of "discount" is generally current in large markets for the promotion and boosting of trade — for example, in a shop there is some discount on every item; that is, if the price of an item is one thousand, there is a twenty-five per cent discount on it, meaning that this item will now be given to the customer for only seven hundred and seventy-five. This customary form of buying and selling is correct according to the Sharīʿah. (Aham Masāʾil jin meṉ Ibtilāʾ ʿĀmm Hai: 8/227).
