### TITLE
A non-Muslim giving the ritual bath to a Muslim who has died

### QUESTION
If the deceased is a Muslim woman and a non-Muslim woman gives her the ritual bath, what is the ruling? And what is the ruling if a non-Muslim man gives the ritual bath to a Muslim man?

### ANSWER
If a disbeliever gives the bath while a Muslim is present, it is disliked. But if no Muslim is available and a disbeliever gives the bath, it is valid, though it will be contrary to the sunnah. Apparently there is no difference between a man and a woman in this, except that if the deceased is a Muslim man and only women are present, the Muslim women should teach a disbelieving man how to give the bath, and he should then bathe the Muslim,

لأن نظر الجنس إلى الجنس أخف.

Consider what al-Durr al-Mukhtār states:

وليس للكافر غسل قريبه المسلم وفي الشامي: أي إذا لم يكن للمسلم قريب مسلم بين نساء معهن كافر يعلمنه الغسل ثم يصلين عليه فتغسل الكافر المسلم فيه للضرورة فلا يدل على أنه يمكن من تجهيز قريبه المسلم عند عدمها خلافاً للزيلعي رحمه الله، أفاده في البحر. (الدر المختار مع رد المحتار: ۲/۲۳۱، سعيد. وكذا في حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ۱/۳۷۹. والبحر الرائق: ۲/۱۹۱. والفتاوى الهندية: ۱/۱٥۹)

Badāʾiʿ al-Ṣanāʾiʿ states:

ولو لم يكن فيهن امرأته ولكن معهن رجل كافر علمنه غسل الميت ويخلين بينهما موفقة في الدين. (بدائع الصنائع: ۱/۳۰٤، سعيد)

Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah states:

A bath given at the hands of a non-Muslim does count as a valid bath, since it is not a condition that the one giving the bath be legally responsible.

وأنه يسقط وإن لم يكن الغاسل مكلفاً. (شامي: ۱/۸۰٥)

There are, however, two objections to it:

(1) A bath given at the hands of a non-Muslim is not in keeping with the sunnah.

(2) The funeral preparation and shrouding of a Muslim is an obligation on Muslims, and the responsibility for it remains with them. It must therefore be carried out at the hands of Muslims in the sunnah manner, whether this takes place in a hospital or at home. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 1/373).
