### TITLE
Trading in opium

### QUESTION
Is trading in opium permissible or not?

### ANSWER
In the case asked about, trading in opium is permissible and valid. It does not fall under precisely the same ruling as wine, and it has genuine benefits, since it is used in medicine and the like. The principle is that anything which can be put to a lawful use may lawfully be traded in. However, where there is a strong likelihood that a particular person will put it to an unlawful use, selling it to him is disliked.

See what is stated in al-Durr al-Mukhtār:

وصح بيع غير الخمر ومفاده صحة بيع الحشيشة والأفيون. وفي الشامية: قوله وصح بيع غير الخمر أي عنده خلافاً لهما في البيع والضمان، لكن الفتوى على قوله في البيع. (الدر المختار مع فتاوى الشامي: ٦/٤۵٤، كتاب الأشربة، سعيد).

وفي حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر: ويجوز بيعها ويضمن متلفها قيمتها عنده وقالا: لا يجوز البيع ولا يضمن المتلف وعن أبي يوسف رحمه الله يجوز بيعها إذا طبخ فذهب أكثر من النصف وأقل من الثلثين والفتوى على قوله في البيع. (حاشية الطحطاوي على الدر المختار: ٤/۲۲۵، كوئته).

Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

The buying and selling of opium is permissible in the sharia. The law of the land allows it only under licence, but the sharia lays down no such restriction, and the money taken as its price is permissible and lawful. (کفایت المفتی: ۹/۱۲٤، کتاب الحظر و الاباحۃ).

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

In former times opium was not much used in medical treatment; it was generally used for intoxication. For that reason some jurists (may Allah have mercy on them) wrote that its sale is disliked. In our day, however, opium has come to be widely used as a medicine and has gained great importance and renown in treatment — indeed it has reached the level of a pressing need. Its sale is therefore permissible without any dislike. But selling it to a person about whom there is a strong likelihood that he will use it for intoxication is strictly disliked. (احسن الفتاوی: ٦/٤۹٤).

Mufti Taqī says:

The principle in regard to sale is that anything which can be put to some lawful use may be sold, even if that thing is commonly used for unlawful purposes. It is then the buyer's concern to put it to a lawful use. (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ٤/۱۷).

Elsewhere he writes:

Opium is an intoxicant, and in ordinary circumstances its use is not permissible; yet its sale is permissible. The reason for this is that a lawful use of opium is also possible: it can be used in medicines, in treatment, in external applications, in poultices and so on. Its sale is therefore permissible. (اسلام اور جدید معاشی مسائل: ٤/۱۳).
