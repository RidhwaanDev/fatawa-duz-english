### TITLE
The ruling on the names Ḥarb and Murrah

### QUESTION
In the light of the hadith, giving the names Ḥarb and Murrah is forbidden; yet among the Companions we find the names Ḥarb ibn Junādib and Ḥarb ibn al-Ḥārith al-Muḥāribī, and likewise Murrah was the name of several Companions. The Prophet ﷺ did not order these three to change their names. What was the reason for this?

### ANSWER
If a name contains polytheism, then changing it is obligatory — such as ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā, ʿAbd al-Hubal and the like. But if a name carries a suspicion of an evil omen while on the other side good meanings could also be drawn from it, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ advised changing it, then changing it is not obligatory; his ﷺ instruction was at the level of advice, not something whose acceptance was binding — although accepting it was recommended. Ḥarb and Murrah are of this kind, for good meanings can be taken in them: Murrah, that is, bitter against falsehood, and Ḥarb, that is, doing battle against the enemy. That is why he did not order them to be changed, and some Companions were named Ḥarb and Murrah. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ advised Ḥazn, the grandfather of Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab, to change his name, but he did not change it; Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab used to say that harshness has continued in our family ever since.

See the report about Murrah and Ḥarb:

عن أبي وهب الجشمي، وكانت له صحبة قال: قال رسول الله ﷺ: تسموا بأسماء الأنبياء

وأقبحها حرب ومرة. (رواه أبو داود، رقم: ۲/٦۷٦، باب في تغيير الأسماء).

Mullā ʿAlī al-Qārī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in Mirqāt:

وأقبحها حرب ومرة: لان الحرب يتطير بها وتكره لما فيها من القتل والأذى، وأما مرة، فلأن المركريه، ولأن كنية إبليس أبو مرة. (مرقاة المفاتيح: ۹/۱۲۰، باب الأسامي).

But in the early days of Islam taking evil omens was current; later, through the reports of "no evil omen," taking evil omens was done away with. Therefore it is now permissible and correct, without any dislike, to give such names.

وإنما غير من الأسماء من أراد الأخذ فيه بالأفضل دون من أراد حمله على الجائز، ولذلك أقر حزناً على ما أراد من الاستمساك باسمه ورضيه وكره تغييره ولوكان ذلك محرماً لم يقره على ذلك ولذلك أقر حرباً ومرة على أسمائهما ولم يأمرهما بتغييرهما. (المنتقي شرح الموطأ: ۷/۲۹٦).

Yes, giving a name that contains polytheism is impermissible, and changing it is obligatory.

The Prophet ﷺ advised the changing of some names, but this was not binding, because they did not contain polytheism. See:

عن عبد الحميد بن جبير بن شيبة قال: جلست إلى سعيد بن المسيب، فحدثني أن جده حزناً قدم النبي ﷺ فقال: ما اسمك؟ قال: اسمي حزن، قال: بل أنت سهل، قال: ما أنا بمغير اسماً سمانيه أبي قال ابن المسيب: فما زالت فينا الحزونة بعد. (رواه البخاري: ۲/۹۱٤، باب تحويل الاسم إلى اسم أحسن منه).

ʿAllāmah al-Qasṭallānī says in Irshād al-Sārī:

وفي الحديث: أن التغيير ليس على وجه المنع من التسمي بالقبيح بل على وجه الاختيار فيجوز تسمية الرجل القبيح بحسن والفاسق بصالح لأنه ﷺ لم يلزم حزناً لما امتنع من تحويل اسمه إلى سهل بذلك ولو كان لازماً لما أقره على قوله ما أناْ بمغير اسماً سمانيه أبي. والله الموفق للصواب. (ارشاد الساري: ۹/۱۱۲).

The reason for mentioning the meanings of difficult names, and the origin of the Arabic language:

Nowadays people give the Islamic names of former times, and those names are Hebrew or Syriac or from other languages. Then ordinary people ask about the meanings of these names, and when they are told that these names are Hebrew or from other languages and that their exact meanings are not known, they insist: "Please find some good meanings for these names; we will telephone later." For this reason, as far as we were able, we have written the meanings of these names from the dictionaries and from some works of tafsīr, and sometimes from our own defective opinion, so that the questioners may be satisfied and may keep the good meanings in view when giving the name.

The scholars have written that the root of the Arabic language is Hebrew and the root of Hebrew is Syriac. Even now there are hundreds of Arabic words in Hebrew that appear to be shared by the two. Today Hebrew is the official language of Israel. The Jews say shalom for salām, which is a corrupted form of salām. Jerusalem too is in origin Arḍ al-Salām, which was turned into "Jerusalem"; Tel Aviv too is in origin Tall Rabīʿ, or perhaps Tall Ḥabīb — tall means a mound, and rabīʿ and ḥabīb are obvious. And Allah knows best.

In our view, however, the correct position is that the Arabic language is the origin and the other languages came out of it. The proof of this is that after Allah Most High created Ādam (upon our Prophet and upon him be blessings and peace) in the heavens, He said to him: say to the angels

"السلام عليكم"

and listen to their reply. So Ādam (peace be upon him) gave the greeting, and the angels said in reply:

"السلام عليك ورحمة الله"

These are Arabic words. See what Bukhārī Sharīf states:

عن أبي هريرة رضي الله عنه، عن النبي ﷺ قال: لما خلق الله آدم على صورته طوله ستون ذراعاً، فلما خلقه قال: اذهب فسلم على أولئك نفر من الملائكة جلوس فاستمع ما يحيونك فإنها تحيتك وتحية ذريتك، فقال: السلام عليكم، فقالوا: السلام عليك ورحمة الله، فزادوه: ورحمة الله". متفق عليه. (رواه البخاري: ۲/۹۱۹، رقم: ۳۳۲٦، ومسلم، رقم: ۲۸٤۱).

Besides this, the Muslims of South Africa in particular, and those of other places in general, ask about the meanings of various names. Along with some of the names we were asked about, we have included other names as well in the fatwas, so that the general public, the students, and we ourselves may benefit from them. We tried to correct some corrupted names and searched out their origin, and for some names a great deal of thought, reflection and investigation was needed to discover the origin. This amusing point is also worth mentioning, that the meanings of some names became known through a dream. Some time ago one of our students, Mawlānā Wasīm Khān of America, requested that I travel with him for ʿUmrah and to Bayt al-Maqdis. But I was hesitant about whether I should go or not, because my South African passport records my Pakistani birth in bold letters, and the people of the northern province of Pakistan also resemble the Afghans in appearance. I was in this state of indecision — whether to go or not — since Israel's hostility to religion is known to all. Then I saw in a dream that I was sitting in my neighbourhood with my friends, and they were, it seems, talking and smiling. One of them is named Nūr al-Amīn Khān Yūsufzai and the other Tamrīz Khān Yūsufzai, and both are connected with poetry and letters. When I awoke from sleep I was at ease. I interpreted Nūr al-Amīn as the journey to Bayt al-Maqdis, because Nūr al-Amīn is like Rūḥ al-Amīn: Jibrīl the trustworthy is Rūḥ al-Amīn, and he is luminous — rather, he is light — and he is trustworthy in conveying revelation; and Jibrīl's (peace be upon him) connection with Bayt al-Maqdis is obvious, for the Messenger of Allah ﷺ went to Masjid al-Aqṣā in his company. In Nūr al-Amīn there is also the possibility that the described is annexed to the description, that is, "like light, and trustworthy." Likewise Tamrīz is in origin tamr-rīz, that is, the distributing of dates, which was an indication of ʿUmrah. All praise be to Allah, the journey to Bayt al-Maqdis and the ʿUmrah passed off in well-being and safety. It should be remembered that the role of names in the interpretation of dreams is established from the noble hadith.
