### TITLE
Remembrance of Allah aloud in mosques

### QUESTION
Nowadays some people criticise remembrance of Allah aloud in mosques and call it an innovation. What is the ruling of the sharia on this?

### ANSWER
Remembrance aloud and in a group is permissible in the mosque according to the sharia, and our senior scholars have acted upon it.

Here are the translations and the gist of some hadiths traced back to the Prophet ﷺ:

(۱) قال النبي ﷺ: أناْ عند ظن عبدي بي وأناْ معه إذا ذكرني فإن ذكرني في نفسه ذكرته في نفسي وإن ذكرني في ملأ ذكرته في ملأ خير منهم، الحديث. (رواه البخاري برقم ٦۸۵٦).

The Prophet ﷺ said that Allah Most High says: I deal with My servant as he expects of Me, and when he remembers Me I am with him. If he remembers Me by himself, I remember him by Myself; and if he remembers Me in a gathering, I remember him in a gathering better than his. Remembering Him in a gathering covers both aloud and quietly; indeed, as ʿAllāmah al-Suyūṭī said, the point of a gathering shows itself in remembrance aloud.

Here "in himself" means "alone", because it is set against a gathering, and this expression is used in the sense of being alone. ʿAllāmah al-Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him), explaining this hadith in his commentary on Muslim, says:

مراد الحديث أي إذا ذكرني خالياً أثابه الله وجازاه عما عمل بما لا يطلع عليه أحد. (شرح النووي: ۲/۳٤۱).

In grammar the noun is defined in these words: "كلمة تدل على معني في نفسه غير مقترن بأحد الأزمنة الثلاثة" — that is, a noun is a word that indicates its meaning by itself, unlike a particle, which needs something to support it. For instance, min does not indicate a partial beginning without the word that follows it, whereas beginning in the absolute sense is the meaning of the noun.

The author of al-Kashshāf, ʿAllāmah al-Zamakhsharī (may Allah have mercy on him), explained the verse

﴿قل لهم في أنفسهم قولاً بليغاً﴾

as meaning "خالياً بهم".

"قال النبي ﷺ: إذا صلي أحدكم لنفسه فليطل ما شاء"

— that is, when a person prays by himself he may lengthen it as much as he wishes. In the same way, one possible meaning of the verse

﴿واذكر ربك في نفسك تضرعاً وخيفة ودون الجهر من القول بالغدو والآصال﴾ (سورة الأعراف: ۲۰۵)

is this: when you are alone, what is the good of sitting idle? So remember Allah with humility and awe. There is no one else there, but Allah is. And if you are going to raise your voice, then keep it below a very loud pitch, since there are no others present whose encouragement is intended, so a light raising of the voice is enough. The second meaning is that one should remember Allah in the heart with humility and awe, or should say the words aloud but at less than a very loud pitch.

قال الإمام: المراد أن يقع الذكر متوسطاً بين الجهر والمخافتة والمراد بالجهر رفع الصوت المفرط وبما دونه نوع آخر من الجهر. (روح المعاني: ۹/۱۵٤).

وقال الشيخ عبد الرحمن السعدي المتوفي۱۳۷٦: في نفسه أي مخلصاً خالياً. (تيسير الكريم الرحمن في تفسير كلام المنان، ص ۲۷٦).

وقال ابن عطية الأندلسي: الجمهور على أن الذكر لا يكون في النفس ولا يراعي إلا بحركة اللسان. (تفسير ابن عطية، ص ۷۷۳).

(2) When you pass by the meadows of Paradise, graze in them. The Companions asked: what are the meadows of Paradise? The Prophet ﷺ said: mosques. They asked: and what is grazing, Messenger of Allah? He said: Subḥān Allāh, al-ḥamdu lillāh, lā ilāha illā Allāh, and Allāhu akbar.

(3) When people gather for the remembrance of Allah, a caller announces from heaven: Rise, you have been forgiven, and your evil deeds have been turned into good deeds. (Majmaʿ al-Zawāʾid: 10/76; Musnad Aḥmad: 3/142; and elsewhere. Its chain contains Maymūn al-Marāʾī, whom a group of scholars declared reliable, though there is some weakness in him.)

(4) ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ (may Allah be pleased with him) asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ what the fruit of gatherings of remembrance is. He ﷺ said: Paradise — Paradise. (مجمع الزوائد: ۱۰/۷٦، وإسناده حسن).

(5) On the Day of Judgement the faces of some people will shine with light and they will be on pulpits of pearl, and people will envy them. Someone asked: Messenger of Allah, tell us about them. He ﷺ said: They are people from different families who gathered together in one place for the love of Allah and occupied themselves in His remembrance. (مجمع الزوائد: ۱۰/۷٦).

(6) Some of Allah's finest angels go about looking for gatherings of remembrance. When they reach such a gathering they sit with the people and cover them with their wings. When the gathering is over, Allah — though He knows — asks them: where have you come from? They say: we have come from a group that was occupied in proclaiming Your greatness. (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī: 2/248).

(7) The Messenger of Allah ﷺ came upon a circle of Companions and asked: what has made you sit here? They said: we are sitting in a gathering of remembrance. He ﷺ said: Jibrīl the Trustworthy came to me and brought word that Allah is boasting of you to the angels.

(8) Jābir (may Allah be pleased with him) says that some people saw a light and a fire in the graveyard. When they came there, they saw that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was in the graveyard, saying: hand me the body of this Companion. This was the Companion (may Allah be pleased with him) who used to remember Allah in a loud voice. (Abū Dāwūd: 2/451).

The reports quoted above, and many others like them, establish remembrance in a group and aloud. In the books of fiqh, remembrance aloud is recorded as permissible, and in some places as recommended.

A questioner once asked Muftī Maḥmūd al-Ḥasan Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him) about remembrance aloud — the question is left out here for brevity — and he wrote in reply: Why get into an argument with your friends? Do your remembrance quietly and in private, in a voice that causes no disturbance to anyone sleeping or praying and so on. For the rest, remembrance aloud is established by the call to prayer, the sermon and the takbīr of tashrīq themselves.

أجمع العلماء سلفاً وخلفاً على استحباب ذكر الله تعالى في المساجد وغيرها من غير نكير إلا أن يشوش جهرهم على نائم أو مصل أو قارئ كما هو مقرر في كتب الفقه. (الحموي على الأشباه: ۲/۲۳٤، وكذا في الشامي: ۱/٦٦۰، سعيد).

Secondly, this remembrance is a form of treatment, and for that it is enough that it should not be against the principles of the sharia, just as with the remedies prescribed by a physician or a doctor. (Tarbiyat al-Ṭālibīn, p. 59).

In answer to another question he says: Shāh ʿAbd al-Raḥīm Wilāyatī (may Allah have mercy on him) used to sit on a hill and do remembrance, and his voice would carry a long way. Mawlānā Rashīd Aḥmad Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him) did remembrance aloud until the end of his life; he would shut the door of his room, and anyone standing outside at the door could hear his voice. Mawlānā Muḥammad Ilyās did remembrance aloud right up until he became bed-ridden. Remembrance aloud or quietly, alone or in a group, is all permissible. (Sulūk wa Iḥsān: Irshādāt-e-Faqīh al-Ummat, p. 343, compiled by Mawlānā Muftī Fārūq).

In Tadhkirat al-Rashīd, in the account of Mawlānā Rashīd Aḥmad Gangohī (may Allah have mercy on him), it is recorded: In the end I got up myself, performed ablution and came into the mosque. In one corner Ḥājī Imdādullāh (may Allah have mercy on him) was engaged in his own devotions; I went and stood in another corner, prayed voluntary prayers with the intention of tahajjud, and began the remembrance of negation and affirmation aloud. He then says: In the end I began the remembrance aloud; my voice was good and there was strength in my body. When I came to him in the morning, he said: You did remembrance like someone long practised in it. From that day I came to love remembrance aloud, and after that I never wished to give it up, nor did any reason in the sharia for forbidding it ever appear to me. (Tadhkirat al-Rashīd: 48, 49).

Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) wrote a detailed fatwa on remembrance aloud and in a group in volume five of Imdād al-Fatāwā. In it he writes: In this writer's humble opinion, the position of those who permit it — that is, those who hold raising the voice to be permissible — is correct; and among them the position of those who distinguish between cases — that is, those who say that in some situations raising the voice is better and in others keeping it low is better — appears to be the stronger, since in this way all the verses, hadiths and statements of the scholars are reconciled: "inna khayra al-umūri aʿdaluhā". So once the lawfulness of raising the voice is established, it is not tied to any particular manner or posture; rather, since the evidence is unqualified, it is general: whether alone or in a group, in a circle or in rows, standing or sitting, it is permissible in every way. After that, having quoted some Arabic passages, he wrote: So it is established that remembrance aloud is permissible in every manner, and no one should be prevented from any manner of it. This is the strongest and soundest view. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 5/151–154).

In his view this remembrance aloud and in a group is permissible in the mosque as well, for in the same fatwa he quoted, among the proofs of those who permit it:

قال الله تعالى: ﴿ومن أظلم ممن منع مسجد الله أن يذكر فيها اسمه وسعي في خرابها﴾

It is obvious that no one can prevent remembrance without coming to know of it, and coming to know of it is inconceivable unless it is aloud. (Imdād al-Fatāwā: 5/152).

The upshot is that since remembrance aloud is permissible in the mosque, and since in his view remembrance aloud is permissible in every form — in a group or alone, in a circle or in rows — remembrance aloud and in a group in the mosque is necessarily permissible.

Mawlānā Shaykh Muḥammad Yūnus, Shaykh al-Ḥadīth of Maẓāhir al-ʿUlūm, Saharanpur, who holds a high standing in the science of hadith and in the other sciences, writes in al-Yawāqīt al-Ghāliyah: In group remembrance the collective form is not what is sought in itself; rather, the aim is that by gathering together and seeing one another, eagerness and enthusiasm are produced. There is also the point that some spiritual guides direct their attention to the heart of the disciple during the remembrance so that his heart may become engaged, and everyone's remembering together in the guide's company helps in gaining his attention. Then, a few lines further on, he writes: It is possible that Ibn Masʿūd's (may Allah be pleased with him) objection rested on some particular factor — for example, that those people regarded the practice as necessary. (اليواقيت الغالية: ۲/٤۱۸).

The report of Ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) in which he objects to the circle of remembrance is a report that stops at a Companion, and against a report traced to the Prophet ﷺ it is the weaker of the two. Let me set down one hadith by way of example:

لا يقعد قوم يذكرون الله إلا حفت هم الملائكة وغشيت هم الرحمة ونزلت عليهم السكينة وذكر الله فيمن عنده. (رواه مسلم، باب فضل الاجتماع على تلاوة القرآن وعلى الذكر.

Furthermore, the report of Ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) is unstable. In al-Dārimī's report the incident takes place before the dawn prayer, while in al-Ṭabarānī's al-Muʿjam al-Kabīr it takes place between the sunset and night prayers. In al-Dārimī's report it happens in the mosque, while in Muṣannaf ʿAbd al-Razzāq there is mention of "ilā al-bariyyah", the open country. In al-Dārimī's report one man recites and the others recite after him, while in al-Ṭabarānī's report they were all reciting together. Some reports say that he turned them out, as in al-Ṭabarānī, while another report says that he merged the two groups into one. All these inconsistencies are evidence of the weakness of the report. Moreover, all the chains apart from al-Dārimī's are weak. As for al-Dārimī's chain, it contains ʿAmr ibn Yaḥyā: although Ibn Maʿīn declared him trustworthy at first, later, once he had seen him, he declared him weak and said, "laysa ḥadīthuhu bi-shayʾ, qad raʾaytuhu", and on one occasion he said, "lam yakun yurḍā". Ibn Maʿīn does sometimes use "laysa bi-shayʾ" for one who has few hadiths — see al-Rafʿ wa'l-Takmīl, p. 153; Qawāʿid fī ʿUlūm al-Ḥadīth, p. 263; Taʿlīq al-Rafʿ wa'l-Takmīl, p. 215; and Dr Bashshār ʿAwwād's introduction to his edition of Sunan Ibn Mājah — but here the wording is "laysa bi-marḍī". Besides, when both criticism and endorsement are reported about a narrator and it is not known which came first, that report is not accepted; rather judgement is suspended on it. For the references in detail, see our book Dhikr-e-Jahrī wa Ijtimāʿī Sharīʿat ke Āʾīne meṅ, pp. 125–131.

Some people raise the objection that in the view of Imam Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) raising the voice in remembrance is an innovation except in the places specified by the texts. But Shāmī (may Allah have mercy on him) has quoted from Sharḥ al-Munyah al-Ṣaghīr that the difference of opinion is about which is better:

وفي شرح المنية الصغير: ويوم الفطر لا يجهر به عنده وعندهما يجهر وهو رواية عنه والخلاف في الأفضلية أما الكراهة فمنتفية عن الطرفين. (فتاوى الشامي: ۲/۱۷۰، سعيد).

Ṭaḥṭāwī ʿalā Marāqī al-Falāḥ states:

قال الحلبي: والذي ينبغي أن يكون الخلاف في استحباب الجهر وعدمه لا في كراهية وعدمها فعندهما يستحب وعنده الإخفاء أفضل وذلك لأن الجهر قد نقل عن كثير من السلف كابن عمر رضي الله عنه وعلى رضي الله عنه وأبي أمامة الباهلي رضي الله عنه والنخعي رحمه الله وابن جبير رحمه الله وعمر بن عبد العزيز رحمه الله وأبي ليلي رحمه الله وأبان بن عثمان رحمه الله والحكم رحمه الله وحماد رحمه الله ومالك رحمه الله وأحمد رحمه الله وأبو ثور رحمه الله ومثله عن الشافعي رحمه الله ذكره ابن المنذر رحمه الله في الاشراف. (حاشية الطحطاوي على مراقي الفلاح، ص ۵۳۱. وشرح منية المصلي، الكبير، ص ۵٦۷، سهيل).

Those who say that aloud is better say so because it brings enjoyment, engages the heart and yields other benefits; so its superiority is because of these other factors. Otherwise, in itself, most scholars hold quiet remembrance to be better.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
