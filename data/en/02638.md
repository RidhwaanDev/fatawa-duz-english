### TITLE

The ruling of ḥijāb

### QUESTION

The ruling of ḥijāb.

### ANSWER

It is impermissible for a woman to go out without covering her whole body. The face must be completely covered; permission is granted to leave the eyes open in order to see the way, though it is better that a mesh be fitted over that as well. Mawlānā Muḥammad Yūsuf Ludhyānwī (may Allah have mercy on him) says: It comes in the aḥādīth that when the verse of ḥijāb was revealed, the Muslim women would go out in such a manner that only one eye remained open in order to see the way. (آپ کے مسائل اور ان کا حل: ۸/۴۶، طبع جدید) ۔

روي الشيخان وغيرهما عن أم عطية رضي الله عنه قالت: أمرنا رسول الله ﷺ أن نخرجهن في الفطر والأضحى: العواتق، والحيض، وذوات الخدور، فأما الحيض فيعتزلن الصلاة، ويشهدن الخير ودعوة المسلمين، قلت: يا رسول الله! إحدانا لا يكون لها جلباب؟ قال: لتلبسها أختها من جلبابها. واللفظ لمسلم. (رقم: ۸۹۰).
قال الحافظ ابن حجر: وفيه امتناع خروج المرأة بغير جلباب. (فتح الباري: ۱/ ۴۲۴).
وقال بدرالدين العيني: ومنها أي من فوائد الحديث امتناع خروج النساء بدون الجلابيب. (عمدة القاري: ۳/ ۱۶۶، دار الحديث، ملتان).
قال في روح المعاني: في تفسير قوله تعالى: ﴿وليضربن بخمرهن على جيوبهن﴾ والخمر جمع خمار
وهو المقنعة التي تلقيها المرأة على رأسها من الخمر وهو الستر، والجيوب جمع جيب وهو فتح في أعلي القميص يبدو منه بعض الجسد
والمراد من الآية كما روي ابن أبي حاتم عن ابن جبير أمرهن بستر نحورهن وصدورهن بخمرهن لئلا يري منها شيء وكان النساء يغطين رؤوسهن بالخمر ويسدلنها كعادة الجاهلية من وراء الظهر فيبدو نحورهن وبعض صدورهن، وصح أنه لما نزلت هذه الآية سارع نساء المهاجرين إلى امتثال ما فيها فشققن مروطهن فاختمرن بها تصديقاً وإيماناً بما أنزل الله تعالى من كتابه. (روح المعاني: ۱۸/ ۱۴۲).

Maʿārif al-Qurʾān states:

وليضربن بخمرهن على جيوبهن،
that is, let them draw the ends of their scarves over their bosoms. Khumur is the plural of khimār, which is the cloth that a woman uses upon her head such that the throat and the chest are also covered by it. Juyūb is the plural of jayb, which means the neck-opening of a garment; and since from ancient times it has been usual for the neck-opening to be upon the chest, covering the juyūb means covering the chest. At the beginning of the verse there was a prohibition of displaying adornment; in this clause there is an emphasis upon concealing adornment and a description of one form of it, the underlying purpose of which is the effacing of a custom of the age of ignorance. In the age of ignorance women would place the scarf over the head and let its two ends fall behind the back, so that the neck-opening, the throat, the chest and the ears remained exposed. Muslim women were therefore commanded not to do so, but rather to fold the two ends of the scarf one over the other, so that all these limbs would be covered. (معارف القرآن: ٦/۴۰۲) ۔
Ḥalāl wa Ḥarām states:
According to the general body of jurists it is not permissible either to uncover the face and hands before a stranger without need; and if there is fear of desire and temptation — as is observed in our times — then complete purdah from the stranger is necessary. When this verse of the Noble Qur'an itself was revealed —
﴿يدنين عليهن من جلابيبهن﴾
— then, according to the statement of Umm Salamah (may Allah be pleased with her), the Anṣārī women put on such black garments that it was as though crows were upon their heads; for jilbāb is the name of a garment that covers the whole body. (حلال و حرام، ص۱۸۷) ۔
Kitāb al-Fatāwā states:
It is better that such a veil be used as has a mesh over the portion of the eyes too; nevertheless, according to the explicit statements of the jurists, permission is granted to keep the eyes open so that there may be ease in walking. The real issue is the form of the veil itself: the veil should ordinarily be of thick cloth in which there is no attractiveness. Nowadays veils of ever new and eye-catching designs are made, and this undermines the aim and purpose of the veil. (کتاب الفتاوی: ٦/۸۳) ۔
Further proofs for ḥijāb from the Qur'an and the aḥādīth have already passed above; reference may be made there, for repetition would occasion undue length.
