### TITLE
The ruling on certain ambiguous passages of Dalāʾil al-Khayrāt

### QUESTION
Some passages of the book Dalāʾil al-Khayrāt apparently clash with the positions of the Ahl al-Sunnah wa al-Jamāʿah. For example, in the wording of the durūd, concerning the Prophet ﷺ: "اللهم صل على كاشف الغمة" — "the remover of distresses" (p. 84); and:

اللهم صل على محمد نبيك وإبراهيم خليلك وعلي جميع أنبيائك وأصفيائك من أهل أرضك وسمائك عدد خلقك ورضي نفسك وزنة عرشك ومداد كلماتك ومنتهي علمك وزنة جميع مخلوقاتك صلاة مكررة أبداً عدد ما أحصي علمك وملأ ما أحصي علمك وأضعاف ما أحصي علمك صلاة تزيد وتفوق وتفضل صلاة المصلين عليهم من الخلق أجمعين كفضلك على جميع خلقك. (دلائل الخيرات، ص ٤۸)،

In this passage a doubt arises that the knowledge of Allah Most High is finite.

اللهم صل على محمد وعلي آل محمد حتى لا يبقي من الصلاة شيء وارحم سيدنا محمداً حتى لا يبقي من الرحمة شي ء" (ص ٤٤)

In these words a doubt arises that the attribute of mercy of Allah Most High is finite; and other such expressions are present. Likewise, among the names of the Prophet ﷺ:

"سيدُنا شافٍ" (ص ۲۹)،

In this passage the Messenger of Allah ﷺ has been called shāfī (healer), whereas in the hadith it has come "لا شافي إلا أنت" — the healer is Allah Most High alone.

سيدُنا كاشفُ الكرب

and

"سيدُنا صاحبُ الفَرَج" (ص ۳۲)،

In this passage the Messenger of Allah ﷺ has been called kāshif al-karb (the remover of anguish), whereas the One who removes hardship is Allah Most High:

وإن يمسسك الله بضر فلا كاشف له إلا هو.

Besides this, all these formulae of durūd are printed together with an Urdu translation. Now the general public cannot construe them as figurative or metaphorical, because they are not acquainted with these technical usages. So should all these formulae be removed from the book, or should the general public be stopped from reading this book?

Second question: what is the meaning of ḥāḍir wa nāẓir (present and watching) with regard to Allah Most High? Is there any evidence for the use of this expression in the books of the Ashāʿirah and the Māturīdiyyah? بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
(1) The word shifāʾ (healing) carries three meanings: first, a satisfying and reassuring answer to spiritual ailments or to doubts and misgivings is also called shifāʾ; second, the treating of outward illnesses is also called shifāʾ; third, the granting and obtaining of health and soundness is also called shifāʾ. Wherever healing is attributed to other than Allah and the author is of sound creed, there whichever of the first two meanings suits the context is to be taken, while the third meaning is exclusive to Allah Most High. The author of the Dalāʾil called the Messenger of Allah ﷺ shāfī, and that is with regard to the first two meanings.

Concerning the construing of the words of a person of sound creed upon a correct meaning, Al-Durrah al-Faridah Sharḥ Qaṣīdat al-Burdah records:

This principle is almost universally agreed upon: when a man of sound creed says something and its apparent sense does not appear to be correct, then a good meaning is to be sought for it — particularly when the speaker is a scholar of religion, in which case it will be necessary to construe his words upon a correct meaning. Included under this heading are all those details which the noble jurists have set out in order to save a Muslim from disbelief. Consider just one passage; ʿAllāmah Ibn Nujaym (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

والذي تحرر أنه لا يفتي بتكفير مسلم أمكن حمل كلامه على محمل حسن. (البحر الرائق: ۵/۱۳۵).

Before setting out examples of this, consider a few passages concerning this principle:

(1) ʿAllāmah Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) says:

فإنه يجب أن يفسر كلام المتكلم بعضه ببعض، ويؤخذ كلامه هاهنا وهاهنا، وتعرف ما عادته بعينه ويريده بذلك اللفظ إذا تكلم به، وتعرف المعاني التي عرف أنه أرادها في موضع آخر، فإذا عرف عُرفه وعادته في معانيه وألفاظه، كان هذا مما يستعان به على معرفة مراده. (مجموع الفتاوي: ۲/۳۷۴).

The gist of the passage is that the meanings of a speaker's words should be explained with due regard to his general temperament, his usage and his habit.

(2) Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Afḍal Khān Ṣāḥib (may Allah have mercy on him), who is among the shaykhs and senior figures of the Jamāʿat Ishāʿat al-Tawḥīd, says the following in his well-known book Nathr al-Marjān min Mushkilāt al-Qurʾān (at the beginning of which appear the commendation and words of praise of Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Muḥammad Ṭāhir Panj Pīrī, the shaykh of the shaykhs of the Ishāʿat al-Tawḥīd), after explaining the correct sense of a couplet of Mawlānā Rūmī (Muḥammad ibn Muḥammad ibn Ḥusayn al-Balkhī, al-Rūmī, d. 672 AH):

"فهذا تأويل لكلام الرومي لكونه معروفاً بصحة العقيدة واتباع السنة، حيث قال في آخر المثنوي: أنت حسبي، أنت كافي ليس غير، ثم التأويل إنما يعتبر في كلام من يكون معروفاً بالاعتقاد الصحيح، لا في كلام من ليس كذلك. فقول "أنبت الربيع البقل" إذا صدر عن الجاهل المنكر لا يتأول فيه، وإذا صدر عن المسلم فيتأول فيه بأن هذا من قبيل الإسناد إلى الزمان، فهو مجاز. (نثر المرجان من مشكلات القرآن، ص: ۱۲۷).

(3) Ḥaḍrat Shaykh Mawlānā Muḥammad Zakariyyā (may Allah have mercy on him) says: Even severe figures such as Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) have consistently interpreted the words of the friends of Allah, and in his Fatāwā the words of the famous senior Sufis have been interpreted in scores of places in great abundance; the words of Ḥaḍrat Shaykh ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Jīlānī (may Allah have mercy on him) in particular have been interpreted abundantly in his Fatāwā. (Akābir ʿUlamāʾ-e Deoband Ittibāʿ-e Sharīʿat kī Roshnī mein, p. 14). (Taken from Al-Durrah al-Faridah: 1/61).

After this, some fifteen examples are presented; they may be consulted there.

Now, for the details of these meanings, consider the following passages. In Urdu, shifāʾ is used in the sense of treatment:

In the end even the physician turned to them for aid;

He came, he smiled, and he gave me healing.

The Prophet ﷺ said concerning Ḥaḍrat Ḥassān ibn Thābit (may Allah be pleased with him):

وقالت: سمعت رسول الله ﷺ يقول: هجاهم حسان فشفي واشتفي. (صحيح مسلم: ۲۴۹۰، ۲/۳۰۱).

Ḥaḍrat ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her) says: I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: Ḥassān, by composing satire against them, gave relief and himself found relief. Here shifāʾ does not mean that he healed an illness; rather it means that he cooled the rage and fury present in hearts.

قال النووي رحمه الله: (هجاهم حسان فشفي واشتفي) أي شفي المؤمنين واشتفي هو بما ناله من أعراض الكفار ومزقها ونافح عن الإسلام والمسلمين. (شرح صحيح مسلم: ۲/۳۰۱).

Imām al-Nawawī (may Allah have mercy on him) says: That is, by tearing to shreds the honour of the disbelievers and by defending Islam and the Muslims, he gave relief to the believers and himself found relief.

قال الأبي: أي شفي الألم الذي أجده بهجوهم، واشتفي هو في نفسه. (إكمال إكمال المعلم: ۸/۳۹۷).

ʿAllāmah al-Ubbī (may Allah have mercy on him) says: That is, Ḥassān healed me of the pain that I felt on account of their satire, and he himself was healed.

Likewise Ḥaḍrat Ḥassān ibn Thābit (may Allah be pleased with him) recited some verses in praise of Ḥaḍrat Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) and in them attributed shifāʾ to him; but the meaning of that too is not healing from illness, but rather the removal of doubts and misgivings from the heart:

(ديوان حسان بن ثابت: ۱/۱۶۸، ط: دار صادر بيروت، وكذا في المعجم الكبير: ۳۵۹۳، ۳/۸۹۸، والمستدرك للحاكم: ۶۳۱۶، ۳/۶۶۱، والاستذكار: ۴۱۳۰۷، ۲۷/۳۲۲، ومجمع الزوائد: ۱۵۵۲۶، ۹/۲۸۴، وطرح التثريب: ۱/۶۳).

Translation: Whenever Ḥaḍrat Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with him) appears in any place, you will see his excellence in every gathering. When he speaks, he leaves no scope for others to say anything, and his speech consists of such choice and flowing words that you will see no gap between them filled by any kind of verbal filler.

Whatever difficulties there may be in hearts, he is sufficient and healing for them all; even for the most acute of men he leaves no doubt or misgiving, whether in earnest or in jest.

You have reached the heights without any toil, and you have traversed the summits of eminence, while being no base or low-ranking man.

By your very nature you are worthy of love and generosity, being victorious and successful in your aim, and there is nothing in your make-up such as cowardice or ignorance.

Besides this, in a great many other hadiths too shifāʾ has been attributed to other than Allah:

وإذا شك في نفسه شيء سأل رجلاً، فشفاه منه، وأوشك أن لا تجدوه، والذي لا إله إلا هو ما أذكر ما غبر من الدنيا إلا كالثغب شرب صفوه وبقي كدره. (صحيح البخاري: ۲۹۶۴، ۱/۴۱۶).

And if a doubt arises within you concerning some matter, ask a scholar, so that you may be set at rest. A time is coming when you will not find any man who can state a ruling exactly. By the Being besides whom there is no deity! What remains of this world is like the water of a pool whose clear and wholesome part has already been drunk and whose turbid part is what is left.

قال العيني: أي: أزال مرض التردد فيه وأجاب له بالحق. (عمدة القاري: ۱۴/۲۲۶).

ʿAllāmah al-ʿAynī (may Allah have mercy on him) says: The meaning of shifāʾ here is the removal of the malady of hesitation and the giving of a correct answer.

ثم رجع إلى أبي ذر رضي الله عنه فقال له: رأيته يأمر بمكارم الأخلاق، وكلاماً ما هو بالشعر، فقال: ما شفيتني مما أردت، فتزود وحمل شنة له فيها ماء، حتى قدم مكة، أي لم تجبني بجواب يشفيني من مرض الجهل. (صحيح البخاري: ۳۸۶۱/۱/۵۴۵).

On his return he told Ḥaḍrat Abū Dharr (may Allah be pleased with him): I saw him myself; he enjoins noble character upon people, and the speech I heard from him is not poetry. At this Abū Dharr (may Allah be pleased with him) said: You have not fully set me at rest concerning the purpose for which I sent you. In the end he himself packed provisions, took a waterskin filled with water, and came to Makkah — that is: you have not given me an answer that would cure me of the malady of ignorance.

قال: فلدغ سيد ذلك الحي فشفوا له بكل شيء لا ينفعه شيء، فقال بعضهم: لو أتيتم هؤلاء الرهط الذين نزلوا بكم لعل أن يكون عند بعضهم شيء ينفع صاحبكم. (سنن أبي داود: ۳۴۱۸، ۲/۴۸۵). وقوله فشفوا له بكل شيء معناه عالجوه بكل شيء مما يستشفي به، والعرب تضع الشفاء موضع العلاج. قال الشاعر:

(معالم السنن: ۳/۱۰۱).

The chief of that clan had been stung by some venomous creature; they treated him in every way, but no benefit was coming about. Then one of them said: If you were to go to these people who have come and encamped here, it may be that one of them has something that will benefit your companion. Here too "they healed him by every means" means that they treated him by every means; and the Arabs use shifāʾ in the sense of treatment. A poet has said: I shall accept the verdict of the soothsayers of Yamāmah and Ḥajr, if they treat me.

فقلن مثل ما قالت، فرجعت إليه، فأخبرتها بقولهن، قال: فقالت: نعم وأشفيك، سمعت رسول الله ﷺ يقول: أهلوا يا آل محمد بعمرة في حج. (مسند أحمد: ۲۶۵۴۸، ۴۴/۱۷۲).

All the Mothers of the Believers gave the same answer. I came back to Ḥaḍrat Umm Salamah (may Allah be pleased with her) and informed her of the answer of all the Mothers of the Believers, whereupon she said: Yes, I shall remove your ignorance. I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: O family of Muḥammad, enter iḥrām for ʿumrah together with ḥajj.

In the light of these reports it becomes clear that shifāʾ in the sense of treatment may be attributed to other than Allah, whereas curing an illness and bringing the treatment to its desired goal is the work of Allah Most High alone.

﴿وإذا مرضت فهو يشفين﴾ [الشعراء: ۸۰].

When I fall ill, it is He who heals me.

فقال ثابت: يا أبا حمزة، اشتكيت، فقال أنس: ألا أرقيك برقية رسول الله ﷺ؟ قال: بلي، قال: اللهم رب الناس، مذهب البأس، اشف أنت الشافي، لا شافي إلا أنت، شفاء لا يغادر سَقَماً. (صحيح البخاري: ۵۷۴۲، ۲/۸۸۵).

Thābit (may Allah have mercy on him) said: O Abū Ḥamzah, I have fallen ill. Ḥaḍrat Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) said: Then why should I not recite over you and breathe upon you that supplication which the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to recite? Thābit (may Allah have mercy on him) said: Please do so. Ḥaḍrat Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) then recited this supplication over him and breathed upon him: O Allah, Lord of mankind, Remover of affliction, grant healing; You alone are the Healer, there is no healer but You; grant such healing as leaves no illness behind.

فسمع جليس للملك كان قد عمي، فأتاه بهدايا كثيرة، فقال: ما هاهنا لك أجمع، إن أنت شفيتني، فقال: إني لا أشفي أحداً إنما يشفي الله، فإن أنت آمنت بالله دعوت الله فشفاك، فآمن بالله فشفاه الله. (صحيح مسلم: ۳۰۰۵، ۲/۴۱۵).

When a courtier of the king, who had gone blind, heard of this, he brought many gifts to the boy and said: All this wealth is yours if you heal me. The boy said: I do not heal anyone; healing is the work of Allah Most High. If you believe in Allah, I shall supplicate Allah and He will cure you. That courtier believed in Allah, and Allah cured him.

In the hadith cited above, "لا شافي إلا أنت", and in other hadiths like it, this same thing is stated: that to crown a treatment with success is the act of Allah Most High alone.

The upshot is that shifāʾ has two meanings: to remove the malady of ignorance or a spiritual malady — in this sense shifāʾ is attributed to other than Allah, کما مر — and, secondly, to grant healing from an outward illness. If this second meaning is intended, then this healing is of two kinds: one is to carry out treatment, and this may be attributed to other than Allah, examples of which have been given; the second is to bring the treatment to its desired goal and to bestow health, and this meaning is exclusive to Allah Most High. The attribution of shifāʾ to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ is construed upon the first and the second meanings; the third meaning is not intended.

2. There are two cases in calling the Prophet ﷺ "كاشف الغمة، كاشف الكرب" and "صاحب الفرج":

(a) The remover of harm (كاشف الضر) is the Being of Allah Most High alone, and if a person uses this expression for other than Allah with the conviction that that man of himself, above the chain of means, removes hardship, then this is shirk.

(b) But if a person holds the belief that a man can remove a hardship within the chain of means, and calls him كاشف الضر in that respect, then this is permissible — as when Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him) had an ailment in his eye and it was cured by the touch of the Prophet ﷺ. Nevertheless it is better to avoid expressions of this kind; and the general public in particular, who cannot distinguish in these fine matters, ought to abstain altogether from beliefs and expressions of this sort.

It should be borne in mind that attributing the removal of hardship to the Prophet ﷺ is likewise correct within the chain of means — for example, that he helped in the distress of hunger, or that he treated an illness.

Yes, to regard other than Allah as disposing of affairs above the chain of means, and to ask him for one's needs, is impermissible and unlawful. Allah Most High has said in the Noble Qurʾān: ﴿إنا سنلقي عليك قولاً ثقيلاً﴾. Various explanations may be given of thaqīlan in it:

(1) ثقيل نزوله،

(2) ثقيل تجويده وتلاوته،

(3) ثقيل علمه،

(4) ثقيل تحمله،

(5) ثقيل دعوته،

(6) ثقيل أجره وثوابه،

(7) ثقيل وزنه في ميزان الحسنات،

(8) ثقيل توحيده.

The tawḥīd of the Noble Qurʾān — that above the chain of means one must ask for one's needs from Allah Most High alone, and that it is not in the power of anyone other than Allah Most High to render help above the chain of means — is the theme of the Noble Qurʾān that runs from beginning to end. In Sūrat al-Fātiḥah, which is the very core of the Qurʾān, we recite again and again in ṣalāh "إياك نعبد وإياك نستعين", namely that the One who, unseen and above the chain of means, makes and mars affairs is Allah Most High alone. For this reason there are innumerable verses and hadiths forbidding the seeking of help above the chain of means from other than Allah:

﴿ولا تدع من دون الله ما لا ينفعك ولا يضرك فإن فعلت فإنك إذاً من الظالمين﴾ [يونس: ۱۰۶].
﴿ومن أضل ممن يدعو من دون الله من لا يستجيب له إلى يوم القيامة وهم عن دعائهم غافلون﴾ [الأحقاف: ۵].
﴿إن الذين تدعون من دون الله عباد أمثالكم فادعوهم فليستجيبوا لكم إن كنتم صادقين﴾ [الأعراف: ۱۹۴].
﴿والذين تدعون من دونه ما يملكون من قطمير، إن تدعوهم لا يسمعوا دعائكم ولو سمعوا ما استجابوا لكم ويوم القيامة يكفرون بشرككم ولا ينبئك مثل خبير﴾ [الفاطر: ۱۳، ۱۴].
﴿أمن يجيب المضطر إذا دعاه ويكشف السوء ويجعلكم خلفاء الأرض أإله مع الله قليلاً ما تذكرون﴾ [النمل: ۶۲].

Seeking help from someone within the chain of means is permitted:

﴿من أنصاري إلى الله﴾ [الصف: ۱۴]، ﴿إن تنصروا الله ينصركم ويثبت أقدامكم﴾ [محمد: ۷]، ﴿آتوني زبر الحديد﴾ [الكهف: ۹۶]

and it is established in this way by innumerable texts.

قلت: كاشف الضر في الحقيقة هو الله تعالى إما بواسطة الأسباب أو بغيرها.

(Rūḥ al-Bayān: 3/23).

ومنها إن ظن أن الميت يتصرف في الأمور دون الله تعالى واعتقاده ذلك كفر. (البحر الرائق: ۲/۲۹۸).

To hold the belief that acts above the chain of means issue from any created being besides Allah Most High is shirk, because to remove the calamities and hardships of the creation with such an attribute is exclusive to Allah Most High. If, however, Ḥaḍrat ʿAlī (may Allah be pleased with him) is called mushkil-kushā (remover of difficulties) figuratively, then shirk is not entailed by it; even so, the general public, who cannot recognise this distinction, ought to avoid holding beliefs of this kind. (Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah: 1/191).

To utter these words with the belief that he is present and watching and knower of the unseen is manifest shirk, while to say them by way of ardour and love is permissible. And to call the Prophet ﷺ mushkil-kushā in the sense that he has been appointed by God to resolve difficulties is falsehood and disbelief; but in the sense that difficulties are resolved through seeking a means of approach by him and through his supplication, it is true and permissible. (Fatāwā Farīdiyyah: 1/72).

He was asked: how is it to say ʿAlī mushkil-kushā? He replied: With an interpretation it is permissible, that is, the resolver of scholarly difficulties; but for the general public it is certainly liable to misconception, and for that reason it is objectionable. (Ashraf al-Aḥkām: 47).

Note: from this justification of the permissibility of these expressions it should not be understood that istighāthah (calling upon another for aid) is permissible. Between istighāthah and tawassul (seeking a means of approach) there is a difference of earth and sky. To call upon other than Allah for aid is utterly unlawful, as has become clear from the discussion above, whereas the Ahl al-Sunnah wa al-Jamāʿah are agreed upon the permissibility of tawassul. (Al-Durrah al-Faridah Sharḥ Qaṣīdat al-Burdah 2/654; Imdād al-Fatāwā 5/101; Nashr al-Ṭīb 247; Kifāyat al-Muftī 1/416; Al-Muhannad ʿalā al-Mufannad 42; Fatāwā Maḥmūdiyyah 3/105; Tawassul o Istighāthah bi-ghayr Allāh awr Ghayr Muqallidīn kā Madhhab).

3. The expressions

اللهم صل على سيدنا محمد

. منتهي علمك

and

وارحم سيدنا محمداً حتى لا يبقي من الرحمة شيء

have also been transmitted in the narrations, from which it becomes clear that the literal sense is not intended; rather this is a figurative expression of abundance, and passages of this kind are invariably construed as hyperbole.

وأخبرنا أبو القاسم الحرفي، أخبرنا أحمد بن سليمان، حدثنا عبد الله، قال محمود بن غيلان: حدثنا أبو أسامة، حدثني خالد بن محدوج أبو روح، قال: سمعت أنس بن مالك يقول: إن داود نبي الله ﷺ ظن في نفسه أن أحداً لم يمدح خالقه أفضل مما مدحه، فإن ملكاً نزل وهو قاعد في المحراب والبركة إلى جنبه، فقال: يا داود افهم إلى ما يصوت الضفدع، فأنصت داود إذا الضفدع يمدحه بمدحة لم يمدحه بها داود. فقال له الملك: كيف تري يا داود؟ فهمت ما قالت؟ قال: نعم. قال: ما ذا قالت؟ قال: قالت: سبحانك وبحمدك منتهي علمك يا رب. قال داود: لا، والذي جعلني نبيه إني لم أمدحه بهذا. (شعب الإيمان: ٦/۳۲۰/٤۲٦۱).
حدثنا محمد بن حموس بن نصر القطان الهمداني، ثنا عمر بن حفص الوصابي الحمصي، ثنا سعيد بن موسى الأزدي، عن سفيان الثوري، عن عمرو بن دينار، عن نافع، عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنه قال: جاءوا برجل إلى النبي ﷺ فشهدوا عليه أنه سرق ناقة لهم، فأمر به النبي ﷺ أن يقطع فولي الرجل وهو يقول: اللهم صل على محمد حتى لا يبقي من صلاتك شيء، وبارك على محمد حتى لا يبقي من بركاتك شيء، وسلم على محمد حتى لا يبقي من السلام شيء، فتكلم الجمل فقال: يا محمد إنه بريء من سرقتي. (كتاب الدعاء للطبراني: ۱/۳۲۲/۱۰۵۵).

4. Concerning Dalāʾil al-Khayrāt, some of the noble scholars have said that if anyone has a disquiet in his mind, he should leave out these ambiguous passages and use passages that are free of ambiguity.

5. The use of the word ḥāḍir (present) for Allah Most High carries the same meaning as that of the verse ﴿وهو معكم أين ما كنتم﴾.

The word nāẓir (watching) for Allah Most High does not carry the meaning that the Muʿtazilah take; rather the meaning intended is that which comes in a hadith, "فناظر كيف تعملون". And the texts are replete with the application of naẓar to Allah, Glorified and Most High:

وكان فيما قال: إن الدنيا حلوة خضرة، وإن الله مستخلفكم فيها فناظر كيف تعملون، ألا فاتقوا الدنيا واتقوا النساء. (سنن الترمذي: ۲۱۹۱).
باب ما جاء في النظر، قال الله تعالي: عسى ربكم أن يهلك عدوكم ويستخلفكم في الأرض فينظر كيف تعملون. [الأعراف: ۱۲۹]، وقال: إن الذين يشترون بعهد الله وأيمانهم ثمناً قليلاً أولئك لا خلاق لهم في الآخرة ولا يكلمهم الله ولا ينظر إليهم يوم القيامة ولا يزكيهم ولهم عذاب أليم.

[آل عمران: ۷۷].

(Kitāb al-Asmāʾ wa al-Ṣifāt of al-Bayhaqī: 2/424).
