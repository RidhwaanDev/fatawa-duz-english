### TITLE
Whether Minā and Muzdalifah are joined to Makkah

### QUESTION
If a person reaches Makkah on the first of Dhū al-Ḥijjah and intends to stay for twenty days, will he pray the full prayer during his stay in Makkah, or will he shorten it? Is such a person a resident or a traveller?

### ANSWER
حامداً و مصلیاً: That person is not a resident but a traveller. He should shorten his prayers in Makkah, and he should shorten them at Minā, ʿArafāt and Muzdalifah as well, and so on. بحر: ۲/۱٤۳، (فتاوی محمودیہ: ۳/۱۸٤).

Note: this passage shows that Makkah and Minā are two separate places. The intention to stay will therefore not be valid; the person remains a traveller as before and will shorten his prayers.

Khayr al-Fatāwā states:

There is no need for much discussion about ʿArafāt, because the pilgrims do not spend the night there, and going off somewhere during the day does not affect an intention to stay. Spending the night at Muzdalifah, however, will invalidate an intention to stay in Makkah, because Muzdalifah is not part of Makkah, nor is there any evidence before us that it falls within the fināʾ (the outskirts that serve a town) of Makkah. Moreover, Muzdalifah is not joined to Minā; the Valley of Muḥassir lies between Minā and Muzdalifah. Al-Durr al-Mukhtār states: المزدلفۃ کلہا موقف الا وادی محسر ہ و واد بین منی و مزدلفۃ. Even supposing they were joined, it makes no sense to treat the whole of Muzdalifah, which extends for about two miles, as a dependency of Minā. If, for example, a plain ten miles long and broad adjoins a city, how could it be right to regard that entire plain as the outskirts of the city? If Muzdalifah does not fall within the outskirts of Makkah, then ʿArafāt does so even less, since the distance between Minā and ʿArafāt is about six miles. That the security posts are located beyond ʿArafāt for military and administrative purposes proves nothing, because such posts are presumably built along the whole route, just as posts have been built along the road between Makkah and Madīnah. As for Minā and Makkah, according to the explicit statements of the jurists they are beyond doubt two independent places, each with its own fixed boundary — here begins Minā — and in terms of the rites of Hajj too these two places will always be regarded as towns in their own right.

The rulings connected with Minā are to be performed in that area alone; it is not valid to perform them in Makkah, and the same holds the other way round. Besides, when a person sets out from Makkah and enters the limits of Minā, it is true to say of him that he has left Makkah, and it is correct to say that he is at Minā and not in Makkah. Such a denial is not correct of the different neighbourhoods of a single city: it is not right to say that a man is in Nazimabad and not in Karachi. From these considerations it appears that places which the sharia has treated as independent, whose related rulings are also distinct, and which have clearly fixed boundaries, should be counted as two separate places for the purposes of travel. So in the case in question the person mentioned will not be a resident before the Hajj, exactly as all the jurists have stated.

"أن الحاج إذا دخل مكة في أيام العشر ونوي الإقامة نصف شهر لا يصح لأنه لا بد له من الخروج إلى عرفات فلا يتحقق الشرط". (البحر: ۲/۱٤۳)

فقط واللہ اعلم ۔ (خیر الفتاوی: ٤/۲٤۸).

ʿUmdat al-Fiqh states:

The place in which a person intends to stay must be fit for staying — that is, a town or a village. If he intends to stay in open country, on a river, or on an uninhabited island, the intention is not valid; and Muzdalifah is not such a place.

Further, if someone intends to stay for fifteen days in two places, and those two places are independent and separate — like Makkah and Minā, or Kūfah and Ḥīrah — he does not become a resident. (عمدۃ الفقہ: ۲/٤۱۵ ۔ ٤۱٦).

Muʿallim al-Ḥujjāj states:

If a travelling pilgrim arrives in Makkah at a time when fewer than fifteen days remain before the eighth of Dhū al-Ḥijjah, and he intends to stay in Makkah for fifteen days or more, his intention to stay will not be valid and he remains a traveller, because on the eighth he will certainly go to Minā and on the ninth to ʿArafāt. Such a person should therefore shorten his prayers. (معلم الحجاج: ۱۵۷).

(4) Counting Minā separately is also the easier and more convenient position, because when hundreds of thousands of pilgrims have fourteen days or fewer in Makkah up to the Day of Tarwiyah, then, once the days at Minā are set apart, such a pilgrim becomes a traveller and gains both the concession of shortening the prayer and the concession that, as a traveller, the sacrifice is not obligatory on him.

"إن النبي ﷺ قال لمعاذ بن جبل وأبي موسى الأشعري عند ما أرسلهما إلى اليمن فقال لهما: يسرا ولا تعسرا، وبشرا ولا تنفرا، وكذلك قال عليه الصلاة والسلام: يسروا ولا تعسروا، وبشروا ولا تنفروا، واعلموا أن أحداً منكم لن يدخل الجنة بعمله". (بخاري: ۲/٦۲۲، باب بعث أبي موسى ومعاذ إلى اليمن قبل حجة الوداع، ومسلم).

وقال عليه الصلاة والسلام: لا تشددوا فيشد الله عليكم، فإن قوماً شددوا على أنفسهم فشدد عليهم فتلك بقاياهم في الصوامع. (أخرجه أبو داود برقم ٤۹۰٤).

وقال الله تعالى في رخصة إفطار المريض والمسافر: ﴿يريد الله بكم اليسر، ولا يريد بكم العسر﴾.

ويدل على اعتبار اليسر واقعة تمرة خيبر في الحديث المشهور وفي آخره: لا تفعل بع الجمع بالدراهم ثم ابتع بالدراهم جنيباً. (رواه البخاري: ۱/۲۹۳، باب إذا أراد بيع تمر بتمر).

But making things easy does not mean declaring what is unlawful to be lawful, or what is wrong to be correct.

(5) If, whether the two are joined or separate, common usage and the government's demarcation differ, then it is common usage that should be given weight.

Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

The presence of cultivated fields between two settlements, or a gap of a bowshot — 137.16 metres — is a mark of separation. So if two places are regarded in common usage as two neighbourhoods of a single city, then despite the gap just mentioned the two are to be treated as one locality. (احسن الفتاوی: ٤/۷٤).

Custom and usage should be given preference over the government's demarcation. As long as custom does not conflict with a text of the sharia, custom should be acted upon. Allah says:

﴿وعلي المولود له رزقهن وكسوتهن بالمعروف﴾

What is meant by maʿrūf is what accords with custom and usage.

ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd (may Allah be pleased with him) said:

ماراه المسلمون حسناً فهو عند الله حسن".

The commentator on the Majallah writes:

ان العرف والعادة يكون حجة اذا لم يكن مخالفاً للنص ۔ (۱/۸۱. ۸۹. وشرح عقود رسم المفتي: ٤۱)

Indeed, in some cases, where the text itself rests on custom, custom will be taken as the basis. For example, in former times wheat and barley were sold by volume, so in the hadiths dealing with interest and the like they were treated as goods measured by volume; but when in later times they came to be sold by weight, they are to be treated as goods sold by weight. In our own day, for instance, people do not even know the old dry measure. So treating wheat and barley as goods sold by weight is not a change to the text, but an understanding of the intent and basis of the text.

ʿAllāmah Shāmī has quoted from Ibn al-Humām (may Allah have mercy on him) that the position of Imam Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) is the stronger one:

عن الثاني اعتبار العرف مطلقاً ورجحه الكمال وخرج عليه سغدي آفندي ۔ (الدر المختار: ۵/۱۷٦، باب الربا، سعيد. وشرح مجلة: ۱/۸۱، المادة: ۳٦).

In short, if the text itself follows custom, a change in custom does not entail abandoning the text, because custom is the underlying basis, and so custom is what counts.

(2) Mufti Muḥammad Ḥusayn, Dār al-Iftāʾ wa al-Irshād, Jāmiʿat al-Rashīd, Karachi.

(3) Mufti ʿAbd al-Wāḥid, Jāmiʿah Madīnah, Karīm Park, Wādī Road, Lahore.

(4) Mufti ʿIṣmatullāh, Dār al-Iftāʾ, Dār al-ʿUlūm Karachi.

(5) At the request of Mufti Muḥammad Taqī ʿUthmānī, a question was sent to the Dār al-Iftāʾ of Saudi Arabia, "رئاسة إدارة البحوث العلمية والإفتاء", asking: in earlier times Minā and Makkah were counted as two separate places, but nowadays the two are adjoining and close together, so are these two places to be counted as one, or as separate? To this the "اللجنة الدائمة" replied that they are two separate places, and that their closeness and adjoining count for nothing.

The reply bears the signatures of the senior muftis there, namely:

(1) ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn ʿAbdullāh ibn Muḥammad Āl al-Shaykh. (2) Ṣāliḥ ibn Fawzān al-Fawzān. (3) Aḥmad ibn ʿAlī Sayyir al-Mubārakī.

(4) ʿAbdullāh ibn Muḥammad al-Muṭlaq.

See the photocopy of this fatwa.

(6) In the same way Dr Saʿīd Aḥmad ʿInāyatullāh and others, who are among the teachers of advanced studies at Madrasah Ṣawlatiyyah, sent a question of this kind during the Hajj season of 2004 to the Mufti of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, Shaykh ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, asking whether Minā, Muzdalifah and ʿArafāt are separate places or dependencies of the city of Makkah.

In reply the Mufti told them by telephone that these places have a standing of their own and are not part of the city of Makkah.

(7) Mawlānā ʿAbd al-Ḥafīẓ Makkī, who has lived in Makkah for many years, says that Minā is in every case a place separate from Makkah. He also says: the claim of some people that Makkah and Minā have now become joined is contrary to fact; in the eyes of the sharia they have not become joined. In the company of Mufti Abū Lubābah, Mufti ʿAbd al-Ḥamīd and others, this writer went by car along every road leading from Minā to Makkah and from Makkah to Minā and measured the distance that lies between the two, and on no side was it less than 900 metres. How, then, can they be joined in the eyes of the sharia?

(8) Mufti Aḥmad Mumtāz (may his blessings continue), head of the Dār al-Iftāʾ at Jāmiʿah Khulafāʾ-e-Rāshidīn, Karachi, has a detailed fatwa on this, in which he says: I had occasion to visit Madrasah Ṣawlatiyyah and discussed this subject in detail with Mawlānā Hushaym, the principal of Madrasah Ṣawlatiyyah. At that time his opinion turned on the two places adjoining and facing one another, to which I submitted that in the question under discussion it is not a case of two villages adjoining each other, but of a town and an open waste. In the end he said that up to that day they had not considered the matter in this light.

(9) The fatwa mentioned above also includes the fatwa of Mufti ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Kawthar, son of Mawlānā Mufti ʿĀshiq Ilāhī Bulandshahrī, a teacher at Jāmiʿah Ṭayyibah in Madīnah. Having lately (1428 AH) inspected Minā and its surroundings, he wrote: the two are separate places; as far as we can judge, in common usage Minā is not called a neighbourhood of Makkah, and Minā has no settled population at all.

(10) Mufti Muḥammad ʿAbdullāh, the mufti of Jāmiʿah Khayr al-Madāris, Multan, has likewise denied in every respect that Minā is a part of Makkah: Minā is neither the outskirts of Makkah, nor is it joined to Makkah either in fact or in law.

(11) Mufti Ismāʿīl Toru of Jāmiʿah Islāmiyyah, Saddar, Rawalpindi has also stressed that by common usage Minā and Makkah are separate places, and that Minā is not even the outskirts of Makkah. For if it is being treated as the outskirts because it serves the needs of the city, that has been so from the very first day the Hajj was made obligatory; yet even so the jurists did not treat Minā as the outskirts of Makkah, nor did they rule that the prayer be completed in full there.

(12) Mufti Inʿāmullāh of Jāmiʿah Islāmiyyah, Islamabad has thrown light on every aspect of this question and has answered every proof and every doubt of those who hold the two places to be one.

He says that the jurists hold differing views on how union and separation are defined. He then quotes six views and writes: leaving aside which of these views is the stronger, on the face of it, according to none of the juristic views mentioned above are the two places joined; nor are the two places one in common usage — they are separate. This is because:

1. If in common usage Minā had already become joined to Makkah, there would have been no need to debate this question at all.

2. The earlier custom, by agreement, was that the two were separate, and there is no solid evidence that this old custom has changed.

3. People say: on the Day of Tarwiyah the pilgrims will go from Makkah to Minā; they are going; they have gone. And after the stoning and the sacrifice the pilgrims return from Minā to Makkah; they come back. All this is proof that the two places are distinct. In the same way:

﴿ثم أفيضوا من حيث أفاض الناس﴾

or

﴿فمن تعجل في يومين فلا إثم عليه﴾

or

﴿فإذا أفضتم من عرفات فاذكروا الله﴾

In these verses too, what is meant is the going to and fro between Makkah, ʿArafāt, Muzdalifah and Minā.

In short, a great many muftis incline to the older position and give their fatwa accordingly, and people are used to that older position. Moreover, Mufti Muḥammad Rafīʿ ʿUthmānī (may his bounties continue), during a discussion of this question, said that as long as there is no agreement or settled research on the matter, the old fatwa should continue to be followed; and in the old fatwa the two places, Minā and Makkah, are counted separately. The muftis at that gathering agreed with Mufti Rafīʿ. He said this in reply to Mawlānā Qārī Muḥammad Ḥanīf Jālandharī, the principal of Jāmiʿah Khayr al-Madāris, who had asked what they should tell the public as long as there is no agreement or settled research. The Mufti replied that the old fatwa should continue to be followed, and the other muftis agreed with him.

A look at the arguments of those who hold the two to be one:

Some of the evidences and doubts of those who hold Minā and Makkah to be one place, together with the answers to them, are set out briefly.

(1) Those who write on this subject generally argue from the fatwa of Faḍīlat al-Shaykh ʿAbdullāh ibn Subayyil.

On this matter it is better to refer to the scholars there rather than to form one's own estimate. Mufti Muḥammad Taqī ʿUthmānī (may his blessings continue) asked the most senior imam of the Sacred Mosque, Faḍīlat al-Shaykh, about this, and the substance of what the Shaykh wrote in reply is:

"Minā has become a part and a neighbourhood of the city of Makkah." The relevant portion of his reply is this:

الذي يظهر لنا أن منى أصبحت اليوم جزء من مدينة مكة

إن حكومة المملكة العربية السعودية تعد منى من مكة على اعتبار أنها حي من أحيائها إلا أن الحكومة تمنع البناء فيها لمصلحة عامة؛ لأنه لا يجوز لاحد أن يتملك ولا يختص بمنى ولا غيرها من المشاعر لقول النبي ﷺ: "منى مناخ من سبق".

Answer: Mufti Aḥmad Mumtāz of Jāmiʿah Khulafāʾ-e-Rāshidīn, Karachi has answered this:

(1) It is not correct to treat the opinion of Samāḥat al-Shaykh alone as the predominant and best-known usage.

(2) There is an apparent contradiction in the Shaykh's fatwa. He says that the government treats Minā as a neighbourhood of Makkah, and he mentions that this is his own inclination too; then he goes on to say, "the government forbids building and construction there." Now, being a neighbourhood and having no buildings cannot go together, for an open plain lying outside the settled area of a city is in no way called a neighbourhood of the town. Indeed, even an inhabited neighbourhood adjoining a city that has for some reason been demolished is no longer regarded as a neighbourhood of the city. How, then, can Minā be a neighbourhood of Makkah? Neighbourhoods that are inhabited in the vicinity of a city are, of course, called part of the city.

So if it is his own particular opinion that a plain lying outside the settled area is part of the city, that is not binding proof for everyone.

(3) The third point is that the words

"لأنه لا يجوز لا حد أن يتملك

"

show that the government wishes to keep Minā and the other sacred sites distinct from the city of Makkah, and for that reason does not allow anyone to build there.

(2) Some say that Minā should be called the outskirts of Makkah for four reasons: (1) the place of sacrifice is there; (2) it is the gathering place of the pilgrims; (3) it is a parking area for vehicles; (4) it is a place of recreation.

Answer: this is not correct, for several reasons.

(1) There is nothing among the amenities there that belongs specifically to Makkah. All the arrangements there are made for the performance of the rites, and the people of Makkah benefit from them only incidentally; but incidental benefit does not make them amenities of Makkah. The place of sacrifice too is for the performance of the rites, for the sacrifice of thanksgiving and so on. If, along with that, the people of Makkah are required to offer their Eid sacrifice there as well, that arrangement is merely incidental, and it does not make Minā the outskirts of Makkah.

(2) Even supposing the place of sacrifice were called an amenity of the city and Minā were therefore called outskirts, the outskirts would be only the particular enclosure set aside for the place of sacrifice; that would not make the whole plain of Minā outskirts.

(3) People park their vehicles there when the need arises, because it is near the city; the place is not designated for parking.

(4) There is no special park or the like there for recreation; people simply go there as they please.

(5) Is the Friday prayer valid at Minā or not? According to the Shaykhayn (Abū Ḥanīfah and Abū Yūsuf) it is valid; according to Imam Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him) it is not. Had Minā been the outskirts of Makkah, this difference between the Shaykhayn and Imam Muḥammad (may Allah have mercy on him) would never have arisen, because the Friday prayer is valid in the outskirts in every case, whether they adjoin the city or are separate from it.

This disagreement is clear proof that Minā is not the outskirts of Makkah.

(6) Even supposing that Minā is a place of recreation and sport for the people of Makkah, this still does not come under the needs or amenities of the town.

Al-Muwāfaqāt states:

أما الحاجات فمعناها أنها مفتقر إليها من حيث التوسعة ورفع الضيق المودي في الغالب إلى الحرج والمشقة

(الموفقات: ۲/۹).

(7) If Minā is called the outskirts of Makkah because the place of sacrifice is there, then Muzdalifah and ʿArafāt should also be called outskirts because they are places of standing; wherever the cause is found, the ruling follows. And if someone says that, since Muzdalifah and ʿArafāt are far from Makkah, they cannot be treated as the outskirts of Makkah, then according to the research of ʿAllāmah Shurunbulālī this objection carries no weight: in his view what counts is the amenities, not the distance.

(8) The reports quoted about Minā being outskirts are all passages from the Book of Jumuʿah. The Friday and Eid prayers are among the needs of a town, but shortening the prayer is not. Marāqī al-Falāḥ states:

ولا يلحق فناء المصر بالمصرفي حق المسافر، يلحق الفناء بالمصرفي حق صلاة الجمعة. (مراقي الفلاح: ص ۲۵٤).

(3) A common claim, on account of which this discussion became necessary, is the statement: "the position now is that the built-up area of Makkah has spread even beyond Minā, and Minā is a part of Makkah" — although formerly the two were separate. It is on this basis that many hold the two to be one, and they all say the same thing in different words. Some muftis said that they had written a fatwa at Madrasah Ṣawlatiyyah in Makkah during the Hajj season in the year 2000, corresponding to 1420 AH, and that it also carries the endorsements of the muftis who were visiting Madrasah Ṣawlatiyyah, such as Mufti Shabbīr Aḥmad Murādābādī, Mufti Fārūq Mīrathī, Mufti Aḥmad Khānpūrī, Mufti Salmān Manṣūrpūrī and others. The same thing was said at the tenth fiqh seminar of the Islamic Fiqh Academy: "formerly the built-up areas of the two were separate, but now the city of Makkah has expanded and become joined to the built-up area of Minā." Then those who wrote papers — Mawlānā Khālid Sayfullāh, Mawlānā ʿUbaydullāh Asʿadī, Mawlānā Irshād al-Qāsimī and others — all spoke with one voice and made this their proof, that the built-up areas of the two have become joined. (Taken from Taḥqīq, series no. 6, Idārah Ghufrān, Rawalpindi.)

Answer: the answer to this is obvious, namely that the claim is contrary to fact. That Minā was inhabited in the distant past is accepted, but in the present day Minā has no settled population at all, so that it could be treated as joined to Makkah. Moreover, a neighbourhood is a place where there are houses and streets, and Minā is not like that.

(4) Someone may raise the doubt that although Minā is not inhabited the whole year round but only during the Hajj season, remaining inhabited throughout the year is not a necessary condition, كما قال الآفندي رحمه الله: "بقاؤها مصراً ليس بشرط", and so during the Hajj season it should be treated as a town.

Answer: this idea is not correct. What ʿAllāmah Āfandī (may Allah have mercy on him) meant by his words "بقاؤها مصراً ليس بشرط" is that for a place to be a town it is not a condition that its status as a town continue permanently; it may be a town temporarily as well. But a place can become a town temporarily only when it is already a village; open country and wasteland never become a town.

(5) Another argument offered is that both come under one municipality. (Nidā-e-Shāhī, p. 53, December 2004.)

Answer: since no work goes on at Minā throughout the year, the municipality of Makkah does the work there and no separate municipality has been needed. This is no proof of its being a part of Makkah. Besides, some people say that it has its own municipality. واللہ اعلم بذلک

(6) If one looks at the state of Minā today, it appears to be a developed European city: there are modern air-conditioned tents, there are offices, and many other facilities besides, so it cannot be called open country — it is a settled area.

Answer: (1) These dwellings are not permanent structures, and the jurists lay down the condition of permanent structures. (2) No one takes up permanent residence in tents, and for a settled area it is a condition that the place be one of staying and settling. (3) Arab princes pitch even finer tents than these in open country, yet no one calls them a settled area. (4) There is a royal palace at Minā where servants and guards live, but their living there does not count towards its becoming a settled area.

In sum, at Minā the dwellings are not contiguous, it is not a place of settling, and people do not habitually take up residence there; indeed, in the sharia it is not even good to reside there.

عن عائشة رضي الله عنه ا قالت: قلت يا رسول الله! ألا نبني لك بناء بمنى يظلك؟ فقال رسول الله ﷺ: "لا منى مناخ من سبق". (ترمذي: ۱/۱۷۷، باب ما جاء في أن مني مناخ من سبق وكذا في أبو داود وابن ماجه).
