### TITLE
The Jamʿiyyat al-ʿUlamāʾ standing in the place of a judge

### QUESTION
Is the decision of the Jamʿiyyat of a country at the level of the decision and ruling of a judge or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, in disputes, transactions, social matters and the like, the decision of the Jamʿiyyat is equal to the decision and ruling of a judge; but in the prescribed punishments, retaliation and the like the ruling of the Jamʿiyyat cannot be enforced. Fatāwā Shāmī states:

مطلب في حكم تولية القضاء في بلاد تغلب عليها الكفار، وفي الفتح: وإذا لم يكن سلطان ولا من يجوز التقليد منه كما هو في بعض بلاد المسلمين غلب عليهم الكفار كقرطبة الآن يجب على المسلمين أن يتفقوا على واحد منهم يجعلونه والياً فيولي قاضياً ويكون هو الذي يقضي بينهم وكذا ينصبوا إماماً يصلي بهم الجمعة. وهذا هو الذي تطمئن النفس إليه فليعتمد، نهر. (فتاوى الشامي: ۵/۳٦۹، سعيد).

Ḥaḍrat Mawlānā Ashraf ʿAlī Thānawī (may Allah have mercy on him) writes in al-Ḥīlat al-Nājizah:

In those lands where no sharʿī judge is present — as is the case in all the cities of India apart from the Islamic states — those officials there who hold authority from the government to settle matters of this kind: if they are Muslims and give their decision in accordance with the sharīʿah, their decision too takes the place of the decision of a judge. And if no Muslim official is available, or a decision in accordance with the sharīʿah is not given by his court, then, in accordance with the Mālikī school, a group of religious Muslims should form a panchayat and carry out an investigation in the manner described above; and if they issue a decision after a complete investigation, this decision too will have the status of the decision of a judge. (الحیلۃ الناجزۃ، ص ۱۰۷، ۱۰۸)۔
