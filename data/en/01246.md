### TITLE
The kinds of tawassul and their ruling in the sharia

### QUESTION
Suppose a person goes to a graveyard, presents himself at the grave of a pious man, and says: "O such-and-such saint, pray to Allah for me that He grant me a child." Or he says: "By Allah's command, grant me a child." Or he says: "I supplicate to Allah through the mediation of this pious man." What is the ruling of the sharia on these three cases?

### ANSWER
(1) Saying "pray for me" rests on the question of whether the dead can hear, which has been disputed since early times. Some detail on this has already been given in the miscellaneous rulings on funerals. That the dead hear a greeting of peace is established from the narrations, but anything beyond that is disputed. These words should therefore not be used.

(2) Saying "grant me a child" is shirk, and it is necessary to avoid it. It cannot be treated as a rational metaphor either, for a rational metaphor is, for example, "أنبت الربيع البقل" — "the spring made the herbage grow." The one who says this believes that the spring itself can do nothing; the spring is merely the time and occasion of the growth. Here, by contrast, ordinary people take the saint to be everything. A second point is that the spring is at least the outward cause of growth, whereas the saint is not the outward cause of a child.

(3) The third case, supplicating through the mediation of a pious man, is sound according to the scholars of Deoband. Ḥāfiẓ Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) also transmitted tawassul from the early generations, and he declared tawassul through a person himself to be permissible where what is meant by it is tawassul through love for him. See the photocopy of his book "قاعدۃ جلیلۃ فی التوسل و الوسیلۃ".

Shaykh al-Islām ʿAllāmah Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) accepts tawassul through a person himself with this interpretation; yet certain incautious writers of our own age count it as plain shirk and regard whoever does it as outside the religion and condemned to remain in the Fire for ever.

Shaykh ʿAbd al-Wahhāb Najdī (may Allah have mercy on him) also adopted a moderate position on the question of tawassul. He says that the question of tawassul belongs to the questions of fiqh, and that although in our view it is disliked — neither unlawful nor an innovation — we still do not condemn those who do it, because in matters of independent reasoning people may not condemn one another.

Consider his words:

فقال: فالفرق ظاهراً جداً، وليس الكلام مما نحن فيه، فكون بعض يرخص بالتوسل بالصالحين، وبعضهم يخصه بالنبي ﷺ، وأكثر العلماء ينهي عن ذلك ويكرهه، فهذه المسألة من مسائل الفقه، وإن كان الصواب عندنا قول الجمهور من أنه مكروه، فلا ننكر على من فعله، ولا إنكار في مسائل الاجتهاد. (فتاوى الشيخ الإمام محمد بن عبد الوهاب في مجموعة المؤلفات القسم الثالث: ص ٦۸).
