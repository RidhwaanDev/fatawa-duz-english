### TITLE
Taking cash from a medical-aid company

### QUESTION
Is the form of medical aid in which a sick person receives a sum of money and then arranges his own treatment permissible or not?

### ANSWER
This form of medical aid — that money be taken from them for treatment and the like — is absolutely impermissible, because the sum received will inevitably be more than the sum you deposited, and the excess you receive is not in exchange for anything. Being an addition to your money, it falls squarely within the category of plain interest and is therefore impermissible.

Consider what al-Taʿrīfāt states:

الربا هو في اللغة: الزيادة، وفي الشرع: هو فضل خال عن عوض شرط لأحد العاقدين. (التعريفات للجرجاني، ص ۱۱۲).

Iʿlāʾ al-Sunan states:

الفضل المشروط في القرض ربا محرم لا يجوز للمسلم من أخيه المسلم أبداً، لا جماع المجتهدين على حرمته. (إعلاء السنن: ۱٤/۵۱۸، إدارة القرآن).

Ḥujjat Allāh al-Bālighah states:

الربا هو القرض على أن يؤدي إليه أكثر وأفضل مما أخذ. (حجة الله البالغة: ۲/۲۸۲، قديمي).

One should not take the money oneself from the company under the heading of medical treatment; rather the company itself should undertake responsibility for the treatment in return for that money and arrange the treatment. In that case, in return for the money you deposited with the company, the company has provided you with the facility of treatment. Although in this form the consideration for the price is undefined, since this indefiniteness does not lead to dispute and people do transact on this basis, there is room for it. Nevertheless, some scholars forbid even this form and declare it impermissible, so out of caution one should not enter into such a transaction. And even where there is room for it, these companies generally engage in interest-based dealings, so the harm of cooperating with such companies will certainly follow. For the evidence, see the detailed fatwa on medical aid given above.

واللہ سبحانہ و تعالی اعلم
