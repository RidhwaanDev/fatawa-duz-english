### TITLE
The ruling on an heir waiving his share before the estate is divided

### QUESTION
A man passed away leaving four sons. One of them waived his right; has his right thereby lapsed or not? Some people say that the right of inheritance does not lapse merely by being waived. Similarly, a bequest was made in favour of a certain man, and the legatee waived his right before the estate was divided; can he now claim it again or not? On this question there appears to be a contradiction in the fatwas of the senior scholars (may Allah have mercy on them). بینوا توجروا۔

### ANSWER
As a general rule the jurists have laid down the maxim:

"الإرث جبري لا يسقط بالإسقاط"

that is, inheritance is compulsory and does not lapse by being waived. In other words, if an heir says, "I have given up my share," or "I do not want it," his right does not lapse. Some jurists, however, have written that before the division the right of inheritance is liable to lapse and does lapse when it is waived: that is, if the heir says, "I have waived my right," or says, "I have forgone my right," or says, "I release you from any claim," then the right of claim is gone. Consider what the jurists have written:

ولو قال: تركت حقي من الميراث أو برئت منها ومن حصتي لا يصح وهو على حقه لأن الإرث جبري لا يصح تركه. (فتاوى الشامي: ۸/۸۹، سعيد).
(وكذا في لسان الحكام، ص ۲۳٦، ط: القاهرة، وتنقيح الفتاوى الحامدية: ۲/٤۳، كتاب الدعوي).

Other jurists have written that the right of inheritance does lapse when it is waived. Consider what al-Ashbāh states:

وذكر الشيخ الإمام المعروف بـ خواهر زاده، أن حق الموصي له وحق الوارث قبل القسمة غير متأكد يحتمل السقوط بالإسقاط، انتهى

فقد علم أن حق الغانم قبل القسمة

وحق الوارث قبل القسمة على قول خواهر زاده يسقط بالإسقاط. (الأشباه والنظائر: ۳/ ۵٤، ۵۵، الفن الثالث: الجمع والفرق، ما يقبل الإسقاط من الحقوق وما لا يقبل). (وكذا في فتاوى الشامي: ۵/٦٤٤، سعيد، وفتاوى قاضيخان بهامش الهندية: ۳/۲۱٤، والبحر الرائق: ۵/۲٤۳، دار المعرفة).
وفي حاشية الهداية: والإبراء عن الدعوي صحيح فإن من قال لغيره: أبرأتك من دعوي هذا العين صح ولو ادعي بعد ذلك لم يسمع. (حاشية الهداية: ۳/۲٤۷، كتاب الصلح).
وفي الهداية: وله أن القسمة قضاء على الميت إذ التركة مبقاة على ملكه قبل القسمة حتى لو حدثت الزيادة تنفذ وصاياه فيها وتقضي ديونه عنها بخلاف ما بعد القسمة. (الهداية: ٤/٤۱۲، كتاب القسمة).
وينظر: (فتح القدير: ۹/٤۳۰، والبناية: ۸/٦۱۸، والكفاية على هامش فتح القدير: ۷/۳۸۳، رشيديه).

It is recorded in Jāmiʿ al-Fuṣūlayn:

قال أحد ورثته: برئت من تركة أبي، يبرأ الغرماء عن الدين بقدر حصته

لو قال وارث: تركت حقي، لا يبطل حقه إذ الملك لا يبطل بالترك، والحق يبطل به، حتى أن أحد الغانمين لو قال قبل القسمة: تركت حقي، بطل حقه. (جامع الفصولين: ۲/۳۹).

From the wording of Jāmiʿ al-Fuṣūlayn it emerges that when a thing is a man's property, giving it up does not extinguish his ownership; but where a man merely holds a right over something, then if he waives his right it does lapse.

This is further clarified by the passage from Fatāwā Qāḍīkhān that follows. If a man owns a watercourse which runs through another man's land, and the owner of the land sells the land and the owner of the watercourse consents, his ownership is still not extinguished and he may take the price of it from the purchaser. But if the watercourse is not his property and he merely holds the right to conduct water through it, then once he gives up that right it is extinguished and he cannot claim it again. The difference between the two is precisely this: in the first case he has ownership, and in the second he has only a right.

قال: رجل له مسيل ماء في دار غيره فباع

وإن كان له حق إجراء الماء دون رقبة النهر، لا شيء له من الثمن، ولا سبيل له على المسيل بعد ذلك، كرجل أوصي لرجل بسكني داره فمات الموصي، فباع الوارث الدار ورضي به الموصي له جاز البيع وبطل سكناه

وذكر في الكتاب: إذا أوصي لرجل بثلث ماله ومات الموصي، فصالح الوارث الموصي له من الثلث على السدس جاز الصلح. (فتاوى قاضيخان بهامش الفتاوى الهندية: ۳/۲۱٤).

Consider now the apparently conflicting fatwas of the senior scholars:

From the following fatwas it appears that the right of inheritance does not lapse when it is waived: (1) It is recorded in ʿAzīz al-Fatāwā:

Question: A man refused to take his right from his father's property. Are his children now entitled to receive their lawful share of their grandfather's property or not? Answer: The children of the one who gave up his right may take a share of their grandfather's property,

كما في الشباه والنظائر (۳/۵۳) لو قال الوارث: تركت حقي لم يبطل حقه الخ وفي الشرع ضابطة؛ أنه إن كان ملكاً لأنه مالم يبطل بذلك كما لو مات عن ابنين فقال أحدهما تركت نصيبي من الميراث لم يبطل لأنه لا زم لا يترك بالترك.

(ʿAzīz al-Fatāwā, vol. 1, p. 740).

In another place it is recorded:

Zayd's ownership of his lawful share, namely the sixth, remains, and his right has not lapsed; that share will therefore go to his heirs, and his renunciation will not be given effect. (ʿAzīz al-Fatāwā: 1/739, ed. Dār al-Ishāʿat).

(2) Aḥsan al-Fatāwā states:

The considered position on giving up a share is this: if the right lies in a debt — which includes the dower — then the right lapses through a release, whether express or implied. But if the right lies in a specific item of property, a release is not valid; a gift is required. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 9/277). In another place it is recorded: Pardon and release apply to debts; pardon and release of a specific item of property are not valid. (Aḥsan al-Fatāwā: 9/279).

(3) Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah mentions:

Inheritance is a compulsory right; no one can of his own accord annul or extinguish it. (Fatāwā Ḥaqqāniyyah: 6/561), on the authority of Takmilah Radd al-Muḥtār:

الإرث جبري لا يسقط بالإسقاط. (تكملة رد المحتار: ۷/۵۰۳، مطلب في واقعة الفتوى، سعيد).

(4) Ashraf al-Aḥkām, the supplement to Imdād al-Fatāwā, states:

For women to waive their right of inheritance is not valid in the Sharīʿah. He said: The refusal of those daughters who, the moment their father dies, decline to take the income and the land is not to be given effect. In the first place, at that moment the grief is fresh, and in grief a person gives no thought to her own gain or loss. Secondly, since the custom has taken hold that sisters are regarded as excluded from inheritance, they are afraid of the disgrace of taking their right. Thirdly, they do not even know what their right is — how much it is and of what extent. Let the time of grief pass, and then tell them, "This is your lawful right and you will have to take it." Then let them see the size of their income and enjoy the benefit of it; and if after that any of them gives it away, there is no harm in it. But we will show you that after that, one or two out of a hundred will turn out to waive their right. So the way sisters nowadays waive their right in favour of their brothers is not valid in the Sharīʿah, and it is stated plainly in the hadith: "Beware! The property of a believer is not lawful to take without his heartfelt consent." On the authority of Rajāʾ al-Liqāʾ, p. 24. (Ashraf al-Aḥkām, p. 242).

From the following fatwas it appears that the right of inheritance does lapse when it is waived: (1) Kifāyat al-Muftī states:

A husband claiming again a share his wife had waived: Once the sister waived her share in your favour, none of her heirs any longer has the right to claim it. (Kifāyat al-Muftī: 13/439, Jāmiʿah Fārūqiyyah).

(2) It is recorded in Āp ke Masāʾil aur un kā Ḥal:

If a sister genuinely and gladly gave up her share, her children have no right of claim, because the children's right is established only after their mother's death. During their mother's lifetime they have no right over her property, and so if she renounces it in someone's favour, the children cannot prevent her. (Āp ke Masāʾil aur un kā Ḥal: 7/400).

(3) A detailed fatwa is given in Imdād al-Muftīn; the translation of one Persian passage from it is as follows:

It is not that in India brothers withheld their sisters' shares in imitation of the Hindus. The real reason is that when Muslim sisters saw and heard of the customs and usages of the Hindus, then in the surge of the love that sisters bear their brothers they gave up their share of the inheritance and did not take it, of their own free will; and the words of renouncing any claim and of waiving their right have been on the lips of their womenfolk from the beginning down to this day. They say with one voice that it were better for such a sister's tongue to be cut out than that she should utter a claim to her share. This is well known and famous in the Hindi language among the sisters of all the respectable families of this country, and the matter has reached the level of mass transmission. So this waiving has become a general custom and a settled practice, and it is not contrary to any explicit text.

في الأشباه والنظائر: وذكر الشيخ الإمام المعروف بخواهر زاده: أن حق الموصي له وحق الوارث قبل القسمة غير متأكد يحتمل السقوط بالإسقاط.

(Fatāwā Dār al-ʿUlūm Deoband, vol. 2, p. 480).

For further detail see: (Jawāhir al-Fiqh: 4/404, إقامة العرف مقام الثبوت في سقوط بعض الحق بالسكوت, and Fatāwā ʿAzīzī, p. 620).

Where, however, some Muslims in imitation of other communities have seized their sisters' rights and made them waive them under compulsion, this is a coerced waiver and the right does not lapse by it — as is the practice in certain parts of India.

Consider what is recorded in Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah:

To take anything from someone, or to make use of it, requires his glad consent. So if at any time the circumstances make it clear that a person gave permission for his property to be used under some pressure or out of embarrassment, and that in his heart he is not content with it, such permission will not be counted as permission; indeed it will not even be lawful for the other person to use the property.

Bear in mind too that to deprive daughters of their inheritance, and for the sons to divide among themselves the share the daughters should receive from the estate, also falls under this same ruling. It is gravely forbidden and is an injustice against the sisters. To discharge the lawful right of the daughters (the sisters) — and of every other heir besides them — is absolutely necessary and binding. The division of inheritance is a divine law; to act in accordance with it is a source of great merit and reward, and there is a severe warning of the Fire for violating it, and so on. (Fatāwā Raḥīmiyyah: 5/507, Maktabat al-Iḥsān, Deoband).
