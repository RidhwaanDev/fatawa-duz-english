### TITLE
Selling five-rand coins with a difference in amount

### QUESTION
Since our former head of state (Nelson Mandela) has turned 90, the Reserve Bank has issued five-rand coins bearing his portrait. They have issued this coin only in a limited quantity. If it is obtained from the banks of South Africa its value is just five rand, but abroad as much as a thousand rand can be obtained in exchange for it, because it carries his portrait, he is famous, and people collect such things. So may I buy these coins here and sell them? Is this permissible or not?

### ANSWER
In the case in question, if it is sold with a difference in amount for a currency other than rand, it is entirely permissible and correct. And if it is sold for a larger number of rand, then the scholars differ: in the view of some muftis it is permissible, because it does not possess the standing of a real medium of price. Hence, acting upon the position of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) and Imām Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him), there is scope for it. Al-Hidāyah states:

ويجوز بيع الفلس بالفلسين بأعيانهما عند أبي حنيفة رحمه الله وأبي يوسف رحمه الله وقال محمد رحمه الله لا يجوز

ولهما أن الثمنية في حقهما تثبت باصطلاحهما إذ لا ولاية للغير عليهما فتبطل باصطلاحهما وإذا بطلت الثمنية تتعين بالتعيين. (الهداية:، باب الربا، ۳/۸۱).

Fiqhī Maqālāt states:

Acting upon the position of Imām Abū Ḥanīfah (may Allah have mercy on him) and Imām Abū Yūsuf (may Allah have mercy on him) can be envisaged in those coins which are sought after in themselves, as material objects. You will have seen that some people have the habit of collecting the coins and currency notes of various countries; their purpose in collecting them is not exchange or sale, nor to gain a profit through them, but they collect them merely as a historical keepsake, so that in time to come, when the currency is withdrawn, it remains with them as a memento. To all appearances, in this kind of currency scope can be found for declaring exchange with a difference in amount permissible, acting upon the position of these two imāms. (فقہی مقالات: ۱/۳۷) ۔

And if he buys it in its capacity as a medium of price as well — as when new notes are sometimes obtained in a lesser amount in exchange for old notes — this too is permissible in the view of some muftis.

Consider what Niẓām al-Fatāwā states:

A note is neither measured by volume nor by weight; rather it is counted by number, and therefore exchanging it with a difference in amount is permissible. (نظام الفتاوی، کتاب المعاملات، ۱/۲۹۹) ۔

Fatāwā Farīdiyyah states:

Question: If a person sells a thousand-rupee note for a ten-rupee note, is this permissible?

Answer: Since both these notes are of paper, neither of silver nor of gold, this exchange is not impermissible in the sharīʿah, nor is it interest. Since their intrinsic worth is negligible while their conventional and official value is considerable, this exchange will be forbidden by convention, not forbidden by the sharīʿah. In the same way, exchanges of dollars, visas, tickets and paper currency with a difference in amount will be forbidden by convention, not forbidden by the sharīʿah. (فتاوی فریدیہ: ۲/٦۷۷) ۔
